What to Pack—and Leave Behind—for Spain's 800-Kilometer Camino de Santiago

September 07, 2026

Planning for the legendary 800-kilometer Camino de Santiago pilgrimage is less about collecting the right gear and more about mastering the art of letting go.

  • Gear choice: Soft running shoes invite plantar fasciitis on rocky paths; proper high-cut hiking boots sized 10mm larger than normal are essential to combat foot swelling.
  • The packing trap: Toe socks and excessive gear like walking poles often cause friction and useless weight rather than preventing blisters.
  • Fit over brand: A backpack's brand matters far less than fitting your torso length so the weight rests on your hips, not your shoulders.
  • Financial reality: Budget roughly 1,500,000 won (about $1,100) total, averaging €40 to €80 a day, to allow for comfort and flexibility.

The Non-Negotiables: Footwear and Fit

The romance of the pilgrimage fades the moment foot pain sets in. While online lists often recommend lightweight running shoes, seasoned hikers know that rough terrain demands ankle support and proper arch protection. Buying shoes a full size larger is also non-negotiable; feet inevitably swell and slide forward over hundreds of kilometers, making exact-fit shoes a recipe for lost toenails. Similarly, chasing trends like toe socks often backfires by creating extra friction. Instead of over-engineering blister prevention, the simplest rule on the trail is keeping feet dry by airing them out at every break.

Weight management is just as critical as footwear, starting with how a pack sits on the body. Carrying a bag without measuring your torso length turns a light load into an exhausting burden, as improper weight distribution strains the shoulders instead of the hips. Pilgrims should also skip packing bulky everyday toiletries and gadgets from home, buying cheap consumable basics like petroleum jelly locally instead. Reducing bag weight is ultimately about freeing the mind to focus on the landscape.

Budgeting and the Mental Pace of the Trail

Financial stress can ruin a journey just as quickly as physical pain. While it is possible to scrape by on €40 a day, pinching pennies to the point of starvation defeats the purpose of the trip. Setting a flexible budget allows pilgrims to enjoy local food and comfortable accommodations—such as pre-researched private albergues (pilgrim hostels)—without guilt. Ultimately, the biggest obstacle on the Camino isn't the physical distance, but the invisible weight of competition and impatience. Success on the trail comes from walking at your own pace, shedding the urge to rush, and treating the journey as a personal experience rather than a race.
