Samsung Eyes Record $730 Billion Profit Milestone as Governance Doubts Drag Shares

November 02, 2026

Samsung Electronics is projected to generate an unprecedented cumulative operating profit exceeding 1,000 trillion won (roughly $730 billion) across 2026 and 2027, yet its stock remains depressed by global semiconductor panic and persistent criticism over weak shareholder returns.

  • Unprecedented profit outlook: KB Securities forecasts Samsung will log 609 trillion won ($445 billion) in operating profit in 2026 and 640 trillion won ($468 billion) in 2027, bolstered by High Bandwidth Memory (HBM4) supply contracts signed at prices 150% higher than the previous generation.
  • Global tech rout spooks Seoul: Despite robust fundamentals, Samsung’s share price has tumbled toward the 60,000-won mark, pulled down by a 7.2% drop in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and Intel’s 28% single-day plunge amid doubts over Big Tech's artificial intelligence monetization.
  • The corporate governance bottleneck: Domestic analysts and investors argue that Samsung's management is failing to address the structural "Korea discount," hoarding capital rather than offering proactive shareholder returns like U.S. competitors.
  • Capital rotates to heavy industry: As chip volatility tests retail nerves, institutional interest is shifting toward defensive manufacturing, highlighted by shipbuilder Hanwha Ocean securing a 357.5 billion won ($261 million) medium landing ship contract from the U.S. Navy.

Earnings Potential Clashes with Shareholder Frustration

On paper, Samsung's medium-term trajectory looks formidable. The tech giant has reportedly locked in high-margin HBM4 deals with major global clients, positioning itself to rebound after initially trailing SK Hynix in the AI memory race. Shipments beginning in the fourth quarter should cement record earnings. Yet, foreign funds have continued offloading spot shares, unnerved by international media reports questioning the firm's technological moat and aggravated by a leadership team that treats investor communication as an afterthought.

This valuation gap illustrates why structural market reforms remain urgent. While the current administration under President Lee Jae-myung has sought to restore rational economic policymaking following the governance disruptions of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure, Korea's top conglomerate still operates under an insular legacy mindset. Without aggressive share buybacks or transparent dividend strategies to cushion macro downturns, multi-billion-dollar profit projections fail to protect everyday shareholders from market shocks.

Divergent Flows and Emerging Defense Angles

Despite heavy selling in the cash market, smart money is preparing for a cyclical turn: foreign investors have quietly maintained net-long positions in domestic futures contracts, signaling confidence that the current tech panic is an overreaction. At the same time, Korea's broader market narrative is branching beyond silicon. Hanwha Ocean's historic warship win via Philadelphia's Philly Shipyard—alongside HD Hyundai's push to establish U.S. shipbuilding bases by 2029—proves that Seoul's industrial champions can capture substantial value as critical defense partners to Washington, offering resilient alternatives while semiconductors bottom out.
