South Korea’s Ruling Party Pushes Special Counsel Bill on Prosecutorial Abuse, Triggering Opposition Filibuster Threat

November 05, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea unilaterally passed a controversial special counsel bill through the parliamentary judiciary committee on November 4, setting off a fierce legislative standoff with the opposition.

  • What happened: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea pushed the "Manipulated Prosecution Special Counsel Bill" through the Legislation and Judiciary Committee, preparing for a full floor vote despite fierce resistance from the main conservative opposition People Power Party.
  • What it covers: The expanded bill mandates an independent counsel to investigate alleged targeted probes, evidence fabrication, and abuse of prosecutorial power during Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure, as well as investigations involving former President Moon Jae-in and the West Sea official shooting incident.
  • What it changes: The People Power Party announced an all-out filibuster starting November 5, labeling the bill an unlawful legislative rampage designed to shield ruling party figures and undermine judicial independence.
  • Who is affected: The gridlock threatens to paralyze the National Assembly, stalling negotiations on the country’s 821 trillion won (about $615 billion) budget for next year.

Political Warfare over Judicial Reform and Retaliation

The battle over the special counsel bill highlights deep structural distrust in South Korea’s justice system. The ruling Democratic Party argues that the prosecution service weaponized its powers during Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure to eliminate political rivals through forced testimony and biased charges. By establishing an independent special counsel, progressive lawmakers aim to re-examine controversial past probes and dismantle remnants of political prosecution.

Conversely, the opposition People Power Party views the move as a partisan shield intended to derail ongoing judicial proceedings involving President Lee Jae-myung and wipe away past offenses. With the bill's scope expanded to cover probes into the Moon Jae-in administration, conservatives argue that the ruling party is using its legislative majority to rewrite judicial outcomes and dismantle constitutional checks and balances.

Budget Deadlock and Broader Legislative Fallout

The confrontation extends far beyond prosecutorial reform, threatening to derail critical government operations. With the opposition planning to filibuster all upcoming plenary items, parliamentary business faces near-total paralysis. Crucially, negotiations over the government's proposed 821 trillion won (about $615 billion) national budget—including a contested 162 trillion won (about $120 billion) future growth fund—are now caught in the crossfire.

As both parties dig in ahead of the full floor vote, the legislative impasse leaves pending public livelihood bills in limbo. Unless a last-minute compromise is reached, the widening rift risks paralyzing governance and stalling key economic policy debates in the National Assembly.
