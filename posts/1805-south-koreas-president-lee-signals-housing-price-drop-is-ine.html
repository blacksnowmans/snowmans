South Korea’s President Lee Signals Housing Price Drop Is Inevitable Despite 90-Week Rally

November 01, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung has issued a direct warning to property speculators, sharing an article predicting an inevitable fall in Seoul housing prices and framing current borrowing pain as a necessary "period of endurance" to deflate real estate bubbles.

  • What happened: President Lee publicly endorsed analyses forecasting a decline in Seoul real estate on October 31, using social media to declare that the current asset price trajectory is unsustainable.
  • The context: Media outlets noted that Seoul apartment prices have recorded 90 consecutive weeks of gains, but the administration views this trend as a lagging bubble left over from former President Yoon Suk-yeol’s debt-fueled growth policies.
  • What it changes: The direct presidential intervention signals that the government will double down on credit restrictions and demand-suppression measures instead of offering short-term stimulus to bolster transactions.
  • Who is affected: Highly leveraged property investors face rising downside risk, while prospective homebuyers and young adults are being asked to endure borrowing constraints in exchange for long-term housing affordability.

A Direct Signal to Pop the Seoul Housing Bubble

Executive signaling in South Korean real estate is usually cautious, making President Lee’s social media post a rare, aggressive effort to reshape market psychology. By explicitly framing price drops as an inevitable step toward "asset market normalization," Lee is challenging the long-held belief that Seoul real estate is an untouchable investment. The message serves as a stark warning to leveraged buyers that the administration will not step in with deregulation or artificial market propping.

Major news outlets and critics from the People Power Party, South Korea's main conservative opposition party, pointed to statistics showing 90 straight weeks of price rises to argue that the president's stance is out of touch with market data. However, the administration views these metrics as residual inertia from Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure, during which easy credit pushed household debt levels higher. Rather than turning back to short-term stimulus to shield approval ratings, Lee is signaling a firm resolve to let the correction play out.

Political Risks and the "Period of Endurance"

Framing the current market squeeze as a "period of endurance" is a calculated political gamble. High borrowing costs and strict loan limits are placing immediate financial pressure on middle-class households and young buyers who purchased homes near the peak. Yet, Lee’s strategy rests on the calculation that enduring short-term pain is the only way to resolve the country's deep-seated structural housing crisis.

If the administration maintains strict policy consistency, the combination of tight credit and clear executive messaging aims to dismantle speculative expectations. The ultimate goal is to force excess inventory onto the market and bring property prices back to levels manageable by actual household income.
