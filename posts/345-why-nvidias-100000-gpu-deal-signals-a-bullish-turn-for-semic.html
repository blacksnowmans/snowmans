Why NVIDIA’s 100,000 GPU Deal Signals a Bullish Turn for Semiconductors

September 07, 2026

  • The "AI Bubble" is largely market noise: Despite fears of "circular trading" and economic instability, institutional capital remains heavily committed to real infrastructure.
  • Physical infrastructure is scaling up: A massive contract between AI robotics firm Figure AI and data center provider Nscale for 100,000 NVIDIA chips proves that the transition to human-labor replacement is already underway.
  • The SoftBank-OpenAI axis: Deep financial integration between Masayoshi Son’s SoftBank (a Japanese tech investment conglomerate) and OpenAI (the creator of ChatGPT) ensures continued, multi-billion dollar investment in AI data centers.
  • Korean opportunities: Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix—South Korea’s dominant memory chipmakers—are poised to benefit as the global AI arms race forces competitors to buy high-performance, advanced packaging chips regardless of macroeconomic volatility.

The current malaise in the stock market, characterized by retail investors panic-selling at the sight of "red" (market-decline) accounts, stems from a fundamental misunderstanding of the current cycle. While media outlets and skeptics warn of an "AI bubble" fueled by debt, they are ignoring the massive, tangible capital pouring into physical infrastructure. The fear surrounding U.S. employment data and geopolitical tensions in the Strait of Hormuz is largely noise. In reality, the U.S. labor market remains structurally tight due to immigration policies, and geopolitical shocks, while unsettling, rarely destroy the fundamental cash-generating power of major tech firms like Microsoft and Google.

The Reality of Infrastructure: 100,000 GPUs

The most concrete evidence that the AI revolution is far from "bursting" is the recent partnership between Figure AI and Nscale. By securing 100,000 NVIDIA "Vera Rubin" platform units—each costing approximately $18 million (₩25 billion)—these companies are not playing with speculative tech; they are building the hardware backbone required for the next generation of humanoid robotics. This level of infrastructure spending is essentially the "factory construction" phase of the AI era, mirroring the massive data center rollouts seen previously by giants like Amazon and Google.

This creates a permanent tailwind for South Korean semiconductor leaders. Even as "hyperscalers" (massive cloud computing providers) attempt to develop custom internal chips, the sheer intensity of the global competition ensures that demand for high-end memory and advanced packaging will remain high. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are the essential suppliers in this ecosystem. Furthermore, while Samsung's stock has faced criticism over its dividend policy and lack of aggressive share buybacks, its current valuation—trading at roughly 3 times price-to-earnings—presents a compelling entry point for long-term investors.

Geopolitics and the "Tariff" Mirage

Recent anxiety over potential U.S. tariffs on imported semiconductor chips is another example of market overreaction. The logic of Washington’s trade policy is not to punish manufacturers, but to force them to build facilities on U.S. soil. Once companies like Samsung and SK Hynix finalize their U.S. plant expansion blueprints, these "tariff risks" will evaporate. The market is currently punishing these stocks based on fears that have clear, manageable, and inevitable corporate solutions.

For the busy investor, the strategy is clear: stop watching the daily employment reports and start watching the capital expenditure. When billions of dollars are being poured into building physical data centers, that is not a bubble—it is the construction of a new economy. Those who can look past the noise and identify where the money is actually flowing will be the ones to capture the gains of the coming cycle.
