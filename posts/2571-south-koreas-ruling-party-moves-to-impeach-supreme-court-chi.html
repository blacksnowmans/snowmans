South Korea’s Ruling Party Moves to Impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice

November 21, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has officially adopted a party platform to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, initiating an unprecedented political assault on the head of the nation's judiciary.

  • What happened: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) finalized a decision during a closed-door caucus to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, establishing a special task force led by lawmaker Yang Bu-nam to pass the motion before the end of the year.
  • What triggered it: The breaking point was a series of Supreme Court decisions—most notably the remanding of a case involving former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo, alongside constitutional grievances regarding President Lee Jae-myung's remand trial—which the DPK condemned as politically biased.
  • The reaction: The main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP), led by Han Dong-hoon, slammed the move as "political madness" and a retaliatory assault on judicial independence, vowing total resistance.
  • Who is affected: The decision pits the ruling legislative majority directly against the judiciary, while straining internal DPK cohesion as dissenting lawmakers voice concern over public backlash.

A Direct Clash Between the Ruling Majority and the Judiciary

The ruling DPK's decision to pursue impeachment marks a point of no return in its feud with the Supreme Court. Party leadership argues that recent decisions, particularly the court's remand of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo's trial, demonstrate that the judiciary under Cho has abandoned legal neutrality. By targeting four core legal and constitutional violations, the DPK is framing this move as a necessary measure to restore judicial justice and public trust.

However, the opposition is launching an all-out counteroffensive. PPP leader Han Dong-hoon harshly criticized the DPK leadership, asserting there is a zero percent chance the Constitutional Court would ever uphold the impeachment. Describing the effort as an unconstitutional act of retaliation, PPP lawmakers have taken to the streets in Seoul to mobilize public opposition, signaling an intense battle ahead.

Internal Friction and the Constitutional Hurdle

Despite the DPK leadership's push to pass the motion before the end of the year, party unanimity is not absolute. DPK lawmaker Gwak Sang-eon publicly broke rank to oppose the party line, warning that impeaching the head of the judiciary simply over disagreeable court rulings risks setting a dangerous precedent and turning public sentiment against the party.

While the DPK explores tactical cooperation with minor opposition groups like the Cho Kuk Rebuilding Party, its ultimate challenge will be legal proof. To succeed beyond a legislative vote, the ruling party must convince the Constitutional Court that Chief Justice Cho committed clear constitutional violations—a high legal bar that could easily backfire politically if the impeachment is ultimately thrown out.
