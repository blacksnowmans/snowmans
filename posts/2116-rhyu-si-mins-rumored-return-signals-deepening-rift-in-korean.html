Rhyu Si-min’s Rumored Return Signals Deepening Rift in Korean Politics

November 08, 2026

A recent public opinion poll showing overwhelming support for political commentator Rhyu Si-min’s sharp criticism of President Lee Jae-myung has ignited widespread speculation that growing external pressure may force the veteran liberal figure back into active politics.

  • Public backing for criticism: A survey by media outlet Cheonji Ilbo revealed that 55.2% of South Koreans—including 59.6% of respondents in their 20s—found Rhyu’s public critiques of President Lee Jae-myung’s administration to be appropriate.
  • Presidential staff turnover: The abrupt resignation of Hong Ik-pyo, Senior Presidential Secretary for Political Affairs under President Lee, has removed a vital moderate voice within the presidential office, opening the door for loyalist replacements.
  • Targeted investigation risks: Pro-government online commentators are calling for law enforcement to reopen investigations into Rhyu regarding the past Sillajen biotech fraud scandal, raising concerns of a politically motivated probe.
  • Backlash among core supporters: Targeting Rhyu, a symbolic intellectual closely linked to the legacy of late President Roh Moo-hyun, threatens to severely alienate the core base of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea.

Growing Discontent and Presidential Isolation

The strong public endorsement of Rhyu’s critiques—particularly among voters in their 20s—exposes deep-seated fatigue with President Lee Jae-myung’s top-down governance style. Rather than heeding constructive feedback from traditional liberal allies, the presidential office has doubled down on direct communication channels like official YouTube broadcasts, risking an echo chamber effect. The departure of Senior Secretary Hong Ik-pyo further highlights an administration sidelining seasoned negotiators in favor of aides willing to execute executive orders without friction.

The Danger of Pushing Rhyu Back to the Front Lines

Reports that pro-administration figures might push the Serious Crime Investigation Agency to target Rhyu over the Sillajen affair represent a high-stakes political gamble. For many base voters in the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, Rhyu carries the emotional legacy of late President Roh. Weaponizing state investigative power against such a prominent liberal icon could spark intense backlash, invoking deep historical trauma within the progressive movement.

While Rhyu has repeatedly stated his desire to remain a writer rather than a candidate, an aggressive legal assault could leave him with little choice but to re-enter the political arena. Just as former President Moon Jae-in was thrust into leadership by political circumstance, a targeted prosecution of Rhyu may end up creating the very political heavyweight the administration seeks to avoid.
