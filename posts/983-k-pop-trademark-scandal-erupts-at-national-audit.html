K-Pop Trademark Scandal Erupts at National Audit

October 11, 2026

A heated confrontation at the National Assembly’s Culture, Sports and Tourism Committee audit has shifted from a procedural dispute over leaked documents to allegations that the government effectively handed over the intellectual property of a major K-pop project to a private entertainment agency.

  • The spark: Opposition People Power Party (the main conservative party) lawmaker Bae Hyun-jin revealed private contracts between the government and JYP Entertainment, leading to a fierce row over how the documents were obtained.
  • The project: The controversy centers on "Phenomenon," a massive upcoming K-pop festival. Critics allege that JYP Entertainment—led by producer Park Jin-young—registered the event's trademark in its own name rather than the state's.
  • The fallout: Government officials and the ruling Democratic Party (the party of President Lee Jae-myung) accused the opposition of "stealing" sensitive documents, while the opposition argues the ruling bloc is using "procedural" complaints to cover up the privatization of national assets.

A Clash of Priorities: Privacy vs. Public Interest

The audit descended into chaos when a Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism official admitted under pressure that the ministry had been unable to maintain control over sensitive contract documents. While the ruling Democratic Party focused on the "unauthorized" nature of the leak, demanding to know how the documents reached lawmaker Bae Hyun-jin’s office, the opposition pivoted to the substance of the deals themselves.

Bae Hyun-jin defended her staff, labeling the ruling party’s focus on the leak as a "smokescreen." She argued that if the government signs "shoddy" contracts that favor private firms, it is the duty of the National Assembly to bring those facts to light, regardless of the retrieval method. She criticized the ministry for attempting to frame her aides as "thieves" to escape accountability for what she characterized as a humiliating giveaway of national intellectual property.

Policy Failure or Political Theater?

The core of the issue is the role of the Presidential Committee for Popular Culture Exchange, which counts JYP’s Park Jin-young as a co-chair. Lawmaker Yoon Yong-geun (People Power Party) pointedly asked why a state-sponsored committee would allow a private company to hold the rights to a trademark that should be a national asset. While the government claims it is a safeguard to prevent third-party squatting, critics see a clear case of conflict of interest and corporate favoritism.

This audit highlights a troubling lack of administrative rigor in the current government's cultural policy. Whether it is the confusion over the committee’s legal status or the casual handling of tax-funded projects, the transparency gap is widening. As the committee and ministry struggle to explain why they ceded control to a private entity, the "leak" of these contracts is being used as a convenient distraction from a failure of public oversight. Ultimately, the public is left wondering why, in the name of promoting K-pop, the state has allowed private capital to dictate the terms of national cultural initiatives.
