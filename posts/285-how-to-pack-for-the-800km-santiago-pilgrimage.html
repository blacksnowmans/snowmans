How to Pack for the 800km Santiago Pilgrimage

September 07, 2026

The secret to successfully completing the Camino de Santiago—the legendary 800km pilgrimage across Spain—is not how much you bring, but how much you are willing to leave behind.

  • The 10% Rule: Experienced pilgrims maintain that your backpack should weigh no more than 10% of your body weight.
  • Weight vs. Health: Every unnecessary gram adds exponential strain on your joints, particularly on downhill stretches where impact force can reach three times your body weight.
  • The "Just in Case" Trap: Most gear failures are caused by packing items for hypothetical scenarios rather than daily realities.
  • Foot Care is Priority One: Proper footwear and a strategy for blister prevention are more important than any other piece of gear.

The Art of the Minimalist Pack

On the Camino, your backpack is your mobile home. While modern services offer luggage transport, the core experience—and the physical challenge—remains carrying your essentials over 20–30km daily. The goal is to reach a base weight of about 6kg (roughly 13 lbs), including water and snacks. Invest in a pack with a high-quality "suspension" back system and a sturdy hip belt to distribute the load off your shoulders. Use packing cubes to organize your life, and prioritize gear that serves multiple functions, such as a bandana that can act as a towel, pillowcase, or sun shield.

Footwear and the "Engine" of the Pilgrim

Your feet are the engine of your journey. Many pilgrims make the mistake of wearing heavy, rigid hiking boots, which cause unnecessary fatigue on paved city sections. A lighter, sturdy trekking shoe is generally preferred. A critical strategy is the "double sock" method: wearing toe-socks as a base layer under cushioned merino wool socks to eliminate friction. Once you reach an albergue (a specialized hostel for pilgrims), change immediately into supportive sandals. Avoid flimsy flip-flops; your feet need structural recovery after hours on the trail. If you have a history of foot pain, bring custom insoles—replacing your gear is easy in Spain, but replacing an injured foot is not.

A Mindset of "Less is More"

The most common mistake is packing as if you are going to a remote wilderness. The Camino is a series of well-connected European villages; you can buy soap, food, and water along the way. Your biggest challenge will not be the trail, but the psychological burden of "just in case" items. By shedding the weight of extra clothing and gadgets, you strip the journey down to its essence. Remember, the goal of the pilgrimage is not just to reach the destination, but to simplify your routine to the point where you can focus on yourself. If you are struggling, don't blame your strength—check your pack, adjust your routine, and remember that the most successful pilgrims are not the strongest, but those who care for their own well-being best.
