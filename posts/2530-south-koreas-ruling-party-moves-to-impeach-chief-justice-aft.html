South Korea’s Ruling Party Moves to Impeach Chief Justice After “Insurrection” Ruling

November 20, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party has officially launched a bid to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, accusing the Supreme Court of granting a "judicial pardon" to a key official involved in the illegal actions of the previous administration under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

  • Supreme Court Retrial: On November 19, the Supreme Court partially overturned a 15-year prison sentence for former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo, signaling a move toward leniency for his role during the previous administration.
  • Impeachment Resolution: The Democratic Party (DP), South Korea's ruling party, declared the ruling a "judicial death sentence" and resolved to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae for failing to uphold constitutional order.
  • Legislative Escalation: DP Chairman Kim Min-seok announced the immediate formation of an impeachment task force, with plans to finalize the motion as a formal party platform by November 20.
  • Opposition Backlash: The People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition party, condemned the move as an "institutional coup" and an act of political revenge against the judiciary.

A Crisis of Judicial Legitimacy

The fury within the ruling Democratic Party stems from the Supreme Court's decision to send former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo’s case back for retrial. Han had been sentenced to 15 years for his involvement in the failed martial law attempt and alleged insurrection led by Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was later impeached and remains imprisoned. For the DP and President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, this ruling is seen not as a technical legal correction, but as a betrayal. They argue that the court is providing a "narrow legal loophole" for collaborators who stood by or assisted as the constitutional order was threatened.

DP Chairman Kim Min-seok characterized the court’s logic as "an excuse for historical opportunists." The ruling party’s perspective is that the judiciary has a duty to issue a stern warning against high-ranking officials who fail to protect the constitution during national crises. By moving to impeach the Chief Justice, the DP is signaling that it will not tolerate what it perceives as a retreat into "opportunistic neutrality" by the nation’s highest court regarding the crimes of the previous era.

Institutional Warfare and the Path Forward

This move has triggered a fierce confrontation between the legislative and judicial branches. The opposition People Power Party has reacted with alarm, with figures like Rep. Han Dong-hoon warning that the DP is attempting to "paralyze the judiciary" to suit its political agenda. The opposition views the impeachment of a Chief Justice—unprecedented in modern South Korean history—as a "suicide bomb" maneuver that destroys the principle of judicial independence. They argue that disagreement with a court verdict should never be grounds for removing the head of the judiciary.

However, the DP is not acting alone; the Rebuilding Korea Party, a minor liberal party, has already signaled its support, creating a solid bloc against the court. As the ruling party prepares to formalize the impeachment motion, the country faces a significant constitutional test. If the motion passes the National Assembly, it will effectively decapitate the Supreme Court leadership, potentially freezing high-profile trials and deepening the divide in a nation still grappling with the legal aftermath of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's residency. For the ruling party, the risk of institutional chaos is secondary to the necessity of "cleansing" a judiciary they believe has failed its most fundamental test of constitutional defense.
