Jensen Huang’s Sudden Exit and Price-Fixing Allegations Rattle Global AI Chip Market

November 16, 2026

The sudden resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang following allegations of GPU price manipulation has sent shockwaves through the global tech industry, unsettling major South Korean memory suppliers and disrupting AI infrastructure investments.

  • What happened: Jensen Huang abruptly resigned amid a personal scandal and allegations that Nvidia used circular financing transactions to artificially inflate GPU prices, leaving a leadership vacuum right before the company's high-stakes earnings report.
  • Supply chain crisis: A $34 billion pipeline of data center projects in Southeast Asia—originally set up to bypass U.S. export controls on AI chips to China—is on the brink of collapse amid Huang's exit and intensifying U.S. congressional scrutiny.
  • Market fallout: Prominent investor Michael Burry expanded short bets against Nvidia in favor of rivals AMD and Broadcom, while Elon Musk warned that Nvidia risks losing critical deals without Huang's leadership.
  • Impact on Korea: Top South Korean chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix face margin pressures and supply chain adjustments, even as Korean retail investors bought roughly $325 million (450 billion won) of Nvidia stock during the sell-off.

Nvidia's Crisis Hits Korea's Semiconductor Giants

The sudden leadership vacuum at Nvidia strikes at a delicate time for South Korea's tech sector. Memory giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, which supply High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) to Nvidia, face potential delays in technology roadmaps and price renegotiations if Nvidia adjusts its capital deployment. As Washington accelerates probes into Southeast Asian trade channels used to circumvent export limits on China, Korean manufacturers are likely to face heightened U.S. pressure to increase domestic production inside the U.S., adding substantial cost burdens.

Despite these mounting headwinds, South Korean retail investors—known locally as "Seohak Ants" for their aggressive buying of foreign, mostly U.S., tech stocks—have poured roughly $325 million into Nvidia shares during the dip. This retail frenzy contrasts sharply with institutional moves, where figures like Michael Burry are betting heavily against Nvidia's stock in expectation of a sustained downturn.

A Market Shift Toward a Multipolar AI Ecosystem

The circular financing allegations threaten to undercut the profit margins that fueled Nvidia’s dominance. If regulatory enforcement curtails artificial GPU price inflation, the cost structure for global AI deployments will require a massive reset. The key beneficiaries of this turmoil are rival chipmakers AMD and Broadcom, who stand to capture market share from tech buyers eager to diversify their hardware suppliers away from a single, unstable vendor.

With U.S. lawmakers tightening export enforcement and Nvidia's core strategies upended, the era of absolute market control by a single dominant supplier appears to be unraveling. For South Korean chipmakers, navigating this transition will require balancing shifting American policy demands against volatile demand from global cloud providers.
