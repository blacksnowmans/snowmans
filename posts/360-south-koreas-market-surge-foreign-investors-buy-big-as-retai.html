South Korea’s Market Surge: Foreign Investors Buy Big as Retail Traders Panic

September 07, 2026

  • Foreign dominance: While the KOSPI (Korea Composite Stock Price Index) rallied on September 7, foreign investors injected roughly $2.2 billion (2.9 trillion KRW) into the futures market, effectively driving the index higher.
  • Retail exodus: Conversely, retail investors sold off about $6.3 billion (8.5 trillion KRW) worth of stocks, continuing a trend of panic-selling during periods of volatility.
  • Structural shifts: AI-driven demand for hardware and the massive power deficit in the U.S. are turning infrastructure players like Doosan Enerbility and Doosan Fuel Cell into long-term growth stories rather than temporary themes.

The recent rebound in the KOSPI was fueled by a "Goldilocks" moment for AI hardware. As new AI models move toward autonomous task completion, the demand for NVIDIA's Blackwell chips and the electricity required to power the data centers running them has become a matter of national security for the U.S. and a goldmine for Korean hardware suppliers.

However, the market remains on thin ice. While AI creates a compelling narrative, looming macroeconomic pressures—specifically concerns over U.S. interest rate adjustments and inflation data—mean any sign of a policy shift could trigger a rapid cooling. The market is currently rewarding real, tangible infrastructure capabilities—such as power grid equipment and nuclear plant technology—over speculative, "soft" AI plays.

Infrastructure is the New Blue Chip

The "shortage" narrative is the defining force of the current market. In the U.S., a massive queue for power grid connections has forced regulators to audit projects, separating viable infrastructure plans from speculative fluff. This environment has turned companies like Doosan Enerbility (a major power plant and nuclear energy equipment provider) and Hyosung Heavy Industries (a key electrical grid component manufacturer) into essential players. They are no longer just cyclical stocks; they are the backbone of the AI power revolution.

Similarly, the semiconductor equipment sector is seeing a shift toward high-tech precision. Firms like Pierotics are gaining traction with "Through Glass Via" (TGV) technology, which aims to replace traditional silicon substrates in packaging. As tech giants like Samsung Electronics—the world’s largest memory chip manufacturer—and Samsung Electro-Mechanics double down on these next-gen processes, the suppliers who can deliver zero-defect results are becoming the new power brokers in the semiconductor supply chain.

For the average investor, the lesson from this week’s massive sell-off is clear: retail investors are repeatedly being shaken out of their positions by the same fear-driven impulses. While the market may face short-term technical resistance near the 60-day moving average, the underlying trend is being set by policy-driven capital and structural demand. Those who continue to trade based on fear are likely to remain on the sidelines, while those who identify the firms providing the physical "plumbing" for the AI era are better positioned for the long term.
