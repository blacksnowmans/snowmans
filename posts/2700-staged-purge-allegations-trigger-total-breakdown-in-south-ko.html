Staged Purge Allegations Trigger Total Breakdown in South Korea's Conservative Opposition

November 24, 2026

Internal warfare inside the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition party, reached a boiling point on November 23 after a party insider revealed that the disciplinary expulsion of former leader Han Dong-hoon was intentionally timed to distract from criminal proceedings against imprisoned former president Yoon Suk-yeol.

  • The Staged Purge: A member of the PPP Ethics Committee alleged that Han Dong-hoon’s expulsion was prearranged to coincide with prosecutors demanding the death penalty for Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, using Han as a political sacrifice to cover up headline news.
  • Factional Clash: Han accused PPP leader Jang Dong-hyeok of orchestrating a targeted purge, while Jang dismissed the leak and insisted the party leadership’s disciplinary push remains justified.
  • Taming Local Leaders: The PPP announced a system overhaul for evaluating incumbent local government heads, requiring a 50 percent weight on polls of dedicated party members—a move critics call a bid to control future election nominations.
  • Deflection Tactics: Amid mounting scandal, party leadership attempted to shift focus by launching unverified attacks against the ruling Democratic Party, claiming 43,000 members of the controversial Shincheonji religious group had infiltrated the ruling party.

Factional Feuds and Staged Discipline

The revelation that the PPP Ethics Committee coordinated its disciplinary calendar with court dates exposes a alarming breakdown in formal party governance. Rather than operating as an independent body, the committee was allegedly deployed to manage the news cycle surrounding Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s trial by sacrificing one of the party’s most prominent figures. This orchestrated action demonstrates how deep factional fractures have grown within the conservative camp, as leadership prioritizes internal score-settling over procedural fairness.

Despite the mounting pressure, former leader Han Dong-hoon has vowed to return and fight from within, framing himself as the victim of an illegitimate leadership while aiming his public rhetoric at President Lee Jae-myung’s ruling Democratic Party. However, open warfare between Han and Jang Dong-hyeok severely weakens the PPP's ability to function as an effective opposition force, reducing party strategy to defensive spin and mutual destruction.

Centralizing Power and Political Distraction

Beyond internal disciplinary fights, the leadership’s new policy requiring local government heads to be evaluated with a 50 percent weight on dedicated party member polls is a clear move to enforce factional control. By prioritizing party sentiment over administrative performance, Jang is laying the groundwork to tame incumbents ahead of candidate selection for upcoming local elections. Local officials will now be forced to focus on pleasing party bosses rather than serving their broader public constituencies.

To cover these internal power grabs, the PPP leadership has resorted to reckless attacks against the ruling party, including unsupported claims of religious infiltration. This combination of staged internal purges, nomination taming, and conspiracy claims highlights a conservative party caught in a spiral of self-absorption, leaving it ill-equipped to challenge the Lee Jae-myung administration on actual policy issues.
