South Korean Politics Paralyzed by Internal Feuds and Judicial Impeachment Battles

November 24, 2026

South Korea’s political landscape is currently defined by a dual crisis: a breakdown in leadership discipline within both major parties and a high-stakes standoff over the impeachment of the nation’s Chief Justice.

  • Ruling Party Turmoil: Kim Min-seok, leader of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), faces a staggering 67.4% disapproval rating on his 100th day in office, exacerbated by public shouting matches over alleged cult infiltration.
  • Opposition Purge Allegations: The People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, is reeling from whistleblower claims that party leadership orchestrated a "planned purge" to expel prominent member Han Dong-hoon.
  • Judicial Warfare: The DPK is finalizing an impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, a move the opposition labels a "suicide mission" designed to shield President Lee Jae-myung from legal scrutiny.
  • Third-Party Rebranding: The Rebuilding Korea Party, led by Cho Kuk, is seeking to remove the leader’s name from its official title to transition into a more mainstream "value-based" party as its polling hits 10%.

Leadership Crises in the DPK and PPP

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the party of President Lee Jae-myung, is struggling to maintain a unified front. Leader Kim Min-seok’s "ABC Plan"—intended to focus on "Aptitude, Breadth, and Communication"—has been sidelined by internal bickering. A recent leadership meeting descended into chaos over rumors that members of Shincheonji, a controversial religious group often viewed as a cult in Korea, had infiltrated the party. The public nature of these disputes, involving verbal warnings and tearful exits by senior officials, has sent Kim’s approval ratings into a tailspin, signaling a significant loss of political momentum for the ruling party.

Simultaneously, the opposition People Power Party (PPP) is facing its own existential threat. Whistleblowers within the party’s Ethics Committee allege that PPP leader Jang Dong-hyeok conspired to expel Han Dong-hoon to coincide with court rulings related to Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol (the former president currently serving a prison sentence). Han has characterized these revelations as a "planned execution," demanding accountability from the party leadership. This internal warfare suggests the PPP is more focused on settling internal scores than acting as an effective check on President Lee Jae-myung’s administration.

The Politicization of the Judiciary

The most consequential battle is the DPK’s aggressive push to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. By targeting the head of the judiciary, the ruling party risks being seen as undermining the separation of powers to protect its own interest. The PPP has retaliated by accusing the DPK of "bulletproof impeachment"—a term used to describe using legislative power to block criminal trials. To further complicate the legal landscape, the PPP has threatened to sue individual judges for "law distortion," a move that suggests both sides are now viewing the courtroom as just another branch of the campaign trail.

This judicialization of politics is reaching a breaking point. When court rulings are met with impeachment motions and judges are personally targeted by political parties, the institutional credibility of the South Korean legal system is the primary casualty. For international observers and markets, this instability signals a high level of political risk, as the traditional "rules of the game" are being rewritten by whichever faction holds the legislative majority. The rise of the Rebuilding Korea Party, which is attempting to broaden its appeal by dropping Cho Kuk’s name from its branding, suggests that some voters may be looking for an alternative to this relentless two-party warfare.
