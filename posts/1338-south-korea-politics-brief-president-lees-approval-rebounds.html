South Korea Politics Brief: President Lee’s Approval Rebounds to 37.3% as Minor Opposition Pushes Chief Justice Impeachment

October 20, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating rebounded to 37.3 percent after a three-week slide, even as the ruling Democratic Party continues to trail the conservative opposition within the margin of error amid escalating clashes over the judiciary and public budgets.

  • Presidential rebound: President Lee’s approval rating rose 2.1 percentage points to 37.3 percent in a Realmeter survey released on October 19, buoyed by visible visits on everyday economic concerns and tough talk on violent crime.
  • Deadlocked parties: The main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) polled at 40.9 percent against the ruling Democratic Party’s 36.2 percent, maintaining a tight contest within the survey's 4.7-percentage-point margin of error.
  • Judicial offensive: The progressive minor Rebuilding Korea Party unveiled an impeachment motion against Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae over the judiciary's posture following the martial law crisis under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, demanding the ruling Democrats formally join the push.
  • Fire service budget feud: Lawmakers clashed over emergency services funding following controversial remarks by Democratic lawmaker Choo Mi-ae and a fatal factory fire in Bucheon, drawing attention to the fact that the central government covers just 8.6 percent of nationalized fire service costs.

Judicial Confrontation Puts the Ruling Party in a Bind

The Rebuilding Korea Party’s aggressive bid to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae places the ruling Democratic Party in an uncomfortable strategic bind. Minor progressive factions argue that the judiciary compromised its independence by responding passively during the unconstitutional martial law attempt by Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was later impeached and imprisoned. However, Democratic floor leaders are moving cautiously, aware that impeaching the head of the judiciary risks triggering a severe public backlash and grinding parliamentary business to a halt.

Conservative lawmakers have already denounced the impeachment motion as a partisan ploy to neutralize the courts. For the ruling party, managing this balance is delicate: moving forward could swallow the legislative agenda in an endless institutional brawl, but hesitating risks alienating reform-minded supporters who demand immediate accountability for key institutions that operated under the previous administration.

Voter Fatigue Keeps Both Camps on a Tight Leash

President Lee’s modest approval bump provides temporary breathing room, but the broader numbers point to an electorate that remains deeply skeptical of both major parties. Despite losing the presidency and being relegated to the opposition after Mr. Yoon's ouster, the People Power Party continues to cling to roughly 40 percent support, sustained by loyal conservative voters even as it struggles with internal disputes over candidate appointments.

At the same time, the ruling Democratic Party has failed to capitalize on its governing mandate to build a commanding lead. Persistent cost-of-living pressures, high youth unemployment, and unforced political missteps—such as the recent public spat over local fire service finances—have left ordinary citizens unconvinced by legislative infighting. With neither side commanding broad public trust, voters are adopting a watchful stance, waiting to see whether politicians can deliver tangible relief rather than recycled partisan warfare.
