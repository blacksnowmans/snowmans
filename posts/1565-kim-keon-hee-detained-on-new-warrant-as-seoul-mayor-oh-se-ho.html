Kim Keon-hee Detained on New Warrant as Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon Cleared on Appeal

October 28, 2026

South Korea’s Supreme Court has issued an additional arrest warrant to keep Kim Keon-hee in custody over evidence tampering concerns, even as Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon secured a dramatic appellate acquittal on campaign finance charges tied to power broker Myung Tae-kyun.

  • Additional warrant for Kim: Following a 44-minute remote hearing from the detention facility, the Supreme Court ruled that Kim must remain detained to prevent flight and the destruction of evidence, rejecting her claims of poor health.
  • Mayor Oh cleared: The Seoul High Court overturned a lower-court ruling against Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, a leading figure in the conservative opposition People Power Party, dismissing a 10 million won (about $7,500) fine that would have stripped him of his mayoralty.
  • Special counsel appeal: Special Prosecutor Min Joong-ki’s team announced an immediate appeal to the Supreme Court, disputing the appellate bench's decision to discard Myung's testimony regarding 33 million won (about $25,000) in alleged illicit polling payments.
  • Legislative clash: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) initiated a parliamentary investigation targeting Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, accusing elite judicial networks of showing political favoritism toward conservative leaders.

Two Divergent Rulings Deepen Political Divisions

Kim Keon-hee, the wife of ousted and currently imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, sought release during her hearing from the Seoul Southern Detention Center by citing declining health. The court remained unmoved, emphasizing the severity of allegations involving illicit meddling in government appointments and party nominations. Despite loyalist rallies outside the detention facility, the judiciary determined that detaining Kim remains necessary while trials unfold regarding her involvement with political broker Myung Tae-kyun.

In stark contrast, Mayor Oh Se-hoon exited the appellate court completely vindicated. The presiding judge dismantled the prosecution's case by ruling that Myung’s testimony—the cornerstone of the initial conviction—lacked credibility. The decision completely reverses a lower-court finding that Oh had received unauthorized polling assistance to defeat internal rivals ahead of the 2021 Seoul mayoral by-election, instantly revitalizing his political future and positioning him once again as the conservative camp's chief presidential contender.

Backlash Mounts Against the Judiciary

The juxtaposed rulings have triggered intense fury from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, which views Oh's swift acquittal as evidence of institutional bias within senior appellate judges residing in Seoul's affluent legal districts. Party leadership argues that while evidence against political figures is routinely brushed aside, ordinary defendants face unbending scrutiny.

With the DPK moving forward on an unprecedented parliamentary inquiry into Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae's administrative conduct, the battle lines have expanded far beyond the courtroom. As the special counsel takes Oh's case to the highest court and lawmakers prepare to probe the judiciary, South Korea’s legal system faces an intensifying test of public trust.
