Global Far-Right Alliances Fracture as Populist Momentum Stalls

November 06, 2026

The global far-right front is showing deep structural cracks as European and Asian hardliners distance themselves from the fading influence of the American MAGA movement.

  • European nationalist parties are pivoting away from Donald Trump following U.S. election setbacks that proved right-wing populism is no longer a guaranteed path to power.
  • In the United Kingdom, Nigel Farage of Reform UK (a right-wing populist party) faced public embarrassment after France’s National Rally (RN) denied his claims of a high-profile strategic meeting.
  • France remains gripped by civil unrest with 600,000 protesters taking to the streets, creating a volatile vacuum that both far-left and far-right factions are attempting to exploit for political gain.
  • In Japan, the Liberal Democratic Party (the country's dominant conservative force) is navigating internal realignments while grappling with a surge in Russian-backed disinformation and online xenophobia.

The "MAGA" Divorce and the Price of Opportunism

The "MAGA" brand has rapidly shifted from a political asset to a liability on the global stage. As U.S. Democrats secured key victories, European leaders who once touted their ties to Trump are now scrambling to protect their own domestic prospects. This isn't a shift in core values, but a cynical survival tactic. These parties, built on a foundation of "nationalism first," are proving that they are fundamentally incapable of maintaining long-term international alliances when the political winds shift. When the "strongman" umbrella disappears, the movement reverts to infighting and isolation.

This fragmentation highlights a critical weakness in far-right logic: their rhetoric of national security and economic sovereignty often collapses when faced with real-world crises. In Europe, the energy price hikes and inflation sparked by global instability—partially fueled by the very hardline foreign policies these groups supported—have left them with no answers for their constituents. They have spent years blaming incumbent governments for economic pain, only to realize that their ideological allies were the ones stoking the fire.

Lessons for Korea’s Conservative Opposition

For observers in Seoul, these developments serve as a cautionary tale for the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition. The global trend shows that relying on "hate-driven" politics and unverified social media narratives is a dead end. In Germany, the Alternative for Germany (AfD) recently faced humiliation after spreading Russian-made fake news, a tactic that mirrors the disinformation often seen in the darker corners of Korean political YouTube. As the PPP attempts to rebuild its identity, it must avoid the trap of tethering itself to failing global populist movements.

The current political climate under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung demands a more substantive approach to governance than what the far-right currently offers abroad. The era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol demonstrated the dangers of a leadership style that prioritizes division over policy. If the global far-right’s current collapse proves anything, it is that voters eventually prioritize their "dinner table" economics over conspiracy theories. For any conservative movement to survive, it must offer a sustainable national vision rather than just a revolving door of convenient enemies.
