Great-Power Realpolitik Takes Center Stage at APEC as North Korean Denuclearization Gets Sidelined

November 12, 2026

A scandal involving Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and a draft agenda focused on tri-lateral AI nuclear control between the US, China, and Russia threaten to push North Korean denuclearization off the priority list at the upcoming APEC summit in Shenzhen.

  • What happened: Reports emerged that US President Donald Trump was briefed aboard Air Force One regarding Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s alleged connection to a suspected Chinese spy, throwing Huang’s participation in the Shenzhen Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit into doubt while Tesla CEO Elon Musk remains set to attend.
  • What it changes: The US, China, and Russia are drafting joint statements centered on artificial intelligence nuclear control and tech supply chains, effectively sidelining North Korean denuclearization talks from top-tier summit priorities.
  • Who is affected: South Korea's Lee Jae-myung administration faces an increasingly complex diplomatic landscape as major powers engage in direct transactional diplomacy, while Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi faces isolation after Chinese President Xi Jinping signaled he will avoid a bilateral meeting over her previous Taiwan remarks.

Great Power Politics Sidelining Northeast Asian Priorities

The upcoming APEC summit in Shenzhen marks a clear return to direct, transactional diplomacy among major powers. With Washington, Beijing, and Moscow focusing on managing AI-driven military capabilities and stabilizing global supply chains, traditional multilateral concerns are taking a back seat. For South Korea, the absence of North Korean denuclearization from primary summit discussions is a clear signal that the world's largest powers are prioritizing their own strategic competition and technology controls over Northeast Asian security agreements.

Simultaneously, technology security has emerged as both a flashpoint and a bargaining chip. The espionage warnings surrounding Nvidia underscore how deeply microchip supply chains are intertwined with national security. Even as US Ambassador to China Nicholas Burns warned that economic security equals national security, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent left the door open for AI dialogue. Washington is attempting a delicate balancing act: maintaining strict technology controls while using figures like Elon Musk to preserve commercial supply chain stability.

Strategic Implications for South Korea and Japan

This great-power reshuffling leaves regional allies vulnerable to being sidelined. Japan is already experiencing this shift: Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi faces diplomatic freezing from Beijing over her past comments on Taiwan, sparking concerns in Tokyo over "Japan passing" as US-China-Russia dynamics dominate the agenda.

For President Lee Jae-myung’s administration in Seoul, these developments demand a careful diplomatic balancing act. South Korea must protect its key semiconductor and battery industries from the crossfire of US-China tech rivalry while ensuring its leverage on Korean Peninsula security isn't eroded by great-power bargains.
