Inside South Korea's Polling Scandal: How Data Manipulation Shook the Political Establishment

September 03, 2026

    Court rulings have confirmed that a series of political polls managed by political broker Myung Tae-kyun were intentionally fabricated and used as a tool for backroom political deals.
  • Key witness Kang Hye-kyung's detailed testimony and internal records proved that the Mirae Korea Research Institute manipulated 14 separate opinion polls to favor specific candidates.
  • Providing free polling data has been formally classified as a violation of the Political Funds Act, equivalent to offering illicit political bribes in exchange for nomination influence.
  • The scandal exposes deep vulnerabilities in South Korea's electoral system, showing how corrupted data can weaponize public opinion against voters.

When Polls Become Poison: The Anatomy of a Setup

Recent court verdicts have pulled back the curtain on South Korea's political machinery, revealing a reality stranger than fiction. At the center of the scandal is Myung Tae-kyun, the de facto head of the Mirae Korea Research Institute, who treated public opinion surveys not as a compass for democracy, but as a clay to be molded. Rather than capturing genuine public sentiment, data sets were tweaked and sample values adjusted to serve targeted political ambitions. When unverified or free polling data is funneled to specific campaigns to secure political leverage or nominations, it ceases to be research — it becomes a structured, high-stakes bribe.

What makes this case particularly troubling is the reliance on insiders like Kang Hye-kyung, whose courageous testimony pierced through layers of political deception despite personal risk. Alongside corroborating statements from figures like Kim Tae-yeol and Seo Myung-woong, the court established that Myung operated at the nexus of backroom influence peddling. Yet, the broader question remains: was Myung an isolated operator, or merely a convenient cog in a much larger machine operated by unseen power brokers? As voters head toward future elections, this scandal serves as a stark warning to look beyond flashy graphs and demand strict transparency from those who weaponize data for political gain.
