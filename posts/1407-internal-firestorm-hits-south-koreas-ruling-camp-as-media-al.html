Internal Firestorm Hits South Korea’s Ruling Camp as Media Ally Turns on President Lee Jae-myung

October 22, 2026

Just 24 hours after police raided his offices, prominent pro-government YouTuber Lee Jong-won declared a complete rupture with President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, threatening to unveil high-level corruption inside the presidential office.

  • What happened: Lee Jong-won, head of the influential political media outlet Sisa Tapa, severed ties with the administration and pledged to release evidence proving Blue House political secretaries accepted illicit golf outings and entertainment from regional real estate developers.
  • What it changes: The high-profile betrayal exposes a catastrophic fracture within the ruling progressive coalition, as former loyalists turn into whistleblowers and accuse the administration of abusing state power while purging internal critics.
  • Who is affected: President Lee Jae-myung, presidential aides facing corruption allegations, and prominent progressive leaders—including Gyeonggi Governor Choo Mi-ae—who are increasingly targeted by internal factional attacks and questionable agency probes.

Cracks in the Progressive Camp

The rift represents far more than an emotional outburst by an online commentator. Lee Jong-won was once a key media voice mobilizing the base that helped bring President Lee Jae-myung to power. Now, he claims the administration is using state authorities to suppress internal dissent while overlooking corruption within its inner circle. By targeting Blue House officials over alleged bribery in Cheongju, North Chungcheong Province, the whistleblower threatens to tear down the moral high ground that the ruling Democratic Party of Korea has claimed over the main opposition, the conservative People Power Party.

This fallout underscores a wider purge within the governing camp. Commentators and former allies argue that hyper-loyalist "New Lee" factions are actively ostracizing established party heavyweights, including former minister Choo Mi-ae and key figures aligned with past progressive administrations. Critics have drawn scathing comparisons, warning that aside from martial law, the current government is beginning to mirror the coercive habits seen during the presidency of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was impeached and is currently in prison.

Constitutional Strains and the Illusion of Stability

Compounding the political storm is a growing legal controversy over government norms. The administration recently drew sharp backlash for routinely having the heads of investigative bodies—such as the National Police Agency and the newly created Serious Crime Investigation Agency—attend Cabinet meetings alongside the president. When legal experts called the practice an unconstitutional threat to investigative independence, President Lee raised eyebrows by citing Google’s Gemini AI model on social media to defend the arrangement, drawing heavy criticism across the political spectrum.

While recent National Barometer Survey (NBS) polls show President Lee maintaining a headline approval rating of 61%, underlying data reveals deep vulnerability. Disapproval of housing and real estate policies stands at 51%, while 52% of young voters under 30 identify as politically unaligned. Meanwhile, the newly budgeted 6.8 billion won (about $5 million) Serious Crime Investigation Agency is facing accusations of jurisdictional overreach for launching targeted investigations into figures like Governor Choo. If the administration continues to ignore economic grievances while relying on agency pressure and factional purges, it risks severe political backlash from its own core base.
