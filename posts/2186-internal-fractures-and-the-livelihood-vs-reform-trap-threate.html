Internal Fractures and the 'Livelihood vs. Reform' Trap Threaten South Korea's Ruling Party

November 10, 2026

Despite holding the presidency under Lee Jae-myung, South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is undermining its own governing momentum through bitter factional infighting and a false dichotomy between economic livelihoods and structural reform.

  • The reform stall: Moderate party factions are using bread-and-butter "livelihood" issues as an excuse to avoid friction with entrenched interests, halting institutional and regulatory overhaul while delivering few tangible economic gains.
  • Distorted factional politics: The battle between pro-Lee loyalists and anti-Lee critics has degenerated into power-seeking and sycophancy, with opportunists claiming absolute loyalty to President Lee while hostile political YouTubers drive party discourse.
  • Echoes of past failures: The current impasse mirrors earlier missteps in modern progressive history—notably the 2000s centrist caucus "Angaemo" (Gathering for Stable Reform)—where calls for cautious compromise alienated core supporters and drained political capital.
  • The demand on leadership: Party insiders argue that President Lee and veteran leadership must decisively cut ties with aggressive online fringe groups and pursue structural reform and economic stability as complementary goals.

The Trap of Moderation and Digital Echo Chambers

Within the DPK, an increasingly vocal camp argues that pursuing aggressive institutional reforms risks public backlash and distracts from pressing economic concerns. In practice, however, this cautious posture functions as a convenient shield against necessary confrontation. Treating bread-and-butter welfare and systemic reform as mutually exclusive is a strategic blunder; genuine economic stability cannot take root without dismantling the structural monopolies and systemic corruptions that strangle fair competition. By chasing compromise for the sake of quiet governance, the ruling party risks alienating its core reformist base while appearing indecisive to the broader electorate.

This inertia is worsened by lawmakers' growing subservience to hyper-partisan YouTube channels and political influencers. Rather than exercising independent strategic judgment, members of parliament increasingly calibrate their positions to placate militant online subscriber bases. Conflating the outrage algorithms of new media with the sentiment of the general public turns elected politicians into hostages of fandom politics, degrading the party's capacity for coherent policy formulation.

Purging Sycophancy to Secure the Mandate

Equally destabilizing is the toxic dynamic emerging along factional lines. While internal dissenters are routinely branded as adversaries, the real threat to President Lee’s administration stems from sycophants cloaking personal ambition in unquestioning loyalty. Historically, corrupt flatterers embed themselves within the inner circle, shielding leadership from critical ground realities. The administration must learn from the earlier Moon Jae-in presidency, when senior figures had to actively marginalize aggressive fringe groups that weaponized personal attacks and poisoned the party's public standing.

If the DPK is to avoid squandering its tenure in power, President Lee Jae-myung and senior leaders, such as lawmaker Kim Min-seok, must enforce internal discipline. This requires a structural overhaul of internal party communication, an outright rejection of malicious influencer-driven hate politics, and a resolute recognition that cleaning up institutional abuses is the fastest route to improving ordinary citizens' lives.
