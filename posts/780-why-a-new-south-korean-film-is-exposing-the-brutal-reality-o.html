Why a New South Korean Film Is Exposing the Brutal Reality of Caregiving

September 16, 2026

  • What happened: South Korean indie film "Cheoldeul Muryeop" (roughly translated as "As We Grow Up") has premiered in cinemas, drawing critical acclaim at the Busan International Film Festival for its brutally honest take on family caregiving.
  • What it changes: The movie shatters the traditional East Asian ideal of self-sacrificing filial piety, reframing long-term caregiving not as a noble duty, but as an exhausting, soul-crushing labor forced onto families by systemic welfare gaps.
  • Who is affected: Millennial and Gen Z South Koreans caught between aging parents and demanding care structures, alongside policymakers facing criticism over outsourced eldercare.

For decades, popular culture across East Asia has leaned heavily on tear-jerking narratives about the unconditional warmth of family. But a new conversation around director Jung Seung-oh’s semi-autobiographical film is stripping away that romanticized veneer. Drawn from Jung’s own grueling, seven-year experience caring for an ailing father after decades of estrangement, the film uses dark comedy and biting realism to tackle a taboo subject: the resentment, frustration, and quiet desperation that come with looking after elderly family members.

The Nightmare of 'Filial Duty' Without a Safety Net

At the heart of the film’s buzz is its refusal to coat suffering in sentimentality. Through painfully relatable setups—including a satirical "filial contract" that outlines a child's legal obligations to an aging parent—the movie exposes how the sacred bond of family frequently devolves into a cold, transactional cage. When a daughter in the film bitterly asks her father, "So is this your way of getting revenge on me?" it captures the psychological toll borne by younger generations expected to sacrifice their youth, careers, and mental health.

What makes this indie release strike such a nerve is its pointed critique of South Korea's social safety net. By dumping the immense financial and physical burden of eldercare onto private households under the guise of "family values," the system routinely turns both patients and caregivers into hollowed-out versions of themselves. It’s a sharp, necessary reality check for a society that prefers to bury its elders' struggles behind closed doors rather than fund adequate public solutions.
