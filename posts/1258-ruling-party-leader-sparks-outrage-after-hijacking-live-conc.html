Ruling Party Leader Sparks Outrage After Hijacking Live Concert in Sunchang

October 18, 2026

A senior lawmaker from South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea is facing fierce public backlash after festival organizers abruptly cut off a singer's live concert to let him take the stage for a political greeting.

  • What happened: On October 15 at the Sunchang Fermented Sauce Festival in North Jeolla Province, the host interrupted vocalist HYNN (Park Hye-won) two songs into her set, teasing an appearance by an "enormous fan" who turned out to be Democratic Party Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok.
  • The takeover: Stage displays abruptly switched from the concert feed to Kim’s official portrait and bio, forcing the singer into the wings while the politician delivered self-serving remarks and name-dropped local figures and cabinet allies.
  • The reaction: Video of the incident racked up over 600,000 views in 12 hours, drawing intense ridicule across mainstream online communities like Theqoo, where commenters labeled the interruption an arrogant display of authoritarian protocol.
  • Who is affected: The ruling Democratic Party, which now faces accusations of high-handedness and cultural disrespect, undermining its standing with moderate voters.

Vanity Protocol Collides with Public Culture

Regional festivals in South Korea are funded largely by municipal tax dollars, intended to promote local culture and draw tourists. Yet organizers frequently hijack these platforms to curry favor with visiting political heavyweights. Parachuting a suit-clad politician onto a stage in place of a performer who was in the middle of a set exposes a deeply entrenched, old-school mindset: that the public's cultural enjoyment takes a back seat to the ego and protocol of visiting elites.

What compounded the audience's irritation was Kim's lack of tact. Rather than offering even a perfunctory nod to the artist whose microphone he had usurped, Kim launched straight into standard stump-speech rhetoric, praising local ties to Figures such as Minister Chung Dong-young and regional lawmakers. The transition from an energetic pop concert to partisan glad-handing left the crowd visibly stunned and the artist stranded backstage.

Erosion of the Democratic Party's Reform Image

The incident carries serious political baggage for the governing Democratic Party. Having regained power under President Lee Jae-myung after vehemently condemning the arrogance that marked the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the ruling camp promised a humble, citizen-first style of governance. Displaying this kind of high-handed privilege at a provincial festival makes the ruling leadership look indistinguishable from the authoritarian practices it once railed against.

While die-hard party partisans stayed conspicuously silent on community boards, the broader public reaction has been scathing. Treating an artist as an inconvenient opening act and citizens as a captive audience for regional politicking is precisely the kind of tone-deaf overreach that alienates young, culturally minded swing voters.
