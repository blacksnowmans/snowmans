South Korea’s Ruling Democrats Fracture Over Baseless Cult-Smear Allegations

November 04, 2026

Democratic Party lawmaker Kim Min-seok has triggered a fierce intra-party revolt after offering a closed-door apology for fabricating claims that internal rivals secretly colluded with Shincheonji, a controversial fringe religious sect.

  • What happened: During a party caucus, Kim conceded that an internal investigation yielded no evidence of a secret pact between anti-leadership party members and Shincheonji, yet he refused to release the probe’s findings or issue an open apology to rank-and-file members.
  • The backlash: Grassroots party members and senior figures, including Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee, condemned Kim’s emotional display as a cynical "martyr routine" designed to dodge legal fallout rather than restore the honor of falsely accused members.
  • Who is affected: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), whose approval ratings slipped to roughly 37% amid accusations that leadership loyalists are trading promises of future parliamentary nominations for silence.
  • What it changes: The scandal has derailed party cohesion at a critical juncture for President Lee Jae-myung’s reform agenda, igniting calls for an early party convention to replace the current leadership.

From Smear Campaign to the "Martyr" Playbook

During the party's recent national convention, Kim attacked internal critics by accusing them of forming an unholy tripartite alliance with anti-leadership factions, splitters, and Shincheonji. Dedicated party members who had paid dues and rallied for years suddenly found themselves branded as cultists. Yet instead of taking direct public accountability once the claims fell apart, Kim wept before lawmakers behind closed doors, offering to "take all the arrows" to preserve unity while keeping the investigation's final report buried.

This staged contrition backfired. Loyal junior lawmakers rushed to defend Kim online as a noble sacrifice, which only intensified the outrage among the party’s base. By framing the slanderer as the victim, party leadership added insult to injury, alienating key voting blocs and handing an unnecessary advantage to the conservative People Power Party, the main opposition.

Legal Defenses and Growing Demands for Resignation

Kim’s evasive posture appears less like leadership and more like legal self-preservation. South Korea’s Political Parties Act strictly penalizes the dissemination of false information to sway party elections—a standard reminiscent of the false statement rulings that saw Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol convicted. By claiming he acted on incoming reports rather than malice, Kim is seeking an escape hatch from legal exposure while effectively blocking internal task force investigators, such as lawmaker Park Sun-won, from presenting the full facts.

Worse, the dispute exposes deep dysfunction within the ruling camp. Whispers that party bosses are privately guaranteeing 2026 general election nominations have paralyzed critical debate, reducing lawmakers to passive cheerleaders. If President Lee Jae-myung’s administration is to retain public trust for judicial and legislative reforms, the ruling party cannot treat its grassroots supporters as collateral damage. Papering over manufactured smears with tears will not hold; sweeping personnel changes at the top may now be the only way forward.
