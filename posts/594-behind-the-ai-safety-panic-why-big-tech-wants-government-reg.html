Behind the AI Safety Panic: Why Big Tech Wants Government Regulation

October 02, 2026

Big Tech companies pushing loudest for strict artificial intelligence regulations are not trying to save humanity from extinction—they are building legal shields to protect their monopolies and dodge future liability.

  • The real agenda: Leaders like OpenAI and Anthropic are lobbying for pharmaceutical-style pre-approval systems to secure legal immunity before catastrophic AI failures happen.
  • The jobs myth: Recent U.S. Census Bureau data shows no measurable spike in unemployment among recent college graduates due to AI adoption, despite widespread panic.
  • The open-source split: Meta and open-source advocates are fiercely pushing back against market leaders, accusing them of using exaggerated doomsday scenarios to crush competition.
  • The cost of entry: As promotional pricing ends, premium AI tiers are surging—symbolized by OpenAI's new $500 (about 700,000 won) monthly plans—turning advanced tools into a luxury for corporate giants.

The global conversation around artificial intelligence is dominated by a false binary of utopian breakthroughs and dystopian joblessness. But beneath the breathless media coverage lies a cold economic chess game. When billionaire executives weep on television about human extinction, they are usually distracting the public from the fine print: closed ecosystems, government-sanctioned safety harbors, and the systematic elimination of smaller rivals.

This protectionist lobbying strategy mimics the pharmaceutical industry's FDA approval process. Tech giants want governments to bless their systems with official safety certifications, effectively trading compliance for total legal immunity when things go wrong. Meanwhile, the Donald Trump administration's hands-off deregulation stance leaves the market in a chaotic free-for-all, creating a widening chasm where only heavily capitalized players can survive the legal and computational costs.
