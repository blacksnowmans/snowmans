South Korea’s Property Tax Base Set to Rise in 2025 Despite Valuation Freeze

November 03, 2026

South Korea’s local governments have begun finalizing updated property valuations for 2025, signaling higher holding taxes for real estate owners in key markets even as the central government freezes official assessment ratios at roughly 69% of market value.

  • Market prices driving tax base: Although the government is holding official valuation ratios near 69%, rising real estate transaction prices in Seoul's northern and outer districts will automatically lift tax bases.
  • Public appeals open: Municipalities such as Yongin (4,740 land parcels) and Gunsan (1,023 land parcels) released updated valuations, opening a formal objection window through November 27.
  • Local housing supply crunch: Sejong City, South Korea's administrative hub, faces zero incoming apartment move-ins next year despite an influx of relocating civil servants, driving up jeonse (lump-sum rental deposit) costs.
  • Political tax reforms: The current Lee Jae-myung administration is tasked with repairing tax predictability after the volatile assessment policies seen during Yoon Suk-yeol's term in office.

Rising Tax Base Hits Seoul Homeowners

South Korea uses officially assessed property values (gongsi jiga) to determine local property taxes and the national Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax. While maintaining the assessment ratio near 69% aims to prevent artificial tax surges, recent price increases across Seoul’s outer districts—such as Seongbuk, Nowon, Gangseo, and Gwanak—mean actual tax burdens will rise regardless. For single-home owner occupants who bought during recent market shifts, rising assessed values bring higher taxes without a corresponding increase in cash income, raising concerns over household financial strain.

Local Supply Shortages and the Policy Road Ahead

Regional real estate dynamics present contrasting pressures. In Sejong City, an impending wave of relocating government officials collides with a total lack of new housing supply in 2025, pushing up rental costs and creating broader price instability. Meanwhile, prime areas in Gangnam remain locked under South Korea's strict "triple-regulation" zone rules, compounding holding tax liabilities for high-value property holders.

These pressures highlight the policy challenge facing President Lee Jae-myung's government. After the erratic assessment formulas used during Yoon Suk-yeol's time in office created widespread market confusion, the current administration must establish a transparent valuation framework. For the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, the key test will be delivering tax fairness on unearned capital gains while implementing targeted relief to protect primary homeowners from excessive fiscal stress.
