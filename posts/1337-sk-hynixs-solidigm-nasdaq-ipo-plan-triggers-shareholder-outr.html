SK Hynix’s Solidigm Nasdaq IPO Plan Triggers Shareholder Outrage and Governance Concerns

October 20, 2026

South Korean memory chipmaker SK Hynix is facing severe backlash over plans to list its US NAND flash subsidiary, Solidigm, on the Nasdaq—a move critics condemn as corporate governance abuse that dilutes parent-company shareholder value.

  • What happened: SK Hynix has selected major investment banks, including Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, to prepare a Nasdaq IPO for Solidigm, an enterprise solid-state drive (SSD) business acquired from Intel in 2020 for about $9 billion.
  • What it changes: A separate listing dilutes SK Hynix’s 100% stake in Solidigm—which accounts for over 15% of SK Hynix’s total operating profit—transferring future cash flows to new US investors while triggering a holding discount on the parent company.
  • Who is affected: SK Hynix retail investors face depressed valuation multiples despite record corporate earnings fueled by the artificial intelligence boom.

Regulatory Loopholes and the "Korea Discount"

South Korean capital markets have long suffered from the "Korea Discount," where domestic equities trade at lower valuation multiples than global peers due to poor corporate governance. A major driver of this discount is corporate parent companies carving out profitable subsidiaries for separate stock listings—a tactic infamously highlighted when LG Chem spun off its battery business as LG Energy Solution. Although South Korean financial regulators recently introduced guidelines to curb harmful physical spin-offs, SK Hynix is exploiting structural loopholes. Because Solidigm was an overseas acquisition rather than an internal corporate split, and because the IPO is aimed at Nasdaq, SK Hynix avoids mandatory domestic shareholder protections and exchange reviews.

The maneuver has sparked bipartisan criticism in South Korea. Ahn Cheol-soo, a lawmaker from the main conservative opposition People Power Party, publicly urged authorities to intervene, warning that stripping core assets from parent companies threatens the entire domestic stock market. Lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party, including Oh Gi-hyoung, have similarly demanded that the company justify how a separate listing protects existing shareholders.

Strategic Contradictions in the AI Era

Beyond corporate governance concerns, spinning off Solidigm directly contradicts SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won’s flagship strategy for the artificial intelligence era. SK Hynix has actively promoted a "Memory as a Service" (MaaS) framework, which promises customers customized, turnkey solutions bundling high-bandwidth memory (HBM), DRAM, and ultra-high-capacity enterprise SSDs. Decoupling the NAND flash business into an independently listed foreign entity fractures this integrated memory ecosystem at the precise moment AI data centers demand unified hardware architectures.

While defenders argue that a US listing will help raise funds to build local fabrication plants and mitigate US-China trade friction, SK Hynix's massive cash flows from HBM sales offer ample alternative financing options. By prioritizing a subsidiary IPO over shareholder protection, SK Hynix risks deepening investor distrust and reinforcing the structural headwinds holding back South Korean equities.
