OpenAI Cuts Costs by Two-Thirds as GPT-6.2 Astra Enters 3D Engineering

November 19, 2026

OpenAI has launched GPT-6.2 Astra alongside a companion model, Sol, slashing computing costs to less than a third of the previous generation while turning AI into a practical agent for 3D modeling, legal review, and scientific research.

  • Smarter at a fraction of the price: Released on November 18 just eight days after Fable 5.2, Astra raised overall benchmark scores by 5.2 points and science scores by 9 percentage points while using 22% fewer tokens on real projects and doubling legal task pass rates at one-sixth of Fable 5.2's cost.
  • An aggressive API price war: Astra input tokens drop 20% to $8 per million ($0.40 cached), while Sol trails Astra by just 0.5 points on code edits at one-fifth the cost; OpenAI also cut its smaller GPT-5.6 Luna model by 80% to $0.20 per million input tokens to undercut Google's Gemini Flash lineup.
  • From chatbot to 3D engineer: Ranked first in blind 3D evaluations ahead of Fable 5.2 and Sol, Astra writes Python physics scripts for Blender animations, turns architectural designs into buildable CAD layouts, and hits a 73% first-try success rate on 3D tasks.
  • Built-in budget awareness, with rough edges: In a test creating a virtual tour of a roughly 66-square-meter (20-pyeong) South Korean two-bedroom apartment, Astra picked the cheaper Kling 3.0 Turbo over flagship video generators to save money, though OpenAI's official demo game, "Meridian Sound Racing," still shows obvious AI-generated textures.

The Price-Cutting Race Behind the Hype

What stands out in this release is not raw intelligence alone, but how OpenAI is weaponizing efficiency. By having Sol rewrite its own serving kernel code—trimming end-to-end overhead by 20% and lifting token generation efficiency by over 15%—OpenAI is turning AI optimization into a self-reinforcing flywheel. Pricing Luna at $1.40 combined against Gemini 3.6 Flash's $9 signals that the frontier race is shifting from who builds the smartest model to who can supply reliable reasoning cheaply enough to power long, multi-step agent loops.

Why Imperfect Agents Still Win Enterprise Budgets

For individual creators, spending about $22 (30,000 won) on a single racing video or more than $700 (1 million won) on a complex multi-tool run still feels steep, especially when prompts exceeding 272,000 tokens double input rates and raw outputs still need cleanup. For engineering, design, and legal firms, however, $700 is a fraction of traditional human labor costs, making "good enough to iterate on" commercially viable overnight.

The clearest lesson from early tests is that standalone AI still hits a ceiling without orchestration. Astra shines not as a fully autonomous studio, but as a cost-conscious coordinator that routes tasks across Blender, specialized 3D tools, and human editors—giving professionals who know how to direct the final 20% a decisive edge.
