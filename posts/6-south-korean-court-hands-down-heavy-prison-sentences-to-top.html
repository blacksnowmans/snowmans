South Korean Court Hands Down Heavy Prison Sentences to Top Military Commanders for Martial Law Plot

September 24, 2026

  • A South Korean court has sentenced former Defense Security Command chief Yeo In-hyung to 18 years in prison for his role in plotting a failed martial law attempt.
  • Four other senior commanders received prison terms ranging from 9 to 15 years, including former Capital Defense Commander Lee Jin-woo.
  • The court ruled that the military leaders engaged in a premeditated conspiracy to paralyze constitutional institutions, backed by chilling phone notes detailing plans to manufacture security crises.

Military Commanders Fall Hard in Landmark Martial Law Verdict

The Seoul Central District Court delivered heavy sentences to South Korea's top military brass for their involvement in a conspiracy to subvert constitutional order. Yeo In-hyung, former head of the Defense Security Command—tasked with military counterintelligence—received the harshest penalty at 18 years, with judges citing his absolute lack of remorse and central role in the plot. Other high-ranking figures, such as former Capital Defense Commander Lee Jin-woo (15 years) and former Army Chief of Staff Park Ahn-su (10 years), were similarly punished for turning the military's power inward against the state.

This ruling marks a critical turning point for South Korea's judicial reckoning with the martial law crisis. The court's findings dismantle any narrative of isolated command overreach, establishing instead that key military leaders actively worked to subvert democratic governance. The severity of the sentences sends an unambiguous warning that the military must remain politically neutral and subordinate to civilian leadership.

Inside the Plot: "Noah's Ark" Scenarios and Manufactured Crises

The trial exposed chilling details from the personal phones of the accused commanders, revealing a meticulously planned conspiracy to manufacture national emergencies. Court documents highlighted notes written by Yeo and Lee outlining extreme scenarios labeled "minimum: security crisis" and "maximum: Noah's Ark," alongside plans to exploit drone incursions, cyberattacks, and border shellings to justify declaring martial law.

These revelations transform what might have seemed like disorganized political posturing into documented evidence of a premeditated plan to destabilize the country. By detailing how the commanders coordinated with top political figures like former President Yoon Suk Yeol and former Defense Minister Kim Yong-hyun to target lawmakers and electoral agencies, the court has locked down a permanent historical record of the conspiracy. While the legal battles will continue into higher courts, this verdict stands as a stark reminder of how fragile democratic institutions can be when subverted from within.
