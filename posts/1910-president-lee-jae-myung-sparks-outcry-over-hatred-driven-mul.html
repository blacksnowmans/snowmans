President Lee Jae-myung Sparks Outcry Over "Hatred-Driven" Multicultural Policy Review

November 03, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung has triggered a fierce backlash from both his supporters and the immigrant community by suggesting the abolition of support for multicultural families after endorsing a post from a right-leaning online community.

  • Targeting Multicultural Benefits: Lee retweeted a post from FM Korea (a male-dominated forum known for meritocratic and anti-immigrant sentiment) criticizing priority access for multicultural mothers at public postpartum care centers, promising to review or scrap such "preferential treatment."
  • Economic Contradictions: Critics argue the President is ignoring the vital role of foreign laborers, who make up about 3% of the workforce and sustain the "3D" (dirty, difficult, dangerous) sectors like agriculture and manufacturing.
  • Alienating the Base: While courting conservative youth, Lee labeled his own progressive supporters "irresponsible extremists" for demanding faster prosecution reforms, signaling a sharp departure from traditional party values.
  • Internal Power Struggle: Observers claim a "New Lee Jae-myung" faction is attempting to purge the legacy of former liberal presidents—Kim Dae-jung, Roh Moo-hyun, and Moon Jae-in—to consolidate power within the Democratic Party of Korea (South Korea's ruling liberal party).

A Shift Toward Populist Resentment

President Lee’s decision to signal-boost FM Korea marks a startling departure for a leader of the Democratic Party. By questioning the necessity of supporting multicultural families, Lee is tapping into a vein of populist resentment that blames immigrants for perceived inequities in social welfare. This rhetoric is particularly jarring given the recent Aricell battery factory disaster, where 18 of the 23 victims were foreign workers. These individuals sustain the Korean economy by taking risks locals avoid, yet the President’s recent comments frame them as a net drain on national wealth because they remit earnings abroad.

The President’s focus on a single grievance from an online forum—the ranking of multicultural families alongside national heroes for postpartum care—is being viewed as a "political blunder." Instead of addressing the structural labor shortages in rural and industrial zones, the administration appears to be stoking social conflict for short-term political gain among 2030-generation male voters. This strategy risks legitimizing xenophobia under the guise of "fairness" and "meritocracy."

Purging the Base to Build a "New" Party

Beyond policy, this incident highlights a growing rift within the ruling camp. President Lee’s administration appears to be pivoting away from the traditional inclusive values of his predecessors. By labeling loyalists who demand reform as "extremists" while validating the grievances of right-wing forums, Lee is effectively realigning his political foundation. Critics point to the rising influence of the Gyeonggi Dongbu Alliance—a controversial radical faction—suggesting that the President is surrounding himself with a "New Lee Jae-myung" guard that values ideological loyalty over the party's historical democratic principles.

This internal friction is compounded by what many see as an unstable leadership style. Lee’s frequent, emotionally charged social media interactions late into the night have led to concerns about his "loss of control." During the previous administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol (who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment), the Democratic Party championed human rights and inclusion. Now, as the ruling power, the party faces a crisis of identity. If the President continues to prioritize the volatile opinions of online communities over his core constituency, he risks a total collapse in public trust and a fractured party mandate.
