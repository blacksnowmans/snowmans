SK Hynix Smashes Records with 104 Trillion Won Revenue on AI Supercycle

October 28, 2026

South Korean semiconductor powerhouse SK Hynix posted historic third-quarter earnings, breaching the 100 trillion won quarterly revenue threshold for the first time as booming demand for artificial intelligence hardware propelled operating margins to an unprecedented 80%.

  • Record-shattering figures: Revenue reached 104.62 trillion won (about $78 billion), up 328% year-on-year, while operating profit hit 83.3 trillion won (about $62 billion) with an EBITDA margin of 84%.
  • High-value product pivot: Full-scale mass production of next-generation HBM4 (High Bandwidth Memory), a 30% sequential rise in server DDR5 sales, and strong demand for 321-layer TLC enterprise solid-state drives lifted DRAM average selling prices by the high-20% range quarter-on-quarter.
  • Massive shareholder return: The company confirmed a 100 trillion won (about $75 billion) capital return program through 2026, allocating over 30 trillion won to cash dividends and over 60 trillion won to share repurchases and cancellations.
  • Secured order backlog: Long-term supply agreements already account for more than 50% of total output for 2027 and 2028, with substantial customer commitments sold out through 2029.

Disciplined Supply and Unprecedented Margins

SK Hynix's blockbuster quarter signals that memory chips are no longer behaving like cheap, volatile commodities. By tying capacity expansions directly to the financial health and verified commitments of hyperscale clients—a play straight out of the foundry playbook used by Taiwan’s TSMC—the chipmaker has largely insulated itself from the sector’s traditional oversupply traps. The company closed the quarter with 98.7 trillion won (about $74 billion) in net cash and slashed its debt-to-equity ratio to just 5%, giving it an impregnable balance sheet to fund advanced 10-nanometer sixth-generation fabrication internally. Corporate leadership has also benefited from a calmer operating environment under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, which has emphasized stable regulatory footing and supply chain security for national strategic industries after the policy uncertainty that weighed on corporate spending during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure.

The Big Tech Reckoning and the Next Memory Tier

The primary risk to this momentum does not stem from internal execution, but from Silicon Valley. Because SK Hynix’s record earnings mirror the dizzying capital expenditure of cloud giants such as Google and Microsoft, any slowdown in end-user enterprise AI monetization could eventually prompt downstream order revisions. Management is countering this vulnerability by moving beyond standard memory sales into architectural co-design. By embedding custom SSD performance metrics directly into client workloads and developing next-generation memory hierarchies like High Bandwidth Flash (HBF), SK Hynix is working to cement its status as an indispensable infrastructure partner rather than an easily replaceable components supplier.
