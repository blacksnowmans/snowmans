Samsung’s Leadership Test Deepens as TSMC Hikes Packaging Prices

October 28, 2026

Four years into Jay Y. Lee’s tenure as executive chairman, Samsung Electronics faces an uphill battle to restore its technological dominance in memory and foundry while global rivals squeeze margins across the hardware sector.

  • Lee's fourth-anniversary scorecard: Samsung’s leadership faces mounting pressure to close the gap in next-generation high-bandwidth memory (HBM4) and turn around its lagging contract manufacturing (foundry) business amid internal security leaks and regulatory oversight.
  • TSMC’s pricing power: Taiwan's TSMC plans to hike advanced packaging prices by up to 20%, exploiting critical bottlenecks in packaging capacity (such as CoWoS) and passing higher production costs onto tech manufacturers.
  • Rising component costs squeeze devices: Higher semiconductor prices are triggering "chipflation," compressing device margins for Samsung's Galaxy smartphone division and panel makers like LG Display.
  • Domestic ecosystem push: The administration of President Lee Jae-myung is facilitating technology transfers from state energy and tech sectors to private suppliers, while specialized component firms like Dongwon Parts prepare for public listings.

Samsung's Dual Front: AI Foundry and Consumer Squeeze

Samsung’s integrated device manufacturer (IDM) model—long seen as an untouchable strength—is creating friction under current market dynamics. While Samsung needs soaring memory prices to revive its semiconductor balance sheet, those very costs are hurting its smartphone unit. The mobile division faces an awkward choice between trimming hardware specifications on mid-tier models or lifting retail prices in an already sluggish consumer market. A shareholder return program worth about $80 billion (110 trillion won) reflects management's urgency to reassure jittery investors while the core business navigates this structural trap.

Meanwhile, TSMC's reported move to raise advanced packaging fees by up to 20% underscores where the real leverage sits in the artificial intelligence supply chain. Bottlenecks in advanced 3D packaging mean hardware producers have little choice but to absorb price hikes, amplifying cost pressures across consumer electronics. Samsung’s foundry unit urgently needs breakthrough yields in advanced packaging to present itself as a viable alternative for global chip designers.

Geopolitics and the Grassroots Supply Chain

Beyond boardrooms, South Korean chipmakers remain constrained by the ongoing tech standoff between Washington and Beijing. Securing waivers and equipment to upgrade NAND flash fabrication plants in China remains an unresolved headache for both Samsung and SK hynix. In response, Korean industrial policy under President Lee is pushing to fortify the domestic supply chain, backing local clusters in Pyeongtaek and Yongin alongside municipal workforce training programs to reduce strategic vulnerabilities.

Despite cyclical anxiety, veteran institutional voices like Mirae Asset Financial Group Chairman Park Hyeon-joo argue that the AI hardware buildout is merely 10% to 20% complete. Yet even if long-term demand remains intact, South Korea's chip giant cannot rely solely on broader market tailwinds; it must deliver technical parity in HBM4 and foundry execution to reclaim its traditional edge.
