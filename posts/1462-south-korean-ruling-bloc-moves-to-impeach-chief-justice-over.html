South Korean Ruling Bloc Moves to Impeach Chief Justice Over Perceived Bias

October 26, 2026

South Korea’s ruling alliance has signaled a full-scale offensive against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, threatening impeachment and a parliamentary investigation over allegations of political bias and administrative incompetence.

  • The Rebuilding Korea Party, a minor progressive party, is demanding immediate impeachment, while the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DP) prefers a preliminary parliamentary probe to build a formal case.
  • Public anger surged after the second-instance acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, which the ruling bloc views as evidence of a "double standard" favoring conservative elites.
  • Chief Justice Cho, appointed during the administration of the now-imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, is being blamed for chronic trial delays and leaving Supreme Court seats vacant for over seven months.
  • The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition party, has dismissed the move as "judicial stalking" and an attempt to seize control of the court system.

The conflict represents a breaking point in the relationship between the administration of President Lee Jae-myung and a judiciary largely shaped during the previous era. For the ruling Democratic Party, the recent acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon served as proof that the courts remain a stronghold for the old guard. They argue that while progressive politicians face rigorous legal scrutiny, conservative figures receive "get out of jail free" cards. This perceived imbalance, combined with a seven-month vacancy in Supreme Court justice positions, has allowed the ruling bloc to frame the Chief Justice not as a neutral arbiter, but as a remnant of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s disgraced tenure.

However, the ruling camp’s strategy is split between speed and procedure. Cho Kuk, leader of the Rebuilding Korea Party, is stoking the flames by calling this the "last chance" for judicial reform, warning that any hesitation will lead to a counter-offensive by conservative judicial "cartels." Meanwhile, DP leadership, including Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok, is treading more carefully. They are opting for a parliamentary investigation first to secure public consensus and ironclad evidence before attempting the high-stakes gamble of a formal impeachment, which would ultimately be decided by the Constitutional Court.

A Structural Battle Over "Imperial" Judicial Power

The dispute goes beyond personal grievances against Cho Hee-dae; it is a fundamental challenge to the centralized power of the Chief Justice. In South Korea, the head of the Supreme Court holds immense authority over judge assignments, disciplinary actions, and personnel appointments nationwide. The ruling bloc argues that this "imperial" structure allows a single individual’s political leanings to paralyze the entire justice system. By pushing for a parliamentary investigation, the DP intends to expose the inner workings of this system and pave the way for legislation that redistributes administrative power within the courts.

Conversely, the opposition People Power Party views this as a direct assault on the separation of powers. They argue that the ruling party is using its legislative majority to intimidate judges who deliver unfavorable rulings. The PPP claims that the DP’s real goal is to create a "special counsel for manipulated prosecutions" to shield its own members from legal accountability. As the DP prepares to launch its investigation following the current audit season, the standoff is expected to paralyze the legislative agenda, turning the National Assembly into the primary battleground for the future of the Korean judiciary.
