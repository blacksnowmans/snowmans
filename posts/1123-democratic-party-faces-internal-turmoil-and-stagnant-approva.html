Democratic Party Faces Internal Turmoil and Stagnant Approval Ratings

October 14, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party (DP), the party of President Lee Jae-myung, finds itself battling a deepening crisis characterized by plummeting approval ratings, aggressive legislative infighting, and accusations of internal infiltration.

  • Approval Crisis: Senior DP lawmaker Choi Min-hee has openly admitted that the party’s approval rating has sunk to around 35%, signaling a loss of support from core voters.
  • Legislative Brawl: Tensions with the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) have reached a breaking point, with the DP considering legal and procedural measures to punish "hate speech" and insults in parliament.
  • Judicial Pressure: The DP has intensified its campaign against Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, demanding his resignation over alleged financial irregularities.
  • Security Breach Claims: The party is investigating reports that members of the Shincheonji religious group may have systematically infiltrated its membership ranks.

A Party Under Pressure

The Democratic Party is currently struggling to reconcile its legislative agenda with a growing sense of public disillusionment. While the party holds the presidency, the recent drop in approval to the 35% range reflects a failure to meet the expectations of its core support base. Lawmaker Choi Min-hee’s candid assessment—that the party cannot simply blame the media or the opposition for its woes—suggests a growing realization within the DP that they must pivot from political bickering to tangible policy achievements to avoid further alienation.

Compounding these external challenges is an internal security concern. The official investigation into allegations that the Shincheonji religious organization organized a mass enrollment of its members into the DP is a major headache. If true, it raises questions about the integrity of the party's internal decision-making and democratic processes, necessitating a swift and transparent cleanup to restore member confidence.

Legislative Stagnation Amid Toxic Rhetoric

The National Audit process, typically a tool for policy oversight, has devolved into a series of mud-slinging matches. Tensions between DP Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok and PPP lawmaker Joo Jin-woo have escalated to the point of criminal complaints. The DP is now proposing amendments to the National Assembly Act that would allow for the suspension of lawmakers who engage in "excessive" verbal abuse—a move intended to raise the floor of political discourse but one that threatens to further deepen the divide between the two parties.

Despite the toxic atmosphere, the DP is attempting to push forward on "livelihood" legislation. This includes efforts to curb deceptive advertising on platforms like Coupang and the introduction of a "Farmland Special Act" to distinguish between speculative land ownership and genuine farming interests. For President Lee’s administration, the path forward is narrow: it must move beyond the "cyber-wrecker" style of political bickering and demonstrate its competence through effective governance. Otherwise, the current cycle of scandal and infighting will likely keep its approval numbers stagnant as voters continue to grow weary of the political theater.
