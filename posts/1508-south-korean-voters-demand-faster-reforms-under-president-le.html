South Korean Voters Demand Faster Reforms Under President Lee Jae-myung

October 26, 2026

  • What happened: During a recent livestream hosted by political commentator Park Si-young, supporters of President Lee Jae-myung expressed both strong backing for his administration and growing frustration over the slow pace of reform.
  • The mood: While citizens are enthusiastic about the current political direction, they are demanding that the Democratic Party of Korea (South Korea's main liberal party) act more decisively to dismantle the legacies of the previous administration.
  • Key takeaway: The liberal base is calling for internal unity, warning that internal divisions and hesitation could jeopardize the gains made since the transition of power from the disgraced former leader, Yoon Suk-yeol, who is currently imprisoned.

A Grassroots Call for Decisive Governance

The livestream served as a barometer for the current political climate, highlighting a base that refuses to remain a passive audience. Viewers were vocal about their desire to see the Democratic Party—which now holds a dominant position in the National Assembly—leverage its power to produce tangible results. Comments from long-time supporters emphasized that they are not looking for lukewarm compromises; they are looking for radical, structural changes that reflect their desire for social justice and democratic restoration.

The feedback suggests a growing impatience with the current speed of administration. Several viewers pointedly criticized the party for failing to move fast enough on pending national tasks. For a base that spent years pushing for a change in government, the perceived caution of the current administration is increasingly seen as a source of frustration. There is a palpable fear that if the government fails to lead with conviction, it may lose the momentum required to heal the damage left behind during the years of Yoon Suk-yeol.

Unity and Resilience in the Face of Opposition

The broadcast also highlighted the active role of citizens in shaping political discourse and protecting media voices. When it was announced that prominent liberal intellectual Rhyu Si-min would be appearing on an upcoming program, the engagement in the chat surged, underscoring the deep emotional connection between the liberal base and its key figures. Participants viewed the protection of these voices as essential to maintaining the strength of the movement.

A Battle on Cultural Fronts

The struggle for the nation’s future is also playing out in the cultural sector. The film Assassins recently surpassed 3.3 million viewers, a significant milestone that the audience attributed to a grassroots effort to combat organized boycotts and sabotage by far-right groups. This achievement is being framed as a victory for civic solidarity—a sign that when supporters mobilize, they can overcome obstructionist tactics from political opponents.

Looking ahead, the message from the stream was clear: the liberal coalition must prioritize solidarity. Park Gi-won, a Supreme Council member who appeared on the broadcast, urged supporters to "not tear each other apart," but rather to stay united and protect key figures. For a constituency that has been through years of intense political strife, this call for internal cohesion is not just a slogan—it is viewed as the only strategy for surviving the long, difficult road of national reconstruction.
