Internal Friction: President Lee Jae-myung’s 'Extremist' Crackdown

October 06, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung is facing intense pushback from within his own party, the Democratic Party of Korea, after launching a series of aggressive social media attacks against what he labels "irresponsible extremists" among his supporters.

  • What happened: President Lee posted 19 messages in just five days on social media, using the term "irresponsible extremist" (or mu-geuk-pa) to describe party members who demand radical prosecutorial reforms.
  • The catalyst: The tension stems from disagreements over judicial appointments, specifically concerns that the president is favoring former prosecutors for key roles in the newly planned investigative agencies.
  • The reaction: High-profile party figures, including former Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae, have publicly pushed back, arguing that the president is framing legitimate policy critiques as personal ambition or internal sabotage.
  • The outcome: Despite a four-hour private meeting between the President and key legislator Jeong Cheong-rae to smooth things over, the internal rift remains unhealed as reformists continue to voice their concerns.

A Party Divided by Distrust

The core of this conflict is a fundamental breakdown in trust. President Lee appears frustrated, feeling that his efforts to manage the complex, often messy process of state governance are being undermined by "purist" factions within his party. To the President’s camp, these vocal critics are not just reformers—they are political liabilities who prioritize their own agendas over the broader strategy of the Lee administration.

However, many in the Democratic Party view the President’s rhetoric as a form of "gaslighting." Having lived through the political consequences of failing to properly vet officials during the era of the now-imprisoned former leader, Yoon Suk-yeol (who was ousted via impeachment), these reformers argue that their skepticism is a necessary safeguard. They fear that by labeling legitimate, policy-based dissent as "extremism," the President is effectively insulating himself from the very accountability that his supporters fought to establish.

Leadership in Isolation

The situation highlights a dangerous pattern: the President is becoming increasingly isolated, while his aides seem more focused on defending his social media output than facilitating genuine dialogue. By framing the debate as a choice between "loyalists" and "selfish extremists," the administration is closing off the channels of communication needed to actually implement the reforms they promised.

Rather than managing the party through collaboration, the administration's reliance on public social media broadsides is turning internal policy debates into toxic, zero-sum infighting. If the President continues to treat his own party’s reformers as adversaries, he risks alienating the coalition that brought him to power, ultimately undermining the very reforms he claims to champion.
