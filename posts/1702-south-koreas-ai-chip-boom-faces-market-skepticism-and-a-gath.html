South Korea’s AI Chip Boom Faces Market Skepticism and a Gathering Power Crisis

October 30, 2026

Despite posting unprecedented headline earnings, South Korea’s semiconductor sector is grappling with sharp internal corporate imbalances, rapid asset sell-offs, and an escalating energy bottleneck that threatens the global artificial intelligence supply chain.

  • Samsung's profit illusion: Samsung Electronics logged a record-breaking 107 trillion won (about $78 billion) in semiconductor operating profit, yet its shares tumbled after it postponed shareholder return plans and revealed ongoing losses in its consumer device business.
  • SK’s all-in AI pivot: SK Square, the investment arm of SK Group (South Korea’s second-largest conglomerate), is pushing to sell control of navigation and mobility platform Tmap Mobility for around 800 billion won (about $580 million) to funnel cash entirely into AI and semiconductor chips.
  • Domestic fabless milestone: South Korean chip design startup FuriosaAI closed an 820 billion won (about $600 million) Series D round, earning a valuation of 3.8 trillion won ($2.8 billion) amid surging appetite for specialized AI silicon.
  • Nuclear power drafted for chips: State-run Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power obtained life-extension approvals for the Kori 3 and 4 nuclear reactors, citing the urgent need to power energy-intensive AI data centers and advanced manufacturing plants.

Record Profits Mask Deepening Structural Cracks

Samsung’s staggering earnings print looks formidable on the surface, but it reveals an unstable domestic tech giant. Semiconductors accounted for 99 percent of the company’s operating profit, masking persistent deficits in its finished goods division. While high-bandwidth memory (HBM)—critical for accelerating AI workloads—is reportedly sold out through next year, management’s decision to defer shareholder returns due to macroeconomic uncertainty spooked investors. In a market where supply constraints and execution speed dictate valuations, sheer capacity without shareholder alignment is no longer enough to satisfy Wall Street or Seoul.

SK Group’s response has been far more decisive. By moving to offload a marquee consumer app like Tmap Mobility to institutional buyers including Goldman Sachs, SK is demonstrating that capital discipline now means ruthless pruning. In the current environment, running capital-intensive consumer platforms is a luxury; staying competitive alongside Nvidia, TSMC, and domestic rival Samsung requires every available dollar to be directed toward the memory and fab ecosystem.

The Industrial Bottleneck: Running Out of Watts

The entire AI supply chain is quickly crashing into hard physical limits. Fabricating advanced chips and operating dense hyperscale data centers requires an unprecedented amount of baseload electricity. The South Korean government's move to extend the operational lifetime of older nuclear reactors under Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power underscores this reality: environmental and political debates over nuclear energy are being superseded by the existential need to keep chip facilities and AI servers running.

From advanced packaging like glass substrates to novel water-treatment technologies, Korean firms are racing to solve heat, material fatigue, and power loss. As Big Tech spending strains global supply chains, the real winners of this phase will not just be the companies designing larger models, but the manufacturers that can secure reliable power grids, stable yields, and high-efficiency hardware.
