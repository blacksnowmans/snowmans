South Korea's Live Commerce Shift: The Rise of AI and "Content-First" Sales

November 14, 2026

South Korea’s live commerce sector is undergoing a qualitative shift, moving away from simple price-cutting wars toward AI-driven automation and highly engaging, celebrity-led content.

  • AI Integration: E-commerce solutions provider Cafe24 announced its "Expand 2027" strategy, focusing on AI-driven global expansion and process automation for sellers.
  • The "Celeb" Effect: Shinsegae Live Shopping saw a 23% increase in viewership by integrating celebrities into their beauty broadcasts, proving that talent-driven content drives traffic.
  • Gamification: Online retailer 11st's "PlayTrip" travel service achieved a 6x higher conversion rate by gamifying the shopping experience.
  • Financial Discipline: Major players like Kurly (a leading grocery delivery service) and Oasis Market are shifting their focus from aggressive expansion to achieving profitability through logistics efficiency.

The AI-Driven Future of Retail

The boundary between reality and digital content is blurring rapidly. A prime example is "Kim Ham-zzi," an AI-generated creator with 710,000 followers, who recently sold out an entire inventory within 10 minutes of a live broadcast. This success highlights a new reality: the most effective way to capture consumer attention is no longer just a charismatic human host, but the strategic fusion of AI IP (intellectual property) and authentic engagement.

Beyond content creation, AI is breaking down language barriers in real-time. As Korean brands look outward to combat a saturated domestic market, AI-powered multi-language translation is becoming a staple in live commerce. Platforms are increasingly prioritizing these technologies to help domestic sellers reach global audiences, with the competitive landscape in Southeast Asia—particularly Vietnam—serving as the current primary battlefield for these new tools.

Beyond Price: Quality and Profitability

While tech headlines dominate, the real story is the cooling of the "growth-at-all-costs" mentality. Companies like Kurly and Oasis Market are proving that logistics and supply chain optimization are just as critical as marketing. Oasis Market, for example, reported a 143% surge in operating profit this quarter, signaling that the era of unsustainable subsidies is fading in favor of mature business models.

For brands and local retailers, the message is clear: live commerce is evolving into a branding channel rather than just a liquidation tool. Whether it is Samsung C&T leveraging a sports championship to drive sales or local brands in Daegu using government-supported digital training to go national, success now hinges on the ability to provide an immersive experience. If you aren't using the stream to build a community, you are likely just waiting for your margins to erode.
