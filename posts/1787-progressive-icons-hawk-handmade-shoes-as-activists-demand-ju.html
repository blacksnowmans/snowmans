Progressive Icons Hawk Handmade Shoes as Activists Demand Judicial Purge

October 31, 2026

Prominent liberal commentators drew thousands to a charity shoe sale in Seoul over the weekend, turning an artisan pop-up into a rallying ground for progressive fury against South Korea’s top judge.

  • What happened: Author Rhyu Si-min and veteran journalist Byun Sang-wook worked a six-hour shift as shoe fitters at AGIO, a Seongsu-dong brand run by deaf craftspeople, drawing massive crowds and more than 120,000 livestream viewers.
  • The political subtext: Between hawking custom sea-fishing footwear and ultra-lightweight police tactical boots, Rhyu took barbed swipes at state prosecutors and police raids conducted during the tenure of former leader Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • The broader escalation: Outside the shop, progressive civic group Candlelight Action staged protests demanding the immediate impeachment and suspension of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.
  • Who is under fire: Activists directed intense anger at the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, accusing party leadership of political cowardice for shelving Cho's impeachment motion out of fear of a conservative backlash.

Charity Sales and Sharp Political Satire

The event at "Dreaming AGIO" was ostensibly organized to support disabled workers’ employment through AGIO, a social cooperative known for handcrafting luxury leather footwear. Yet with Rhyu and Byun at the counters, the mood quickly blended community solidarity with political satire. Presenting a lightweight boot supplied to police mobility units, Rhyu quipped to the crowd that the shoes were designed for flat city streets rather than mountains—and jokingly warned buyers not to wear them when carrying out unwarranted prosecutorial search-and-seizure raids, a clear reference to the aggressive investigations seen during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s administration.

The showcase demonstrated strong commercial appeal, clearing out discontinued stocks and offering leather upcycling classes. But for the crowds packing the alleys of Seongsu-dong, the event served as a cultural check-in for a progressive base that remains on high alert following the political turmoil of recent months.

Grassroots Frustration with the Democratic Party

The festive atmosphere inside the workshop stood in stark contrast to the boiling anger on the pavement outside. Protesters led by Candlelight Action argued that Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae is systematically undermining judicial accountability. Activists accuse Cho's court of running defense for remnants of the previous regime, citing delayed trials regarding the December insurrection attempt, stalling investigations into the 2022 Itaewon crowd crush, and routinely rejecting arrest warrants.

More critically, the demonstrations exposed an intensifying rift between grassroots activists and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea. Despite holding a commanding legislative majority of more than 170 seats under President Lee Jae-myung's administration, Democratic Party leadership recently blinked, shelving a formal impeachment resolution against Cho in favor of a watered-down parliamentary inquiry. To the street activists who weathered the martial law crisis, this calculated hesitation looks like rank opportunism. For Korea's progressive base, patience with legislative caution has run out; they expect their legislative supermajority to wield its constitutional weapons, starting with the judicial bench.
