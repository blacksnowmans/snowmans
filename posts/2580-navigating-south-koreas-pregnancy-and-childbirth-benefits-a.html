Navigating South Korea’s Pregnancy and Childbirth Benefits: A Step-by-Step Guide

November 21, 2026

Pregnant women in South Korea can access more than 14 million won (about $10,500) in combined national support during a child's first year, but missing strict registration windows can mean forfeiting substantial financial benefits.

  • Immediate perks: Confirming pregnancy at 5–6 weeks unlocks the National Happiness Card voucher worth up to 2 million won (about $1,500), along with free public health center checkups and supplements.
  • Workplace rights: Labor laws guarantee pregnant employees a two-hour reduction in daily work hours without wage cuts during early and late pregnancy stages.
  • Crucial deadlines: Postpartum care center bookings and fetal insurance must be secured early—insurance specifically before week 11—to avoid booking shortages or coverage rejections.
  • 2026 subsidies: The state provides a 2 million won ($1,500) initial voucher, 1 million won ($750) monthly parent allowance, and 100,000 won ($75) monthly child allowance, alongside localized regional cash grants.

Early Timelines: Vouchers, Workplace Rights, and Insurance

South Korea operates its pregnancy and maternity support on an application-only basis, meaning unregistered entitlements simply expire. Upon receiving a pregnancy certificate from an OB-GYN around weeks 5 to 6, expecting mothers should request multiple copies to register for the state-funded National Happiness Card (Kookmin Haengbok Card). This voucher provides 1 million won ($750) for single births and up to 2 million won ($1,500) for multiples to cover medical and pharmacy expenses.

Registering at a local public health center provides free prenatal blood and urine tests, free folic acid and iron supplements, and 30 percent discount vouchers for KTX and SRT high-speed trains. Working mothers are protected under Article 74 of the Labor Standards Act, which permits a two-hour daily work reduction up to week 12 and after week 32 without any reduction in salary. Meanwhile, parents seeking fetal insurance must finalize policies before week 11, as any high-risk findings during the first anomaly screening can disqualify newborns from critical coverage riders.

Late Pregnancy Perks and Navigating 2026 Subsidies

In mid-to-late pregnancy, mothers receive a mandatory 20 percent discount on out-of-pocket expenses for outpatient medical visits across clinics and hospitals, alongside free flu shots and required pertussis (Tdap) vaccinations. Korea Post offers a completely free public-interest insurance policy for pregnant women up to week 22, covering rare and congenital pediatric conditions. Expecting travelers can also access airport fast-track security lanes at major transportation hubs like Incheon International Airport.

Once the child is born, South Korea's standard 2026 maternity safety net kicks in. National benefits deliver 2 million won ($1,500) via the "First Meeting Voucher" (3 million won for a second child), 1 million won ($750) per month in Parents' Pay for infants under one year old, and 100,000 won ($75) per month in Child Allowance. Local municipal subsidies add further cash incentives, though eligibility often requires continuous residence in the municipality for 180 days to one year. All post-birth cash subsidies and utility discounts can be claimed in a single submission through the government's "Happy Birth One-Stop Service" portal.
