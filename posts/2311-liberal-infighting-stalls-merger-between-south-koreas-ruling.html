Liberal Infighting Stalls Merger Between South Korea’s Ruling Party and Cho Kuk’s Faction

November 14, 2026

The Rebuilding Korea Party has flatly denied claims by the ruling Democratic Party that merger talks are underway, exposing a deep rift within the liberal bloc that supports the administration of President Lee Jae-myung.

  • The "1mm" Denial: Cho Kuk, leader of the Rebuilding Korea Party (RKP), stated that merger discussions with the Democratic Party have progressed "not even 1mm," calling the ruling party's claims "deceptive media play."
  • Demanding an Apology: Before any talks can begin, Cho is demanding a formal apology from Democratic Party leadership for past slander and rumors regarding secret "power-sharing" deals.
  • A Divided Base: Public opinion among liberal supporters is split down the middle, with a recent Gallup poll showing 44% in favor of a merger and 43% opposed.
  • Legislative Risk: The friction threatens the unified front that President Lee Jae-myung needs to maintain legislative momentum in a polarized National Assembly.

The "Media Play" Accusation

The friction began when senior members of the Democratic Party—the ruling party currently holding a majority in the National Assembly—suggested that a high-level meeting between the two parties was imminent. Kim Min-seok, a member of the Democratic Party's Supreme Council, hinted that the two sides would meet soon to discuss consolidating the liberal camp to better support the Lee Jae-myung administration.

Cho Kuk, the former Justice Minister who founded the RKP to provide a "sharper" alternative to the main liberal party, reacted with visible anger. He characterized the Democratic Party's behavior as "show-tong" (a portmanteau of "show" and "communication," implying fake outreach). From Cho’s perspective, the ruling party is attempting to bully his smaller faction into submission by leaking unconfirmed reports to the press to create a sense of inevitability.

"A forecast without actual dialogue is not politics; it is a lack of basic courtesy," Cho stated, signaling that his party will not be absorbed without significant concessions.

A Battle for Liberal Identity

This dispute is more than just a scheduling conflict; it is a battle over the future of the liberal movement under President Lee Jae-myung. The Democratic Party views the RKP as a necessary but secondary satellite that should eventually be folded back into the main fold to maximize political efficiency. However, RKP supporters often view the Democratic Party as too cautious or bogged down by internal bureaucracy. They see the RKP as the "vanguard" of the movement—the group that took the hardest line during the movement to impeach and imprison Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

The internal polling reveals the difficulty of the situation. With the liberal base almost perfectly divided on the merger, President Lee faces a dilemma. If he pushes for a forced merger to consolidate power, he risks alienating the passionate minority that views Cho Kuk as a principled martyr. If he allows the parties to remain separate, he risks a "divided house" that the opposition People Power Party could exploit.

For now, the ball is in the Democratic Party’s court. Unless leadership offers a sincere apology for past friction and treats the RKP as an equal partner rather than a target for acquisition, the "1mm" of progress is unlikely to grow any further. In the sharp world of Seoul politics, a lack of courtesy is often a dealbreaker.
