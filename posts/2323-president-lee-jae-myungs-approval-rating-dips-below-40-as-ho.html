President Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Rating Dips Below 40% as Housing Missteps Backfire

November 14, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating fell to 39% in a Gallup Korea poll released on November 13, crossing below the critical 40% psychological threshold as public frustration over soaring housing prices and policy confusion mounted.

  • What happened: Gallup Korea reported that approval for President Lee dropped two percentage points to 39%, while disapproval climbed to 50%, with real estate policy cited as the primary reason for negative ratings (17%).
  • Policy flip-flop: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea proposed raising the Comprehensive Real Estate Tax deduction threshold for single-home owners to 1.4 billion won (about $1 million USD), only to retract the plan just four days later following pushback from the presidential office and party hardliners.
  • Market pressure: Seoul apartment prices extended their upward rally to 92 consecutive weeks, heightening anxiety among prospective homebuyers in the capital area.
  • Political fallout: The main conservative opposition, the People Power Party, seized on the tax policy retreat to attack the administration's leadership and question the government's policy consistency.

Housing Missteps Threaten Political Capital

Real estate has repeatedly proven to be the most volatile political issue in South Korean politics, but President Lee’s latest poll slump points to a broader breakdown in public trust. Disapproval is driven not merely by high home values, but by perceived policy incoherence. The four-day retreat on property tax relief exposed clear friction between the ruling Democratic Party leadership and the presidential office, sending conflicting signals to a housing market already suffering from supply anxiety.

Compounding the administration’s troubles is a rise in moral and ethical criticism, which reached 12% among negative poll responses. Voters appear increasingly tired of official messaging that deflects blame onto market conditions or the former administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. While President Lee continues to earn decent marks on macro-level economic performance, surging housing and rental costs directly erode household purchasing power, souring sentiment in key metropolitan constituencies.

What Comes Next

Falling below 40% support deprives the president of crucial momentum needed to drive his domestic agenda through a polarized legislature. Unless the administration can deliver a clear, consistent strategy on housing supply and stabilize the rental market, the ruling party faces severe headwinds in maintaining its political footing in Seoul and surrounding urban centers.
