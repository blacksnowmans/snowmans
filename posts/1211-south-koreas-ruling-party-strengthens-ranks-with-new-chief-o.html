South Korea’s Ruling Party Strengthens Ranks with New Chief of Staff and Policy Push

October 16, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) has rallied behind President Lee Jae-myung’s newly appointed presidential chief of staff while launching a sharp counter-offensive against opposition attacks and moving to delay cryptocurrency taxation.

  • Key Appointment: DPK leadership welcomed Im Kwang-hyun as Presidential Chief of Staff, praising him as a practical operator to streamline coordination between the party, executive branch, and presidential office.
  • Opposition Clash: DPK Floor Leader Han Byung-do demanded the resignation of Jang Dong-hyuk, leader of the main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP), accusing him of politicizing national safety issues.
  • Crypto Tax Delay: DPK lawmaker Park Min-gyu announced legislation to postpone the implementation of cryptocurrency gains taxation by two years to 2029.
  • Scandal Containment: DPK leaders rejected PPP demands for forced investigations over allegations involving the Shincheonji religious group, insisting the matter will be handled strictly through internal party regulations.

Strategic Pivot to Pragmatic Governance

The endorsement of Im Kwang-hyun as chief of staff reflects the Lee Jae-myung administration's desire for operational efficiency as it navigates its second year in office. DPK leader Kim Min-seok characterized Im as a "quietly strong" figure suited for policy execution. Meanwhile, the leadership is actively cultivating its talent pool for upcoming local elections, floating potential new leaders such as Lawmaker Kang Hoon-sik for the Seoul mayoral race.

This pragmatic orientation extends to foreign relations. In a meeting with Japanese Ambassador Koichi Mizushima, Kim Min-seok signaled a balanced approach to diplomacy, noting that South Korea must "not forget the past, but build the future together." The stance aims to safeguard national interests and maintain stable regional ties while avoiding both extreme posture and unreciprocated concessions.

Deflecting Attack and Driving the Reform Agenda

Alongside its policy push, the ruling party is battling severe political headwinds. In response to PPP claims regarding alleged mass party enlistments by the controversial Shincheonji group, the DPK leadership is attempting to contain the narrative through standard procedural channels rather than letting it devolve into a broader partisan brawl.

At the same time, the DPK is going on the offensive by pushing for a special counsel investigation into alleged prosecutorial abuse and "fabricated indictments" during Yoon Suk-yeol's term in power. By pairing policy measures—such as crypto tax relief and elder care protections—with firm reform stances, the ruling party is seeking to consolidate its base and regain momentum in public opinion polls.
