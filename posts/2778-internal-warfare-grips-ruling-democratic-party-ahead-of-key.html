Internal Warfare Grips Ruling Democratic Party Ahead of Key Disciplinary Vote

November 27, 2026

On November 26, the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) descended into a multi-front internal conflict as attempts by party veterans to mediate a growing rift between top leaders ended in a public snub.

  • Choi Min-hee, a member of the DPK’s Supreme Council (the party’s top decision-making body), faces a high-stakes disciplinary hearing on November 27 regarding controversial public remarks and alleged interference in internal party elections.
  • A "reconciliation dinner" organized by 12 senior party veterans failed after key rivals Kim Min-seok and Jung Chung-rae both refused to attend, signaling a total breakdown in internal communication.
  • The conflict has escalated into "counter-litigation" within the party, with rival factions filing retaliatory complaints against lawmaker Park Sun-won over his "Japanese collaborator" remarks.
  • The police have officially begun a defamation investigation into Choi’s "hyena" comments, adding external legal pressure to the party’s internal ethics review.

A Party Divided Against Itself

The conflict centers on Choi Min-hee, whose fate has become a lightning rod for broader factional animosity within the DPK. While she was originally under fire for calling her critics "a pack of hyenas," her hearing has expanded to include allegations that she meddled in the election of the party’s women’s committee head. The severity of her potential punishment—which could range from a simple warning to a one-year suspension of party rights—is being viewed as a litmus test for which faction holds the upper hand in President Lee Jae-myung’s ruling party.

The failure of the November 26 "elders' dinner" highlights the depth of the grudge. Jung Chung-rae, a high-ranking party official, snubbed the meeting and publicly lambasted the mediation attempt as "a beating disguised as a truce." With senior lawmakers like Woo Won-shik (the current Speaker of the National Assembly) and veteran Park Ji-won unable to bring the warring sides to the table, the DPK is struggling to maintain a unified front. The party is effectively paralyzed by what some insiders are calling a "psychological divorce" between its leading members.

Escalation Beyond the Hearing

This is no longer just a debate over rhetoric; it has turned into a full-scale institutional battle. Hardline party members have launched a petition with a goal of 100,000 signatures to expel Lawmaker Park Sun-won, retaliating for his perceived insults against colleagues. Meanwhile, supporters of the "pro-Jung" faction are planning a street rally on November 28 to demand the resignation of Kim Min-seok, another top party leader.

This "disciplinary civil war" threatens to overshadow the government's policy goals. As supporters on both sides move from online petitions to physical protests and counter-lawsuits within the party’s Ethics Tribunal, the DPK faces a period of extreme volatility. The outcome of the November 27 hearing will likely determine whether the party can find a path to de-escalation or if it will fracture further ahead of the upcoming legislative sessions.
