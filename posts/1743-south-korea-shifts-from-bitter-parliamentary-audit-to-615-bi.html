South Korea Shifts from Bitter Parliamentary Audit to $615 Billion Budget Battle

October 31, 2026

Fresh off a contentious parliamentary audit widely criticized for toxic political drama, South Korea’s National Assembly has plunged into a fierce showdown over a record 821 trillion won (about $615 billion) national budget proposal.

  • What happened: The annual parliamentary audit concluded with harsh public criticism, immediately shifting the legislative focus to intense negotiations over the 2025 national budget.
  • What it changes: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea is pushing an expansionary budget to boost economic growth, while the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) pledges to slash up to 134 trillion won (about $100 billion).
  • Who is affected: Local municipalities scrambling for central funding, alongside vulnerable populations relying on underfunded social safety nets such as elderly community care.

From Soundbite Drama to Legislative Gridlock

The 2024 parliamentary audit left the public deeply disillusioned. Intended as a crucial mechanism to hold the executive branch accountable, the proceedings devolved into shouting matches engineered for social media short-form videos. Important economic and administrative concerns were overshadowed by political posturing, while the executive branch under President Lee Jae-myung faced heavy pushback from lawmakers for withholding 318 out of 432 requested audit documents from the presidential office.

With the audit receiving an "F" grade from civic watchdogs, lawmakers have moved straight into reviewing the largest budget proposal in the nation's history. Yet the lingering animosity guarantees that the budget review will be equally adversarial.

A Clash Over Fiscal Philosophy

The primary battleground is the government's proposed 162 trillion won (about $121 billion) "Future Response Fund." The ruling Democratic Party of Korea frames the fund as a vital investment to address AI transformation, climate change, and demographic decline. Conversely, the conservative opposition PPP condemns the initiative as an unchecked slush fund designed to buy political support ahead of upcoming elections while pushing national debt to dangerous levels.

This ideological divide leaves little room for practical compromise on everyday welfare needs. Essential projects, including local integrated care programs for elderly and disabled residents, are struggling to secure modest budget increases while major political factions clash over macroeconomic grandstanding. As seen during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's past tenure, fiscal standoffs threaten to push negotiations past the legal December 2 deadline, raising the risk of another rushed, behind-the-scenes compromise.
