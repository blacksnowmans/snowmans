South Korea Positions Itself at the Center of the Physical AI Boom

November 11, 2026

Artificial intelligence is stepping out of virtual chat windows and into factories, farms, and logistics hubs, with South Korea emerging as a primary testing ground for this physical AI revolution.

  • Nvidia spotlight: A top Nvidia executive has named South Korea the global hub for physical AI over the next decade, partnering with local tech firms and startups to integrate hardware and software.
  • Corporate alliances: Traditional heavyweights Samsung and Hyundai are joining forces to combine semiconductor prowess with robotics and next-generation mobility.
  • Regional adoption: Local governments like Gimje are transforming regional hubs into agricultural robotics testbeds to combat rural labor shortages.
  • Economic security: The South Korean government and foreign policy agencies are treating physical AI infrastructure and data governance as core national security issues.

The race toward physical AI marks a shift from pure software models to machines that need to operate seamlessly in the real world. For South Korea, its deep manufacturing roots and semiconductor ecosystem make it a natural fit for Nvidia and other global players looking for a robust testing ground. By combining Nvidia’s computing power with domestic hardware manufacturing, the country is positioning itself to avoid being left behind in the hardware-software transition.

At the same time, the domestic consolidation between major conglomerates like Samsung and Hyundai signals a pragmatic "co-opetition" model aimed at fending off foreign competition in autonomous mobility and robotics. As edge computing and ultra-low latency networks develop, physical AI is fast moving from a futuristic concept into a core pillar of South Korea’s industrial and economic strategy under the current administration.
