Feud Among South Korean Political Commentators Unravels Hypocrisy and Legal Troubles

October 26, 2026

A bitter public fallout between online commentators has laid bare backroom favor-seeking, double standards, and drunk-driving records inside South Korea's influential political YouTube ecosystem.

  • What happened: Online broadcaster Lee Jong-won of Sisatapa TV exposed lawyer and commentator Noh Young-hee for soliciting local political candidacy favors and land sales, triggering revelations of her past drunk-driving convictions.
  • What it changes: The unraveling controversy has ignited criminal defamation lawsuits and shattered Noh's standing among liberal audiences after she launched unfounded attacks against prominent intellectual figures.
  • Who is affected: South Korea's hyper-partisan political media landscape, as well as the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, which faces growing supporter frustration over candidate vetting and leadership passivity.

From Local Solicitations to Repeated Drunk Driving

The feud began when lawyer and political commentator Noh Young-hee criticized the audience of Sisatapa TV, a popular liberal YouTube channel. In response, channel head Lee Jong-won disclosed that Noh had previously asked him to use personal connections to secure a municipal council nomination in Pocheon, Gyeonggi Province, and to help sell her real estate there. Noh responded not with a denial or legal action, but by bizarrely advertising her Pocheon property live during her broadcast, effectively confirming the claim.

The controversy deepened as details emerged of Noh’s criminal record, showing two drunk-driving convictions within ten months, in December 2008 and October 2009. When confronted, Noh dismissed the offenses as occurring "20 years ago" and downplayed getting caught twice in a single year. Critics, including former Democratic Party candidate evaluator Hwang Hee-doo, quickly highlighted the glaring hypocrisy, pointing out that Noh had previously called for strict moral accountability and imprisonment for drunk drivers during her past television appearances.

Conspiracy Theories, Lawsuits, and Political Disillusionment

As liberal viewers turned away, Noh escalated her antics by targeting Rhyu Si-min, a prominent author and respected liberal intellectual. Noh claimed to analyze Rhyu’s facial expressions to accuse him of mocking former presidents, and baselessly suggested he be investigated in connection with a corporate stock scandal. Noh's pattern of unverified claims escalated when actress Kim Boo-sun filed civil and criminal defamation lawsuits against her over sensational YouTube shorts videos that racked up over a million views.

Noh’s trajectory—eventually joining the fringe Sonamu Party as its top proportional representative candidate—reflects a broader breakdown of trust in South Korea’s political commentary scene. Coupled with supporter dissatisfaction toward ruling Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok over his cautious approach toward judicial reforms, the episode highlights how opportunistic online figures and weak party discipline risk alienating core voters.
