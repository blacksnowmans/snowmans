President Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Plummets Amid Rural Backlash and Judicial Scandals

October 14, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating has fallen to 35.6%, a dangerous low that signals a potential early "lame duck" period for the current administration.

  • Broad Disapproval: Negative sentiment has reached 61.8%, with support collapsing even in the Honam region, the traditional stronghold of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea.
  • Opposition Surge: The conservative People Power Party (PPP) has overtaken the Democratic Party in public support for two consecutive weeks.
  • Policy Backlash: A nationwide audit of farmland has sparked fury among rural voters, while the "dismissal of indictments" controversy fuels accusations of double standards.
  • Third-Party Rise: The Rebuilding Korea Party, led by former Justice Minister Cho Kuk, is surging toward 10% support, attracting disillusioned younger voters.

A "Dead Cross" for the Ruling Party

Recent data from EveryResearch shows a "dead cross"—where negative evaluations far exceed positive ones—mirroring the political paralysis seen during the final days of the now-imprisoned Yoon Suk-yeol-ssi. The collapse is most striking among the youth; only 30.4% of those aged 18-29 approve of President Lee’s performance. More alarming for the Democratic Party of Korea (DP) is the breach of the "50% line" in the liberal heartland of Gwangju and the Jeolla provinces, where support has dipped into the high 40s.

This erosion of the base is matched by a crisis in party leadership. The DP’s support stands at 31.0%, trailing the People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, which sits at 34.6%. The shift suggests that the public is no longer viewing the DP as a competent alternative to the previous administration. Critics within the party point to a closed-off, "one-man" leadership style and a lack of fresh faces in the cabinet as primary causes for this stagnation.

Policy Blunders and Judicial Fatigue

The Farmland Fiasco

Much of the recent anger stems from a nationwide audit of farmland. In an attempt to crack down on real estate speculation, the government targeted roughly 1.36 million hectares of land. However, the move backfired by treating elderly farmers—who often utilize "entrusted farming" due to aging—as criminals. With threats of heavy fines amounting to 25% of land value, rural communities feel besieged by "desk-bound" bureaucrats who do not understand the reality of South Korea’s vanishing countryside.

Judicial Controversies

The administration is also struggling with the fallout from the "dismissal of indictments" (gongso-chuiso) scandal. This controversy, involving the dropping of charges against figures linked to the current power structure, was cited by pollsters as the leading cause of the approval drop. To middle-of-the-road voters, this looks like the same "rules for thee, but not for me" politics they hoped to move past. Combined with soaring rents in Seoul and the capital region, the government's moral and practical authority is being tested on all fronts.

"If the administration continues to ignore the plight of ordinary citizens while focusing on internal power struggles and judicial defense, the slide into the 20% range is only a matter of time."

The rise of the Rebuilding Korea Party to 9.0% support highlights a growing appetite for a more aggressive or distinct progressive alternative. As the DP leadership under Kim Min-seok faces accusations of incompetence, the president's inner circle—including the recently resigned Chief of Staff Kang Hoon-sik—is being scrutinized for failing to provide a clear vision for the second half of the term.
