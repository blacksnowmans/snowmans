South Korean President Lee Jae-myung Taps Veteran Heavyweight to Shore Up Party Discipline

October 27, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung hosted former National Assembly Speaker Woo Won-shik for a high-protocol luncheon at the presidential office, signaling an aggressive push to steady his legislative agenda amid faltering floor management within the ruling party.

  • What happened: President Lee held a formal state luncheon at Cheong Wa Dae for former National Assembly Speaker Woo Won-shik, departing sharply from his usual quick office meals with inner-circle confidants.
  • What it changes: The outreach effectively sidelines the party's current floor-management apparatus led by Representative Kim Min-seok, positioning Woo as the executive branch's preferred power broker inside the legislature.
  • Who is affected: Lawmakers in the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), who now face a rebalancing of power as the president seeks tighter control ahead of upcoming leadership contests.
  • The broader stakes: Lee is assembling experienced legislative muscle to push his core agenda past the main conservative opposition, the People Power Party, in the volatile aftermath of the impeachment and removal of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

Why Woo Won-shik Matters

Woo carries rare institutional weight within the ruling DPK. A veteran democracy activist and respected labor mediator, he secured the speakership in the 22nd National Assembly by sweeping an internal parliamentary secret ballot against fierce competition. That vote proved he commands an independent legislative base that President Lee cannot easily bypass. Though conservative critics previously targeted Woo over state-funded overseas diplomatic travel totaling around $6 million (8.2 billion won), his stature among rank-and-file lawmakers remains largely intact.

For President Lee, whose political playbook has always centered on practical results over factional purity, Woo represents an indispensable operator. Lawmakers have increasingly questioned the current floor leadership under Kim Min-seok, criticizing its inability to enforce cohesion and counter conservative stall tactics. By bringing Woo directly into his confidence, Lee acquires a battle-tested bridge across the party's various factions.

A Pragmatic Rebalancing of Power

President Lee rarely rolls out formal presidential protocol without expecting a tangible return. The lavish luncheon serves both as an olive branch and a task assignment: Woo is being drafted to act as the administration’s informal enforcer in parliament. With the DPK heading toward its 2026 national party convention amid fierce factional polling between figures like Jung Chung-rae and Kim Min-seok, the presidential office is moving early to secure its own leverage.

By courting Woo as a potential future party leader or emergency committee chief, Lee is hedging his bets against internal fracturing. It is a clear message to underperforming allies that presidential favor hinges entirely on legislative execution. With major reform bills on the line, Lee is willing to reshuffle the ruling party's hierarchy to ensure his agenda moves forward without delay.
