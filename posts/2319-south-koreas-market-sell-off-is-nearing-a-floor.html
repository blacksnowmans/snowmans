South Korea’s Market Sell-Off Is Nearing a Floor

November 14, 2026

South Korea’s recent stock market slump was driven not by deteriorating corporate fundamentals, but by an acute liquidity crunch triggered by massive, forced liquidations from British pension funds and American hedge funds.

  • British pension outflows: Out of nearly 200 trillion won (about $145 billion) in foreign selling through September, roughly 97 trillion won (about $70 billion) came from UK pensions liquidating profitable Korean shares to raise cash following domestic inheritance tax reforms.
  • Mechanical hedge fund selling: Leveraged US hedge funds triggered automatic stop-loss selloffs after tech sector corrections crossed 5% to 7% loss thresholds, amplifying the downturn.
  • Semiconductor disruptions: SK Hynix hit headwinds as the US Commerce Department froze equipment upgrade reviews for its Dalian plant in China, while governance controversies around Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang delayed planned partnership talks in Seoul.
  • Stimulus rumors denied: The Financial Services Commission (FSC), South Korea’s financial regulator, explicitly rejected market rumors that the government was preparing an emergency liquidity package to boost the tech-heavy Kosdaq.

The Mechanics Behind the Panic

The bitter joke currently circulating among retail investors—that "escaping the Korean market is a sign of intelligence"—reflects broken liquidity rather than broken companies. Retail investor deposits on the domestic exchange fell from 130 trillion won (about $94 billion) earlier this year to 85 trillion won (about $61 billion), drying up the pool of capital needed to absorb foreign outflows. When UK pensions dumped their most profitable liquid assets and US systematic funds dumped leveraged positions, the market lacked the domestic bid to counter them. However, with UK tax-related selling largely exhausted and foreign investors turning net buyers in recent sessions, the supply overhang has effectively hit a floor.

The divergence between Wall Street and Seoul also highlights domestic overreaction. While US markets separated Nvidia’s specific corporate governance and sanction troubles from the broader AI sector—lifted by strong capital expenditure signals from cloud provider CoreWeave—Korean equities panicked across the board. While SK Hynix faces real friction in upgrading its Chinese operations under strict US export controls, Washington’s tightening chokehold on advanced memory exports simultaneously delays rival Chinese fabricators like CXMT, safeguarding South Korea's technological lead over the medium term.

The 2027 Outlook: A Stock-Picker's Grind

Institutional targets for 2027 have abandoned euphoric calls of a 10,000-to-12,000 index breakthrough. Realistic projections now frame the market within a range of 5,100 to 5,200 on the lower end and roughly 9,000 at the top, pointing to a slow, grinding recovery rather than a rapid V-shaped surge. Rather than waiting for unconfirmed regulatory rescues, investors will need to pivot from broad index exposure to the roughly 10% of market leaders capable of delivering earnings growth independent of macroeconomic turbulence.
