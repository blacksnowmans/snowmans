Global Scuba Update: Artificial Reefs, Stricter Dive Rules, and South Korea’s Marine Eco Push

November 22, 2026

South Korea is aggressively investing in coastal ecosystem restoration while global diving destinations move to enforce strict safety laws following high-profile accidents and rapid market growth.

  • Major Shipwreck Reef: The iconic ocean liner SS United States was sunk off Florida to create the world's largest artificial reef, triggering a massive wave of technical and wreck diving bookings.
  • Regulatory Crackdowns: Cyprus is drafting mandatory recreational diving legislation following seven fatalities at the famous Zenobia wreck, while Malaysia announced compulsory guide licensing enforcement beginning April 2027.
  • Korean Marine Eco Investments: South Korea's Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries teamed up with shipbuilder Hanwha Ocean to create a 1.59-square-kilometer underwater forest off Geoje Island, alongside a 100.7 billion won (about $72 million) regional project in Pohang to restore seagrass and protect sea turtles.
  • Safety Alerts & Tech Innovations: Consumer watchdogs warned against hazardous full-face snorkel masks prone to carbon dioxide buildup, while Korean tech startup DIVEROID launched software turning standard smartwatches into dive computers.

Rising Risks Amid a Global Dive Boom

The marine leisure industry is walking a tightrope between rapid commercial expansion and lagging safety standards. The deployment of the SS United States as an artificial reef and aggressive promotional campaigns—such as the Philippine Department of Tourism actively targeting South Korea’s fast-growing freediving and mermaid market—are driving high consumer interest. However, attracting unqualified divers to complex underwater environments increases fatal risks. Recent deaths at Cyprus’s Zenobia wreck underscore the dangers of unmonitored guided tours. Institutional mandates, such as Malaysia’s upcoming licensing checks and Cyprus's legislative push, mark a vital transition from optional safety guidelines to legally enforced accountability.

South Korea’s Strategic Coastal Stewardship

Domestically, South Korea is combining corporate sponsorship with government research to combat ocean desertification and habitat loss. The joint initiative between the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries and Hanwha Ocean near Geoje Island, alongside major funding for Pohang’s Homigot Peninsula, signals a concrete attempt to restore coastal biodiversity. However, government-led reef projects have historically faced criticism when artificial structures were left unmanaged post-deployment. For these multi-million-dollar eco initiatives to deliver lasting results, state agencies must partner with local civilian diving groups for continuous underwater maintenance and trash removal.
