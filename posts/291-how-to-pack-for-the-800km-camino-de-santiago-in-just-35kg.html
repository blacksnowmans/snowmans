How to Pack for the 800km Camino de Santiago in Just 3.5kg

September 07, 2026

Completing the Camino de Santiago—the legendary 800km (approx. 500-mile) pilgrimage across Spain—is less about athletic endurance and more about the radical act of shedding unnecessary weight.

  • The Golden Rule: Aim for a pack weight of no more than 10% of your body weight. For the average person, 7kg (approx. 15 lbs) is the "danger zone"; exceeding it increases the probability of injury to 90% within the first three days.
  • Pack Light, Travel Far: Successful pilgrims don't ask "what should I bring," but rather "what can I leave behind?"
  • The 3.5kg Standard: Using a high-quality, ergonomic backpack (like those from The North Face) with a weight-distributing hip belt allows for a minimalist 3.5kg (approx. 7.7 lbs) load that saves your knees and ankles.
  • Documentation: To receive your Compostela (the official completion certificate), you must carry a Credencial (pilgrim passport) and collect at least two sellos (stamps) per day to prove you walked the required 100km minimum.

The Science of a Minimalist Pack

The most common mistake pilgrims make is packing for "what-if" scenarios. This leads to heavy bags that eventually get abandoned in hostels along the way. Your backpack should prioritize a waist-focused carry system over shoulder straps. If you have extra space in a 30–40 liter bag, don't fill it with gear; use that room for local supplies like fruit or bread. Forget about taking umbrellas, bulky towels, or excessive toiletries—Spanish towns are well-equipped with pharmacies and shops, making local procurement the smarter, lighter choice.

Clothing strategy should follow the "wash and dry" principle. A few moisture-wicking layers, a windbreaker, and perhaps a dress for women are sufficient. Footwear is the biggest weight culprit; avoid carrying both heavy hiking boots and extra sneakers. Many seasoned pilgrims swear by sturdy, closed-toe sandals or high-quality trail runners, paired with an "inner and outer" sock strategy to prevent the inevitable blister.

Beyond the Gear: The Pilgrim's Mindset

The journey is an exercise in stripping away the non-essential, both in your bag and your life. Many pilgrims find that the most valuable part of the trip isn't the physical certificate at the end, but the connections made on the trail. Whether you are walking for religious reasons or personal reflection, the "Donkey Service" (luggage transport) is available if your body reaches its limit, but the true goal is to find your own pace.

Avoid the trap of keeping up with others; 80% of injuries occur early on because pilgrims push too hard to match someone else's speed. If you find your bag is too heavy or your body is aching, don't be afraid to stop early. The Camino is not a race, and the most successful pilgrims are those who arrive at the Santiago Cathedral with a lighter pack and a mind finally cleared of the clutter they brought from home.
