How to Spot Market-Resistant Real Estate in a Volatile Korean Market

September 30, 2026

For most prospective homebuyers in South Korea, the first question is always: “Is this the peak?” Author Song Hee-gu, in his latest guide My First Real Estate Textbook, argues that obsessing over market timing is a distraction that causes buyers to miss opportunities. Instead, he urges investors to focus on the "rebound power" of assets—the ability of a property to maintain value and recover after a downturn.

  • Stop market timing: Avoid chasing price peaks or trying to catch the bottom; focus on assets with fundamental value.
  • Clarify your strategy: Decide early between "living-in" (primary residence) or "gap investment" (buying with a tenant already in place).
  • Watch for landmines: Always verify if a current tenant has already exercised their "contract renewal claim"—a legal right that could block you from moving into your own home.
  • Protect your "base camp": Your primary income (your career) is your most important asset; don't gamble it away on high-risk bets before your financial foundation is secure.

The Anatomy of a Smart Buy

Entering the property market requires shifting your mindset. Trying to buy a 1 billion won (about $700,000) apartment without a clear plan leads to paralysis. If you opt for a gap investment, failing to check the legal status of existing tenants is a common pitfall. If a tenant has not yet used their legal right to renew their lease for an additional two years, you may find yourself unable to move in, potentially leading to costly and stressful litigation.

Furthermore, Song warns against the influence of family and social pressure. Parents, often motivated by a desire to shield their children from stress, may discourage buying, which can lead to "regret-buying" years later when prices have risen beyond reach. True investment starts by ignoring the "lifestyle inflation" seen on social media and maintaining a frugal, long-term perspective. Song notes that he keeps his 14-year-old car not because he lacks the funds for a new one, but to maintain the financial discipline required to stay in the game.

"Location" is Not Just a Coordinate

While "location" is the golden rule of real estate, many buyers are misled by the term "near the station" (yeok-se-gwon). A subway station in a remote, mountainous area or a line that is inconvenient to major business districts adds little value. Real value comes from superior accessibility to job centers and a homogeneous, high-quality residential environment. As the birth rate declines, school districts (hak-gun-ji) are becoming the ultimate defense against market drops; parents will stay in these areas for years, creating a stable floor for both rent and property values.

As the market approaches 2026, concerns over "gap investment" risks are rising. In areas where the "jeonse-to-purchase price ratio" (the ratio of the landlord's deposit to the property's market value) hits 80%, a minor market dip can lead to a "tin can" scenario—where the landlord cannot return the deposit because the property value has fallen below the debt. Investors must look past flashy, overpriced listings and focus on cold, hard data: transaction history and the trends of real-time rental inventory. The goal is not to find a 100-point dream home, but to find an 80-point property that fits your budget and allows you to build equity without endangering your livelihood.
