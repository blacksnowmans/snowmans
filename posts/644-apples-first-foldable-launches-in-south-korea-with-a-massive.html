Apple's First Foldable Launches in South Korea With a Massive $700 Price Gap Against Samsung

September 10, 2026

  • Apple released its first foldable smartphone, the iPhone Duo, in South Korea with a starting price of 3.29 million won (about $2,450), while top-tier models exceed 5 million won (about $3,730).
  • Samsung's competing foldable, the Galaxy Z Fold 8, is priced significantly lower at 2.27 million won (about $1,700)—creating a staggering 1.01 million won (about $750) gap between the two devices in the Korean market.
  • While the two phones differ by only $100 in the US, Apple applied an aggressive, conservative exchange rate policy in Korea, whereas Samsung deliberately priced its device below standard currency conversions to defend its domestic market share.
  • The extreme pricing disparity has sparked intense debate among South Korean consumers and regulators over fairness, brand loyalty, and the soaring cost of premium mobile devices.

The Anatomy of a 1-Million-Won Gap: Apple's High Exchange Rate vs. Samsung's Defense

When Apple unveiled the iPhone Duo globally at $1,999, the local pricing in South Korea shocked consumers by pushing past the 3 million won threshold, including a 10% value-added tax and an effective exchange rate hovering near 1,450 to 1,500 won per dollar—far higher than the actual market rate. Analysts note that Apple routinely builds a conservative buffer into its Korean pricing to hedge against currency fluctuations and regulatory oversight, treating the country as a reliable premium testing ground.

Conversely, Samsung took the opposite approach with the Galaxy Z Fold 8. Despite a US price of $1,899.99, Samsung launched the device in South Korea using an artificially low virtual exchange rate, undercutting expected local costs by roughly 520,000 won (about $390). Facing mounting pressure in the global foldable market from aggressive Chinese manufacturers and Apple's historic entry into the foldables category, Samsung chose to sacrifice short-term margins to protect its home turf and secure its dominant market share.

Consumer Backlash and the Future of Premium Pricing

The resulting 1.01 million won price difference has deeply divided South Korean buyers. Apple loyalists argue that the iPhone Duo’s advanced specs—such as its dual-screen configuration and deep iOS integration—justify the luxury price tag. Meanwhile, critics and budget-conscious consumers view the 5-million-won ceiling for maximum-spec models as an exploitative markup, amplifying long-standing frustrations that Korean buyers are treated as high-paying testing grounds by multinational tech giants.
