South Korean Daycares Turn Children's Flea Markets into Nationwide Charitable Giving

November 12, 2026

Daycare centers across South Korea are transforming early childhood education into community welfare by donating millions of won raised through nationwide secondhand charity bazaars.

  • What happened: Preschools across the country reported donating proceeds from "Anabada" charity markets—a Korean civic acronym standing for "save, share, exchange, and reuse"—to local welfare agencies and pediatric cancer funds on November 11.
  • Key contributions: Individual facilities raised notable sums, including Areumdeuri Daycare in Asan donating 2.2 million won (about $1,600) and Municipal Gamjeong Hessen Daycare in Gimpo contributing 1.512 million won (about $1,100).
  • What it changes: The initiative converts standard preschool activities into practical lessons on financial literacy, environmental sustainability, and civic duty, while supplementing municipal welfare funds.
  • Who is affected: Vulnerable local residents, pediatric cancer patients, and low-income senior citizens receive direct community support, while toddlers gain early exposure to social solidarity.

Grassroots Welfare Driven by Early Childhood Education

The "Anabada" movement—originally launched in South Korea as a civic effort to encourage resource conservation during economic downturns—has evolved into a staple of the national early childhood curriculum. Daycare centers leverage these flea markets to teach toddlers the basic principles of money, trade, and recycling. Facilities like the Busan City Hall Daycare specifically targeted their earnings toward pediatric cancer treatments, giving young children a clear, empathetic connection to how their efforts help peers in need.

These events operate through tight coordination among teachers, parents, and municipal welfare departments. Publicly funded and municipal daycares, such as Gran Hills Daycare in Incheon and the Suwon Association of Public-Type Daycares, frequently lead the charge to set operational standards. While individual sums ranging from $500 to $1,600 appear modest, the combined donations across dozens of regional facilities provide a significant, flexible supply of funding for local welfare centers operating under strict budget constraints.

Building Community Resilience Amid Demographic Shifts

Beyond immediate financial aid, these charity drives reinforce local community networks, particularly in smaller cities and rural areas such as Hwasun and Jinan in the Jeolla regions. In areas experiencing rapid population aging and declining birth rates, daycare centers often double as key social hubs. Organizing flea markets brings young families and local residents together, fostering social cohesion in neighborhoods that lack broader community infrastructure.

The main challenge for administrators is ensuring the long-term sustainability of these programs without overworking staff. Planning and running bazaars requires considerable unpaid effort from daycare teachers alongside their normal caregiving duties. To prevent these drives from devolving into repetitive annual photo-ops, early childhood experts advocate for structured follow-up lessons, showing children exactly how their donated proceeds helped support their neighbors.
