South Korea's Iconic Eunma Apartment Clears Major Hurdle in 23-Year Rebuild Bid

October 05, 2026

    Eunma Apartment in Seoul's affluent Daechi-dong district has finally secured project implementation approval, moving closer to a 49-story, 5,800-unit reconstruction after 23 stalled years.
  • The project faces severe financial and logistical hurdles, including an estimated return rate (proportional rate) languishing in the mid-80% range due to soaring construction costs.
  • With over 60% of current residents being tenants and a massive commercial arcade on-site, the upcoming 2027 relocation phase threatens to trigger a severe rental crisis in Daechi-dong, Seoul's premier private education hub.
  • Legal disputes, including an ownership battle tied to the late Hanbo Group founder, loom over the complex's multi-layered commercial ecosystem.

The approval of the Eunma Apartment reconstruction project marks a seismic shift for South Korea's housing market. As a historic symbol of Gangnam real estate speculation and development since its 2003 promotion committee was formed, clearing this administrative hurdle is a massive milestone. The plan allows the aging complex to rise to 49 stories under a 330% floor area ratio. However, the celebration is heavily muted by the grueling realities of tenant displacement, soaring material costs, and complex stakeholder infighting that will test the patience of owners and residents alike.

Beneath the modern glass designs and park plans lies severe financial and social friction. Because South Korean housing reconstruction laws classify these projects as private rather than public works, tenants have no legal right to relocation compensation and face immediate eviction once management plans are finalized. Since renters make up nearly 70% of the complex, forcing them out will flood the local Daechi-dong market with displaced families desperate to stay near the elite cram schools, threatening local rental spikes. Compounding this, the sprawling on-site Eunma Arcade—a massive commercial hub—and lingering ownership lawsuits tied to the defunct Hanbo Group add layers of unpredictable legal chaos that could easily delay construction well into the 2030s.
