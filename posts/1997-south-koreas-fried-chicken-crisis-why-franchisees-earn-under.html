South Korea’s Fried Chicken Crisis: Why Franchisees Earn Under $1.50 per Bird

November 06, 2026

A structural squeeze driven by monopolistic delivery platforms and predatory franchise headquarters has left South Korean fried chicken store owners earning less than 2,000 won ($1.50) in net profit per order, even as consumer prices approach 30,000 won ($22).

  • Wholesale price gouging: Raw chicken market prices dropped 37.7%, but franchise headquarters cut wholesale supply prices to store owners by only 9.2%, absorbing most of the savings into corporate margins.
  • Crushing fee structure: On a standard 23,000 won ($17) order, raw materials consume 51% and delivery platform fees take 25%, leaving owners just 24% to cover rent, labor, utilities, and taxes.
  • Loss of operational control: Delivery apps undercut store-level takeout discounts with platform promotions, retain all customer data, and restrict owners from managing their own deliveries.
  • Rising business failure: Despite working 15-hour daily shifts, franchisees face cash-flow traps and mounting tax debts, driving the 2-to-3-year small business closure rate to 14.74%.

A Broken Franchise Supply Chain

South Korea’s iconic fried chicken market is trapped in a structural imbalance where neither consumers nor store owners benefit when commodity prices fall. When raw chicken prices plummeted by nearly 38%, corporate franchise headquarters—such as major chain Goobne Chicken—refused to pass along the savings, reducing wholesale supply rates by less than a quarter of that decline. Franchisors extract profits through mandatory supply markups rather than transparent royalty fees, creating an asymmetrical system. As Pi Se-jun, head of the Korea Chicken Franchisee Association, pointed out in a recent industry discussion, fixed-price supply contracts allow headquarters to raise prices instantly when wholesale costs rise, while delaying reductions when market prices fall.

Platform Monopoly and Owner Insolvency

The rise of dominant delivery apps has turned small business owners into captive operators. According to industry disclosures from the Fair Trade Commission, South Korea's antitrust regulator, fried chicken outlets generate an average monthly revenue of 18.29 million won ($13,500). However, high sales figures create a dangerous illusion of profitability. Delivery platforms unilaterally control driver dispatch, block direct customer marketing by withholding contact data, and force store owners to absorb the costs of arbitrary order cancellations. After standing in front of deep fryers for 15 hours a day, store owners end up with less than $1.50 per bird, leaving many unable to cover tax obligations despite steady gross revenue.
