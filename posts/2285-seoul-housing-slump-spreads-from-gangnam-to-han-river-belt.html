Seoul Housing Slump Spreads from Gangnam to Han River Belt

November 13, 2026

Home price drops in Seoul are spreading beyond the affluent southern districts of Gangnam into prime riverfront neighborhoods as high interest rates and heavy tax burdens freeze buyer sentiment.

  • What happened: Apartment prices in Seongdong and Gangdong districts turned negative for the first time in 7 to 8 months, expanding the number of declining districts in Seoul from three to five.
  • The current trend: Seocho and Gangnam districts have fallen for 14 consecutive weeks, while Songpa dropped 0.35% in a single week to mark its 10th straight weekly decline.
  • The statistical illusion: Although overall Seoul apartment prices technically rose 0.07% for a 92nd consecutive week, the market is heavily polarized, buoyed only by lower-cost outer areas like Seongbuk district (+0.39%).
  • Who is affected: High borrowing costs and property holding taxes are forcing leveraged homeowners to list urgent discounted sales, while prospective buyers shift into a strict wait-and-see mode.

Cracks in the Riverfront Defense Line

The price adjustments that began in the "Gangnam 3"—the wealthy southern districts of Gangnam, Seocho, and Songpa—have officially crossed the Han River. Seongdong, a central riverside hub, and Gangdong, a major eastern district, have both seen their months-long rallies come to an end. This shift has weakened the long-held market belief in the invincibility of prime Seoul real estate.

As the price gap between Gangnam and adjacent riverside districts narrowed, additional upside momentum evaporated. With transactions drying up across major apartment complexes, the market has rapidly pivoted to favor buyers over sellers. Prospective homeowners who once rushed to upgrade to premium locations are now staying on the sidelines, waiting for further price cuts as only heavily discounted listings manage to trade.

Statistical Illusion vs. High-Interest Reality

While headline statistics show Seoul extending its overall growth streak to 92 consecutive weeks, the 0.07% weekly rise masks a deeply divided market. The slight positive figure is largely driven by budget-conscious buyers seeking relatively affordable homes in outer districts like Seongbuk, which jumped 0.39%. Meanwhile, frustrated buyers priced out of Seoul's purchase market are shifting into rental markets in neighboring Gyeonggi Province and Incheon, keeping lease prices firm there.

However, real estate history shows that outer districts rarely remain immune when prime neighborhoods slump. With high mortgage interest rates and holding taxes weighing heavily on owners, urgent listings at reduced prices are expected to persist. Barring major policy shifts or rapid rate cuts, Seoul's broader housing market appears headed for a broader downturn.
