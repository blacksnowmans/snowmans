Conspiracy and Crisis: The Democratic Party’s Internal War Over Cult Allegations

November 04, 2026

Representative Kim Min-seok of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is facing a fierce internal backlash after alleging that a controversial religious cult has infiltrated the party’s power structure without providing evidence.

  • The Allegation: Kim claimed the existence of a "three-party secret alliance" involving Shincheonji—a fringe religious group widely viewed in Korea as a cult—intended to manipulate internal party affairs.
  • The Resignation Calls: Nine DPK member organizations have issued a joint ultimatum demanding Kim’s resignation, calling his claims "baseless conspiracy theories" that damage the party’s reputation.
  • The Political Context: Critics argue these tactics undermine the transparency promised by the administration of President Lee Jae-myung and distract from the party's legislative agenda.
  • The Failed Defense: Senior party figures and several younger lawmakers have been accused of "shielding" Kim from accountability rather than demanding proof for his serious accusations.

A Regression to Manipulative Politics

The Democratic Party rose to power on a platform of restoring democratic norms following the chaotic era of Yoon Suk Yeol 씨, who is currently imprisoned after being removed from office via impeachment. However, by weaponizing unverified claims of cult involvement to attack internal rivals, Kim Min-seok is accused of reviving the very "maneuver politics" the DPK once condemned. For many supporters, this is seen as political self-harm that erodes the party’s rational and debate-centered identity.

The core of the issue is a lack of accountability. Despite the gravity of accusing fellow party members of colluding with a group like Shincheonji, Kim has offered no data or witnesses. Instead, he has been criticized for using "emotional marketing"—specifically shedding tears during his 100th-day anniversary press conference—to deflect questions about the truth of his claims. As the ruling party, the DPK’s internal credibility is now at risk; if the leadership can manufacture "ghosts" to win internal power struggles, it loses the moral authority to lead the nation.

The Erosion of Leadership and Youth Politics

The controversy has also exposed a vacuum in party oversight. Park Ji-won, a veteran lawmaker and former intelligence chief, has been criticized for his "both-sides-ism," attempting to play the role of a neutral mediator rather than calling out the lack of evidence. By telling both sides to "be careful," he effectively grants Kim a pass for spreading misinformation. This "old-school" approach to conflict management is increasingly at odds with a younger generation of voters who demand factual transparency.

Equally concerning is the behavior of the party’s younger representatives, such as Jeon Yong-gi and Kim Dong-ah. Rather than acting as a reformist force, they have been described by critics as "bullet shields" for the party leadership, prioritizing their standing within the party hierarchy over political integrity. If the DPK continues to ignore the ultimatum from its own members, it risks long-term damage and provides the People Power Party, South Korea’s main conservative opposition, with a potent narrative of a ruling party drowning in its own fabrications.
