Ruling Party Infighting Deepens as DPK Approval Hits Record Low

November 16, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is facing an escalating leadership crisis after party chair Kim Min-seok ordered an unannounced ethics inspection into Senior Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee, sparking internal boycotts and procedural disputes as the party's approval rating fell to a record low of 34.6%.

  • Leadership boycott and convention rifts: DPK Supreme Council member Han Min-soo stated that three council members boycotted a leadership meeting because Kim ordered a late-night inspection into Choi without consulting her, linking the friction to unresolved grievances from the party's August 7 national convention.
  • Kim’s defense and a canceled hearing: Although the DPK Ethics Tribunal abruptly canceled a scheduled review of Choi’s case, Kim stated on November 16 that he referred the investigation's findings under party rules and urged members to respect an independent disciplinary process separate from politics.
  • Sinking poll numbers: A Realmeter weekly survey for the second week of November 2026 showed DPK support dropping to 34.6%, trailing the main conservative opposition People Power Party at 43.0%, while the Rebuilding Korea Party—described as both an ally and rival—climbed from 6.9% to 8.7%.
  • Pressuring the tribunal: Commentator Hong Sang-woo and YouTube channel "Kim Seung-hyun’s Thesis" criticized a joint statement by 20 to 30 DPK lawmakers demanding heavy punishment, arguing it undermines the nine-member tribunal's independence while alleged rule violations by Kim and aligned lawmakers go unaddressed.

A Test of Impartiality and Leadership

At the heart of the dispute is whether the ruling party’s disciplinary bodies are operating strictly by the book or being pulled into factional politics. Former DPK Supreme Council member Park Kyu-hwan noted that a party chair's direct inspection order is an extraordinary measure typically reserved for severe crises such as bribery, sexual misconduct, or campaign-damaging remarks. Park also stressed two norms: complete non-interference from leadership and a "first-in, first-out" docket, pointing out that an earlier complaint against Kim himself was already awaiting review before Choi’s case arrived.

The conflict also exposes broader frustration over communication and performance inside the party. Critics on "Kim Seung-hyun’s Thesis" questioned Kim’s transparency regarding general election task force funds and accused leadership of reviving old factional disputes instead of driving major reforms. Meanwhile, Han detailed how internal leaks regarding alleged Shincheonji religious group memberships and an August 27 workshop where a former convention rival—who won 46% of the vote—was repeatedly singled out deepened distrust within the party.

With President Lee Jae-myung’s administration relying on the DPK to shepherd its legislative agenda, the timing of this internal clash carries real political costs. While Kim maintains that the ethics process will remain strict and independent, the rising support for the Rebuilding Korea Party suggests that reform-minded voters are already watching closely. Unless the DPK leadership establishes transparent communication and applies party rules consistently across all factions, procedural battles risk overshadowing governance.
