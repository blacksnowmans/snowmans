South Korea's New Hit Knit Jacket Balances Luxury Finish with Practical Design

November 03, 2026

  • What happened: A newly launched collaborative knit jacket line designed with apparel brand Design May (led by CEO Lee Jin-ah) and media figure Kim Ou-joon has drawn massive attention in South Korea for combining high-end hand-finished details with everyday utility.
  • What it changes: The release proves that locally backed merchandise can bypass fast-fashion shortcuts, offering Merino wool-blend outerwear that mimics luxury craftsmanship at a fraction of the cost.
  • Who is affected: Consumers looking for versatile, multi-season layers (from early autumn to deep winter) will find broad unisex sizing options ranging from extra-small to extra-large.

Crafted for Durability and Everyday Utility

The secret behind the jacket's instant popularity isn't just the star power involved, but the rigorous attention paid to its construction. Unlike typical knitwear that tends to sag or lose its shape, this jacket uses a Merino wool blend designed to maintain a structured, tailored silhouette like a traditional blazer. Key practical additions include a clever dual-pocket layout—split horizontally and vertically—allowing wearers to stow phones and keys without ruining the jacket's clean exterior lines.

Furthermore, meticulous hand-finishing on the internal seams gives it a luxury feel rarely found at this price point. Available in both short and long versions, the line addresses common outerwear complaints by offering adjustable internal waist strings for a tailored silhouette and a versatile weight that works as a standalone piece in autumn or as a layered liner beneath a heavier coat in winter.

Our Take: More Than Just Merchandise

In a retail landscape flooded with cheap, disposable fast fashion, projects like this point to a shift toward intentional consumption. By resisting the temptation to cut corners on complex manufacturing processes—such as hand-stitching internal linings and engineering a true unisex fit—the creators have delivered something surprisingly robust. It treats casual apparel not as a throwaway item, but as a reliable daily tool.

Critics might roll their eyes at celebrity-backed fashion, but the execution here speaks for itself. By making gender-neutral comfort and smart tailoring accessible across a wide size range, it offers a refreshing antidote to rigid style norms. If this is the direction of modern Korean direct-to-consumer design, it’s a trend worth watching.
