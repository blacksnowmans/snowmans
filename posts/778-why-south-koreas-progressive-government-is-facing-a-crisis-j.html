Why South Korea's Progressive Government is Facing a Crisis Just One Year In

October 06, 2026

  • What happened: Prominent economist Choi Bae-geun publicly withdrew his support for South Korean President Lee Jae-myung, warning that the ruling Democratic Party faces a crushing defeat in upcoming elections unless it radically shifts course.
  • What it changes: The rare public break by a key intellectual exposes deep progressive disillusionment, eroding the government's reform mandate and handing momentum back to the conservative opposition.
  • Who is affected: Everyday workers struggling with stagnant real incomes, a ruling party paralyzed by internal factionalism, and a galvanized conservative opposition staging aggressive street rallies.

One year into the presidency of Lee Jae-myung, South Korea's progressive camp is grappling with a profound sense of betrayal. Economists and former allies are warning that if an election were held today, the ruling Democratic Party—South Korea's main liberal party—would suffer a historic defeat in Seoul and the wider metropolitan area. Critics argue the administration has abandoned its foundational promise to uplift ordinary citizens, prioritizing half-measures over structural economic reform.

The core grievance centers on economic inequality. Despite grand rhetoric about shared growth, working-class households have seen little tangible improvement in their daily lives, while the gap between major conglomerates and small businesses widens. Rather than implementing robust social safety nets, the government has relied on temporary holiday subsidies—a top-down approach critics liken to royal handouts rather than institutional rights. Meanwhile, prosecutors and conservative factions once sidelined by scandal are seizing on the administration's faltering approval ratings to mount aggressive counter-attacks.

For international observers watching Seoul's political arena, the situation highlights the perennial struggle of translating campaign populism into durable economic justice. When a progressive administration adopts cautious, business-friendly pragmatism to appease the political center, it risks alienating its base entirely. Unless President Lee's government moves beyond performative apologies and delivers systemic reform, it risks becoming another cautionary tale of a wasted reform mandate.
