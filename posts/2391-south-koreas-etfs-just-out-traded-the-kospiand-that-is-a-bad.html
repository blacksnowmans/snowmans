South Korea’s ETFs Just Out-Traded the KOSPI—and That Is a Bad Sign

November 16, 2026

Daily trading volume in exchange-traded funds (ETFs) has surpassed South Korea’s benchmark KOSPI stock market for the first time on record, exposing a severe liquidity drought in Korean equities rather than a healthy fund boom.

  • A hollowed-out main board: Last month, average daily ETF turnover reached about $14.56 billion (20.38 trillion won), edging past the KOSPI’s $14.54 billion (20.36 trillion won) only because main-board volume has plunged nearly 60% from past bull-market peaks above $35 billion (50 trillion won).
  • Capital flight to Wall Street: While the flagship domestic tracker KODEX 200 and the US-focused TIGER US S&P 500 both hold roughly $15 billion (21 trillion won) in assets, fresh inflows heavily favored the US fund at $6 billion (8.38 trillion won) versus about $3.6 billion (5 trillion won) for the Korean index.
  • Tied to Nasdaq futures: With local institutional and retail buyers largely absent, passive algorithmic flows linked to US Nasdaq futures drive Seoul’s intraday swings, forcing Korean shares to absorb daytime drops while missing overnight US rebounds.
  • A polarized retail crowd: Local individual investors remaining in the market are either gambling on rebounds—pouring about $2.9 billion (4 trillion won) into 2x leveraged ETFs last month—or retreating into safety, shifting over $7 billion (10 trillion won) this year into bond funds and high-payout covered-call ETFs.

A Market Running on Empty

On the surface, South Korea’s ETF sector looks like a runaway success, having reached $225 billion (315.7 trillion won) in net assets across 1,058 listed products. In reality, investors have simply given up on picking individual Korean companies. As trading dries up, order books thin out, causing minor selloffs to trigger sharp swings in heavyweight chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix—which pushes even more traders out of single stocks and into index baskets or US funds.

That shift carries a real structural cost. When basket ETFs dominate a shallow market, constituent stocks rise and fall together regardless of corporate earnings. Worse, because active domestic buyers have stepped back, Seoul’s exchange increasingly behaves like a daytime shadow of New York, stripped of independent price discovery.

Desperation Meets Caution

The behavior of retail investors still trading at home underscores how fragile sentiment has become. Market data from January 2020 through June 2026 shows individual investors’ purchases of KODEX Leverage ETFs aligned with positive daily returns just 12.5% of the time—compared with 55.8% for foreign investors—yet traders keep doubling down to recoup losses. Meanwhile, cautious savers are flocking to products like TIGER Dividend Covered Call, which sell away future upside for cash distributions, risking capped gains and principal losses if share prices keep sliding. Until domestic capital regains trust in Korean corporate fundamentals, ETF volume beating the KOSPI will remain a symptom of market exhaustion, not strength.
