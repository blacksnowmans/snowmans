South Korea’s Ruling Party Advances Special Counsel Bill to Probe "Fabricated" Prosecutions

November 05, 2026

A bill to establish a Special Counsel to investigate allegations of evidence fabrication and political bias within the state prosecution service has cleared a key legislative subcommittee, signaling a major push by the ruling party to overhaul the nation’s judiciary.

  • The Legislation and Judiciary Committee’s subcommittee passed the "Fabricated Prosecution Special Counsel Bill" on November 4, driven by the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DP), the country’s main liberal party.
  • The legislation targets historical cases where prosecutors are suspected of distorting evidence or pursuing politically motivated indictments, particularly during the tenure of the now-imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • The main opposition, the conservative People Power Party (PPP), has denounced the move as "legislative tyranny" and vowed to launch a filibuster to block the bill during the plenary session scheduled for November 5.
  • During the review, the DP removed a controversial clause that would have granted the Special Counsel the power to unilaterally withdraw existing indictments, a move intended to preserve legal stability while focusing on the investigation.

Curbing the "Monopoly on Indictment"

For decades, South Korea’s prosecution service has held an exclusive "monopoly on indictment," a power critics argue was frequently used as a political weapon. This bill is the Democratic Party’s most aggressive attempt yet to bring the agency under public scrutiny. By focusing on the era when Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol served as Prosecutor General and later as president before his impeachment, the DP aims to dismantle what it describes as a "prosecutorial dictatorship" that targeted political rivals through selective evidence gathering and relentless raids.

The strategic removal of the indictment-cancellation clause is particularly noteworthy. While it limits the immediate corrective power of the Special Counsel, it focuses the probe on the act of fabrication itself. This narrows the legal battlefield, making it harder for the opposition to argue that the bill violates the separation of powers. In my view, the DP’s leadership is positioning this not as a shield for President Lee Jae-myung, as the opposition claims, but as a systemic fix to ensure prosecutors can no longer use their authority to destroy lives for political gain.

Gridlock in the National Assembly

The political stakes are at an all-time high as the bill moves to the plenary session. The People Power Party views this as a direct assault on the judicial system designed to rewrite the legal history of the current administration’s rivals. By threatening to filibuster every item on the legislative agenda, the PPP is signaling a potential shutdown of the National Assembly. They argue that the DP is taking the country’s legislative business hostage to settle political scores from the previous era.

However, the ruling party appears undeterred. With a solid majority, the DP is expected to push through the filibuster using procedural tools. This reflects a broader sentiment within the party that judicial reform is a prerequisite for a functioning democracy. From the DP’s perspective, the "Special Counsel" is the only tool left to investigate an agency that has historically excelled at "protecting its own." As the session approaches, the focus remains on whether this investigation will finally expose the alleged "dirty deals" hidden behind decades of official indictments.
