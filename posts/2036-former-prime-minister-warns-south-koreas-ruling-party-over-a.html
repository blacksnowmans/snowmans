Former Prime Minister Warns South Korea's Ruling Party Over Arrogance and Toxic Factionalism

November 06, 2026

Former Prime Minister Kim Boo-kyum has delivered a stinging rebuke to South Korea's ruling Democratic Party, warning that ideological complacency and aggressive hardline factions are alienating young voters ahead of upcoming legislative elections.

  • The critique: Kim accused the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) of resting on grand democratic rhetoric while failing to ease economic inequality, soaring living costs, and job insecurity for younger generations.
  • The factional threat: A militant, exclusionary pro-leadership faction—often dubbed the "New Lee" loyalists—is attacking internal party critics, repeating toxic patterns that drove swing voters away during past administrations.
  • A communications pivot: President Lee Jae-myung has cut his posting on X (formerly Twitter) by more than half, pivoting toward scripted, long-form YouTube conversations to curb messaging missteps, though critics warn against self-congratulatory spin.
  • Legal overhang: Lingering disputes over vague clauses in the Public Official Election Act—underscored by the recent conviction of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol for spreading false campaign statements—continue to fuel calls for legislative reform to protect political discourse from weaponized prosecutions.

Grand Narratives vs. Bread-and-Butter Frustration

Kim’s intervention targets the core vulnerability facing the ruling camp. Recent National Barometer Surveys show President Lee's approval rating hovering at around 50%, with negative evaluations closely trailing at 45%. While the DPK has championed major institutional battles, ordinary citizens in their twenties and thirties face harsh economic realities that grand slogans cannot fix.

Recent housing controversies highlight this disconnect. Government messaging touted modest price dips in ultra-luxury enclaves like Seoul's Gangnam district as proof of policy success, even as housing costs in middle-class neighborhoods across the capital region continued to climb. For younger workers shut out of the housing market, such rhetoric reads less like progress and more like bureaucratic indifference.

The Cost of Fanatic Partisanship

Compounding this policy gap is an increasingly toxic internal culture. Hardline partisan groups are blacklisting moderate lawmakers who offer critical feedback, branding them as disloyal. Political data consistently demonstrates that when militant wings capture a party's public identity, moderate and independent voters simply tune out or stay home on election day.

Switching communications channels from abrasive social media spats to in-depth YouTube sessions offers President Lee a chance to articulate a calmer, more coherent vision. Yet cosmetic format changes cannot substitute for genuine discipline. Unless the DPK leadership explicitly distances itself from aggressive online factions and pivots toward tangible bread-and-butter relief, it risks turning voter fatigue into a bruising electoral defeat.
