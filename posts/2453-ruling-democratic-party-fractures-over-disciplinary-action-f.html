Ruling Democratic Party Fractures Over Disciplinary Action for Supreme Council Member Choi Min-hee

November 18, 2026

A deepening divide within South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) has burst into the open as dozens of first-term lawmakers demand the resignation of a top party leader, clashing directly with senior members who favor a lenient approach.

  • Freshman Revolt: 55 first-term DPK lawmakers issued a formal statement on November 17 calling for Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee to resign and face "severe disciplinary measures" over controversial leaked transcripts and public gaffes.
  • Veteran Pushback: Senior party figures, led by veteran lawmaker Park Jie-won, countered that harsh punishment would spark a "civil war" and worsen internal instability within the party of President Lee Jae-myung.
  • Formal Referral: The case has been officially referred to the party’s Ethics Tribunal, moving the conflict from informal internal debate to a formal legalistic process that could determine Choi’s political future.
  • Broader Instability: While the DPK struggles with internal discipline, the main opposition People Power Party is simultaneously facing its own ethics scandals involving its leader, Han Dong-hoon, and lawmaker Bae Hyun-jin.

A Generational Divide in the Ruling Party

The conflict centers on Choi Min-hee, a member of the DPK’s Supreme Council—the party's highest decision-making body. The controversy stems from leaked recordings from October and subsequent confusing explanations she offered regarding the impeachment of Prime Minister Han Duck-soo. When Choi claimed she had "confused" various political issues, the original whistleblower publicly mocked the excuse, further embarrassing the party leadership. This prompted Deomincho, the influential group of freshman DPK lawmakers, to demand she take responsibility for damaging the party’s image.

However, the freshman group is not a monolith. Reports indicate that lawmakers closely aligned with the President Lee Jae-myung administration—the "pro-Cheong" faction—declined to sign the protest statement. This suggests that the push for Choi's resignation is as much about internal power dynamics and party reform as it is about her specific comments. By going public with their demands, these 55 lawmakers have effectively forced the party leadership into a corner where they must choose between upholding strict ethical standards or maintaining a fragile internal peace.

The Fear of a "Psychological Divorce"

Senior party members are sounding the alarm that the DPK is approaching a "psychological divorce," a state where different factions coexist in name only but are functionally at war. Park Jie-won, a veteran political strategist known for his influence within the party, has been vocal in his opposition to heavy discipline. He argues that punishing a high-ranking official like Choi under pressure from junior members would provide a "spark for internal strife" that the ruling party cannot afford as it manages the national agenda under President Lee.

The situation is now in the hands of the party's Ethics Tribunal. This body must weigh the freshman group's demand for "strict accountability" against the veterans' warning of a "civil war." If the tribunal issues a light warning, it may signal that the party's old guard still holds the reins, potentially alienating the younger, reform-minded wing. Conversely, a heavy sanction could trigger the very "war" Park Jie-won fears. With the main opposition People Power Party also embroiled in its own internal ethics disputes, the coming weeks will reveal which of Korea's major parties can successfully purge internal scandals without collapsing into total factional warfare.
