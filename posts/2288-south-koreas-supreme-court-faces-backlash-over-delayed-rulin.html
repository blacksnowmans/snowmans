South Korea's Supreme Court Faces Backlash Over Delayed Ruling on Ex-PM Han Duck-soo

November 13, 2026

South Korea’s Supreme Court is facing fierce political and public backlash after setting a final ruling date for former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo on charges of aiding an insurrection just one day before his legal detention expires.

  • The Eleventh-Hour Verdict: Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae scheduled the en banc ruling for November 19, leaving only 24 hours before Han’s pretrial detention mandate lapses on November 20, ending six months of judicial inaction since the appeal was filed in May.
  • Allegations of a Double Standard: Lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea argue the delay reveals bias, contrasting the six-month wait with the mere nine days the court took to rule on an election law case involving current President Lee Jae-myung.
  • Growing Calls for Resignation: Democratic Party lawmakers and civil reform factions have demanded Chief Justice Cho step down, alleging the top court is intentionally stalling to facilitate the release of key insurrection figures.
  • Policy Discipline Amid the Storm: Despite judicial friction, the Lee Jae-myung administration and the Democratic Party moved swiftly to maintain focus on everyday livelihoods, dispelling rumors of policy rifts over real estate tax exemptions and tightening coordination on the state budget.

Judicial Stalling and Constitutional Trust

The controversy centers on whether South Korea's judiciary is extending leniency to key officials tied to the unconstitutional actions under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was later impeached, removed from office, and imprisoned. Han Duck-soo stands accused of playing a critical role in that constitutional subversion. By sitting on the case for half a year and scheduling a verdict on the eve of his detention deadline, the Supreme Court risked allowing a high-profile insurrection defendant to walk free on a procedural technicality. Critics, including Democratic Party lawmakers Han Min-soo and Roh Jong-myeon, contend that the high court's foot-dragging sends a chilling message to lower court judges handling sensitive cases, rewarding delaying tactics and undermining strict judicial enforcement.

The Government’s Push for Stability

While the court faces scrutiny, the Lee Jae-myung administration has focused on insulating its governance agenda from the judicial controversy. Presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jeong stressed that the executive branch and the legislature must act as one to pass next year's budget. The ruling party also moved swiftly to extinguish internal policy discord: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok rejected reports that the party officially proposed lifting the comprehensive real estate tax basic deduction threshold to 1.4 billion won (about $1 million), emphasizing that the administration’s housing policy remains uniform and steady.

The standoff underscores an escalating institutional divide between the ruling bloc and the judiciary. If the Supreme Court is perceived as applying double standards between sitting leaders and defendants charged with subverting the constitution, public confidence in the judicial branch faces irreversible damage.
