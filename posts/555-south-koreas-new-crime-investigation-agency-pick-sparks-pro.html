South Korea's New Crime Investigation Agency Pick Sparks Pro-Government Backlash

October 01, 2026

  • President Lee Jae-myung publicly clashed with a prominent supporter on social media over the nomination of Kim Ji-yong to head the newly established Serious Crime Investigation Agency.
  • Critics, including grassroots supporters and progressive politicians, argue that appointing a former prosecutor tied to past administrations undermines prosecutorial reform.
  • The nomination process has stalled in the National Assembly, fueling internal friction within the ruling Democratic Party and drawing fierce attacks from the conservative opposition.
  • Public opinion polls show high levels of uncertainty and opposition regarding the appointment, highlighting deep voter fatigue over ongoing prosecutorial battles.

South Korea's political landscape is once again embroiled in an intense controversy over high-level state appointments, this time centering on the inaugural chief of the Serious Crime Investigation Agency. The fallout from President Lee Jae-myung’s push for Kim Ji-yong has exposed a widening chasm between the presidential office and the grassroots supporters who brought the administration to power. When a moderate supporter posted a polite appeal on X urging the president not to appoint a figure widely viewed as a political prosecutor, the response from the top office was unexpectedly sharp.

Rather than absorbing the critique, President Lee directly reposted the message, dismissing the feedback as malicious state interference based on falsehoods. This defensive reaction immediately weaponized the online debate, turning a legitimate policy concern into an internal factional war. Supporters who expressed doubts about the nominee were suddenly branded as obstructionists or bad actors, plunging the ruling party's online communities into chaotic infighting. For an administration that rode to power on promises of open communication and reform, weaponizing social media against loyal voters marks a troubling shift toward defensive insularity.

Reform in Name Only? The Contradictions of the Nominee

The core of the public outcry is not merely about bureaucratic pedigree, but about the symbolic weight of putting a career prosecutor in charge of an agency designed to check prosecutorial overreach. While the Blue House defends Kim Ji-yong by pointing to his past demotions under the conservative administration of Yoon Suk Yeol and his involvement in past investigative orders, the public is suffering from acute fatigue regarding recycled judicial elites. Opinion polls indicate that public skepticism runs high, with substantial numbers opposing the pick or remaining undecided as the legislative confirmation drags on.

By framing pushback from his own base as an attack and leaning on his personal history as a target of past investigations, the president risks alienating the very public he serves. Leadership requires absorbing difficult truths rather than delegitimizing civilian critique as sabotage. As the launch date for the new investigative body looms closer, the administration’s handling of this appointment suggests that political maneuvering and self-preservation are once again overshadowing genuine institutional reform.
