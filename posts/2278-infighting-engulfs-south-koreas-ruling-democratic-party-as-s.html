Infighting Engulfs South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party as Senior Lawmakers Clash

November 13, 2026

A bitter public feud between senior lawmakers Kim Min-seok and Jung Chung-rae has plunged South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea into open factional warfare, threatening to stall the legislative momentum of President Lee Jae-myung’s administration.

  • Jung Chung-rae rebuffed a dialogue offer from party leader Kim Min-seok, calling Kim’s broadcast appearance disingenuous "self-promotion" and demanding a sincere apology to grassroots party members before any sit-down.
  • The dispute is rooted in an unresolved scandal regarding an alleged secret alliance involving the fringe religious sect Shincheonji, which critics accuse Kim of trying to whitewash with a superficial apology.
  • The confrontation broadened into an institutional showdown when allies of Jung demanded an internal ethics audit into a party task force, alleging it was conducting a targeted political witch-hunt against him.
  • Attempts at mediation by veteran elders, including lawmaker Park Jie-won, have failed to bridge the divide, leaving the ruling party leadership paralyzed ahead of Kim’s 100-day milestone.

Sectarian Allegations and Weaponized Party Machinery

At the center of the breakdown is an alleged "three-party secret alliance" involving Shincheonji, a controversial fringe religious sect. Allegations that the group meddled in internal party affairs have provoked intense fury among Democratic Party loyalists, who see any entanglement with the sect as a violation of the party's core values. Although Kim Min-seok offered a public apology to quell the uproar, Jung’s camp and grassroots supporters dismissed the gesture as an evasive "self-apology" meant to bypass a full investigation.

The rift worsened when former Supreme Council member Park Gyu-hwan formally demanded an inspection into the party's "Party Member Reorganization Task Force." Jung’s faction alleges that the leadership repurposed this internal organ into an instrument to purge political rivals. Critics within the party have gone so far as to argue that using internal regulations to silence dissent resembles the heavy-handed tactics seen during Mr. Yoon Suk Yeol's term in office, compounding bad blood between the rival camps.

Paralysis at the Top of the Ruling Coalition

The total collapse in trust demonstrates that the ruling party currently lacks the mechanisms—or the elder statesmanship—to resolve internal disputes quietly. Interventions by senior politicians like Park Jie-won have fallen flat, as both sides focus on rallying their respective supporter bases through the media rather than negotiating behind closed doors. With the central leadership absorbed by factional skirmishes, basic organizational discipline is beginning to fray, extending to embarrassing local controversies such as a municipal councilor's abuse-of-power scandal in South Gyeongsang Province.

This prolonged civil war comes at an inopportune moment for President Lee Jae-myung's government. Instead of providing unified legislative backing for the administration's economic and domestic policy priorities, the Democratic Party is squandering political capital on internal investigations and hostile media campaigns. Unless party leadership resolves the Shincheonji controversy with a transparent accounting rather than procedural maneuvers, this factional gridlock will continue to weigh heavily on the governing coalition.
