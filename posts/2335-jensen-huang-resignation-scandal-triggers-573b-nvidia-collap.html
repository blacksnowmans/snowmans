Jensen Huang Resignation Scandal Triggers $573B Nvidia Collapse and Threatens South Korean AI Deals

November 15, 2026

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation following an internal deception scandal has wiped out $573 billion in market value within a week and thrown global semiconductor supply chains into turmoil.

  • What happened: Huang resigned after audits revealed he lied to Nvidia executives about an inappropriate relationship with an individual named Alice Huang, falsely framing it as routine business communication.
  • The fake letter hoax: Market panic intensified after a fraudulent document—plagiarized from OpenAI's 2023 employee revolt letter—falsely claimed top executives threatened a mass exodus to SpaceX unless Huang was reinstated.
  • Who is affected: South Korea faces a full review of a planned $510 billion joint AI project, while suppliers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix confront severe customer concentration risks.
  • What changes: Nvidia CFO Colette Kress has emerged as the leading leadership candidate, signaling a shift toward financial conservatism over high-risk infrastructure expansion.

Governance Collapse and Leadership Vacuum

The sudden departure of Jensen Huang has exposed extreme structural vulnerability within Nvidia, ending three decades of single-leader dominance. Internal morale is deeply divided, evidenced by over 1,600 conflicting comments on company message boards between Huang loyalists and employees critical of his ethical breach. While rumors of recruiting Arm CEO René Haas were denied by Nvidia's board, CFO Colette Kress has taken center stage. Known for pushing back against Huang's aggressive capital expenditures and startup funding, Kress's potential appointment marks a pivot toward cautious fiscal management. This leadership shift threatens to slow down next-generation chip development while competitors like AMD, Broadcom, and Google accelerate their own AI hardware initiatives.

South Korea Braces for Supply Chain and Geopolitical Fallout

The shockwaves have hit South Korea’s technology sector immediately. The Presidential Committee on National AI Strategy—the top advisory body under President Lee Jae-myung’s administration—convened an emergency meeting to assess supply chain risks after a proposed $510 billion joint AI initiative with Nvidia was thrown into doubt. South Korea’s chipmakers, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, are now under pressure to diversify their client base as their heavy reliance on Nvidia for High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) orders exposes them to leadership turbulence in Silicon Valley.

Compounding the corporate crisis is a growing geopolitical threat. Allegations in Washington that export controls to China were loosely managed under Huang’s tenure have reignited calls for tougher US semiconductor sanctions. In response, Chinese experts have warned of potential retaliatory export curbs on rare earth minerals, threatening to drag the post-Jensen Huang fallout into a broader resource war between major powers.
