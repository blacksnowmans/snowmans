Progressive Pundit Rhyu Si-min Debunks Wealth Rumors and Bets on President Lee’s Market Vision

October 29, 2026

Rhyu Si-min, one of South Korea’s most influential progressive commentators and bestselling authors, has publicly pushed back against viral rumors claiming he amassed a fortune of 100 billion won, revealing instead that his net worth sits below $3.7 million and that he recently began trading stocks for the first time in his life.

  • Wealth rumors dismissed: Addressing persistent online allegations of holding between 10 billion and 100 billion won (about $7.4 million to $74 million), Rhyu clarified that his total assets do not even reach 5 billion won (about $3.7 million).
  • Zero savings at 55: The former health minister stated he had virtually no savings when he retired from frontline politics in 2013 at age 55, building his current savings solely through royalties by publishing a book every year.
  • First-time investor: Rhyu disclosed that he liquidated bank deposits to enter the equity market for the first time, buying semiconductor, renewable energy stocks, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) on faith in President Lee Jae-myung’s pledge to propel South Korea's benchmark stock index to 5,000 points.
  • New commentary doctrine: Returning to broadcast commentary, Rhyu announced he will drop moralistic "normative assessments" in favor of an emotionally detached, "alien observer" lens to evaluate politics objectively.

Royalties, Not Riches

Accusations of covert wealth have long been a favored weapon among conservative critics looking to paint progressive figures as champagne socialists. Rhyu, who served as Minister of Health and Welfare in the 2000s and remains a key intellectual voice aligned with the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, noted that even personal acquaintances had started asking whether the online fabrications were true. By laying bare his finances—crediting steady book sales such as his European travel series rather than lucrative business dealings—he sought to shut down smear campaigns aimed at undermining his credibility.

His disclosure about buying equities is equally telling. Despite decades in public life, Rhyu had never owned stocks until President Lee Jae-myung championed a target of driving the domestic index past 5,000 points. For a figure of Rhyu's profile, directing personal capital into green energy and microchips is both a financial wager and a public vote of confidence in the Lee administration’s industrial agenda.

Critique Through an "Alien" Lens

South Korea’s political sphere remains deeply partisan following the turmoil of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure and subsequent impeachment. In this charged environment, commentary that offers unqualified praise risks being dismissed as sycophancy, while any criticism of the ruling camp draws fury from staunch loyalists. Rhyu admitted that his previous commentary often relied too heavily on subjective judgments of what is "virtuous" or "vulgar."

His pivot to observing political developments like an "alien studying Earth" reflects the growing difficulty progressive commentators face in maintaining analytical independence. Rhyu clearly wants President Lee's administration to succeed, but recognizes that knee-jerk ideological cheerleading helps neither the government nor its voters. Whether an outspoken partisan warrior can genuinely sustain the cold detachment of an extraterrestrial observer remains an open question, but his shift marks an effort to inject data and pragmatism into South Korea’s heated political discourse.
