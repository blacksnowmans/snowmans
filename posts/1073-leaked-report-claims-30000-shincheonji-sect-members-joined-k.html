Leaked Report Claims 30,000 Shincheonji Sect Members Joined Korea's Ruling Party

October 13, 2026

An internal party probe has revealed that an estimated 30,000 to 50,000 members of the controversial Shincheonji religious sect systematically registered as card-carrying members of South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK).

  • The Findings: According to a scoop by the financial daily Maeil Business Newspaper, a DPK verification task force led by lawmaker Park Sung-won uncovered the mass enrollment and submitted its findings to senior leadership under party leader Kim Min-seok.
  • The Political Reversal: The revelation mirrors past controversies that plagued the conservative People Power Party (PPP), leaving the ruling party vulnerable to charges of hypocrisy after years of attacking its rivals over alleged sect ties.
  • Grassroots Outrage: Rank-and-file party members have reacted with fury over the leadership’s handling of the sensitive audit, demanding total transparency and resignations ahead of a pivotal October 25 runoff election.
  • Mysterious Data: Serious questions remain over how party investigators obtained cross-referencing material, as matching party rolls to sect members typically requires confidential state investigative records or internal church registries.

The Hypocrisy Trap and Leadership Turmoil

The scandal strikes at a particularly sensitive nerve for the DPK. The party built significant political momentum in past cycles by targeting the conservative opposition over its suspected links to Shincheonji—an apocalyptic religious group that faced intense public backlash during the COVID-19 pandemic. Now that an internal audit suggests up to 50,000 sect members may have entered the DPK's own membership base, the ruling party faces severe blowback and risks losing the moral high ground to the PPP.

The leak also exposes deepening fractures within party headquarters. The fact that an internal investigation of this magnitude surfaced via media reports rather than an official briefing suggests severe breaches of party discipline. Grassroots loyalists, already sensitized by bruising factional contests, are openly questioning whether leadership under Kim Min-seok sought to suppress the findings, fueling calls for accountability and senior resignations.

A Test for the Post-Impeachment Ruling Coalition

The controversy surfaces as the DPK navigates growing pains under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung. Having returned to power following the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the progressive bloc has faced criticism from its base for slipping into establishment complacency and abandoning structural reforms in favor of ambiguous centrist compromise.

With an internal runoff scheduled for October 25, the DPK cannot afford a prolonged dispute over sectarian infiltration. Core supporters are signaling that partisan loyalty will not excuse evasive answers or institutional cover-ups. How the leadership resolves the Shincheonji audit will serve as a crucial test of whether the ruling party retains its reformist credibility or succumbs to the very backroom compromises it once denounced.
