APEC Summit Outlook: Navigating the U.S.-China-Russia Shift

October 05, 2026

As global powers realign ahead of the Apec summit, South Korea faces an urgent choice between blindly trusting traditional US security guarantees and forging an independent foreign policy.

  • The Changing Global Order: Potential US-China-Russia talks signal a shift away from a US-led unipolar world toward a multipolar reality.
  • Fading US Influence: Diminishing American security commitments in the Middle East and Latin America show that relying solely on Washington is a dangerous gamble.
  • Domestic Political Distractions: While global alliances fracture, South Korea's political class remains bogged down by petty framing and historical controversies.
  • Strategic Necessity: Seoul must pursue independent defense capabilities, including the urgent transfer of wartime operational control from Washington.

The upcoming Apec summit is more than a diplomatic photo opportunity; it is a symptom of a rapidly shifting international order. For decades, South Korea's foreign policy has rested on the comfortable assumption that the United States would always underwrite regional stability. But recent events in the Middle East—where Saudi Arabia sought alternative missile suppliers after receiving no help from Washington against attacks—prove that the old rules no longer apply. Traditional allies from Europe to Canada are hedging their bets, and Seoul must do the same.

On the Korean Peninsula, this shifting landscape creates both risk and opportunity. Intelligence assessments suggest a fair chance of renewed US-North Korea dialogue under a Trump administration eager for quick wins. Yet South Korea’s security establishment remains largely passive, paralyzed by over-reliance on Washington and fearful of regional friction. Meanwhile, Japan’s quiet push toward potential nuclear latency and expanded rearmament poses an immediate, tangible threat to Korean security—one that conservative leaders in Seoul have dangerously ignored.

Ultimately, a nation that fails to build independent diplomatic and defense capabilities reduces itself to a pawn in great power games. South Korea must drop its passive stance, take charge of its own security destiny, and look past short-term domestic political bickering before it is left entirely on the sidelines of history.
