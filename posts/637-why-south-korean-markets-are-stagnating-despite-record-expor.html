Why South Korean Markets are Stagnating Despite Record Exports

October 03, 2026

South Korean investors are currently facing a frustrating paradox: despite record-breaking export figures in key sectors like cosmetics and heavy machinery, stock prices remain trapped in a stagnant, uninspiring range. The primary culprit isn't a lack of production, but a market-wide obsession with the "peak-out" fear—the belief that the current semiconductor boom will hit a ceiling by 2027.

  • The 2027 Peak-Out Fear: Investors are ignoring current strong earnings, looking instead at the potential for a cyclical downturn in the semiconductor industry three years from now.
  • Export Disconnect: While cosmetics and transformer exports have hit all-time highs, currency fluctuations and rising input costs are suppressing stock valuations.
  • The 2028 Benchmark: The market is shifting its focus to 2028, specifically watching whether "hyperscalers"—large-scale cloud providers like Google and Meta—can transition their AI-heavy spending into positive free cash flow (FCF).
  • High Cost of Capital: Persistent high interest rates are forcing companies to meet much higher return requirements, making it difficult for even strong growth stocks to see price appreciation.

The Paradox of Semiconductors and Exports

The market is currently undergoing a painful "re-rating" of what it considers value. Semiconductor giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are trading at deceptively low price-to-earnings (PER) ratios. While these valuations suggest a "buy" signal, investors are paralyzed by the fear that 2027 marks the end of the current semiconductor super-cycle. Market participants are no longer betting on current production volumes; they are betting on survival in a post-2027 landscape.

This same anxiety is bleeding into other sectors. For instance, Korean cosmetics exports grew significantly (up 28.2% year-on-year), yet the stocks of companies like Cosmax and Silicon Two have faced corrections. Analysts often blame this on rising raw material or packaging costs, but the deeper reality is that markets are questioning the sustainability of these growth rates. As export prices for components like NAND flash memory show signs of softening, the market is signaling that it is tired of "volume-based" growth and is now demanding proof of structural profitability.

Beyond the Casino: Why AI Spending is Under Scrutiny

The current AI investment boom mirrors the early days of the railroad or automotive industries—an era of immense capital expenditure with uncertain immediate returns. The market is waiting for the 2028 "inflection point" when hyperscalers must prove their massive AI infrastructure spending actually translates into sustainable profit.

For investors, the takeaway is clear: the era of betting on "AI" as a generic buzzword is over. We are entering a period of harsh stock picking. Companies that can demonstrate they are clearing the hurdle of high capital costs while maintaining margins will survive; those merely riding the wave of sector-wide volume will likely be left behind. In this environment, raw data—specifically the transition to positive free cash flow—is a much more reliable compass than the broad, volatile swings of the KOSPI index.
