The End of Traditional Finance: A Shift in Global Power

October 05, 2026

South Korean author and financial observer Oh Tae-min argues that the global financial order is undergoing a structural collapse as power shifts from state-controlled institutions to borderless, on-chain ecosystems.

  • The Death of Monopoly: Traditional state control over interest rates, currency issuance, and capital flow is being eroded by decentralized, borderless markets.
  • The Platform Bottleneck: Financial power is migrating from banks to exchange platforms; those who fail to control these bottlenecks risk becoming "financial colonies."
  • Standardization Wars: Ethereum has emerged as the definitive standard for the on-chain economy, effectively becoming the "Yahoo of the internet era."
  • Corporate Hesitancy: South Korean conglomerates (chaebols) are sidelined by a "reputation risk" trap, staying out of the crypto sector to avoid regulatory and public backlash.

The Regulatory Trap and the Loss of Sovereignty

South Korea’s financial regulators, such as the Financial Services Commission (FSC) and the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS), remain trapped in a defensive, 20th-century mindset. By focusing on punishing market manipulation within a "fenced-in" domestic market, regulators are failing to grasp that the real economy has already moved beyond their reach. While South Korea possesses world-class IT infrastructure and ubiquitous smartphone adoption, its restrictive legal environment prevents domestic firms from competing on the global stage. Major corporate retreats—such as the holding company NXC exiting the cryptocurrency exchange KORBIT—highlight how fear of regulatory volatility is stifling local innovation.

The danger is not just a missed business opportunity; it is the erosion of financial sovereignty. As decentralised finance (DeFi) platforms grow—with projections suggesting the market could reach $420 billion by 2035—South Korean investors will increasingly migrate to foreign platforms like Robinhood to trade tokenized assets. When that happens, the government’s attempt to "protect" the market through isolation will be rendered moot, leaving Korea with no influence over the new financial reality.

Infrastructure vs. Speculation

A crucial lesson for the future is that the biggest winners of a technological revolution are rarely the assets themselves, but the infrastructure providers. Much like the gold miners who profited by selling jeans, Nvidia thrived by providing the GPUs necessary for the crypto-mining boom, even as individual tokens fluctuated wildly. The same dynamic applies to the current landscape: Ethereum provides the essential plumbing for the on-chain economy—a necessary utility—while Bitcoin acts as the ultimate, scarce store of value.

As we head toward a "machine economy," where AI agents handle real-time, micro-transactions across borders, the current portal-dominated, fee-heavy web will collapse. In this future, the entities that control the AI infrastructure and the most efficient protocols will dictate the flow of global capital. For the individual investor, the message is clear: stop obsessing over daily price volatility and start looking at the 40-year horizon. Ignoring the shift to decentralized assets is not just cautious; it is a surrender to the inevitable inflation of state-issued currencies.
