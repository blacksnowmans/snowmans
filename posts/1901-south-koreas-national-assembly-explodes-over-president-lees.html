South Korea’s National Assembly Explodes Over President Lee’s $600B Budget

November 03, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung’s policy address for a record 821 trillion won ($600 billion) national budget was temporarily halted by shouting opposition lawmakers, signaling a fierce legislative battle over South Korea’s economic trajectory.

  • What happened: Lawmakers from the main opposition People Power Party disrupted President Lee Jae-myung’s address to the National Assembly, calling the budget a "declaration of future pillaging" and pledging to cut at least 30 trillion won ($22 billion).
  • Minor party reaction: The conservative Reform Party also slammed the budget, warning that state-funded consumer vouchers would fuel inflation rather than provide lasting relief.
  • Crackdown on disinformation: Police referred right-wing commentator Jun Han-gil to prosecutors for spreading false conspiracy theories about President Lee and the historic 1980 Gwangju Democratic Uprising.
  • Global parallels: The political friction in Seoul coincides with rising far-right populism in Europe, where parties like Alternative for Germany (AfD) are adapting tactics to capture mainstream power.

Fiscal Clash: The Battle Over South Korea’s Super-Budget

The scene inside South Korea's National Assembly highlighted the deep partisan divide under President Lee Jae-myung’s administration. The ruling Democratic Party of Korea defended the proposed 821 trillion won budget—which includes a 104 trillion won ($76 billion) fund for future investments and public welfare—as a vital fiscal safety net. However, leaders of the main opposition People Power Party, including party chief Jang Dong-hyeok and floor leader Jeong Jeom-sik, immediately condemned the plan as reckless populism that shifts massive debt onto future generations.

The smaller conservative opposition Reform Party, led by lawmaker Lee Jun-seok, offered its own sharp critique, comparing the government's spending forecast to treating a temporary tech boom as permanent revenue. With the opposition digging in to force deep spending cuts, the upcoming budget review promises an uncompromising showdown over fiscal policy.

Legal Repercussions for Extremist Disinformation

Beyond the parliamentary hall, South Korean law enforcement is taking action against online extremist rhetoric. Police officially referred commentator Jun Han-gil to the Prosecution Service on charges of defamation and violating special laws regarding the 1980 Gwangju Democratic Uprising. Jun had repeatedly circulated baseless claims online, including false rumors regarding President Lee's personal life and conspiracy theories alleging North Korean involvement in the Gwangju pro-democracy movement. The case marks a significant legal effort to hold online agitators accountable for fabricating historical narratives and damaging public discourse.

This domestic friction reflects a broader international pattern of political polarization. Across Europe, far-right groups such as Germany's AfD are attempting to soften their anti-EU positions to enter mainstream government by 2027, even as public protests highlight growing societal discontent. Whether in Seoul or European capitals, the erosion of constructive debate in favor of radical rhetoric presents a persistent test for democratic institutions.
