South Korea’s Economic Balancing Act and the Strategy Behind Potential Impeachment

October 28, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung, leader of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), is currently navigating a dual crisis: a disconnect between strong national macroeconomic indicators and the struggling daily lives of citizens, alongside a delicate political strategy regarding potential impeachment proceedings.

  • Economic Paradox: While South Korea recorded the world’s second-largest current account surplus in the second quarter, high-level growth has yet to translate into improved livelihoods for small business owners and job-seeking youth.
  • Strategic Caution: Despite strong internal pressure from party supporters to immediately pursue impeachment against figures linked to the previous administration, the DPK is prioritizing a parliamentary investigation (a "state audit") first.
  • Political Risk: The DPK’s leadership fears that a premature impeachment attempt—if rejected by the courts—would trigger a massive political backlash, potentially derailing their wider reform agenda.

Growth vs. Reality: The Economic Divide

President Lee has explicitly linked the instability seen in various European nations to the dangers of low growth, arguing that when an economy stagnates, the most vulnerable citizens suffer first. He warns that South Korea must avoid this trajectory by focusing on how the "fruits of growth" are distributed, rather than just chasing raw GDP numbers.

The administration’s challenge is to bridge the gap between impressive export figures and the "cold" reality felt on the ground. Policy shifts are now being directed toward boosting domestic consumption and investing in high-tech industries to create tangible opportunities for the youth, who face a shrinking window for advancement compared to previous generations.

The DPK’s Measured Approach to Political Justice

The political landscape is dominated by the shadow of the previous administration. With Yoon Suk-yeol—the former leader who was impeached and removed from office—currently incarcerated, the DPK is under pressure to finish the process of "clearing out the rot" of the old regime. However, the party’s legal strategists are opting for a "build-up" approach. By pursuing a formal parliamentary investigation first, they aim to solidify public opinion and build an unassailable legal case before considering a full-blown impeachment motion.

This is a tactical delay. DPK lawmakers understand that in a democratic system, political legitimacy is tethered to public support. Rushing into a process that ends in a court rejection would be a gift to the main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP). By synchronizing their moves with public sentiment and upcoming judicial calendar milestones—such as the end of the Supreme Court Chief Justice’s term—the DPK hopes to ensure that their push for systemic reform results in a lasting political victory rather than a fleeting, reactive strike.

Ultimately, the Lee administration’s success hinges on whether they can prove that the current chaos is a necessary transition toward a more stable, fair, and prosperous society, rather than just another cycle of political retribution.
