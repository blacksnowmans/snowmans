Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon Emerges as Conservative Unity Figure as Opposition Overtakes Ruling Party

October 27, 2026

Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon has taken center stage in South Korean politics following a legal victory, signaling a major realignment within the conservative opposition as their approval ratings surge past the ruling Democratic Party.

  • Oh Se-hoon’s acquittal in a second-instance trial has cleared his legal hurdles, allowing him to spearhead talks of a conservative "big tent" merger with figures like Reform Party lawmaker Lee Jun-seok.
  • Opposition polling surge: The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main conservative party, has overtaken the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) outside the margin of error for the first time in four months.
  • Leadership consolidation: PPP leader Jang Dong-hyeok has survived internal calls for his resignation, aiming to purge "old-guard" politics while Mayor Oh reportedly builds alliances with former loyalists of the imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • Ruling party anxiety: The DPK, the party of President Lee Jae-myung, is struggling to find a presidential contender capable of matching Oh’s rising profile while facing backlash from labor groups over the abolition of the Mega-regional Special Zone Act.

The "Oh-Lee-Han" Alliance and Conservative Realignment

Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s recent moves indicate a pivot from municipal administration to national leadership. Following his acquittal, Oh held a high-profile dinner with Lee Jun-seok, the influential lawmaker who led the PPP before splitting to form the Reform Party. This meeting, characterized by an atmosphere of reconciliation, suggests the formation of a potential "Oh-Lee-Han" alliance—referring to Oh, Lee, and former PPP chairman Han Dong-hoon. By embracing Lee, who remains popular with younger male voters, Oh is positioning himself as the central axis for a unified conservative front against the Lee Jae-myung administration.

However, this strategy faces significant skepticism. Critics argue that Oh’s rumored outreach to the "Under-Chin-Yoon" group—loyalists to Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol who still hold sway within the party—contradicts his image as a moderate reformer. While securing the party machine is necessary for a presidential run, aligning with remnants of the disgraced former administration could alienate the moderate voters in Seoul who recently boosted the PPP’s poll numbers. The challenge for Oh is to transform this political maneuvering into a coherent vision for the country, rather than a mere factional power grab.

DPK Struggles as Opposition Polls Rise

The latest polling data reveals a significant shift in public sentiment, with the PPP gaining ground in the crucial Seoul metropolitan area. For the ruling DPK, this is a wake-up call. The party is currently grappling with the "Oh Se-hoon problem"—the lack of a heavy-hitting presidential alternative to counter a popular, three-term Seoul mayor. While President Lee Jae-myung remains the dominant figure in the ruling camp, the absence of a "Plan B" makes the party vulnerable to shifts in the political landscape, especially as moderate voters signal fatigue with the current government’s legislative pace.

The DPK’s difficulties are compounded by policy friction. The administration's decision to scrap the Mega-regional Special Zone Act—a law intended to boost regional development—has sparked fierce protests from labor unions and local governments. Labor groups have accused the Lee Jae-myung government of ignoring regional balance and labor rights. If the ruling party fails to mediate these conflicts and instead focuses solely on countering the opposition's personalities, the current dip in approval ratings could harden into a long-term trend, leaving the door wide open for a resurgent conservative opposition.
