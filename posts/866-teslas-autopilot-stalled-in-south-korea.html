Tesla's Autopilot Stalled in South Korea

October 09, 2026

South Korean lawmakers slammed Tesla Korea and government regulators during a parliamentary audit over the blocked deployment of Full Self-Driving (FSD) software and glaring safety blind spots that leave tens of thousands of local owners high and dry.

  • FSD Ghost Town: Only about 45,000 of the 230,000 Teslas on South Korean roads can actually run FSD, with the wildly popular Model Y seeing a dismal 9.6% activation rate.
  • Import Discrimination: U.S.-made Teslas get FSD access under free-trade safety standards, while Chinese-made vehicles—making up 99% of South Korean imports—are blocked entirely due to regulatory gaps.
  • Zero Government Oversight: Despite cumulative FSD driving distances surpassing 100 million kilometers, the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport has never once conducted a safety verification test.
  • Safety Hazards: Recent fatal fires highlighted critical design flaws, including electronic door handles that fail completely when vehicle power is cut, trapping occupants inside.

For years, nearly 180,000 South Korean Tesla owners have paid up to 9 million won (about $7,000) upfront for FSD, only to stare at an inactive "feature preparing" screen. During the October 8 parliamentary audit by the Land, Infrastructure and Transport Committee, Tesla Korea Managing Director Suh Young-deuk admitted that import origins dictate software availability. Because the vast majority of local Teslas are manufactured in China, buyers are locked out of features they paid top dollar for, pushing some desperate owners into illegal hardware hacks that risk criminal penalties.

The blame goes far beyond corporate neglect. South Korea's transport ministry has spent years hiding behind the excuse of lacking international standards, shifting responsibility while failing to utilize existing legal exceptions for safety testing. This regulatory inertia threatens not just foreign imports, but domestic automakers like Hyundai Motor, which plans to launch urban autonomous driving by 2028 but faces a complete vacuum in government safety standards. As global markets race toward autonomous robotaxis, South Korea's bureaucratic paralysis is turning its roads into a Wild West of unvetted software and unprotected consumers.
