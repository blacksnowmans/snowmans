The 800km Reality Check: What I Learned Packing for the Camino de Santiago

September 07, 2026

  • The core lesson: Every extra item in your backpack is a physical tax on your body; ruthless minimalism is the only way to survive an 800-kilometer trek.
  • What works: Dual-purpose items like cross-strap sports bras, everyday hip packs, and knee braces are non-negotiable for injury prevention.
  • What fails: Specialty gear like wide-brim hats and silicone water bottles quickly become dead weight due to bulk and hygiene issues.
  • The psychological shift: Stripping down your daily luggage forces you to confront and shed the mental baggage of consumerist life.

The Anatomy of a Minimalist Pack

The biggest trap for first-time pilgrims is the "just in case" mindset, which routinely blows a sensible 6kg (13 lbs) pack up to a punishing 10kg (22 lbs). Experienced travelers quickly learn that outdoor clothing should be strictly limited to one set for moving and one set for resting. The same logic applies to gear: bulky wide-brim hats are impossible to stow efficiently, and silicone water bottles are nearly impossible to sanitize properly in basic hostel environments. Instead, veteran trekkers rely on everyday items like a 750ml plastic bottle that can be easily replaced, and simple ziplock bags for food storage and organization.

For female travelers, physical realities require specific planning. Relying on local, unfamiliar feminine products in remote areas can derail your trip; bringing trusted tampons provides essential peace of mind. Similarly, managing posture is vital when carrying weight for weeks. Swapping standard straight-strap sports bras for X-band crisscross designs distributes shoulder and back pressure far more effectively, staving off the dreaded pilgrim "turtle neck" posture.

Protecting Your Body and Mind on the Road

An 800km trek is as much a psychological test as a physical one. Sleep quality dictates walking quality, making noise-canceling earbuds and white noise apps essential defenses against noisy hostel dormitories (albergues). When it comes to physical maintenance, knee braces should be treated as mandatory equipment rather than optional accessories. Daily rituals—like massaging sore feet with simple homemade balms and wearing knee supports consistently—keep chronic joint pain at bay.

Ultimately, stripping your life down to what fits in a single backpack offers a rare clarity. In a culture driven by relentless competition and accumulation, carrying only what is strictly necessary proves just how little we actually need to survive and move forward.
