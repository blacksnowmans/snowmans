Kang Hyung-wook Returns: A Trainer’s Journey Through Public Scrutiny

October 10, 2026

Renowned celebrity dog trainer Kang Hyung-wook has returned to the spotlight, reflecting on a grueling period of public defamation and private struggle that followed baseless workplace harassment allegations.

  • The Outcome: In February 2025, police officially closed the investigation into Kang’s company, Bodeum Company, clearing him of all charges regarding alleged employee privacy violations.
  • The Impact: Before his vindication, Kang faced a massive exodus of subscribers and was effectively blacklisted from the broadcast industry, leading to the closure of his business.
  • Personal Toll: Kang revealed that during the height of the media storm, he prioritized the safety of his family above all else, remaining silent while enduring intense public harassment.

From Public Hero to Pariah and Back

For years, Kang Hyung-wook was South Korea’s "national dog trainer," a household name synonymous with canine behavioral science. However, the speed at which his reputation collapsed following unsubstantiated claims of workplace misconduct exposed the darker side of South Korean internet culture. As he recently recounted on television alongside golf legend Park Se-ri and comedian Lee Young-ja, he spent months effectively trapped in his own home, unable to defend himself while his family was subjected to relentless, invasive scrutiny—including drones hovering over his property.

The ordeal transformed Kang’s perspective. Previously known for a rigid, sometimes abrasive approach to training, he admits that he has moved toward a more humble, empathetic philosophy. He now emphasizes that there is no single "correct" way to raise a dog, noting that his experiences with various families across the country—particularly those struggling with their own life hardships—taught him more about humanity than he ever learned from training animals alone.

The Cost of Public "Justice"

The resolution of his legal case—a "no suspicion" ruling from the Gyeonggi Bukbu Provincial Police Agency—has brought a measure of closure, but it does little to erase the trauma of the past year. Kang’s story highlights a recurring issue in South Korean media: the tendency for the public to act as judge, jury, and executioner long before facts are verified.

While Kang has successfully returned to broadcasting and is rebuilding his digital presence, his reflection serves as a sobering reminder of the human cost of "cancel culture." In a society that often demands moral perfection from its public figures, Kang’s experience underscores the irony of a public that views itself as righteous while engaging in what essentially amounts to mob harassment. His return is not just a professional comeback; it is a testament to the endurance of a man who, despite having his life dismantled by public opinion, chose to protect his family over the hollow victory of immediate public vindication.
