Debunking the Sillajen Conspiracy: Why a Failed Prosecution Probe is Resurfacing

October 27, 2026

A long-discredited conspiracy theory linking high-profile liberal writer Rhyu Si-min to the Sillajen stock fraud scandal has resurfaced in South Korea, prompting a forceful rebuttal from the ruling camp and legal experts.

  • The Sillajen Scandal: A 2013–2015 stock manipulation case involving a biotech firm that the previous administration’s prosecution attempted to link to liberal politicians.
  • Failed Prosecution: Investigations conducted during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—the former president currently imprisoned following his impeachment—found no financial ties between Rhyu and the company.
  • Political Rebuttal: Cho Kuk, leader of the Rebuilding Korea Party (a liberal minor party), recently presented data on the Maebul Show, a popular current affairs YouTube channel, to prove the allegations were a manufactured "failed operation."
  • Strategic Timing: The revival of these rumors on far-right forums is viewed as an attempt to stall the Lee Jae-myung administration’s ongoing reforms of the prosecution service.

The Anatomy of a Manufactured Scandal

The Sillajen case centers on a biotech company that saw its stock price soar and crash amidst allegations of insider trading. During the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol and his close associate, former prosecutor Han Dong-hoon, the prosecution's "Special Investigation" units aggressively targeted Rhyu Si-min, the former head of the Roh Moo-hyun Foundation (a prominent liberal think tank). Despite freezing bank accounts and conducting exhaustive audits, they found zero evidence of illicit funds or equity holdings. Rhyu’s only connection was a single congratulatory speech at a 2015 Pusan National University Hospital event, which conspiracy theorists have distorted into proof of "masterminding" the fraud.

The financial reality tells a different story. The scandal involved a $32 million (45 billion KRW) stake purchase by private equity funds in 2013 and the acquisition of 4.5 million shares in 2015. Documents presented by Cho Kuk show that these capital flows were entirely tied to professional investors and management, with Rhyu and the Roh Moo-hyun Foundation holding "zero dollars" in interest. The investigation, which had the full weight of the state's investigative powers at the time, ended with a "no charges" conclusion, marking a significant failure for the politically motivated prosecution of that era.

Combating the "Gaslighting" of Public Opinion

The current resurgence of this "zombie rumor" highlights a specific tactic of disinformation: gaslighting the victim. When liberal figures like Cho Kuk or media outlets use facts to debunk the claims, critics often pivot to a new frame, arguing that "bringing it up only makes it an issue" or "it's better to let it stay buried." This strategy seeks to force silence upon the victims; if they remain silent, the lie is treated as truth, and if they speak up, they are accused of "unnecessarily amplifying" a controversy.

However, the Lee Jae-myung administration and its supporters argue that such tactics must be met with "fact-bombs" rather than retreat. The source material emphasizes that silence allows disinformation to be archived as historical fact in the digital age. By dismantling the Sillajen conspiracy with clear timelines and financial records, the liberal camp aims to expose the "dirty remnants" of the previous prosecution's overreach. For the busy reader, the takeaway is clear: the Sillajen-Rhyu connection remains a fiction of the past, revived today primarily as a tool for political friction.
