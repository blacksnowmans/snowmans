Moving Beyond Samsung: A Three-Pillar ETF Strategy for Uncertain Markets

September 11, 2026

  • Market Fatigue: Investors overly reliant on tech giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are struggling as macro uncertainty and interest rate concerns dampen performance.
  • The "Three-Pillar" Strategy: To build resilience, portfolios should be balanced across three core assets: growth stocks, dividend-paying stocks, and commodities (like gold).
  • AI as Infrastructure: Despite volatility, AI remains the market’s primary engine, shifting from a speculative theme to essential infrastructure (GPUs and power grid equipment).
  • Global Diversification: Experts advise shifting from single-stock dependence toward a mix of US and Korean index-tracking ETFs to capture long-term compounding.

The "Three-Pillar" Defense Against Volatility

The days of simply "going all in" on large-cap semiconductor stocks are over. With 10-year US Treasury yields hovering above 4.5%, the risk premium for stocks has compressed, making defensive positioning essential. The proposed "Three-Pillar" strategy suggests allocating capital into a balanced structure: growth for appreciation, high-dividend stocks for defensive stability, and commodities (such as gold-spot ETFs) as a hedge against inflation. For an investor with roughly $70,000 (100 million won), a balanced approach would involve splitting the capital into thirds—placing one-third each into index-tracking ETFs, commodity-based inflation hedges, and dividend-focused ETFs. This strategy is not about chasing the highest daily yield, but about surviving the "psychological breakdown" that occurs during market corrections. By using established dividend ETFs—such as those tracking the Schwab US Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD) benchmark—investors can reduce portfolio volatility. The goal is to avoid the trap of reacting to daily news cycles and instead focus on the historical tendency of index funds to converge toward a 10% annual compounded growth rate over the long term.

AI Infrastructure: More Than Just a Hype Cycle

While Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have faced recent headwinds, the AI sector remains the absolute center of gravity for the IT market. Rather than fearing an "AI bubble," investors should view the current volatility as a discount period for high-quality assets. The focus is shifting from simple software to physical infrastructure: data centers, power equipment, and energy storage systems (ESS). For those looking to gain exposure without betting on single companies, the strategy favors ETFs that cover the entire semiconductor value chain. One notable option is the "ACE Google Value Chain Active," which focuses on companies designing essential components like Google’s proprietary TPU chips. For broader semiconductor exposure, the US-listed SMH (VanEck Semiconductor ETF) remains a standard choice due to its low management fees and broad diversification. Ultimately, the lesson here is that market timing is a fool's errand. Whether it is the impact of exchange rate fluctuations on Korean exports or temporary dips in tech stocks, these are secondary to the fundamental "price times quantity" (P×Q) performance of a company. A robust portfolio is one that isn't derailed by temporary external shocks, but instead relies on global diversification and the enduring power of long-term compounding.
