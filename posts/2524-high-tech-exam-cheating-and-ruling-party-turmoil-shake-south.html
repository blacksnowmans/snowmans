High-Tech Exam Cheating and Ruling Party Turmoil Shake South Korea

November 20, 2026

South Korea’s public trust is facing a dual crisis as sophisticated AI cheating devices breach national examinations and internal scandals push approval ratings for President Lee Jae-myung’s administration to new lows.

  • High-tech cheating exposed: Authorities caught 98 test-takers during the College Scholastic Ability Test (Suneung), South Korea's high-stakes university entrance exam, utilizing hidden micro-cameras, smart glasses, and generative AI tools.
  • Belated government crackdown: The Prime Minister ordered the full retention of exam papers to audit clean test sheets with abnormally high math scores, though authorities failed to deploy radio or metal detectors during the test.
  • Ruling party leadership crisis: A News Tomato poll revealed that 51.6% of respondents want Kim Min-seok, leader of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, to resign following leaked audio recordings and internal strife.
  • Plunging executive approval: President Lee Jae-myung's approval rating dropped 3.7 percentage points to 36.9%, driven by a hardline disapproval rating of 47.6%.

Systemic Failures in Examination Security

The discovery of micro-cameras disguised as button lenses and smart glasses in national tests—including the Suneung, the Certified Public Accountant exam, and the Legal Education Eligibility Test—has infuriated students who spend years preparing for these life-defining moments. Despite warnings, the Ministry of Education declined to use thermal or signal detectors in test centers, relying instead on visual proctoring. While the government now plans to cross-reference test papers against camera sales and buyer histories, post-hoc investigations offer no systemic relief to honest applicants pushed out by unfair competition.

Factional Feuds and Falling Approval Ratings

The ruling Democratic Party of Korea is simultaneously consumed by severe internal conflict. Leaked audio excerpts target party leader Kim Min-seok over alleged ties to the Shincheonji religious group and internal power struggles, prompting over half the public—and nearly 45% in the party’s traditional stronghold of the Honam region—to demand his resignation. In response, the party moved to impeach Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae, drawing criticism from internal voices who view the move as a transparent maneuver to deflect from internal party crises.

This political dysfunction directly drags down President Lee Jae-myung’s standing. Lee’s approval rating fell to 36.9%, with strong negative sentiment dwarfing strong support (47.6% strong disapproval versus 13% strong approval). While party insiders argue that Lee’s municipal track record as governor proves performance will eventually restore support, national governance operates under a single-term presidency where bureaucratic inertia, intense parliamentary opposition, and factional infighting compound rapidly.
