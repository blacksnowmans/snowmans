South Korea Unveils 100,000-Home Supply Plan for Greater Seoul, But Delays Loom

October 31, 2026

South Korea’s government has announced a blueprint to supply 100,000 new homes across the Seoul metropolitan area, aiming to stem rising housing anxiety through large-scale suburban developments and expedited urban redevelopment.

  • Suburban Land Releases: The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport has designated six suburban locations—including Guri, Hanam, and Gimpo—to host 76,700 new housing units, partly built on newly unreserved greenbelt land.
  • Fast-Tracking Seoul Redevelopments: The government is backing 31,000 private-sector housing starts in Seoul proper by year-end, offering tax incentives and relocation financing for key project hubs like Yeouido and Mokdong.
  • Legislative Push: Representative Han Byung-do and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea are moving to pass amendments to the Urban Renewal Act before the end of the year to streamline municipal approvals.
  • Severe Time Lag: Initial move-ins for the major suburban developments are not scheduled until 2033, offering little immediate relief for renters facing surging prices and extreme listing shortages.

Suburban Promises vs. Central Demand

Land Minister Hong Ji-sun has repeatedly stressed that housing supply must be felt through actual construction and completion rather than paper announcements. Under President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, the government is attempting to clear severe supply bottlenecks that accumulated during Yoon Suk-yeol's time in office. However, market observers caution that sprawling sites on the outer edges of Gyeonggi Province—such as the 30,000 units planned for Guri and Hanam—do not solve the acute demand for housing inside Seoul proper, where most commuters work.

Compounding the challenge is pushback from municipal governments outside Seoul. Local leaders are demanding guaranteed expansions of regional transit networks before construction proceeds, warning against repeating past suburban developments where residents suffered years of daily traffic gridlock.

A Timing Mismatch in a Severe Rental Crunch

For ordinary home-seekers, the daily reality in the rental market remains grim. In popular Seoul neighborhoods, tenants searching for jeonse—South Korea's unique lump-sum deposit lease system—report receiving double-digit queue numbers just to view a single available apartment. Because greenbelt-cleared sites will not deliver move-ins until 2033, an eight- to nine-year gap remains between policy execution and market relief.

While backing 31,000 urban housing starts this year is a step in the right direction, soaring construction costs and disputes between builder syndicates and property owners continue to delay projects. Without targeted short-term rental stabilization measures and robust transit integration, large headline supply figures alone will fall short of delivering affordable, accessible housing to working-class families.
