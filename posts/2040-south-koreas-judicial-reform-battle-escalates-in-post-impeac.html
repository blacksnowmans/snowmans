South Korea's Judicial Reform Battle Escalates in Post-Impeachment Push

November 06, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea is pushing to finalize major judicial reforms and special prosecutor measures by December, facing sharp pushback from the judiciary and the opposition following the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

  • What happened: South Korea's National Assembly passed a Special Prosecutor Bill targeting manipulated prosecutions, while Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon faces election law violation charges over misleading statements regarding a state audit.
  • What it changes: Pending judicial reform legislation aims to introduce criminal liability for judges who distort laws and eliminate arbitrary court case assignments, threatening entrenched legal privileges.
  • Who is affected: President Lee Jae-myung's administration, Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon of the main opposition People Power Party, and the judicial establishment.

Strict Timelines and Prosecutorial Accountability

Reform strategists argue that key judicial measures and impeachment-related investigations must wrap up by December. Delaying action risks allowing momentum to stall before parliamentary hearings dominate the political calendar next spring. A centerpiece of this legislative push is the newly passed Special Prosecutor Bill designed to investigate cases of manipulated indictments by public prosecutors. Proponents emphasize that stripping the prosecution of its arbitrary power to fabricate charges or manipulate evidence is essential to restoring public trust in Korean justice.

At the same time, Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon of the main opposition People Power Party faces growing legal jeopardy over alleged election law violations. Representative Park Joo-min of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea has highlighted that Mayor Oh publicly misrepresented an official notice from the Board of Audit and Inspection, South Korea's state auditor, regarding his controversial "Han River Bus" water transit project. Mayor Oh claimed the audit agency was withholding results due to ongoing parliamentary reviews, whereas the agency had simply dismissed the request on procedural grounds. With less than 30 days remaining before the statute of limitations expires, police face intense public pressure to execute a swift and impartial probe.

Judicial Resistance and Case Allocation Controversies

Inside the court system, judges under Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae are mounting visible pushback against a suite of reform bills, including measures to criminalize "law distortion" by judges and allow constitutional appeals against court rulings. While conservative judges frame their collective opposition as a defense of judicial independence, reform proponents view it as an effort to protect sanctuary-like judicial immunity from democratic oversight.

This clash is further compounded by allegations raised by Representative Park Joo-min that court case assignments involving Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol during his tenure were manually rigged by senior judges rather than processed via automated random distribution. Recalling the lesson from former party leader Lee Hae-chan that "failing to decide is the worst option," ruling party reformers contend that decisive legislative action is the only way to dismantle court cartels and lock in systemic transparency under President Lee Jae-myung's government.
