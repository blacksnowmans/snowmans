South Korea’s Ruling Party Moves to Impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice

November 24, 2026

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country's ruling party, has officially announced plans to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, signaling an aggressive push to overhaul the judiciary as part of what it calls a national "cleansing" of forces tied to the previous administration.

  • The Move: DPK floor leader Han Byung-do confirmed on November 23 that the party aims to finalize the impeachment motion within the week.
  • The Justification: The DPK accuses Chief Justice Cho of shielding elements involved in alleged "insurrection" under the former Yoon Suk-yeol administration and failing to uphold constitutional values.
  • The Stakes: The impeachment is framed by the DPK as a necessary step to secure the judicial independence required for President Lee Jae-myung’s reform agenda.
  • The Opposition: The main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), has slammed the move as a "bulletproof" political ploy intended to distract from the current administration’s struggles.

The DPK’s strategy reflects a belief that the judiciary remains a stronghold of "old order" loyalists left over from the era of Yoon Suk-yeol (who was removed from office and is currently imprisoned). By labeling the Chief Justice’s tenure as an obstacle to democracy, the ruling party is attempting to shift the judiciary’s institutional culture. Senior DPK figure Park Jie-won, known for his blunt political style, has been a vocal proponent of this approach, declaring that the time for polite negotiation has passed and that the impeachment is a historical necessity.

However, this path is fraught with internal and external friction. While the party leadership pushes forward, some members—including lawmaker Kwak Sang-eon—have expressed concern over the political optics, fearing that the move could be perceived as retaliation for unfavorable court rulings. Meanwhile, the PPP continues to characterize the push as an assault on the separation of powers, pointing to the DPK’s shifting justifications for impeachment as evidence of a chaotic and politically motivated agenda. As the DPK works to finalize the legal language of the motion, the country faces a deepening confrontation between the legislative and judicial branches.
