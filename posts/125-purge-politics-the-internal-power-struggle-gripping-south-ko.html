Purge Politics: The Internal Power Struggle Gripping South Korea’s Main Opposition

September 03, 2026

A disturbing trend of “purge politics” is taking hold within the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), South Korea’s primary liberal opposition party, as internal dissent is increasingly met with calls for expulsion rather than policy debate.

  • What happened: Party member Lee Se-mi is currently facing a petition for disciplinary action and potential expulsion after questioning irregularities in the party's delegate selection process.
  • The "Purge" tactic: Critics argue the disciplinary petition is a weaponized tool used to silence uncomfortable questions and eliminate perceived political threats.
  • The wider context: The incident is unfolding against the backdrop of an intense power struggle within the DPK ahead of its upcoming national convention, involving party heavyweights like Chung Cheong-rae and Kim Min-seok.
  • The impact: By prioritizing internal loyalty tests over public-facing policy, the party risks appearing disconnected from the needs of ordinary voters while fostering a culture of fear.

The High Cost of Loyalty Politics

The case of Lee Se-mi is a textbook example of how modern Korean political parties suppress internal criticism. By labeling Lee’s legitimate inquiries as "party privatization" or acts of disloyalty, party officials are effectively gaslighting those who seek transparency. In this environment, asking a question is no longer a democratic right; it is a fireable offense. The absurdity of labeling young party members who managed a neglected digital communication channel as "privatizers" only highlights how desperate the leadership is to control the narrative.

This is not about holding anyone accountable for actual wrongdoing—it is about maintaining a rigid hierarchy. As the DPK prepares for its August convention, the party has become a theater for a proxy war. The message being sent to the rank-and-file is clear: fall in line behind the party leadership, or you will be made an example of. When a party defines "difference" as "treason," it loses its ability to innovate or expand its base.

A Path to Irrelevance

The energy currently wasted on these petty internal vendettas is staggering. If the party apparatus spent even 10% of the effort they put into online smear campaigns and disciplinary petitions on substantive policy development, they would be in a far stronger position. Instead, they are trapped in a feedback loop of internal combat.

Ultimately, this approach is self-destructive. A party that creates a sanctuary where no one dares to challenge the powerful is a party in decay. While the leadership may succeed in silencing Lee Se-mi, they are simultaneously alienating the very voters who expect a democratic, inclusive organization. In their quest for absolute control, they are forgetting their true purpose: to address the real-world problems facing the Korean public, not to wage war against their own colleagues.
