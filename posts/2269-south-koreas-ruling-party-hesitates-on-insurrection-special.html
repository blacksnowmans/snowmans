South Korea’s Ruling Party Hesitates on Insurrection Special Counsel Amid Legal Delays and Factional Feuds

November 12, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party of Korea is facing mounting public frustration over its hesitation to pass a massive 309-member special counsel bill aimed at rectifying past prosecutorial misconduct and expediting stalled insurrection trials.

  • What happened: Unprecedented judicial delays, including a 168-day hold by the Supreme Court on a judge recusal request, are allowing key defendants in Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's insurrection trial to walk free as detention terms expire.
  • What it changes: The proposed special counsel bill would launch a 309-person investigation into past fabricated indictments, supported by 75.9% of Democratic Party voters.
  • Who is affected: Key figures such as former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo and former Interior Minister Lee Sang-min face imminent release, while Democratic Party leadership faces internal strife over legislative inaction and media leaks regarding candidate nominations.

Judicial Delays Threaten Accountability in Insurrection Trials

Proceedings at the Seocho-dong court complex in Seoul have taken a concerning turn as defendants in the insurrection trials take an aggressive, politicized stance. A critical bottleneck stems from the Supreme Court, which has dragged its feet for 168 days without ruling on a recusal application filed against Senior Judge Lee Jin-gwan by former Deputy Prime Minister Choi Sang-mok. Compared to the national average recusal processing time of just 1.1 months, this deliberate paralysis leaves Judge Lee isolated while undermining tough early rulings against major accomplices.

As a result, key figures involved in the emergency decree crisis are stepping out of detention. Former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo is scheduled for release following the expiration of his detention period, with former Interior Minister Lee Sang-min following shortly after. Legal experts warn that narrowing the legal definitions of insurrection risks diluting the charges against primary instigators, including Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, and effectively grants immunity to high-ranking accomplices.

Ruling Party Inaction and Internal Factional Disputes

Despite holding a powerful legislative majority, the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) remains paralyzed by fear of backlash from conservative voters supporting the main opposition People Power Party (PPP). Although overall public polls show mixed support (37.3% in favor versus 45.3% opposed), an overwhelming 75.9% of DPK supporters favor the 309-person special counsel bill. Leadership's reluctance to push the bill through has drawn sharp criticism from core voters who view the hesitation as political cowardice.

Compounding the legislative gridlock is internal turmoil sparked by unverified media reports. After radio broadcasts claimed reformist factions demanded guaranteed candidate nominations for upcoming elections, Senior Supreme Councilor Choi Min-hee publicly refuted the allegations. Choi clarified that DPK nominations are governed by democratic system rules belonging to party members, not factional bosses, and announced legal action with the Press Arbitration Commission against unvetted reporting.
