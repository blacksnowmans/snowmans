Europe Travel Alert: Entry Delays, Strikes, and Disruptions

September 10, 2026

Traveling to Europe this autumn has become significantly more complex, with new biometric border controls, widespread transit strikes in Italy, and unpredictable natural phenomena threatening to derail even the most carefully planned itineraries.

  • New Biometric Hurdles: The Entry/Exit System (EES) now mandates fingerprint and facial scans for non-EU travelers, leading to massive queues at major hubs like Paris and Frankfurt.
  • Italian Transit Strikes: September is marked by a wave of labor actions affecting trains, flights, and ferries, with a near-total paralysis of transport anticipated on September 26.
  • Seasonal & Natural Disruptions: Switzerland is entering its off-season maintenance period for mountain railways, while continued volcanic activity at Sicily's Mount Etna remains a threat to flight operations.

The "EES" Bottleneck and New Border Realities

The implementation of the EES—a new digital border management system designed to track non-EU citizens—is creating significant administrative bottlenecks. What once took seconds now requires biometric registration, causing wait times at some European airports to stretch up to five hours during peak periods. Despite urgent pleas from organizations like the International Air Transport Association (IATA) to suspend the rollout, the system remains a rigid reality.

For travelers, this means the "golden rule" of European transit has changed. If you are flying into a major hub like Paris or Frankfurt before connecting to another city, you must clear immigration at your first point of entry. Travelers should avoid "tight" connections of less than two-and-a-half hours and steer clear of scheduling evening dinner reservations or tours on the day of arrival, as border delays are now the norm rather than the exception.

Navigating Strikes, Maintenance, and Volcanoes

Italy’s transportation network is facing a volatile September. With strikes frequently announced via government portals, travelers are often left scrambling. While "guaranteed service" periods exist during morning rush hours, these are not universal. Travelers should avoid scheduling city-to-city travel on strike days entirely, or, if unavoidable, attempt to rebook tickets for the earliest possible morning departure to bypass anticipated midday shutdowns.

Meanwhile, those heading to the Alps or Sicily face different obstacles. Switzerland’s mountain cable cars and railways undergo intensive maintenance between late October and mid-November; checking official schedules for route closures is essential to avoid finding yourself at the base of a mountain you cannot ascend. Similarly, the ongoing eruptions at Mount Etna mean that those flying out of Sicily should never link a local flight to an international connection on the same day. Build in at least a 24-hour buffer on the Italian mainland; in the era of modern European travel, flexibility is no longer a luxury—it is a requirement for survival.
