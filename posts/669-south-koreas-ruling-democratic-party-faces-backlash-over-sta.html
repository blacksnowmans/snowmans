South Korea's Ruling Democratic Party Faces Backlash Over Stalled Reforms and Slumping Wages

October 04, 2026

South Korea's progressive administration under President Lee Jae-myung is hurtling toward a crushing defeat in upcoming elections as core supporters abandon a platform they say has watered down key reforms and failed to protect working-class incomes.

  • Fading Mandate: Critics within the progressive camp warn that the current leadership has abandoned structural reform, alienated its base, and allowed conservative opponents to reassert themselves.
  • Economic Pinch: Official data shows real wages have stagnated or declined over successive years, with wealth increasingly concentrated in powerhouse sectors like semiconductors while small businesses and general workers fall behind.
  • Institutional Friction: Tensions are boiling over within the state prosecution service following controversial investigations, exposing deep divisions over the administration's legal and anti-corruption agenda.
  • Voter Fallout: Both President Lee and Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok are seeing synchronized drops in public approval as the government struggles to offer concrete solutions to everyday financial hardships.

The core frustration stems from a widening gap between pre-election promises and actual governance. Figures within the progressive movement argue that what began as a mandate to root out institutional corruption and reform the prosecution service has been scaled back out of fear of centrist backlash. Rather than implementing comprehensive social safety nets or tackling systemic inequality, critics say the administration has relied on superficial, short-term spending announcements reminiscent of past conservative policies.

This policy drift has coincided with worsening economic realities for ordinary citizens. While large conglomerates centered around chip manufacturing continue to thrive, everyday workers face rising inflation alongside shrinking or flat real wages—reversing gains made over the past decade. Compounding these domestic grievances is a perceived weakness in handling historical revisionism and legal challenges from political opponents, leaving the administration's base feeling betrayed and politically homeless.
