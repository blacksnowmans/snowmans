South Korea Slashes Tax Grace Periods in Fresh Crackdown on Multiple Homeowners

October 09, 2026

South Korea's newly revised income tax enforcement decrees have drastically shortened capital gains tax grace periods and imposed strict deadlines on rental property exemptions, sending shockwaves through the country's real estate market and threatening property owners with sharply higher tax bills.

  • Shorter Grace Periods: The tax-exempt grace period for temporary two-home owners in regulated zones is cut from three years to two years.
  • Rental Housing Deadlines: Long-standing capital gains tax exemptions for registered rental apartments now have a hard expiration date, running out the clock on investors who relied on past government incentives.
  • Exploding Tax Burdens: Property tax and comprehensive real estate holding tax exemptions are set to expire, potentially turning minor annual tax bills into massive liabilities.
  • Immediate Impact: Multi-home owners face narrowing exit routes, forcing many to dump properties onto the market quickly to avoid severe penalties.

The latest enforcement decrees represent a dramatic about-face that leaves property owners scrambling. What was once billed as a stable framework for real estate investment has curdled into a high-stakes countdown. By shortening the temporary two-home tax-exempt window from three years to two—and strictly tying it to specific acquisition and sale dates in late 2026—the administration is effectively forcing multi-home owners to liquidate or face punishing taxation. Tax experts warn that many property owners are misinterpreting these strict deadlines, assuming they have more time than the law actually allows.

Compounding the panic is the abrupt revocation of long-term stability for registered rental housing providers. Landlords who spent years abiding by strict rental caps under past government encouragement are now being told their exemptions have a strict expiration date. Once these periods lapse, properties lose their comprehensive real estate tax exclusions, turning routine tax payments into sudden six-figure dollar liabilities (such as leaping from roughly $7,000 to over $70,000). Combined with pre-existing hurdles like tenant occupancy rules and localized transaction permits, property owners find themselves caught in a regulatory trap with little room to maneuver.
