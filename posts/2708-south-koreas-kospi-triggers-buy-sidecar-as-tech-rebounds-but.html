South Korea's KOSPI Triggers Buy Sidecar as Tech Rebounds, But Policy Woes Remain

November 24, 2026

South Korea’s main stock index, the KOSPI, triggered a buy sidecar after regaining the 5,000-point mark following Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia, though analysts warn that government stabilization measures remain severely lacking.

  • What happened: A buy sidecar temporarily halted program buying on the KOSPI after stock futures jumped over 5%, fueled by a massive global semiconductor rally.
  • Government policy criticized: President Lee Jae-myung’s administration announced a 10 trillion won (about $7.2 billion) market stabilization fund and relaxed treasury share buyback rules, but market observers dismissed the move as "empty rhetoric" that pales in comparison to Taiwan's aggressive stimulus.
  • Global tech impact: Nvidia’s resurgence wiped out roughly 15 trillion won ($11 billion) in short positions, lifting key Asian suppliers like SK Hynix and TSMC.
  • What comes next: Despite near-term interest rate pressure from the U.S. Federal Reserve, long-term momentum in the robotics sector—led by Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics—offers dynamic opportunities looking ahead to 2027.

Risk Management in a Volatile KOSPI Rebound

Market analysts warn retail investors against repeating past emotional trading habits—such as panic selling during market dips and chasing tops near peak index levels. Citing Howard Marks’ famous metaphor about crossing a river, market experts stress that measuring the depth of downside risk is far more critical than chasing immediate gains right now. Historically, major downturns like the 1997 Asian Financial Crisis and the 2008 Global Financial Crisis required an average of 18 months to recover, typically followed by rallies ranging from 70% to 126%.

Government Stumbles While Tech Drives the Future

The policy response from President Lee Jae-myung’s government has drawn sharp criticism for lacking real scale and punch. A 10 trillion won market stabilization fund might have had an impact when the KOSPI hovered near 2,000 points, but critics argue an index near 5,000 requires a package of 30 trillion to 50 trillion won. By contrast, Taiwanese authorities actively considered doubling their state stabilization fund to 1 trillion TWD (about $31 billion). South Korean policymakers must deliver bold, unexpected interventions rather than incremental regulatory tweaks to restore international investor trust.

Meanwhile, the broader tech ecosystem is regaining solid ground as Nvidia's stabilization proves the AI supply chain remains effectively "too big to fail." While macro headwinds from the Federal Reserve will linger into next year, sector-specific catalysts—particularly humanoid robotics initiatives from Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics alongside major upcoming global summits—are creating key opportunities for patient capital ahead of a broader market normalization expected by 2027.
