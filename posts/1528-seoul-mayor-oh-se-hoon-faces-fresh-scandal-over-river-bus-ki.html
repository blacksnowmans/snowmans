Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon Faces Fresh Scandal Over River Bus Kickbacks and Questionable Court Rulings

October 27, 2026

    Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon is facing mounting political and legal pressure over alleged kickbacks, a heavily criticized river-bus transit project, and controversial court rulings that cleared him of previous campaign finance violations.
  • An internal Seoul city audit allegedly uncovered a suspicious 320 million won (about $230,000) kickback scheme tied to the "Han River Bus" project, which officials reportedly sat on for over a year.
  • Critics, including opposition lawmakers, accuse the Seoul Metropolitan Government of bypassing central government budget blocks by illegally funneling 1.072 trillion won (about $770 million) through the state-backed Seoul Housing & Communities Corporation (SH).
  • Mayor Oh is also facing potential election law violations for allegedly spreading false information regarding why a state audit into the river bus project was rejected.
  • The controversy comes on the heels of a controversial court ruling that cleared Oh of earlier campaign finance and illegal polling allegations, drawing fierce criticism from transparency advocates.

South Korea's local politics are once again being rocked by allegations of administrative overreach, financial engineering, and selective justice. For international observers watching Seoul's governance, the current crisis surrounding Mayor Oh Se-hoon highlights the blurry lines between ambitious urban revitalization and opaque bureaucratic maneuvering. What began as a romantic vision for commuter ferries on the Han River has quickly devolved into a multi-million-dollar political liability.

At the heart of the scandal is the Han River Bus initiative—a flagship project heavily championed by Oh to boost tourism and public transit. However, leaked audit documents and whistleblower claims suggest the project's financial foundations are built on inflated data, fictitious advertising revenue projections, and questionable internal bailouts. When central ministries flagged the project as financially unviable, the city government allegedly turned to the Seoul Housing & Communities Corporation—an agency meant to secure affordable housing for ordinary citizens—to covertly finance the shortfalls.

The Anatomy of a Questionable Project and Legal Fallout

Beyond the financial engineering of the river bus project, critics argue that the political insulation surrounding Mayor Oh mirrors a broader decay in institutional accountability. Observers have drawn sharp comparisons between Oh's recent legal acquittals—handled by judges who previously presided over cases involving imprisoned former leader Yoon Suk Yeol—and the kid-glove treatment often afforded to high-ranking conservative politicians. When courts adopt strained logic to excuse suspicious financial trails between political patrons and pollsters, public trust in the judiciary takes a direct hit.

As the legal clock ticks down toward the end of the year, the primary battleground will likely shift to whether election law violations stick to Mayor Oh over his public statements regarding the stalled audits. For the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, which has staked its political legitimacy on restoring institutional fairness, cracking down on these kinds of elite-tier administrative dodges will be a critical test of whether the rule of law applies equally in Seoul City Hall.
