South Korean President Lee Jae-myung and Ruling Party Leaders Hold Three-Hour Dinner Focused on Economic Relief

November 12, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung hosted an extended three-hour dinner with top leadership from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea on November 11, emphasizing party-government unity and prioritizing economic relief over politically sensitive tax debates.

  • What happened: President Lee Jae-myung met at length with Democratic Party Leader Kim Min-seok and Floor Leader Han Byung-do to align on national governance priorities.
  • Key proposal: Party Leader Kim formally urged the administration to adopt urgent policy measures to reduce economic inequality and revive sluggish domestic consumption.
  • Notable omission: Controversial topics—including easing the Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax (Jongbuse) and appointing special prosecutors—were left off the agenda entirely.
  • Who is affected: Working-class households and small business owners targeted for economic relief, alongside lawmakers preparing for upcoming legislative pushes in the National Assembly, South Korea's parliament.

A United Front on Economic Inequality

President Lee’s repeated call during the meeting for the executive branch and the ruling party to move as "one mind and one heart" underscores a drive to eliminate policy friction. With Floor Leader Han Byung-do in attendance alongside Party Leader Kim, the three-hour session signaled that the party’s focus on tackling wealth disparity and boosting domestic spending will receive swift legislative and budgetary backing.

Sidestepping Controversies to Preserve Policy Momentum

The explicit exclusion of property tax discussions carries significant political weight. Prior to the dinner, observers speculated that leadership might address potential tweaks to the Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax, a wealth tax levied on high-value property owners. Ruling party officials confirmed after the meeting that real estate tax revisions were not mentioned even once. By avoiding divisive tax debates and politically charged special prosecutor issues, the Lee administration and Democratic Party leadership aimed to signal complete internal alignment while keeping public attention squarely on immediate economic recovery.
