First Lady Kim Hye-kyung Faces Fierce Backlash Over Luxury Military Golf Outing During National Mourning

October 26, 2026

South Korea’s First Lady Kim Hye-kyung is facing intense public fury after an opposition lawmaker revealed she held an exclusive golf outing at a restricted military course alongside a professional golfer, just days after a fatal fire claimed 19 lives.

  • The Revelation: Representative Joo Jin-woo of the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main conservative opposition, revealed that Kim played at the military-run Taeneung Country Club on October 20, receiving private coaching from a renowned pro golfer while utilizing a presidential VIP clubhouse.
  • The Sensitive Timing: The outing took place immediately following a fire in southern Seoul's Yeoksam district that left 19 people dead, and the very day after a funeral service for a fallen Bucheon firefighter.
  • Legal and Security Concerns: Critics allege the trip violated military facility regulations regarding the private use of defense property, while also exposing severe lapses in the presidential security apparatus after photos and videos of the First Lady were leaked.
  • The Official Explanation: The presidential office justified the event by claiming it was part of an official "Golf Task Force" headed by Protocol Secretary Kwon Hyuk-ki to prepare for an upcoming South Korea–U.S. summit, an excuse swiftly derided by the opposition as an amateurish cover-up.

A Floundering Defense and Public Disillusionment

The presidential office's attempt to brand a recreational outing as diplomatic preparation has badly backfired. The claim that the administration formed a dedicated task force involving presidential security and national security officials to practice golf for a foreign summit has drawn harsh ridicule across the political spectrum. Rather than mitigating the fallout, the administration's tone-deaf framing has reinforced public impressions of incompetence and an unwillingness to take accountability.

For an administration under President Lee Jae-myung that promised fairness and transparency, the scandal carries serious political weight. The private use of military assets echoes the entitlement voters hoped to leave behind following the political turmoil and eventual impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. The fact that the First Lady's private leisure was so easily documented by outside observers also indicates a breakdown within the presidential security apparatus itself.

Mounting Headwinds for the Ruling Democratic Party

The fallout arrives at a precarious moment for the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK). Internal unity is already fraying as party leader Kim Min-seok faces dismal performance ratings—recently logging 67.6 percent disapproval in a survey conducted by Jowon C&I for Energy Economy News. Grassroots frustration among liberal supporters is visibly mounting, threatening to trigger quiet voter disengagement ahead of upcoming electoral tests.

By failing to offer a clear apology, the Lee Jae-myung administration risks turning a single bad headline into a structural crisis. When high-ranking officials treat public and military resources as private entitlements during a period of public mourning, technical excuses do little to stem the tide. Unless the presidential office shifts toward genuine contrition and administrative discipline, this controversy will deepen the political isolation of both Cheong Wa Dae and the ruling party.
