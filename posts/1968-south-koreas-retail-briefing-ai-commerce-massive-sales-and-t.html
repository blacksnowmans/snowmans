South Korea’s Retail Briefing: AI Commerce, Massive Sales, and the Live-Streaming Battle

November 05, 2026

South Korea's retail sector is undergoing a massive transformation, with live-commerce platforms aggressively integrating AI tools, entertainment, and deep-discount shopping events to capture budget-conscious consumers.

  • Mega Sales Collide: Major retail events like SSG.com and W Concept's "SSG Day" are driving heavy winter shopping demand alongside travel promotions like Modetour's "Live M" 4th-anniversary broadcasts.
  • AI Enters the Mainstream: Startups like Termit and AI tools developed by Buzzni are automating product page creation and short-form video production, lowering barriers for small merchants.
  • Diverging Platform Fortunes: While e-commerce giant Coupang reported an operating loss of about $4.6 billion (642.3 billion won) for the third quarter, traditional retail heavyweight GS Retail posted record profits driven by quick-commerce growth.
  • Content Meets Commerce: Live-streaming is increasingly blending with entertainment, featuring celebrity golf variety shows and web sitcoms to convert passive viewers into buyers.

The boundary between digital content and online shopping in South Korea has all but vanished. Brands are no longer relying on static product pages; instead, they are deploying celebrity-driven variety shows, golf web series, and real-time interactive broadcasts to keep consumer attention. Even local governments, such as Ganghwa County, are stepping in to train small business owners on how to set up lighting and host their own live-streaming sales to survive the shifting digital landscape.

At the same time, the market is grappling with the pressures of hyper-competition and regulatory loopholes. A recent fraudulent sports broadcast scandal that duped 205,000 viewers into buying unrelated goods under the guise of a live table tennis match highlights the dark side of the booming live-commerce economy. As platforms rush to scale up using AI automation and aggressive overseas expansion—such as K-beauty brands pushing into Australia and China—maintaining consumer trust through authentic pricing and rigorous quality control will ultimately determine who survives the cut.
