South Korea's Progressive Bloc Shaken by Scandals and Plunging Youth Support

September 29, 2026

  • A new Realmeter poll shows South Korea's main opposition Democratic Party trailing the conservative People Power Party by a double-digit margin among voters in their 20s and 30s.
  • Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae faces mounting pressure following revelations of inflated expense reports and luxury dining paid for with public funds.
  • The progressive camp is experiencing intense internal friction after a civic group filed a police complaint against political commentator Rhyu Si-min for criticizing the administration.

South Korea's progressive movement is currently navigating a severe political crisis, caught between collapsing youth support and damaging self-inflicted wounds. Recent polling numbers reveal that the liberal base is bleeding support precisely where it thought it was secure, while leadership missteps threaten to alienate key intellectual allies.

The immediate catalyst for outrage is the judiciary. An audit and investigative reports revealed that Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae's office systematically manipulated expense claims—inflating attendee counts for standard meetings and masking high-end omakase meals to bypass legal spending limits. For an institution tasked with upholding the rule of law, the fallout has been devastating. Critics argue that the ruling and opposition blocks alike are moving too slowly, missing the political golden window to pursue accountability through impeachment.

Compounding these institutional headaches are devastating numbers from a recent Realmeter survey. Among voters in their 20s, the Democratic Party drew just 20.9% support compared to 54.5% for the conservative People Power Party—a catastrophic double-digit defeat. Observers note that decades of empty outreach to youth have finally backfired, as younger Koreans reject a political class perceived as out of touch with economic reality and job market fairness. To make matters worse, progressive leadership blundered into a fresh controversy by mishandling public commentary from veteran liberal commentator Rhyu Si-min, leading to his police complaint by a civic group and sparking fierce internal infighting over freedom of expression.
