AI's Reality Check: Massive M&A, Job Market Shifts, and the Push for Regulation

October 13, 2026

  • Capital Consolidation: Amazon is reportedly pursuing a $7 billion (about 9 trillion won) acquisition of virtual environment AI startup Decart, as tech giants race to dominate "physical AI."
  • Workplace Transformation: Job listings requiring AI tool proficiency surged 170% year-over-year in South Korea, shifting hiring standards from basic familiarity to hands-on prompt and agent use.
  • Regulatory Balancing Act: The administration of South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is offering tax relief and up to a three-year tax audit exemption for over 2,500 AI startups while tightening guardrails on AI errors and accidents.
  • Investor Skepticism: Ongoing debates over OpenAI’s revenue have rattled semiconductor markets, signaling the end of "blank check" AI funding as investors demand proof of actual returns.

The artificial intelligence sector has entered a sober second act where breathtaking technological advances collide with the harsh laws of capital and regulation. The era of loose speculation and uncritical hype is giving way to a much tougher environment, where both tech startups and global conglomerates must prove they can turn raw intelligence into bottom-line utility without triggering regulatory blowback or security nightmares.

In South Korea, this global shift is reshaping everything from local hiring markets to corporate internal networks. As security barriers fall—evidenced by local cybersecurity firms integrating generative AI into official research networks—companies are aggressively adopting tools like ChatGPT and Claude. Yet this rapid integration also brings risks, underscored by global incidents of AI models hallucinating or misbehaving. Policymakers are scrambling to draft mandatory reporting rules for AI accidents even as they try to shelter cash-strapped small businesses from tax burdens.

Ultimately, the core question in the AI race is no longer who can build the biggest model, but who can deploy it safely and equitably. As tech giants spend billions buying up physical AI capabilities and venture capital grows increasingly ruthless about return on investment, the true test will be whether these tools genuinely improve human productivity or simply widen the digital divide. For businesses and workers alike, learning to manage the machine—rather than being displaced by the panic—is the only survival strategy left.
