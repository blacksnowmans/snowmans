Ruling Party Leader Kim Min-seok Faces Resignation Calls as Approval Ratings Crash

November 10, 2026

Kim Min-seok, the leader of South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), is facing an existential crisis as public support for the party plummeted 11.4 percentage points within just 100 days of his tenure.

  • Support Collapse: DPK approval ratings fell from 47.9% to 36.5% according to Realmeter, a local pollster, completely reversing Kim’s pledge to increase support by 10% within his first 100 days.
  • Leadership Vacuum: Kim has recently skipped key public events and party meetings, leading to rumors of a rift with the Blue House (South Korea's presidential office) and President Lee Jae-myung.
  • High Public Dislike: A Research View poll shows that 50.6% of voters "would never vote" for the DPK, a figure significantly higher than the 34% recorded for the People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition.
  • Policy Paralysis: Internal confusion over the Comprehensive Real Estate Tax (a high-value property tax) and the failure to move forward with the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae have left the party base frustrated.

Internal Strife and the "Shincheonji" Blunder

The DPK’s current paralysis stems from what critics call a "ruling party identity crisis." Despite holding the presidency under Lee Jae-myung, the party leadership has failed to balance the administration’s agenda with the public’s desire for reform. Instead of spearheading policy, the leadership has been distracted by internal power struggles. A primary example is the controversial "Shincheonji" religious cult allegations, which Kim reportedly used as a political weapon against internal rivals like Jung Chung-rae. This move backfired, dragging the entire party into a religious controversy that has alienated mainstream voters.

Discipline within the party has also broken down. During a recent general meeting of lawmakers, Chun Joon-ho, the DPK’s floor coordination chief, had to publicly rebuke members for making uncoordinated remarks on tax policy. This lack of a unified message has reportedly irritated the Blue House, leading to rumors that Kim was "summoned" by President Lee Jae-myung to explain the party’s poor performance. Critics are now comparing Kim's passive style to "Lee Nak-yon Season 2," a reference to the former party leader who was often criticized for being overly cautious and failing to deliver on reform promises.

The 100-Day Deadline and Calls for Reshuffle

The upcoming deadline of November 24—marking Kim’s 100th day in office—is being framed by party members as a final ultimatum. With the general election approaching, the DPK risks entering the campaign as a "lame duck" ruling party. While the public's desire to "judge" the government remains high at 57.7%, the DPK is failing to capture that sentiment, with many voters viewing the party as a mere "rubber stamp" for the presidency rather than a proactive legislative body.

To break the deadlock, reformist voices within the party are calling for a radical shift. Proposed solutions include a "National Reform Tour" led by outside figures like former lawmaker Choe Kang-wook to reconnect with the grassroots. However, many political insiders believe that nothing short of Kim Min-seok’s resignation will suffice to shock the party back into relevance. If the leadership does not voluntarily step down to take responsibility for the polling freefall, the DPK may face an uncontrollable internal revolt that could jeopardize President Lee Jae-myung’s legislative agenda for the remainder of his term.
