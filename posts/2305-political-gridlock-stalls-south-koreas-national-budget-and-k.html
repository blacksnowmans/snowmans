Political Gridlock Stalls South Korea’s National Budget and Key Tech Legislation

November 14, 2026

On November 13, South Korea’s National Assembly descended into a stalemate over the 2025 budget, as partisan fighting over minor media subsidies and regional funds threatened to derail major investments in R&D and semiconductors.

  • Regional Funding Crisis: Local government leaders converged on the capital to lobby for survival, as the People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, and the ruling Democratic Party (DP) clashed over regional development priorities.
  • Tech Paralyzed by Media Feud: A dispute over just $5.2 million (7.3 billion KRW) for TBS, a public broadcaster, paralyzed committees overseeing billions in science, technology, and defense spending.
  • Legislative Graveyard: Data reveals a dysfunctional parliament where 70% of all bills proposed in the current 22nd National Assembly remain stuck in committee without a single review.
  • Industrial Uncertainty: Major projects, including a nuclear power export deal to the Czech Republic and semiconductor infrastructure in the Honam region, are facing delays due to demands for parliamentary inquiries and budget vetoes.

The current state of the National Assembly is a study in disproportionate priorities. While the ruling Lee Jae-myung administration seeks to restore R&D funding—previously slashed during the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—to $930 million (1.3 trillion KRW), the process has been hijacked by a relatively tiny $5.2 million dispute over TBS. This reflects a broader trend where "future-readiness" funds are held hostage to settle political scores. For foreign investors and tech partners, the message is clear: South Korea’s strategic industrial policy is currently secondary to domestic political theater.

Furthermore, the legislative backlog is reaching critical levels. Essential "livelihood bills," ranging from expanding legal aid for victims to strengthening health insurance oversight through a special judicial police force, are being deferred indefinitely. The Health Insurance Special Judicial Police Act, intended to crack down on illegal "pharmacy-militias" and fraudulent hospitals, was delayed again due to fierce lobbying from medical groups. This paralysis suggests that unless a bill has significant political leverage, it is unlikely to clear the Legislation and Judiciary Committee, the Assembly's final gatekeeper.

Regional Desperation and the Sejong Question

As the budget deadline nears, mayors from cities like Daejeon, Jeonju, and Chuncheon have essentially moved their offices to the National Assembly. They are fighting for crumbs of the national budget to keep local projects alive. Meanwhile, the PPP is using the Administrative City Special Act—a law meant to further establish Sejong City as the nation’s administrative capital—as a political cudgel. While the opposition claims to support the move, they have shifted the blame for legislative delays onto the ruling DP, turning a matter of national decentralization into a partisan blame game.

Industrial Policy Under Fire

The industrial sector is facing its own set of hurdles. The Trade, Industry, Energy, SME, and Startups Committee saw its budget session collapse over a "Future Response Fund" and semiconductor investments for the Honam region. The PPP opposition characterized these funds as a "trick" by the Lee Jae-myung government to bypass parliamentary oversight, leading to a total walkout.

Simultaneously, the landmark Czech nuclear power project—once hailed as a major export victory—is now facing a potential parliamentary inquiry over allegations of sudden contractor changes and profit-sharing concerns. When combined with the semiconductor industry's desperate plea for exceptions to the 52-hour work week to remain globally competitive, the picture emerges of a business environment increasingly choked by legislative inertia. If the National Assembly cannot separate essential industrial survival from partisan warfare, South Korea risks losing its competitive edge in the global tech race.
