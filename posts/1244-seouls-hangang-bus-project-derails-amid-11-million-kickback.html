Seoul’s Hangang Bus Project Derails Amid $1.1 Million Kickback Scandal

October 16, 2026

  • Seoul's flagship water-bus project has been plunged into a major corruption scandal involving a suspicious $1.1 million (about 1.5 billion won) payout tied to a ship-building contract.
  • The contract was awarded to Gadok Heavy Industries, a newly established shell company with zero track record and no employees at the time, which outsourced work to roughly 40 subcontractors.
  • Pushed by Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon to meet an aggressive October launch deadline, the project suffered severe delays and was pushed back to March next year due to a lack of actual production capability.
  • The controversy has sparked fierce parliamentary scrutiny, document shredding allegations, and accusations of political patronage involving the Seoul Housing and Communities Corporation (SH Corporation).

A $1.1 Million Kickback and a Ghost Company

Seoul's ambitious plan to turn the Han River into a bustling public transit corridor has hit a massive roadblock. At the center of the controversy is a $13 million (17.16 billion won) contract awarded for the construction of six passenger vessels. The recipient, Gadok Heavy Industries—a firm based in Tongyeong—was founded in late 2023, yet managed to secure the massive public project just months later despite having no prior shipbuilding experience.

According to internal findings by the Seoul Metropolitan Government's Audit Committee, around 15억 원 (about $1.1 million) in mysterious payouts was funneled through brokers, allegedly serving as kickbacks for an official at the SH Corporation, a state-funded urban development agency. Compounding the issue, employment insurance records show that the company did not have a single employee on its payroll when the contract was signed. When investigators visited the registered address, they found little more than a small room with a desk and a computer.

Opposition lawmakers during the recent parliamentary audit blasted the city administration for pushing ahead with the deal. Critics point out that Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s rigid public promise to launch the "Hangang Bus" service by October created a rushed, reckless procurement process. Rather than working with established shipbuilders who warned that the timeline was physically impossible, the city pivoted to an unverified entity that ultimately failed to deliver a single boat on schedule, forcing a postponement to March of next year.

Cover-Up Allegations and Political Fallout

The scandal quickly escalated from a procurement failure into an alleged cover-up. Opposition lawmakers presented evidence suggesting that an internal audit document recommending a formal police investigation into the bribery allegations was intentionally suppressed and kept from public view. Although Mayor Oh acknowledged ordering a thorough investigation, he claimed he could not recall the specifics when pressed by lawmakers during the audit.

Adding to the controversy, a public audit request submitted by the Seoul metropolitan government to the Board of Audit and Inspection was summarily dismissed after it was revealed that the application entirely omitted any mention of the kickback allegations. Lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea argued that the omission was a deliberate attempt to secure a quiet dismissal and shield city officials from accountability.

As the project's launch date slips to next year and public trust hits rock bottom, critics argue that the fiasco highlights the dangers of prioritizing political optics over administrative common sense. With the opposition pressuring for aggressive independent investigations, Seoul's grand river-transit vision risks becoming a cautionary tale of rushed execution and political patronage.
