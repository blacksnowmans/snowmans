Opposition Takes to Streets in Busan as Rival Parties Clash Over Special Counsel Bill

November 15, 2026

South Korea’s main conservative opposition party has launched a nationwide street campaign in Busan to derail the ruling party’s special counsel bill, bringing parliamentary business to a complete standstill.

  • What happened: The opposition People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main conservative party, held a large outdoor rally at Busan Station on November 14, launching a nationwide tour to block the ruling Democratic Party of Korea’s (DPK) controversial special counsel legislation.
  • What it changes: Legislative proceedings have frozen during the critical annual budget review period, as the PPP shifts to out-of-parliament public rallies and threatens legal retaliation against cabinet members.
  • Who is affected: The public and financial markets, as bipartisan warfare stalls scrutiny of next year's national budget and spills into debates over stock market stability.

Escalating Street Protests Over Judicial Integrity

Lacking the parliamentary seats to block the ruling DPK’s legislative agenda, the conservative PPP gathered in the rain in Busan—a traditional conservative stronghold—for its first major public demonstration. PPP leader Jang Dong-hyeok and other party figures branded the DPK’s proposed "Manipulated Prosecution Special Counsel Bill" an unconstitutional measure designed to shield President Lee Jae-myung from his ongoing judicial trials. Opposition leaders urged President Lee to let court proceedings move forward normally and warned cabinet ministers that approving the bill could expose them to future criminal liability. Former PPP leader Han Dong-hoon also publicly pressured the cabinet, while the party announced plans to hold its next major rally in Daegu on November 21.

Budget Stalled as Conflict Spills into Market Disputes

The ruling DPK strongly condemned the rallies, accusing the opposition of abandoning its constitutional duty to review the national budget during the regular legislative session. DPK leaders characterized the protest as a political show designed to mock President Lee rather than address public livelihoods. The ruling party maintains that the special counsel is essential to investigate alleged prosecutorial abuse and targeted investigations conducted during Yoon Suk-yeol's time in office, signaling it will leverage its parliamentary majority to pass the bill regardless of opposition boycotts.

The political standoff has rapidly expanded into broader economic and social grievances. The PPP seized on President Lee's recent remark regarding stock market volatility—where he noted that "when the mountain is high, the valley is deep"—to attack the administration's economic competence amid growing investor anxiety. To counter the DPK's legislative drive, the opposition also demanded special counsel probes into suspected political ties involving religious groups such as Shincheonji and the Unification Church. With both sides locked in a zero-sum confrontation, critical fiscal spending reviews remain stranded in parliament.
