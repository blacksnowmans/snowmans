The Timeless Appeal of Polo Ralph Lauren: Why Gen Z is Obsessed

October 10, 2026

Polo Ralph Lauren, the 60-year-old American heritage brand, has completed a remarkable comeback, transforming from a dusty "dad brand" into a symbol of "quiet luxury" favored by global pop icons and Gen Z alike.

  • What happened: After struggling with over-saturation in discount channels, the company aggressively restructured under CEO Patrice Louvet, closing over 1,000 department store and outlet locations to protect brand equity.
  • What it changes: The brand successfully moved away from chasing fleeting trends, instead focusing on its core identity as a "lifestyle" provider.
  • Who is affected: Consumers are choosing high-quality, timeless pieces over fast fashion, while competitors are scrambling to replicate the "Old Money" aesthetic that Polo defines.

From Humble Beginnings to a Global Empire

The brand's success is rooted in the "mysterious confidence" of its founder, Ralph Lifshitz. Born into a poor Jewish family in New York, Ralph had an innate sense of style that transcended his financial reality. After changing his name to Ralph Lauren and dropping out of college, he leveraged his experience in neckwear sales to challenge the status quo. When major retailers like Bloomingdale’s demanded he change his brand name and adjust his designs to match trends, he famously walked away. His refusal to compromise on his vision—even when facing bankruptcy—ultimately forced the market to adapt to him.

This commitment to a specific, aspirational vision saved the company during its darkest hours. When the business hit a wall, Lauren didn't cheapen his designs; he pivoted to high-margin licensing and fragrance deals. His perfume lines alone became cash cows, generating massive revenue (the first few years brought in $7.5 million, $28 million, and eventually $70 million) that allowed the brand to survive while keeping its creative integrity intact. He also famously spent a fortune on marketing, including spending nearly $10,000 (about 10 million won) on a single catalog shoot, treating fashion not as a commodity, but as a cinematic world for customers to inhabit.

Lessons in Unwavering Identity

Ralph Lauren’s resilience offers a stark lesson for today’s business landscape. While many companies pivot their strategies based on the latest algorithm or short-term data, Lauren built a brand on a "fixed point" of principle. By purchasing a ranch in Colorado and immersing himself in the "Western" lifestyle, he turned his aesthetic into a lived reality, making his brand synonymous with an authentic, aspirational way of life that doesn't age.

In the current climate under the Lee Jae-myung administration, where there is an increasing national emphasis on restoring "fairness, common sense, and essence" across all sectors, the Polo story resonates deeply. It serves as a reminder that the most durable businesses—and perhaps the most successful lives—are those that remain anchored to their core values. In a world that demands we be everything to everyone, there is immense power in having the "mysterious confidence" to simply be yourself, regardless of the trends.
