Anthropic’s Nasdaq IPO Triggers Global Cash Crunch and Ripples Through South Korean Stocks

November 17, 2026

Anthropic's upcoming Nasdaq debut on November 19 has drawn oversubscribed orders up to seven times its available offering, sending shockwaves through global markets and causing sharp volatility across South Korea's technology sector.

  • What happened: AI developer Anthropic is moving toward its listing at a reported valuation near $1.4 trillion, attracting massive global demand while strictly excluding institutional capital from China and Hong Kong, as well as South Korean retail investors.
  • What it changes: Global institutional investors are liquidating holdings in major tech stocks and cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin to hoard cash for allocation orders, creating a short-term liquidity drain across Asian markets.
  • Who is affected: SK Telecom, South Korea's leading telecommunications operator, saw its share price bounce thanks to its early equity stake in Anthropic, while semiconductor giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix face foreign selling pressure as global funds reallocate cash.

Exclusion of Korean Retail Traders and the Geopolitical Wall

While subscription access was extended to select Asian hubs like Japan, South Korean retail investors—popularly known as "Seohak Ants" for their aggressive buying of overseas tech shares—were entirely left out of the pre-IPO allocation. Domestic Korean brokerages declined to join the underwriting syndicate, reportedly wary after administrative and execution difficulties during earlier high-profile foreign listings. Excluded from buying at the initial offering price, Korean retail investors are now turning to secondary-market purchases, thematic ETFs, and risky leveraged products to gain exposure.

At the same time, Anthropic enforced strict barriers against Chinese and Hong Kong institutional funds. Driven by U.S. defense regulations and heightened security scrutiny around critical artificial intelligence infrastructure, the company prioritized compliance over capital inflow. This political line-drawing effectively seals off one of the world's largest pools of private capital from participating in the blockbuster listing.

South Korea's Market Split: Telecom Surge vs. Memory Sell-off

The market impact in Seoul has created a clear divide between strategic equity holders and broader tech bellwethers. Shares of SK Telecom rebounded as markets reassessed the value of its early corporate investment in Anthropic. For SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won, the listing serves as a high-stakes test to demonstrate whether early corporate AI bets can deliver tangible long-term value beyond simple paper gains.

Conversely, memory chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix came under sudden pressure as foreign institutions unloaded shares to raise liquidity. This cash-hoarding dynamic mirrors previous market dislocations where foreign capital exited Korean large-cap stocks ahead of major U.S. stock debuts. However, analysts note a potential silver lining: if Anthropic's massive capital raise is immediately deployed into AI compute infrastructure, long-term server memory demand for Samsung and SK Hynix could eventually offset the temporary trading shock.
