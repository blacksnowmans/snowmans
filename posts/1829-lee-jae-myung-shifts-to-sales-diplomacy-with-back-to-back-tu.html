Lee Jae-myung Shifts to "Sales Diplomacy" with Back-to-Back Turkey and East Timor Summits

November 02, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung is set to host the leaders of Turkey and East Timor this week, signaling a strategic pivot toward pragmatic economic diplomacy centered on nuclear energy and defense exports.

  • Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan will make a state visit from November 2 to 4, marking the first such visit by a Turkish leader in eight years.
  • East Timorese President José Ramos-Horta will arrive for an official visit from November 5 to 7, the first-ever official visit by an East Timorese head of state since the two countries established diplomatic ties.
  • The summits focus on "sales diplomacy," specifically targeting multi-billion dollar contracts in nuclear power and defense, as well as strengthening ties with the ASEAN (Association of Southeast Asian Nations) region.
  • The administration aims to demonstrate a restoration of South Korea’s diplomatic system, which it describes as having been "distorted" during the term of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president currently serving a prison sentence following his impeachment.

Restoring the Gateway to Europe and the Middle East

The summit with President Erdoğan on November 3 is the centerpiece of Lee’s push to revitalize South Korea’s standing in the Middle East and Europe. Turkey, a key NATO member situated at the crossroads of two continents, is currently pursuing massive infrastructure projects, including new nuclear power plants and the modernization of its military. For the Lee administration, this is a critical test of its ability to convert diplomatic goodwill into "K-Defense" exports and nuclear construction contracts that could be worth billions of dollars.

The administration's focus marks a departure from the previous era. While the "brotherly" bond between South Korea and Turkey is a long-standing narrative, critics within the current government argue that the term of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol left these strategic channels neglected or mismanaged. By securing concrete agreements rather than mere ceremonial handshakes, President Lee seeks to prove that his Democratic Party of Korea (South Korea's current ruling party) can deliver tangible economic benefits through a "results-first" foreign policy.

Expanding the ASEAN Footprint

The subsequent meeting with President Ramos-Horta on November 6 represents a move to diversify South Korea’s diplomatic reach beyond the traditional focus on the United States, China, Japan, and Russia. East Timor is a strategic emerging partner in Southeast Asia with significant maritime resources and a strong desire for cooperation in climate change mitigation and infrastructure development. As the country prepares for full membership in ASEAN, Lee’s engagement is a calculated move to secure a foothold in a region where South Korea has historically been less active.

However, the success of this "sales diplomacy" will be measured by its follow-through. Historically, Korean summits have often resulted in a flurry of Memorandums of Understanding (MOUs) that fail to materialize into actual trade. To avoid the mistakes of the past, the Lee administration must move quickly to lower trade barriers and establish working-level networks between private sector companies and their counterparts in Ankara and Dili. For a public weary of "diplomatic shows" during the previous administration, only signed contracts and enhanced national security will serve as proof of success.

"Diplomatic value is not found in the glamour of the stage, but in its impact on the livelihoods of the people and the survival of our corporations."
