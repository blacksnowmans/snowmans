Why Reading is Mental Weight Training: A South Korean Perspective on Modern Dogma

September 03, 2026

  • The core claim: Reading is not about collecting information, but acting as a self-renewal process that breaks down rigid thinking patterns.
  • What triggers the shift: Societies that neglect reading grow stagnant, turning rigid boundaries into defensive hostility against differing views.
  • The method: Rather than chasing speed or summaries, readers must practice slow reading, copying text, and deep immersion to rebuild their mental operating systems.
  • The takeaway: Expanding one's inner library diminishes intolerance, fostering empathy and better conflict resolution.

South Korean reading experts and cultural commentators have increasingly pointed out a bitter truth about modern public discourse: as people turn away from books, the boundaries of their thoughts grow as hard as barbed wire. When personal bubbles become entirely rigid, any outside opinion is treated as an attack. This closed-loop thinking fosters hostility rather than dialogue, reducing complex human narratives to background noise.

The solution is not speed-reading or treating books like simple knowledge catalogs. True reading requires upgrading one's mental operating system through slow engagement, transcribing passages, and wrestling with unfamiliar perspectives. By deeply understanding an author's world before offering a critique, readers exercise the critical muscle needed to recognize their own biases. Ultimately, the goal is not to memorize plots, but to leave a text willing to dismantle yesterday's assumptions and make room for others.
