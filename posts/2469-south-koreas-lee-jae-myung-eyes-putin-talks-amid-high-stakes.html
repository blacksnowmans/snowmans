South Korea’s Lee Jae-myung Eyes Putin Talks Amid High-Stakes APEC Diplomacy

November 18, 2026

According to a Korean political commentary citing Kremlin statements and on-site reporting from the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China, South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is nearing a face-to-face meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin following a brief encounter with U.S. President Donald Trump.

  • Kremlin signals a meeting: Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov stated that Putin will meet Lee, opening the door for formal or informal talks to address strained Seoul-Moscow relations.
  • A 50-second icebreaker with Trump: Before the APEC dinner, Lee—of South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea—and First Lady Kim Hye-kyung exchanged a 50-second greeting with Trump, which South Korean journalist Park Hyun-kwang described as a symbolic opening step toward closer talks.
  • A security-and-trade agenda: If a Seoul-Moscow meeting takes place, Lee plans to address sanctions and Russia’s military support for North Korea while exploring cooperation on Arctic shipping routes opening due to climate change.
  • Speculation over North Korea talks: Trump hinted at a possible meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un amid Russian media reports of potential Putin-Kim talks, while Kim recently visited Mount Paektu—a sacred site in North Korean politics traditionally visited before major state decisions.

What Is at Stake for Seoul

The potential Lee-Putin talks point to an attempt at pragmatic, interest-driven diplomacy after years of deep freeze between Seoul and Moscow. Relations deteriorated following the war in Ukraine and Russia’s growing alignment with North Korea. By keeping diplomatic channels open, Lee’s administration appears to be testing whether South Korea’s shipbuilding expertise and economic weight can give Moscow an incentive to curb military assistance to Pyongyang in exchange for future cooperation along the Northern Sea Route.

The choreography in Shenzhen also underscores Seoul’s determination not to be sidelined if Washington, Moscow, and Pyongyang cut deals over the Korean Peninsula. In the cited commentary, supporters argue that Lee’s outreach contrasts sharply with what they characterize as the unprepared diplomacy under Yoon Suk-yeol. Still, turning a 50-second greeting with Trump or a pull-aside conversation with Putin into enforceable security commitments will be far harder than setting up initial encounters.

Reading the Signals from Pyongyang

The biggest wildcard hanging over Shenzhen is Kim Jong-un. Although distance makes an in-person appearance by Kim at APEC unlikely, the convergence of Trump’s remarks, Russian media speculation, and Kim’s Mount Paektu trip suggests quiet backchannel maneuvering may be underway. Whether Kim’s next decision points toward renewed diplomacy or further provocation remains unclear, leaving Lee’s balancing act between deterrence and dialogue facing an immediate test.
