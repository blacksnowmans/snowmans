South Korea's Progressive Grassroots Demand Rapid Overhaul of Ruling Democratic Party

October 31, 2026

Prominent progressive figures and grassroots activists converged on a broadcast hosted by political YouTube channel Park Si-young TV, demanding that South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) radically overhaul its internal structure and pass aggressive reforms under President Lee Jae-myung.

  • High-profile progressive lineup: The live political retreat brought together hardline DPK lawmaker Jung Chung-rae, Chonnam National University philosophy professor Park Gu-yong, and former party supreme council member Park Gyu-hwan, drawing heavy viewership and legacy press coverage.
  • Push for party member sovereignty: Under the rallying slogan "Reform the Democratic Party! Protect the Democratic Party!", speakers called for transforming the DPK into a party driven directly by its more than one million dues-paying members rather than parliamentary elites.
  • Zero patience for bipartisan compromise: Activists and commentators firmly rejected moderation or drawn-out negotiations with the People Power Party, South Korea's main conservative opposition, demanding swift action on long-stalled reforms.
  • Expanded platform for public pressure: A second panel featuring investigative journalists, lawmakers, and police reform figures was unveiled to maintain sustained public scrutiny over key legal and media overhaul measures.

Moving the Center of Gravity from Yeouido to the Base

The event underscores how digital public squares have eclipsed traditional backrooms in Yeouido, Seoul's political district, as the primary engine driving progressive strategy. Rather than treating supporters as a passive audience, grassroots political broadcasts have evolved into organizing hubs where rank-and-file citizens set legislative priorities. The massive live turnout illustrates that the party's core base expects direct influence over candidate nominations and national policy, viewing active fandom as a vital democratic counterweight to elite institutional inertia.

The Urgency to Avoid Past Stumbles

For the ruling DPK, this pressure cooker from the base leaves little margin for hesitation. Following the impeachment and imprisonment of Yoon Suk-yeol and the subsequent transition to the Lee Jae-myung administration, progressive voters are determined not to repeat the cautious compromises that undermined previous center-left administrations. The message from the retreat was unambiguous: sweeping overhauls of the prosecution and media systems must be enacted swiftly to safeguard the administration's mandate.

As the Lee administration pursues its governing agenda, the DPK faces a clear choice. If it retreats into establishment complacency, it risks alienating the passionate grassroots base that propelled it into power. Ensuring that party leadership delivers tangible legislative outcomes is now viewed by party loyalists not as an option, but as the only safeguard against future political rollback.
