Accountability and Double Standards: South Korea’s National Audit Concludes

October 27, 2026

As the annual legislative audit of the South Korean government draws to a close, a fierce battle over the 2025 budget has begun against a backdrop of explosive allegations regarding investigative double standards and "political stalking."

  • Budget Season Begins: Following the conclusion of the audits, the National Assembly is pivoting to review the 2025 budget, facing a strict legal deadline of December 2.
  • "131 vs. 0": Representative Jeong Chun-saeng of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) criticized the prosecution for conducting 131 search and seizure operations against First Lady Kim Hye-kyung over a $72 (100,000 KRW) charge, while conducting zero for the daughter of opposition leader Han Dong-hoon.
  • Academic Fraud Allegations: Lawmakers raised questions regarding Han Dong-hoon’s daughter, who allegedly authored nine English-language papers in a single year and used corporate donations from 3M Korea to secure prestigious awards.
  • Golf Controversy Debunked: Claims by the conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) that First Lady Kim Hye-kyung enjoyed "emperor-style" golf lessons during a fire were refuted by timestamp evidence and a formal denial from professional golfer Lydia Ko.

Selective Justice and Corporate Ties

The final days of the audit focused heavily on the perceived imbalance of the South Korean investigative apparatus. The DPK highlighted a "chilling silence" from the Seoul Central District Prosecutors' Office regarding Mr. Han Dong-hoon’s family. Mr. Han, the former Justice Minister and current leader of the People Power Party (PPP), has seen allegations against his daughter—ranging from ghostwritten academic papers to suspicious volunteer records—stagnate in the hands of investigators. The most damning evidence presented was the "3M connection," where a corporate executive, an alumnus of the same law school as Han’s wife, facilitated a donation of 50 laptops that Han’s daughter then donated under her own name to claim academic awards.

This "selective silence" stands in stark contrast to the aggressive pursuit of members of the current Lee Jae-myung administration. The prosecution's decision to drop investigations into Han’s family, despite evidence of "ghost" volunteer hours reported by local media, reinforces the public's perception of a two-tiered justice system. While the Central Investigation Agency (the agency responsible for investigating high-ranking officials) claims it is "reviewing" the cases, the lack of a single search warrant suggests a lack of political will to touch the "Prince of the Opposition."

Defensive Tactics and the Budget Battle

The opposition PPP attempted to pivot by attacking First Lady Kim Hye-kyung with allegations of a "royal golf outing" at Taereung Country Club, a military-run facility often used by high-ranking officials. However, the attack faltered when it was revealed that the timing of the golf outing preceded a nearby fire by nearly an hour, and world-renowned golfer Lydia Ko—who was allegedly giving the lesson—issued a statement through her representatives threatening legal action for defamation, noting she was at a tournament elsewhere at the time.

Veteran lawmaker Park Jie-won characterized these opposition tactics as "stalking" rather than legitimate oversight. He noted that the PPP’s focus on recording the First Lady’s movements at a public-use facility like Taereung CC demonstrates a desperation to manufacture scandals to offset the ongoing legal troubles of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who remains imprisoned following his impeachment. As the focus shifts to the budget, the DPK aims to protect the President's policy agenda from opposition cuts, while the PPP is expected to use the budget process as leverage to continue its narrative of government overreach.

"When the scales of justice tilt based on the weight of one's political power, the law ceases to be a shield for the public and becomes a weapon for the elite."
