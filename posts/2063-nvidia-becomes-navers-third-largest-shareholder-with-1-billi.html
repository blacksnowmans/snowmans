Nvidia Becomes Naver’s Third-Largest Shareholder with $1 Billion Stake to Build Korean ‘AI Factories’

November 07, 2026

Silicon Valley semiconductor giant Nvidia has finalized a 1.34 trillion won ($1 billion) equity investment in Naver, acquiring a 4.13% stake to become the South Korean internet leader's third-largest shareholder and launch a joint enterprise "AI Factory" initiative.

  • What happened: Nvidia completed its capital payment of 1.3412 trillion won ($1 billion) for a new share issuance by Naver, South Korea's dominant search engine and cloud operator, establishing a direct strategic partnership.
  • What it changes: The deal pairs Nvidia’s advanced GPU hardware with Naver’s proprietary HyperCLOVA X large language model to build customized "AI Factories"—data center environments engineered to produce tailor-made AI services for business and government clients.
  • Who is affected: Domestic enterprises, public institutions, and tech startups gain access to high-performance computing and localized AI solutions at lower costs, strengthening South Korea's technological self-reliance.

Hardware Meets Sovereign AI

Nvidia’s multibillion-dollar equity entry into a foreign platform provider represents a rare level of commitment from Chief Executive Jensen Huang. The investment centers on "Sovereign AI"—the push for countries and companies to build indigenous artificial intelligence grounded in their own languages, cultural context, and sovereign data boundaries. By partnering with Naver, one of the few domestic platforms globally to hold its ground against Google, Nvidia gains a proven gateway into specialized Asian language and enterprise markets.

The deal also underscores a broader recovery in South Korea’s technology sector. After a difficult period under Yoon Suk-yeol, whose administration drew sharp criticism from researchers for sweeping cuts to national R&D budgets, the tech industry is regaining momentum under President Lee Jae-myung’s focus on digital sovereignty. Nvidia's capital infusion signals renewed international confidence in South Korea’s capacity to lead high-tech development rather than remain a passive consumer of Western AI models.

Balancing Strategic Scale with Vendor Risk

For Naver, the joint "AI Factory" model provides immediate technical and financial muscle. Integrating Nvidia’s accelerator chips directly into Naver’s cloud data centers will allow local companies and financial institutions—many of which resist sending sensitive internal data to public foreign clouds—to run highly secure, bespoke AI engines on-premises or in private clouds.

However, the alliance brings long-term strategic questions. While acquiring a 4.13% stake makes Nvidia a major ally alongside institutional investors like South Korea's National Pension Service, relying heavily on Nvidia’s hardware infrastructure risks deepening vendor lock-in. To maximize the benefit of this $1 billion endorsement, Naver must leverage Nvidia’s global distribution power while keeping tight control over its underlying software architectures and localized AI intellectual property.
