Rhyu Si-min’s Warning: Why President Lee Jae-myung Is Fracturing His Own Coalition

October 30, 2026

Influential progressive commentator and author Rhyu Si-min has ignited a fierce political storm by claiming that President Lee Jae-myung is systematically dismantling the traditional progressive coalition that underpins the ruling Democratic Party of Korea.

  • A deliberate internal purge: Commentators argue President Lee is marginalizing traditional liberal factions loyal to former presidents Roh Moo-hyun and Moon Jae-in, driven by deep-seated rivalry, hardline activist allies, and acute anxiety over post-presidency legal risks.
  • Grassroots resistance within the ruling party: Despite coordinated online campaigns and pressure from the presidential faction, mainstream figures like lawmaker Jung Chung-rae defied party-machine intimidation by securing 46% of the vote at the party convention.
  • Controversial policy and rhetoric shifts: Lee has sparked public backlash by questioning foreign migrant labor benefits and reviewing multicultural family perks, prompting even longtime inner-circle confidants like Representative Jeong Seong-ho to warn the president to curb unscripted social media pronouncements.
  • Desperation for party realignment: Stymied in their attempts to gain total control over the Democratic Party, Lee's circle has floated overtures toward external factions, including a potential merger with the minor Rebuilding Korea Party, to dilute institutional opposition.

The Roots of the Coalition Fracture

At the center of this brewing conflict is a clash of political origins. President Lee’s rise was powered by progressive voters disillusioned with the cautious reforms of past liberal governments, yet he never gained the full trust of the mainstream establishment. Instead, his ascent leaned heavily on hardline activist networks—most notably the Gyeonggi Dongbu Alliance, a controversial organization historically linked to the disbanded Unified Progressive Party. For veteran observers like Rhyu, Lee’s current moves are not routine party modernization, but a concerted effort to hollow out the Democratic Party’s traditional identity and remake it into a personalized shield for his political survival.

A High-Stakes Gamble for Party Control

President Lee's camp has increasingly borrowed polarizing tactics—deploying aggressive online fandoms to brand internal dissenters as traitors and testing populist waters with controversial statements on immigration and welfare. This approach mimics an unstable recipe: delivering a quick sugar rush of political mobilization while steadily corroding the party's democratic health. The strategy seeks to replace the party’s traditional base with new, transactional demographics, but it treats internal allies with more suspicion than external opposition.

This factional gambit faces significant hurdles. As demonstrated by Jung Chung-rae's resilient 46% convention showing, the Democratic Party’s institutional foundation is proving far harder to conquer than earlier progressive platforms. By alienating the very loyalists who built the party, Lee's push for total hegemony risks tearing the ruling bloc apart from the inside, leaving his administration politically vulnerable if public sentiment begins to sour.
