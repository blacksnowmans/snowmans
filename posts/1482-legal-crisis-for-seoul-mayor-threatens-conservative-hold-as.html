Legal Crisis for Seoul Mayor Threatens Conservative Hold as By-Election Looms

October 26, 2026

Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon faces an impending court ruling that legal observers warn is unlikely to end in an acquittal, potentially triggering a high-stakes Seoul mayoral by-election.

  • What happened: Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon of the main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) awaits a critical court verdict stemming from election law risks during his previous campaign activities.
  • What it changes: If Mayor Oh receives a fine exceeding 1 million won (about $750), his election will be invalidated, forcing a mayoral by-election that could grant significant momentum to President Lee Jae-myung’s administration.
  • Who is affected: The conservative opposition, which remains fractured following the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, as well as Democratic Party figures like lawmaker Park Joo-min who are preparing for a potential mayoral bid.
  • Societal backdrop: The political friction coincides with widespread public interest in healthcare and quality-of-life issues, notably South Korea's response to its "10 million hair loss population" through advanced transplants and emerging biotech solutions.

Political Restructuring in the Capital

Legal analysts view an acquittal for Mayor Oh as highly improbable given the case structure and lower court rulings. Should his sentence exceed the 1 million won (~$750) fine threshold required to void his election, Seoul will hold a by-election as early as next April. Capturing the mayoralty would solidify President Lee Jae-myung's reform agenda at the heart of the country's governance. Democratic lawmaker Park Joo-min has already signaled his interest in running, positioning himself as a candidate focused on civic welfare over high-profile municipal showcase projects.

Meanwhile, the opposition PPP is navigating intense internal friction. Following the downfall and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, certain conservative factions are privately calculating that Mayor Oh’s exit could clear the way for a broader leadership reshuffle ahead of future parliamentary elections. However, banking on political realignments rather than public trust poses a steep risk for the opposition in a critical Seoul electorate.

Social Pressures and Technological Responses

Parallel to high-level political shifts, South Korean society is increasingly focused on health and personal maintenance, highlighted by a medical sector catering to over 10 million people dealing with hair loss. Medical experts emphasize that genetic factors account for 80% to 90% of cases, shifting public interest away from unproven remedies toward evidence-based pharmaceutical treatments like minoxidil, alongside critical safety warnings regarding its severe toxicity to household cats.

Surgical interventions have also matured, with standard 3,000-graft transplants yielding survival rates around 90%. Demand is rising among active older demographics, prompting new combination therapies. Looking forward, medical researchers expect lab-cultured follicle cloning to reach commercial viability within five years, shifting treatment from redistributing limited hair to infinite cellular generation.
