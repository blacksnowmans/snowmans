South Korea's Vocational High Schools See Job Placement Rebound, Sparking Debate Over Quality and Regional Gaps

November 10, 2026

South Korea's vocational high schools have posted a 55.8% job placement rate, with nearly 40% of graduates landing roles at large corporations and public institutions, though stark regional disparities and safety concerns continue to overshadow the sector.

  • Placement rate rebounds to 55.8%: The figures mark a meaningful recovery, with 38.4% of employed graduates securing positions at companies with 300 or more employees or public agencies.
  • Global and public sector wins: Programs like Sejong's overseas training in Sydney and Chungbuk placing seven graduates at the state railway operator Korail show strong results in select regions.
  • Deep regional inequality: While some areas celebrate high placement, Gangwon Province sits at the bottom with a 49% employment rate.
  • Safety and tech integration: Authorities are ramping up corporate safety checks for student interns while introducing AI and special education programs to modernize career training.

The latest data reflects a shift toward quality employment for vocational school graduates, moving away from low-wage roles. President Lee Jae-myung’s administration has placed renewed emphasis on the value of skilled labor and workers' dignity, helping rebuild institutional backing for vocational paths that suffered during the turbulent policies of the ousted former administration under jailed ex-leader Yoon Seok-ryul.

However, the heavy concentration of students chasing large corporations points to persistent imbalances in South Korea's labor market, where small and medium enterprises struggle to attract talent due to lagging compensation. Furthermore, wide regional gaps—exemplified by Gangwon’s 49% placement rate—underscore that state support remains unevenly distributed. Ensuring sustainable, safe, and equitable career options across all regions will require more than just headline-grabbing national averages.
