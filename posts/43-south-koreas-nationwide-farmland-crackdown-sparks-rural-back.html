South Korea’s Nationwide Farmland Crackdown Sparks Rural Backlash

September 24, 2026

A government-led, nationwide audit of farmland ownership—intended to curb real estate speculation—has triggered widespread panic and hostility across South Korea’s rural communities, threatening to become a major political liability for President Lee Jae-myung.

  • What happened: Following President Lee’s directive to enforce the constitutional "tilling-land-ownership" principle (stipulating that only those who farm should own land), the government launched a massive audit of over 10 million plots.
  • The findings: Officials identified 2.84 million plots—nearly 27% of all farmland—as "suspected violations," including unauthorized leasing or land left fallow.
  • The impact: The policy has effectively branded elderly farmers as potential "speculators," causing land prices to dip and fueling fears of forced government-ordered sales.
  • Who is affected: While the policy targets speculators, its primary victims are elderly farmers who lease their land due to age or physical inability to work, and their descendants who inherited land they cannot personally cultivate.

Policy vs. Reality: Who is Actually Being Targeted?

The core of this controversy lies in the government's rigid, data-driven approach. While the administration claims it is upholding the constitutional mandate to ensure farmland is used for farming, its execution ignores the reality of South Korea’s rapidly aging rural society. Genuine, sophisticated speculators—who know how to exploit legal loopholes and bypass strict regulations—have largely evaded this audit, leaving vulnerable, elderly farmers to bear the brunt of the crackdown.

The label of "suspected violation" is currently being applied to thousands of cases where, in practice, a local farmer has simply rented land from an elderly neighbor in an informal arrangement. By treating these traditional, community-based practices as "illegal," the government has alienated its rural base. Instead of catching speculators, the administration has created a climate of fear, leading to widespread rumors that the state is planning to seize ancestral family land.

A Political Reckoning in the Making

The political fallout is already visible, and the government’s attempts to soothe the public through official briefings have proven ineffective. Even within the President's own political camp, there is visible infighting, with supporters bickering over the policy instead of defending it or offering substantive alternatives. This internal discord, combined with the real-world suffering of the rural population, suggests that the administration has severely underestimated the cost of "administration by statistics."

This situation serves as a stark reminder that even well-intentioned policies, when divorced from the lived experience of citizens, can mutate into a tool of systemic violence. If the administration continues to ignore the voices of farmers in favor of cold data, this farmland audit will likely be remembered as a textbook case of a policy failure that undermined a government's credibility and eroded its political foundation.
