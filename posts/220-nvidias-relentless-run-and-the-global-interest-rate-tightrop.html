Nvidia's Relentless Run and the Global Interest Rate Tightrope

September 07, 2026

Global markets are caught between record-shattering tech earnings and jittery central banks trying to read conflicting inflation data.

    What happened: Nvidia posted stellar data center revenues and raised its forward guidance, while US inflation data showed the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index rising 3.7% year-on-year. What it changes: Big Tech capital expenditures on AI infrastructure have surged past roughly $700 billion (1,000 trillion won), anchoring a massive memory supercycle that directly impacts South Korean chipmakers at the front lines of the supply chain. Who is affected: Global equity investors, retail borrowers facing stagnant domestic rate cuts, and corporate strategists racing to secure high-performance AI chips.

Nvidia's Dominance: Bubble or Hard Reality?

Skeptics love to wave away Nvidia's valuation by applying traditional manufacturing metrics to an AI platform. That is a fundamental framing error. When a company posts $96.2 billion in quarterly revenue and secures $270 billion in order commitments, it is no longer just a volatile theme stock—it is the foundational infrastructure controlling the entire market. While Wall Street analysts debate whether tech stocks are 40% overvalued, the sheer scale of the ecosystem being built makes the "bubble" label increasingly hard to justify.

Meanwhile, the Bank of Korea faces its own high-wire act. Policymakers are split between the necessity of hiking rates to cool a resilient export-driven semiconductor economy and the risk of choking household liquidity. With US inflation data driving rate-cut expectations into a tailspin, everyday borrowers are left holding the bag while central banks play a high-stakes game of economic forecasting.

Navigating the Wave

The current market environment forces a harsh reality check: trying to time central bank pivots or trading purely on "supply shortage" headlines is a loser's game. The real money is moving where the structural cash flows are—straight into the AI infrastructure supercycle led by US tech giants and powered by South Korean memory suppliers.

Markets will always swing between irrational exuberance and unwarranted panic. But pretending that transformative technological shifts are fleeting fads is more dangerous than buying into them cautiously. The takeaway isn't to blindly chase every speculative green arrow on your trading app, but to coldly evaluate whether your assets are aligned with where the global economy is actually putting its money.
