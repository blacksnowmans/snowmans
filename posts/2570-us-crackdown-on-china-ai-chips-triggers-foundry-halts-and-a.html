U.S. Crackdown on China AI Chips Triggers Foundry Halts and a 5% Kospi Crash

November 21, 2026

An intensifying U.S. regulatory crackdown on artificial intelligence chips has forced foundry giants Samsung Electronics and TSMC to halt shipments to Chinese clients, triggering a sharp 5.21% single-day collapse in South Korea’s benchmark stock market.

  • Foundry supply chains choked: Following revelations that the U.S. Department of Commerce previously approved nearly 880,000 AI chip exports to China, Washington pivoted to squeezing contract manufacturers, forcing TSMC and South Korea’s Samsung Electronics to stop delivering select AI chips to Chinese firms.
  • NVIDIA under legal fire: The California Department of Justice issued a subpoena to NVIDIA over allegations of routing more than 40,000 chips to Chinese entities, sparking U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission scrutiny and executive exit rumors.
  • Korean markets hammered: South Korea’s benchmark Kospi stock index dropped 5.21% to the 4,800 level, led by a 7% plunge in Samsung Electronics stock amid foundry disruptions and falling memory prices.
  • Chinese AI capabilities hit: Chinese AI developers, including startup Moonshot AI, face severe hardware shortages that have degraded chatbot services and forced computational limits.

Washington's Policy Backlash Hits Asian Foundries

The sudden escalation stems from intense political friction in Washington after reports surfaced that the U.S. Commerce Department had approved 879,868 advanced AI chip exports to Chinese entities over six months, including 270,000 destined for military-linked firms. Scrambling to close these regulatory loopholes under heavy congressional criticism, U.S. authorities shifted tactics from monitoring design firms to directly restricting contract semiconductor foundries.

This pivot leaves Samsung Electronics—South Korea’s largest technology conglomerate—and Taiwan’s TSMC caught in the middle. By targeting foundry supply lines directly, Washington is forcing Asian manufacturers to absorb massive operational losses as lucrative Chinese customer orders are abruptly cut off. Chinese tech firms attempting to pivot to domestic hardware, such as e-commerce giant Alibaba, are simultaneously hitting roadblocks, including U.S. shareholder class-action lawsuits over unaddressed regulatory risks.

Market Shocks Spill Over to Seoul

The policy fallout has struck South Korea’s financial markets with exceptional force. The benchmark Kospi index plummeted 5.21% in a single session, dropping to the 4,800 level and triggering automated trading halts for the first time in 10 months. Foreign investors launched a massive sell-off, alarmed by South Korea's acute exposure to geopolitical friction in the semiconductor supply chain. Samsung Electronics led the decline with a 7% plunge, compounded by a 20% drop in NAND flash memory prices, while rival chipmaker SK Hynix also suffered severe losses.

The market reaction highlights the structural vulnerability of South Korea’s export-driven tech sector to Washington’s technology containment strategy. As regulatory anxiety spreads globally—even drawing objections from Wall Street bondholders over SpaceX using scarce AI chips as collateral for orbital data centers—Korean chipmakers remain firmly on the front lines of the U.S.-China technology conflict.
