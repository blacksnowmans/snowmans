Short Squeeze Ignites Recovery in Korean Semiconductor Giants

October 28, 2026

The recent explosive rebound in the South Korean stock market was driven by a massive "short squeeze" in SK Hynix, as foreign investors were forced to buy back shares to cover loss-making bearish bets.

  • Short sellers surrender: Triggered by a surge in U.S. AI infrastructure stocks like CoreWeave, global hedge funds scrambled to close their short positions, leading to a chain reaction of buying in Korean tech leaders.
  • Record-breaking margins: SK Hynix, the world's second-largest memory chipmaker, stunned the market with a 79% operating profit margin, far exceeding the margins of global peers like Nvidia (mid-60s) and TSMC (low 50s).
  • Technical bottom confirmed: Analysts suggest the destructive price correction has ended, as the aggressive selling pressure from automated programs and short sellers has finally been exhausted.
  • Large-cap dominance: Unlike the KOSDAQ (South Korea’s secondary, tech-heavy board) where retail investors remain trapped, large-cap semiconductor stocks on the KOSPI (the main benchmark index) are recovering faster due to a "supply vacuum" in trading volume.

The Mechanics of a Forced Rebound

This rally is less about a shift in economic fundamentals and more about the brutal mechanics of the trading floor. For weeks, foreign investors leveraged heavy short positions against the Korean chip sector. However, when the global momentum for AI hardware shifted, these investors faced margin calls. To limit their losses, they had to buy back SK Hynix shares at any price, creating a vertical price spike. On a single day of this recovery, foreign net buying in SK Hynix reached about $275 million (380 billion KRW), effectively reversing weeks of selling gloom.

The Lee Jae-myung administration and the ruling Democratic Party have inherited a market still reeling from the volatility of the previous era. While the government focuses on stabilizing the macroeconomy following the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the market is finding its own floor. The 79% margin reported by SK Hynix is a "dream number" for the industry, signaling that the profit center of the AI revolution is concentrating on high-bandwidth memory (HBM) suppliers in Korea.

Retail Pain and Structural Volatility

Despite the "green shoots" on the charts, the mood among individual investors remains somber. Data suggests that many of the 300,000 retail shareholders in SK Hynix entered the market when prices were near 2 million KRW (about $1,450) per share. For them, a 10% technical bounce is a small consolation for portfolios that are still significantly in the red. There is a psychological risk here: as prices approach break-even points, retail "panic selling" could provide the very liquidity foreign institutional investors need to finish rebuilding their long positions.

Structural issues like single-stock leverage ETFs continue to haunt the KOSPI. These financial products, which track double the daily performance of stocks like Samsung Electronics, amplify both crashes and rallies. During the recent downturn, these ETFs were forced to dump approximately $6.6 billion (9.2 trillion KRW) worth of shares to maintain their leverage ratios. Now, they are being forced to buy back just as aggressively. For the busy reader, the takeaway is clear: while the technical bottom is likely in, expect high volatility to persist as these mechanical forces work themselves out of the system.
