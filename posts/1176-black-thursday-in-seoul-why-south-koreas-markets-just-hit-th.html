Black Thursday in Seoul: Why South Korea’s Markets Just Hit the Panic Button

October 15, 2026

South Korea’s benchmark stock indices plummeted 7.8% in a single session as a toxic combination of sticky US inflation, surging bond yields, and an AI-driven liquidity drain triggered rare double circuit breakers in Seoul.

  • What happened: Both the Kospi (South Korea's main stock index, down to 5,536) and the tech-heavy Kosdaq (falling to 759) suffered massive 7.8% drops on a single "Black Thursday," prompting market-wide trading halts as foreign investors dumped local shares for an eighth consecutive session.
  • What triggered it: A hotter-than-expected US September Consumer Price Index (CPI) report—showing core CPI rising 0.3% month-over-month—pushed 10-year US Treasury yields above 5.3%, while mounting debt costs hit US AI infrastructure firms like CoreWeave (down 16%) and Dell (down 11%).
  • The liquidity vacuum: Global capital is rapidly reorganizing ahead of AI developer Anthropic's planned Thanksgiving initial public offering (IPO) at a targeted $2 trillion valuation, draining liquidity away from emerging markets to back the massive listing.
  • Who is affected: Domestic retail investors and South Korea's flagship semiconductor makers—Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix—bore the brunt of the foreign sell-off, with the Korean won also weakening 8.2 won to 1,351 per dollar.

The Macro Trap and the AI Liquidity Drain

The immediate panic originated in Washington, where persistent service and shelter costs pushed core US inflation past expectations, torpedoing market hopes for near-term Federal Reserve rate cuts. With 10-year US Treasury yields spiking past 5.3%, global liquidity is shrinking fast. At the same time, Anthropic's blockbuster $2 trillion IPO ambition is acting as a giant financial siphon. Much like previous mega-listings, global funds are liquidating positions in Asian markets to raise cash for US allocations. South Korea—a liquid and open market—has once again become an easy cash machine for international institutional investors adjusting their portfolios.

Structural Strain on Seoul’s Tech Sector

Compounding the macroeconomic squeeze is a sudden reality check across the broader AI ecosystem. Sharp declines in AI cloud vendor CoreWeave and server manufacturer Dell highlight growing concern that corporate adoption of AI is lagging behind corporate spending. With high interest rates pushing borrowing costs for leveraged data center operators into the 15–17% range, investors are fleeing high-yield corporate debt for safe-haven US Treasuries. While South Korean chip giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix hold early equity stakes in Anthropic and report resilient operational earnings, Seoul's markets remain painfully exposed to sudden swings in global liquidity, leaving domestic assets under severe pressure despite deeply discounted valuations.
