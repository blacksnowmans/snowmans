Seoul Real Estate Market Splits as Gangnam Dips and Outer District Rents Surge

November 08, 2026

South Korea’s housing market is experiencing extreme polarization as high-end apartments in Seoul’s wealthiest neighborhoods drop in price while outer districts hit new record highs and rental costs soar.

  • Gangnam slides while outer districts rise: Luxury complexes in Seocho and Gangnam—Seoul's affluent southern districts—saw individual price cuts exceeding 1.45 billion won (about $1.08 million), whereas mid-to-low-priced apartments in outer Seoul districts like Nowon, Dobong, and Gangbuk frequently hit new record highs.
  • Rents skyrocket amid long price rally: Even as overall Seoul apartment sales prices logged their 91st consecutive weekly increase, key lump-sum lease deposits (jeonse) rose for an 89th straight week, driving monthly rents above 3 million won (about $2,250) even outside prime areas.
  • Policy crackdowns and supply pushes: The liberal Lee Jae-myung administration announced zero tolerance for market-manipulating fake rumors while introducing new "equity-accrual housing" options to counter a 77% plunge in nationwide apartment supply this year.

A Tale of Two Real Estate Markets

The legendary invincibility of Gangnam is showing rare cracks. High-end complexes in Seocho District recorded massive single-transaction drops, such as a smaller unit at Raemian One Bailey falling from a peak of 7.15 billion won (about $5.3 million) to 5.7 billion won (about $4.2 million). Yet this cooling at the top has not brought relief to average buyers. Instead, lower-priced outer neighborhoods are seeing desperate end-user buying drive up transaction prices. Discrepancies between public and private statistical agencies—with price drop estimates differing by up to 4.8 times—have only deepened confusion for buyers and sellers alike.

The shift is hitting renters hardest. As high interest rates and tight credit push prospective buyers to stay in rental arrangements, Seoul’s jeonse market has tightened severely. The shortage has triggered an unprecedented surge in monthly rent contracts, forcing young working-class residents to spend a major portion of their monthly income on housing. Meanwhile, regional dynamics are widening: Gyeonggi Province tech corridors like Bundang maintain strong price gains (+17.5% this year), whereas areas burdened by heavy supply like Incheon's Geomdan face declining values.

Crackdowns and Looming Supply Shortages

In response to market instability, the government is taking an aggressive stance against market distortion. Land Minister Hong Ji-seon announced strict police investigations into false online rumors that stoke panic, while Land Minister Kim Yun-deok affirmed before Parliament that government policy can stabilize the market. To assist cash-strapped first-time buyers, officials are launching "equity-accrual housing"—a scheme allowing buyers to purchase an initial stake in a home and gradually acquire full ownership while living there—and inspecting public redevelopment land such as the former Airport High School site in western Seoul.

However, policy interventions face severe headwinds from structural supply bottlenecks. Nationwide new apartment supply plunged 77% year-on-year, raising fears of severe shortages over the next two to three years. In Gwanak District in southern Seoul, new apartment move-ins are expected to hit zero by late 2026. Combined with high borrowing costs under strict debt-to-service regulations, South Korean homebuyers and renters remain squeezed between soaring housing overhead and tight credit limits.
