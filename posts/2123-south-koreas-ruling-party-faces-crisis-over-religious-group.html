South Korea’s Ruling Party Faces Crisis Over Religious Group Infiltration Allegations

November 09, 2026

The Democratic Party of Korea, the country's liberal ruling party, is reeling from allegations that followers of a controversial religious sect mass-joined the party to influence its recent leadership elections.

  • The Allegations: Reports claim that members of the Shincheonji Church—a group often described as a cult—submitted bulk membership applications during the party’s national convention, particularly in the liberal stronghold of the Honam region.
  • The Political Fallout: The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, has seized on the scandal, demanding a formal criminal investigation into claims that Democratic Party (DP) leaders conducted a "secret viewing" of membership lists to hide the infiltration.
  • Leadership Under Fire: DP Chairman Kim Min-seok is struggling to maintain party discipline as internal friction grows between his leadership and more radical factions within the party.
  • Presidential Strategy: President Lee Jae-myung is attempting to stabilize the situation by appointing "pro-Roh" figures—loyalists to the late liberal President Roh Moo-hyun—to broaden the party’s base and counter the opposition's offensive.

A Toxic Religious Association

For any political organization in South Korea, an association with the Shincheonji Church is toxic. The group gained international notoriety for its role in a major COVID-19 outbreak and has long been accused of using secretive tactics to influence mainstream politics. The allegation that they successfully infiltrated the DP's membership rolls strikes at the heart of the party’s "member-centered" reform narrative. If the party cannot prove its membership system is secure, it risks losing the moral high ground it has cultivated since the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

The opposition People Power Party is playing its hand aggressively. By calling for "mandatory investigations," the PPP is attempting to frame the DP leadership as complicit in a cover-up. This puts Chairman Kim Min-seok in a precarious position. He is currently touring regional hubs like Gumi and Gwangju to shore up support, but these visits look like reactive damage control rather than a proactive political strategy. Without a transparent audit of the membership lists, the "secret viewing" tag will likely stick, hampering the government’s ability to push through its legislative agenda.

Internal Power Struggles and Judicial Reform

Beyond the religious scandal, the DP is facing a "war of the leaders" between Chairman Kim and Supreme Council member Jung Chung-rae. This internal jockeying for influence complicates the party’s response to the PPP. While the leadership bickers, the "Minjoo National Innovation Conference"—a powerful group of pro-Lee Jae-myung hardliners—has launched its fourth leadership term. They are demanding the immediate passage of a Special Counsel Act to investigate "fabricated prosecutions" they claim occurred during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

President Lee Jae-myung’s decision to bring in figures from the traditional "pro-Roh" faction is a calculated move to project unity. By reaching out to the old guard of the liberal movement, Lee hopes to consolidate the party’s core while expanding its reach into the conservative-leaning Busan and Gyeongnam regions. However, this "unity" strategy is being undermined by smaller scandals, such as a DP city councilor in Hwaseong recently being investigated for a drunken verbal tirade. For a ruling party tasked with stabilizing the nation after a period of intense political turmoil, these lapses in discipline are more than just a distraction—they are a threat to the administration's legitimacy.
