Gen Z in South Korea Is Trading FOMO for the Joy of Missing Out

October 25, 2026

Burned out by algorithmic feeds, hyper-speed trend cycles, and volatile investment chasing, young South Koreans are increasingly embracing "JOMO"—the Joy of Missing Out—by deliberately choosing offline quiet over constant digital connectivity.

  • The cultural shift: Young Koreans are rejecting social media comparison and stock-market anxieties in favor of "happy abandonment," an intentional practice of discarding unnecessary trends to protect mental energy.
  • The analog return: Practical habits are shifting away from digital convenience toward intentional friction, including listening to FM radio over streaming services, cooking meals instead of ordering on delivery apps, and silencing chat notifications.
  • The medical toll: The trend emerges as a direct reaction to severe burnout, with an increasing number of young adults seeking psychiatric support in recent years for stress tied to crypto trading and chronic lifestyle comparison.
  • The social contrast: While digitally saturated youth treat disconnecting as a restorative luxury, the trend also highlights South Korea’s digital divide, where elderly citizens face involuntary exclusion at self-service kiosks and online welfare portals.

Why Hyper-Connectivity Reached a Breaking Point

South Korea’s hyper-connected digital infrastructure and notoriously fast-paced culture turned FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) into a chronic societal stressor. Over the past few years, the imperative to follow every streaming series, visit every trending cafe, and chase speculative stock and cryptocurrency rallies created an exhausting dopamine loop. Rushing to catch every opportunity did not enrich daily life; instead, it eroded attention spans, fueled relative deprivation, and left young professionals physically and mentally depleted.

The rise of JOMO represents a defensive recalibration. Rather than viewing disconnection as a passive withdrawal from society, South Korean youth are treating it as an active assertion of sovereignty over their time. Stepping back from chat rooms and deactivating accounts allows individuals to replace external validation with simple offline routines, such as writing letters by hand or watching rain from a cafe window without checking a screen.

"Happy Abandonment" as a Modern Survival Skill

In a society that has traditionally equated constant striving with virtue, framing selective quitting as "happy abandonment" marks a notable psychological shift. Gen Z is redefining personal sacrifice not as defeat or falling behind, but as a calculated filter against information overload. By setting rigid personal boundaries—such as leaving smartphones in another room before bed or ignoring peer trends—they carve out space for cognitive recovery.

However, the JOMO phenomenon also lays bare a structural contradiction in modern South Korea. Voluntary disconnection is inherently a privilege enjoyed by those fully fluent in technology. While young urbanites choose to put their phones away to find inner peace, millions of older Koreans remain locked out of essential services by mandatory touchscreen ordering kiosks and online-only administrative processes. Moving forward, the challenge for Korean society will be ensuring that while the exhausted retain the freedom to unplug, the vulnerable are not left completely behind.
