Why South Korea's Kospi Is Holding Strong Above 7000

September 12, 2026

Despite persistent global volatility, South Korea's benchmark Kospi index is finding a resilient floor around the 7000 mark, underpinned by heavy corporate share buybacks and a dramatic stabilization in the won-dollar exchange rate.

  • Corporate Defense: Massive share buybacks by tech giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are providing a psychological and structural floor for the market, absorbing selling pressure every day.
  • Currency Relief: The Korean won has sharply rebounded into the mid-1300s against the dollar after previously spiking to 1570, easing foreign exchange pressure and coaxing foreign investors back to local equities.
  • Semiconductor Supercycle: Surging prices for general-purpose DRAM—driven by production lines shifting toward high-bandwidth memory (HBM)—are fueling massive profit outlooks for Korean chipmakers.
  • Secondary Battery Rebound: U.S. crackdowns on Chinese battery workaround models and booming demand for energy storage systems (ESS) are finally giving beaten-down battery stocks room to breathe.

For months, retail investors have been battered by cumulative losses and a lingering trauma from past market corrections, often dumping shares at the first sign of a minor rebound. Yet, foreign and institutional players have quietly accumulated positions, reading the structural data rather than the daily noise. The paradox of declining trading volume as prices recover signals that long-term holders are locking away their shares, confident that the worst of the downward pressure has passed.

The real engine of this resilience remains South Korea's export heavyweights. With major tech firms generating massive revenues abroad, fears of a runaway exchange rate have evaporated. Meanwhile, AI infrastructure spending—exemplified by advanced foundational models requiring hundreds of thousands of specialized chips—guarantees that the global appetite for Korean memory semiconductors will remain insatiable. Even as secondary sectors like Kosdaq-listed small caps lag due to delayed regulatory reforms, the broader corporate fundamentals are sturdier than retail fear suggests.
