South Korea's Prosecutors Revamp Sparks Concerns Over Reforming the Reform

September 10, 2026

South Korea's Ministry of Justice is pushing a reorganization plan that critics argue will resurrect political prosecution under the guise of reform.

  • What happened: The Ministry of Justice proposed a restructuring plan that would split off the prosecution service into a new "Public Prosecution Office," featuring a staff of over 8,400 including more than 2,200 prosecutors.
  • The catch: Despite claims of downsizing, the plan retains about 7,000 investigators and introduces a new "Judicial Control Department" inside local offices.
  • Who is affected: South Korean citizens and political figures facing ongoing investigations, as critics claim the changes preserve the prosecution's de facto investigative powers and ability to exert influence over other law enforcement agencies.

The Illusion of Separation

The proposed reorganization has drawn fierce pushback from reform advocates who argue it preserves the institutional muscle of South Korea's prosecutors rather than stripping away their controversial investigative powers. Lawmakers and critics point out that keeping roughly 7,000 investigators on board means the agency can easily pivot back to active investigations whenever political winds shift, rendering the separation of investigation and indictment mostly cosmetic.

Adding insult to injury for critics is the planned "Judicial Control Department," which would allow prosecutors to oversee other investigative bodies like the police. Opponents argue this creates an unchecked "towering structure" above other agencies, flying in the face of democratic accountability. Given that prosecutors already handle nearly half of all police-forwarded cases through supplementary investigations, critics view this new unit as an attempt by the prosecution to maintain its status as the king of the investigative field under a new name.
