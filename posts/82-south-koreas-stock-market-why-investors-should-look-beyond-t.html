South Korea’s Stock Market: Why Investors Should Look Beyond the Chuseok Holiday

September 27, 2026

As South Korea approaches the Chuseok (Korean Thanksgiving) holiday, local investors are balancing hopes for a market rebound with a deep-seated anxiety that has defined the trading environment for months. The consensus among market experts is that while the desire for a rally is high, the road ahead remains dictated by technical resistance and a lack of confidence rather than just seasonal trends.

  • The "Break-even" Wall: Local retail investors, discouraged by months of stagnation, are currently acting as a massive sell-pressure point. Every time the index rises, many look to exit at their break-even point, creating a stubborn ceiling for growth.
  • The ADR Gap: A growing 40% valuation gap between Korean semiconductor stocks and their American depositary receipts (ADRs) highlights how detached the local market has become from its global counterparts.
  • Infrastructure Reality: The demand for AI hardware is surging, with the required number of GPUs for advanced models jumping from 100,000 to millions, signaling that infrastructure spending will remain a long-term catalyst despite short-term market doubts.

Market Sentiment vs. Global Reality

The Korean stock market is currently trapped in a psychological battle. Historically, October in US midterm election years has been a period of positive performance, often averaging a 3% gain. However, today’s market is not driven by seasonal charts alone. It is held back by the "debt-fueled" nature of retail investment; margin loans (credit trading) have hit an all-time high, exceeding 36 trillion won (about $27 billion). When the index dips, forced liquidations—often referred to as "margin calls"—trigger a cascade of selling that keeps the market suppressed.

The discrepancy between Korean semiconductor stocks and their US peers is particularly telling. While international investors see the massive expansion in AI infrastructure, local sentiment remains focused on "getting out" rather than "growing." This lack of trust is the primary obstacle. Without a major influx of foreign capital to break through the local "break-even" sell walls, the index remains stuck in a range.

The AI Narrative and Geopolitical Juggling

Despite the skepticism, the underlying fundamental story is shifting toward massive infrastructure growth. AI is no longer just a trend; it is an arms race where corporations view advanced infrastructure as a matter of survival. The rapid upward revisions of revenue guidance by industry giants like Nvidia suggest that demand is far outpacing current capacity. For investors, the path to profit lies in focusing on the global leaders of this shift—like Micron and Nvidia—rather than relying on local technical indicators that have been distorted by retail pessimism.

Geopolitically, the cooling rhetoric between the US, China, and Iran offers a glimmer of stability. As the US approaches its election cycle, the tactical easing of trade tensions—or the potential for tariff waivers in exchange for diplomatic cooperation—could act as an unexpected tailwind. While the market often views US-China relations as perpetually hostile, the necessity for economic stability may force temporary compromises that global markets will likely welcome.

Ultimately, the post-holiday market will not be saved by prayer or vague optimism, but by tangible earnings data. If reports from US-based tech leaders confirm the strength of the AI super-cycle, the current market disbelief will gradually be replaced by reinvestment. For now, the most prudent strategy for investors is to reduce leverage, avoid the pitfalls of panic-selling, and wait for the "numbers" to provide the confidence that the market currently lacks.
