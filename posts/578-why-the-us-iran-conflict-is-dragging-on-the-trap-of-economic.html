Why the US-Iran Conflict is Dragging On: The Trap of Economic Isolation

October 02, 2026

    The US-Iran conflict has turned into a protracted "non-military war" driven by aggressive US economic sanctions rather than just battlefield clashes, pushing Iran into extreme isolation.
  • The US is enforcing a total economic blockade—severing banking networks, choking oil exports through secondary boycotts, and pressuring regional partners like the UAE and Turkey to cut ties with Tehran.
  • This strategy has caused the Iranian rial to plummet (reaching 2.35 million per dollar), sending food prices soaring and plunging the country's 90 million citizens into severe economic hardship.
  • However, rather than triggering regime collapse, the extreme pressure has backfired: it has crushed Iranian moderates, empowered hardliners, and inadvertently funneled Iranian oil to China.
  • Washington's refusal to offer an off-ramp, driven by the "winner's dilemma" and a refusal to trust Tehran, has left both sides stuck in a high-stakes war of attrition.

The core paradox of the current standoff lies in how economic warfare actually functions against authoritarian states. While Washington believes it holds all the cards, historical precedent shows that total economic isolation rarely forces a regime to surrender. Instead, it creates a survival dynamic where leaders shift the entire burden of suffering onto ordinary citizens while using external pressure to rally domestic hardliners. By cornering Tehran and offering no diplomatic exit, the US has violated a fundamental rule of strategy: always leave your adversary a way out.

This miscalculation has also reshaped the geopolitical landscape to America's disadvantage. Sidelining Iran's moderate factions has eliminated negotiating partners, leaving hardliners in charge and pushing Tehran closer into Beijing's economic orbit. For the US, maintaining this level of economic warfare carries its own domestic costs—adding to broader fiscal uncertainties at a time when long-term entanglements strain global markets. True hegemony requires the wisdom to secure a stable peace, not just the power to squeeze an opponent until they break.
