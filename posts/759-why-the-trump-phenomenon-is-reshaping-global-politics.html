Why the Trump Phenomenon is Reshaping Global Politics

October 06, 2026

The rise of right-wing populism is not a collection of isolated national incidents but a global trend fueled by a profound sense of alienation from traditional political establishments.

  • The "Outsider" Appeal: Donald Trump’s success lies in his ability to frame himself as a "destroyer" of the entrenched political cartel, rather than a mere member of the Republican Party (the U.S. party of fiscal conservatism).
  • Weaponizing Anxiety: Far-right movements are gaining ground by simplifying complex social issues—such as migration or crime—into intuitive, fear-based narratives that promise immediate relief.
  • The Power of Image: Politics has shifted from policy-heavy discourse to "image politics," where visual spectacles and strongman personas carry more weight than logical, text-based arguments.
  • A Global Warning: The decline of trust in mainstream governance is leaving a vacuum that populists are eager to fill, challenging the foundations of democracy worldwide.

The Collapse of the Establishment

The core of this political shift is a deep-seated disillusionment with the status quo. For decades, citizens have viewed politics as a slow, compromise-driven process that often fails to address their actual lives. Trump and his global counterparts exploit this by presenting themselves as the only authentic alternatives to a corrupt elite. By dismissing nuanced policy debates as elitist jargon, they offer a sense of catharsis to those who feel ignored.

Progressive and liberal factions often make a critical tactical error: they mock the "childish" rhetoric or theatrical stunts of these populist leaders. While intellectuals laugh at a rally performance, they fail to realize that those same displays provide followers with a powerful, tangible sense of belonging and protection. When mainstream leaders ignore the visceral, immediate anxieties of the public, they inadvertently cede the political narrative to those who will offer simple—albeit dangerous—solutions.

Framing the Future

Politics has effectively become a war of "frames." Trump’s "America First" slogan is a masterclass in reconfiguring complex international relations into a simple, digestible narrative. Similarly, the movement to remove the current South Korean administration gained momentum because it united citizens under a single, clear frame of rejecting what they perceived as an irrational status quo. The danger arises when these frames are used to incite division rather than propose actual solutions.

For South Korea, the challenge is clear. The administration under the Democratic Party of Korea (the main liberal opposition) and other political actors must move beyond high-minded rhetoric and address the lived realities of citizens—specifically concerns like inflation, housing, and job security. If the political establishment fails to communicate in a way that resonates with the average person's daily struggle, the temptation toward reactionary populism will only grow. True democracy is not just about resisting the "other"; it is about building a constructive space where divided voices can eventually converge on shared solutions.
