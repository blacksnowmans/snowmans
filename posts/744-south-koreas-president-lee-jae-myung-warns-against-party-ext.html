South Korea's President Lee Jae-myung Warns Against Party Extremism Amid Slipping Approval Ratings

October 05, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung has issued a sharp public rebuke against "irresponsible extremism" within his ruling Democratic Party, as factional infighting and declining poll numbers threaten his administration's reform agenda.

  • What happened: President Lee posted a strong warning on X (formerly Twitter) condemning unverified claims and factional demagoguery, targeting hardline elements within the ruling party who have opposed key appointments like the nominee for the new Major Crime Investigation Agency.
  • The political fallout: The comments highlight deep rifts within the Democratic Party between traditional core supporters and moderate factions, exacerbated by recent disputes over party leadership and local political snubs.
  • Who is affected: President Lee's administration, which has seen its approval rating slide to 37.4% amid voter fatigue over political squabbling and mounting economic pressures.

The core tension in South Korean politics right now is a widening chasm between ideological purity and practical governance. President Lee's intervention attempts to push back against a growing culture of partisan tribalism where emotional label-calling and sensationalist rhetoric masquerade as reform. By criticizing hardliners who prioritize factional signaling over tangible results, the administration is drawing a hard line against internal obstructionism.

However, managing the party's base requires more than top-down lectures. Discontent among core supporters—who feel alienated by perceived slights and heavy-handed internal politics—runs deep. If the administration fails to bridge this emotional gap, the resulting friction could permanently alienate the moderate voters needed to sustain effective governance and push through major legislative reforms.

Shifting Focus from Factional Feuds to Livelihoods

Ultimately, the ongoing public disputes and political posturing are taking a heavy toll on public patience. With everyday citizens grappling with stubborn inflation, housing anxieties, and complex diplomatic challenges, continuous party drama is a luxury the government cannot afford.

President Lee's call for reason and results points to the right priority: steering energy away from destructive internal purity tests and back toward the economy and people's livelihoods. Whether the administration can successfully mend fences with its own base while refocusing on competent governance will define the true success of this presidential term.
