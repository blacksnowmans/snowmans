South Korea's Ruling Democrats Face Escalating Internal Feud After Party Convention

November 03, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) has plunged into bitter internal turmoil following its recent national convention, facing mutinous pushback from grassroots members alongside sharp reform ultimatums from its progressive ally, the Rebuilding Korea Party.

  • Grassroots revolt against leadership: Dues-paying party members have voiced furious backlash against newly installed DPK Chairman Kim Min-seok over procedural irregularities, controversial voting rule changes, leaked member data, and heavy-handed convention rules—including an official ban on wearing yellow clothing.
  • Ultimatum from progressive ally: Shin Jang-sik, leader of the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party, warned that the DPK faces guaranteed defeat in upcoming elections unless it weeds out entrenched, anti-reform incumbents in local districts.
  • Merger controversy reignited: Senior lawmaker Jung Chung-rae sparked factional friction after acknowledging past closed-door party merger talks, taking to Facebook to claim that the intentions of President Lee Jae-myung diverged from those of the party leadership at the time.
  • Threat to legislative momentum: The procedural disputes—highlighted by unexplained delays in electing the DPK's National Women’s Committee chair—threaten to erode voter confidence ahead of high-stakes local elections.

Entrenched Bureaucracy Sparks Grassroots Backlash

Rather than consolidating authority behind Chairman Kim Min-seok, the DPK’s post-convention landscape has exposed deep fault lines between party elites and rank-and-file supporters. Much of the member outrage stems from voting rights and procedural legitimacy. Grassroots members, who pay regular party dues, argue that their influence remains heavily diluted in favor of unelected party delegates. Arbitrary administrative decisions during the convention—such as censoring the color yellow, long a historic progressive symbol—have only fueled accusations that the leadership views the party base as a disruptive force to be managed rather than a constituency to be represented.

This organizational friction reached a boiling point over the delayed election for the head of the party’s National Women’s Committee. For thousands of dues-paying members, the unexplained procedural roadblocks symbolize an entrenched, bureaucratic party machine unwilling to yield power to the wider membership. The growing chorus calling for leadership accountability and administrative suspensions highlights a widening legitimacy deficit at the core of the ruling party.

Allied Pressure and the Battle for the Reform Narrative

Compounding the DPK’s internal headaches is aggressive pressure from the Rebuilding Korea Party. By invoking past factional betrayals that hobbled previous progressive administrations, Rebuilding Korea Party leadership is framing the DPK’s incumbent lawmakers as a complacent political establishment prioritizing self-preservation over actual reform. With threats to run rival reform candidates in underperforming districts, the minor party is positioning itself as the true driver of progressive change, directly challenging the DPK's dominance.

Meanwhile, the public clash surrounding Jung Chung-rae’s past merger negotiations underscores the lack of formal communication within the ruling camp. Although the DPK successfully took power and established the Lee Jae-myung administration following the turbulence of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's presidency, its factional rivalries continue to play out in public social media spats. If the DPK fails to restore procedural fairness and accommodate internal reform demands, its fractured foundation could quickly jeopardize its governance agenda.
