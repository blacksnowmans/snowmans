South Korea Briefing: Clashing Special Probes, Impeachment Threats, and Divided Economic Realities

October 17, 2026

South Korea’s political landscape has plunged into a fierce multi-front conflict as minor progressive leader Cho Kuk demanded a special counsel investigation into conservative opposition chief Han Dong-hoon, while prosecutors sought prison sentences for top aides from the disgraced Yoon Suk-yeol era.

  • Dueling investigations: Cho Kuk, head of the progressive Rebuilding Korea Party, called on the National Assembly to appoint an independent counsel to investigate allegations regarding the academic credentials of Han Dong-hoon’s daughter, which the main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) dismissed as baseless slander.
  • Fallout from Yoon's administration: Special prosecutors demanded four years in prison for former presidential chief of staff Kim Dae-ki and three and a half years for former interior minister Lee Sang-min over illegal contract favors during the controversial relocation of the official residence under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment.
  • Constitutional friction: Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) supreme council member Kim Min-seok signaled moves to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae over alleged judicial bias, drawing sharp rebukes from the conservative PPP, which warned of an unconstitutional assault on judicial independence.
  • Divergent economic signals: While the benchmark KOSPI index broke past 5,500 points amid market reforms, parliamentary audits revealed severe distress in the real economy, with debt indemnity claims at the Korea Credit Guarantee Fund climbing to 8.9 trillion won (about $6.5 billion).

Accountability Campaigns and Deepening Partisan War

The latest political escalation underscores how South Korea's institutional landscape remains gripped by the unresolved aftermath of Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure alongside bitter personal vendettas. Cho Kuk’s demand for an inquiry into Han Dong-hoon explicitly borrows the language of the prosecution service that once targeted Cho's own family, demanding equal scrutiny for conservative elites. Meanwhile, substantial prison demands for Yoon’s top lieutenants signal that legal accountability for executive overreach during Yoon's time in power is entering its final judicial phase, leaving the conservative opposition firmly on the defensive.

However, the ruling liberal DPK is also testing constitutional limits. Threatening to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae marks an aggressive bid to overhaul a judiciary the ruling party views as entrenched and politically compromised. While the hardline faction argues shock therapy is needed to unblock stalled judicial reforms, weaponizing the impeachment mechanism against the head of the nation's highest court carries significant political hazards, playing directly into opposition warnings of legislative overreach.

Executive Transparency Against Ground-Level Economic Strain

For President Lee Jae-myung, public standing remains caught in a structural crosscurrent. Although President Lee's approval rating nudged up to 39% in the latest Gallup Korea poll—bolstered by his insistence on opening cabinet meetings directly to the public—broader voter sentiment remains wary. A notable 47% of respondents favor electing opposition candidates to check the administration in the next legislative race, compared to 34% backing the ruling party, signaling that the electorate is reluctant to grant unchecked power.

This persistent skepticism is rooted in kitchen-table realities. Even as government officials celebrate record-setting financial indices, surging default volumes at state-backed credit institutions highlight the acute vulnerability of small business owners and struggling enterprises. Until policy shifts translate into tangible relief for those falling through the cracks, political theatrics in parliament will do little to reassure an exhausted public.
