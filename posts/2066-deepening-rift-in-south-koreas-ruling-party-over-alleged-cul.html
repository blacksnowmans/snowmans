Deepening Rift in South Korea's Ruling Party Over Alleged Cult Infiltration

November 07, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea is embroiled in an escalating internal feud following allegations that thousands of members from Shincheonji, a controversial fringe religious sect, secretly joined the party to influence internal nominations.

  • Third apology fails to calm tensions: Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) Representative Kim Min-seok issued his third public apology in three days, but failed to appease rival factions demanding deeper accountability.
  • Confidential investigation opened: DPK leadership allowed lawmakers on November 6 to privately review task force data investigating claims that roughly 10,000 Shincheonji-linked individuals—including 8,000 dues-paying voting members—joined the party around 2021.
  • Factional warfare erupts: Pro-Lee Jae-myung faction members called the records "extremely serious," while allies of Rep. Jung Chung-rae, led by Supreme Council Member Choi Min-hee, dismissed the figures as baseless rumors.
  • Push for political accountability: Pro-Jung lawmakers are demanding that Kim Min-seok face legal and political consequences and offer a direct apology to Jung Chung-rae.

A Hegemony Battle Disguised as a Religious Scandal

The controversy centers on Shincheonji, a secretive fringe religious group widely criticized in South Korea for covert recruitment strategies. In South Korean party politics, 8,000 dues-paying voting members concentrated in key regions can easily swing internal nomination primaries. While Rep. Kim Min-seok attempted to extinguish the fire with repeated public apologies, his efforts backfired. Rivals within the DPK viewed the apologies not as a sign of leadership, but as an admission of fault that damaged party credibility.

Rather than resolving the conflict, the party leadership’s decision to offer a private, individual viewing of the task force data only deepened the rift. Pro-Lee faction members claimed the data revealed alarming patterns of organized infiltration during past presidential primaries. Meanwhile, pro-Jung faction members argued the numbers represented mere coincidences or recycled data designed to trap the party in a smear campaign. The lack of public transparency transformed the investigation into a weapon for internal factional warfare.

Strategic Paralysis for the Ruling Party

This internal friction creates a major headache for the ruling DPK under President Lee Jae-myung. At a time when the administration needs strong legislative backing to address economic issues and clear away legacy abuses from Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure, the party finds itself bogged down in a bizarre cult scandal. Rep. Kim’s repeated apologies without clear forensic evidence have only left voters frustrated with the party's handling of the crisis.

Ultimately, this feud highlights a structural vulnerability in how Korean political parties verify digital membership sign-ups. Unless the DPK establishes an objective, transparent audit of its voter rolls, calls for unity will fall flat, leaving the ruling party vulnerable to persistent infighting.
