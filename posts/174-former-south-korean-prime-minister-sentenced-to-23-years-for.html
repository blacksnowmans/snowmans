Former South Korean Prime Minister Sentenced to 23 Years for "12.3" Insurrection

September 05, 2026

A Seoul court has sentenced former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo to 23 years in prison, marking a historic judicial rebuke for his role in the December 3, 2024, emergency martial law declaration.

  • What happened: The court convicted Han of participating in a "self-coup"—an attempt by democratically elected officials to overthrow the constitutional order from within.
  • Why it matters: This is the first major judicial verdict regarding the "12.3 Insurrection," where the administration of former President Yoon Suk-yeol attempted to use military force to neutralize the National Assembly.
  • The takeaway: The 23-year sentence serves as a stern warning that the South Korean judiciary will not tolerate the use of executive power to dismantle democratic institutions.

A Betrayal from Within

The "12.3 Insurrection" stands apart from South Korea’s history of military coups. While previous uprisings in the 20th century were attempts to topple systems from the outside, this incident involved the country’s highest-ranking officials—those sworn to protect the constitution—attempting to dismantle it. By choosing to prioritize this "self-coup" over their legal duties, these officials effectively waged war against the democratic trust placed in them by the electorate.

The court explicitly rejected leniency for Han’s age or his claims of following administrative "customs." Judges noted that a leader at the pinnacle of power cannot excuse participation in a coup by citing the potential for its success. His refusal to acknowledge evidence, including CCTV footage, was viewed not as a defense, but as a cowardly attempt to evade responsibility.

The Weight of the Verdict

Critics of the ruling might argue that 23 years is excessive. However, the court’s decision reflects a modern, democratic South Korea that has far more to lose than it did decades ago. Had the insurrection succeeded, the country would have been forced back into a dark era of dictatorship, stripping citizens of the fundamental rights they enjoy today. By handing down a severe sentence, the judiciary is effectively drawing a line in the sand to ensure such a power grab never finds a foothold again.

The trial exposed the terrifying reality of those six hours when martial law troops entered the National Assembly, and members of the legislature scrambled to pass a resolution to strike it down. The prosecution’s evidence portrayed the former prime minister and his cohorts as operating in complete lockstep with the former president. This verdict confirms that history does not grant infinite immunity to those in power, and that the "system"—the parliament, the courts, and the constitution—has proven resilient enough to hold the architects of this chaos accountable.
