Semiconductor Stocks Top South Korea’s 2026 Consumer Trend List

October 20, 2026

For the first time in the history of the country’s most influential consumer report, a financial asset—semiconductor stocks—has been named the top "product" of the year, signaling a public obsession with securing wealth through the AI revolution.

  • Retail Investment Mania: Shares in companies like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix became the most sought-after "commodity" as the KOSPI (the benchmark South Korean stock index) surged past the 9,000-point mark.
  • Survivalist Spending: The "Ready-core" trend emerged, where consumers aggressively manage their own assets and health to navigate economic uncertainty following the instability of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s term.
  • Digital Shamanism: Despite high-tech advances, traditional fortune-telling (Saju) and Feng Shui saw a massive revival through AI apps as young people sought psychological "insurance" against an unpredictable future.
  • The Analog Pivot: Paradoxically, high-tech fatigue fueled a 2,000% surge in visits to small-town destinations like Mukho Port and a boom in tactile hobbies like custom mechanical keyboards.

The Shift from Speculation to Survival

The Trend Korea 2027 report, published annually by Seoul National University’s Consumer Trend Center, defines 2026 as a year of "struggling to break through uncertainty." In previous years, the top spot was usually held by viral food items or electronics. The rise of semiconductor stocks to number one reflects a shift in the Korean psyche. With the Lee Jae-myung government—the current administration led by the Democratic Party of Korea—pushing to revive the economy and foster high-tech industries, citizens have moved beyond mere consumption toward aggressive self-preservation. This "capitalism-kid" mentality stems from the realization that traditional savings cannot keep pace with rising housing costs in Seoul.

This survivalist streak extends to the "Ready-core" health trend. GLP-1 weight-loss injections, such as Wegovy, ranked 4th on the list, fundamentally changing the fitness industry. Rather than traditional dieting, Koreans are opting for medical intervention to maintain their "physical state" as a form of social currency. Similarly, "skin boosters" (non-surgical cosmetic procedures) have replaced invasive surgery, as healthy-looking skin and a managed physique are now viewed as the most honest indicators of one’s wealth and self-discipline.

The Paradox of High-Tech Loneliness

While the country obsesses over AI and chips, there is a deep-seated hunger for the "human touch." This is visible in the popularity of domestic travel to forgotten small towns and the revival of vintage LPs and film cameras. Travelers are abandoning flashy urban centers for quiet stays in places like Yeongwol, seeking what the report calls "Rustic Life." This movement is supported by President Lee Jae-myung’s emphasis on balanced regional development, which has helped local municipalities turn quiet alleys into trendy "curated" spaces for younger visitors.

Ultimately, the 2026 trends reveal a society that is technologically advanced but emotionally anxious. Whether it is paying about $40 (50,000 KRW) for an AI-powered fortune-telling session or waiting in long lines at the COEX (Seoul's largest convention center) for a book fair, Koreans are searching for narratives to explain their lives. In an era where everything can be delivered with a click, the value has shifted toward experiences that provide a sense of being alive—be it the tactile "click" of a custom keyboard or the grainy noise of a vintage record.
