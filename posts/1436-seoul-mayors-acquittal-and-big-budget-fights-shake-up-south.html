Seoul Mayor’s Acquittal and Big-Budget Fights Shake Up South Korean Politics

October 25, 2026

Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s appellate acquittal has instantly revitalized the conservative opposition’s presidential lineup, triggering furious backlash from the ruling party and escalating clashes across late-stage parliamentary audits.

  • Conservative contender cleared: Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, a senior figure in the conservative People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main opposition party, was acquitted in his second-instance trial, clearing a major legal hurdle ahead of the next presidential race.
  • Ruling party attacks the courts: The governing Democratic Party dismissed the verdict as an unearned reprieve, demanding a Supreme Court appeal while hardliners floated parliamentary inquiries and judicial impeachments against the bench.
  • Showdown over a $117 billion fund: Tensions flared in the National Assembly audit over the government’s 162 trillion won (about $117 billion) "Future Response Fund," which the PPP condemned as an unmonitored slush fund prone to fiscal waste.
  • Labor cap battles in regional hubs: Bipartisan legislation designed to curb regional population decline stalled over corporate demands to exempt designated special economic zones from South Korea's mandatory 52-hour weekly work cap.
  • Democracy icon passes: Former Interior Minister Choi Hyung-woo, a six-term lawmaker and key architect of South Korea's transition to civilian democracy alongside former President Kim Young-sam, died at age 91.

The Dangerous Judicialization of Yeouido Politics

Mayor Oh’s courtroom victory has disrupted the internal balance of power within the conservative PPP while threatening the Democratic Party’s dominant narrative. Yet the immediate reaction from both camps illustrates how dysfunctional Yeouido—South Korea’s political district—has become. Rather than settling debates through legislation, both camps have outsourced political survival to prosecutors and judges, cheering rulings that favor them as righteous justice and attacking unfavorable verdicts as political bias. As Daegu Mayor Hong Joon-pyo noted in the aftermath, politics ceases to function when judicial decrees dictate national direction.

This dynamic also cast a long shadow over the audit of the newly minted Public Indictment Agency. Clashes erupted over the agency's decision to drop previous indictments against President Lee Jae-myung. The opposition blasted the move as an abuse of authority to shield the incumbent head of state, while the ruling Democrats defended it as necessary correction of past prosecutorial overreach. With President Lee’s approval ratings stabilizing around the 40% mark, the administration continues to bypass traditional legislative engagement through direct public messaging, hardening battle lines on both sides.

Industrial Ambitions Collide with Fiscal and Labor Limits

Behind the partisan warfare lies a substantive clash over South Korea's economic trajectory. The administration’s massive 162 trillion won fund reflects an aggressive attempt to jump-start state-backed growth engines, but the opposition’s push for strict debt-repayment mandates exposes growing unease over sovereign balance sheets. Simultaneously, the fight over the 52-hour workweek in regional development zones captures an unresolved dilemma: how to rescue hollowed-out provinces without stripping away core protections for workers. As political stalemates deepen, the cross-aisle cooperation once modeled by old-guard figures like the late Choi Hyung-woo has never looked further away.
