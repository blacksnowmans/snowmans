South Korea's Justice Ministry Confirms Prosecution Leak Scandal

September 09, 2026

South Korea's Justice Ministry has confirmed that confidential prosecution documents were leaked to a prominent politician and weaponized for political attacks, sparking a fierce debate over judicial ethics and deep-rooted corruption within the state's legal apparatus.

  • What happened: South Korea's Vice Justice Minister Lee Jin-su acknowledged in parliament that a prosecution indictment plan published on social media by conservative politician Han Dong-hoon matches confidential internal investigative reports.
  • Why it matters: The documents contain sensitive personal information and case details that are strictly barred from external release, even to lawmakers through formal requests.
  • Who is affected: The scandal implicates senior prosecution officials and former Justice Minister Han, intensifying public distrust in the political neutrality of South Korea's investigative agencies.

A Leak That Exposes the Alliance Between Politicians and Prosecutors

The leak came to light during a plenary session when opposition lawmaker Jeon Yong-gi of the liberal Democratic Party confronted officials with the fact that Han had posted a confidential "indictment plan" online. The document laid out proposed charges and internal assessments regarding a bribery investigation against a political figure, details that only the direct investigative team or top command line could possibly know.

When pressed by lawmakers, Vice Minister Lee admitted that the document was likely a leaked internal report from the prosecution service, noting that such papers should never leave the office under any circumstances. While Han's camp has dismissed the allegations, the admission shatters the defense that the documents were acquired through standard, legitimate channels. It underscores a long-standing practice in South Korean politics where powerful figures with prosecutorial backgrounds retain access to privileged judicial files to shape public opinion and wage political warfare.

Impunity and the Need for a Real Reckoning

Under South Korean law, leaking official investigative files constitutes a serious violation, carrying criminal penalties for public officials and former employees alike under the crime of divulging official secrets. Yet, despite the Vice Minister's confirmation that an illegal leak took place, accountability remains elusive. Investigators and high-ranking prosecutors who oversaw the case—some of whom previously served under Han's tenure—now face intense scrutiny over how private citizens and former officials routinely get their hands on classified state documents.

This incident is far more than a partisan squabble; it is a symptom of a broader institutional disease. For years, critics have warned that South Korea's prosecutorial power is easily weaponized when investigative files are treated as private political currency rather than tools of neutral justice. If authorities sweep this latest scandal under the rug, any talk of genuine judicial reform remains hollow. Restoring public faith in the rule of law requires a transparent, aggressive investigation into the leaks, ensuring that no political figure—no matter how well-connected—stands above the law.
