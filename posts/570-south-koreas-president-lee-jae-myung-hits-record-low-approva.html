South Korea's President Lee Jae-myung Hits Record Low Approval Amid Policy U-Turns

September 10, 2026

  • President Lee Jae-myung's approval rating has dropped for eight consecutive weeks, falling to 37.4% according to a recent Realmeter poll, while negative ratings climbed to 59.5%.
  • Critics point to a widening gap between Lee’s fiery rhetoric on real estate reform and actual policy measures, which critics say fall short of market expectations and penalise ordinary citizens rather than speculators.
  • A new controversy over prosecutorial investigation rules has reignited fears of unchecked prosecutorial power, with critics questioning why the Ministry of Justice—still largely staffed by former prosecutors—retains control over the guidelines.
  • The ruling Democratic Party’s lead in party support has narrowed significantly against the conservative opposition, reflecting growing voter fatigue and disillusionment.

South Korean politics is caught in a familiar, frustrating loop: soaring rhetoric from the top followed by watered-down execution on the ground. When President Lee took office promising aggressive structural overhauls, the public expected a clean break from past political inertia. Instead, the administration's flagship initiatives—particularly in housing reform and prosecutorial oversight—have repeatedly bogged down in compromises, leaving both reformists and everyday citizens disillusioned.

The damage is showing up clearly in the numbers. An eight-week slide in approval ratings is no statistical blip; it is a direct rebuke from a public tired of grand promises that evaporate upon contact with vested interests. When a government blames media distortion or external opposition for its sinking numbers instead of re-examining its own policy follow-through, it loses the moral authority that brought it to power. As the gap between the ruling and opposition parties narrows, the administration is running out of time to prove it can turn legislative intent into real-world results.
