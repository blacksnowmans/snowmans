South Korea’s Ruling Party Signals Early Seoul Mayoral Race Amid Opposition Legal Troubles

October 24, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DP) has openly begun preparations for a snap Seoul mayoral election, anticipating an appellate guilty verdict that would strip conservative Mayor Oh Se-hoon of his office.

  • Preemptive election prep: DP leader Kim Min-seok declared the ruling party is actively reviewing candidates for a potential Seoul mayoral by-election ahead of Oh Se-hoon’s upcoming second-trial ruling.
  • Opposition backlash: The main opposition People Power Party (PPP) slammed the move as blatant judicial interference, accusing the ruling party of attempting to influence the court before a verdict is handed down.
  • Cabinet meeting clash: DP floor leader Han Byung-do dismissed PPP threats to impeach President Lee Jae-myung over allowing crime investigation agency heads into Cabinet meetings, arguing that live-streamed sessions ensure transparent oversight.
  • Internal factional drama: DP leadership briefly descended into an on-air clash over whether to release internal probe results regarding alleged party membership ties to the controversial Shincheonji religious group.
  • Policy and budget push: Simultaneously, the ruling party launched a special committee to back the domestic defense industry and pledged 13.2 billion won (about $9.8 million) for the Itaewon tragedy special investigation committee.

Capitalizing on Opposition Legal Vulnerabilities

By publicly declaring that Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s appeal will likely uphold a seat-forfeiting conviction, the ruling DP is aggressively seizing political momentum. Recapturing Seoul—the nation's political and economic center—would cement the DP's authority under President Lee Jae-myung following their previous local election defeat. The PPP’s furious pushback underscores their vulnerability; losing the capital's top seat would leave the main opposition party severely weakened.

The ruling party is pairing this electoral offensive with structural reforms to state institutions. By promoting live-streamed Cabinet meetings to oversee investigative agency heads, the DP aims to draw a sharp contrast with the secretive power dynamics seen during the administration of Yoon Suk-yeol, who was impeached and imprisoned. However, opposition lawmakers contend this setup is a masked effort to exert executive pressure on active investigations.

Internal Friction and Governance Challenges

Despite its offensive stance, the DP faces internal tensions. A heated dispute between Supreme Council members over whether to publish investigation results on secret religious sect infiltration exposed lingering factional mistrust live on camera. While party leaders moved quickly to contain the dispute behind closed doors, maintaining internal discipline remains a critical challenge for the ruling coalition.

At the same time, the party is attempting to demonstrate governance capacity by backing key budget initiatives, such as funding the Itaewon disaster probe and supporting K-defense exports. Balancing aggressive election maneuvers with actual policy delivery will determine whether the DP can sustain its political dominance in the months ahead.
