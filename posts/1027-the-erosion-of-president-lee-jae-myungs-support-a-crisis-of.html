The Erosion of President Lee Jae-myung’s Support: A Crisis of Credibility

September 23, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung’s administration is facing a plummeting approval rating, driven by widespread disillusionment among his core base over failed campaign promises, particularly regarding judicial reform and personnel appointments.

  • Broken Promises: The administration’s failure to implement promised structural reforms—specifically the separation of investigative and prosecutorial powers—has alienated voters.
  • Personnel Scandals: A series of failed ministerial appointments has highlighted a reliance on a tight-knit inner circle, often referred to as the "Seongnam Line" (referring to the city where Lee served as mayor).
  • Public Sentiment: Polling indicates that over 26% of supporters cite the regression of prosecutorial reforms as the primary reason for their withdrawal of support.
  • Political Infighting: High-profile resignations, such as that of Chief of Staff Kang Hoon-sik, suggest deep-seated power struggles within the Blue House.

The Illusion of Reform

The core of the current political crisis lies in the perceived betrayal of the "prosecutorial reform" agenda. Candidate Lee had campaigned on a platform that included dismantling the existing prosecution structure in favor of independent investigative and public-prosecution agencies. Instead, the administration has been accused of "prosecutor-centric" hiring, filling key positions with career prosecutors. Critics argue this has turned a long-promised democratic reform into a performative "scam," leaving the public feeling deceived.

This betrayal is compounded by an insular hiring process. By favoring loyalists—the so-called "Seongnam Line"—the administration has effectively shut out broader expertise, leading to a string of collapsed ministerial nominations and a general sense of administrative incompetence. The departure of key officials amidst these failures is being interpreted not as an isolated setback, but as evidence of a systemic collapse within the Blue House.

A Call for Decisive Change

The disconnect between the administration and its supporters is widening. While the President’s team has attempted to blame external factors or opposition rhetoric, the reality is that the base is reacting to the administration's own lack of political resolve. There is a growing consensus among critics that the "New Lee Jae-myung" supporters—a group of online influencers and channels often criticized for aggressive, partisan attacks—are doing more harm than good by prioritizing sycophancy over healthy, critical discourse.

For President Lee, the path forward requires more than just rhetoric. To stop the decline, observers suggest a radical clearing of the "Seongnam" inner circle and a return to the administration's foundational goals. The current reliance on personal loyalty over institutional competence is not only damaging the President’s legacy but is increasingly seen as a direct threat to the administration's survival. In a political climate where trust is the primary currency, the administration’s failure to deliver on the "common sense" reforms it once championed may be the final nail in the coffin for its current approval trajectory.
