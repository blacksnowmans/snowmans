Ruling Party Chief Kim Min-seok Turns to Youth as Disapproval Ratings Soar

November 22, 2026

Facing a steep 67.4% disapproval rating ahead of his 100th day in office, Kim Min-seok, leader of South Korea's ruling Democratic Party, launched an aggressive outreach campaign to win back skeptical young voters.

  • What happened: Kim attended the party's "1030 Youth Speech Contest" on November 21 and appointed four young contest winners as official party sub-spokespersons to give younger members a direct voice in national communications.
  • Why it matters: A recent Kuki News poll showed Kim's disapproval rating hit 67.4%, while Jang Dong-hyeok, head of the main opposition People Power Party, registered 61.5%, reflecting deep public exhaustion with mainstream political leadership across the spectrum.
  • The shift: Timed alongside the Democratic Party's 71st anniversary, the move represents a deliberate attempt to shed the party's image as rigid and out of touch with younger generations.
  • Who is affected: Voters in their teens, twenties, and thirties, whose growing alienation over economic pressures and fairness issues has become a major political burden for the ruling party.

Desperation Behind the Youth Outreach

The timing of Kim Min-seok's push is no coincidence. Approaching his 100-day milestone as head of the ruling party, Kim faces mounting criticism over internal factional friction and slowing legislative momentum. The party's traditionally reliable base among young voters has eroded significantly in recent years amid persistent job scarcity, housing unaffordability, and debates over social fairness. By publicly admitting that the Democratic Party may have grown "stuffy" and outdated, Kim is attempting a candid breakthrough to stem falling support before it further weakens the party's leadership.

Structural Reform or Headline Event?

Promoting four young party members to official sub-spokesperson roles is a concrete step beyond typical political photo-ops. Giving young members official party podiums allows them to shape public messaging using their own language and perspective. However, symbolic positions alone will not resolve the Democratic Party's broader trust deficit among younger voters.

To turn this outreach into sustained political support, the leadership must align its rhetoric with policy outcomes. Unless the party converts the grievances heard at youth events into actual legislation on housing, employment, and economic opportunity, appointing young spokespeople risks being seen as another short-lived crisis management tactic.
