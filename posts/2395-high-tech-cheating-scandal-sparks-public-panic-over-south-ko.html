High-Tech Cheating Scandal Sparks Public Panic Over South Korea's National Exams

November 16, 2026

A high-tech cheating scandal involving artificial intelligence and micro-cameras on a professional qualification exam has triggered a fierce national debate over the security of South Korea’s upcoming College Academic Ability Test, known as the Suneung.

  • What happened: Han Won-seok, who earned top scores on both rounds of the national tax accountant exam, confessed to using micro-cameras, AI, and hidden smartphones to solve exam questions in real time.
  • The government response: South Korea's Education Minister drew sharp criticism after making self-deprecating remarks about his age when asked about AI cheating, while the administration faces backlash for relying on post-exam punishment rather than preventive security.
  • What it changes: Widespread doubts over whether current metal detectors and proctor manuals can stop micro-devices have sparked public arguments over whether the nationwide test should be postponed.
  • Who is affected: Hundreds of thousands of high school seniors and parents, alongside politicians from both the ruling Democratic Party of Korea and the main conservative opposition People Power Party.

From Tax Exams to the Suneung: A High-Tech Security Crisis

The scandal erupted when top-scorer Han Won-seok admitted that his result on the tax accountant examination was achieved through a high-tech setup: capturing test questions with a micro-camera, transmitting them to an AI engine, and reading solved answers on a hidden smartphone. This revelation shocked South Korea, where high-stakes standardized exams are viewed as the primary engine for social mobility and fairness.

Anxiety immediately spread to the Suneung, South Korea's university entrance exam taken by hundreds of thousands of students annually. Unlike analog cheating methods such as hidden notes or looking at a neighbor's paper, real-time AI processing bypasses traditional human supervision. Test-takers and parents are now demanding clear proof that test centers can detect signal relays and micro-gear, warning that a single undetected breach could destroy public faith in the national entrance system.

Political Dilemma for the Lee Jae-myung Administration

The government's handling of the situation has only heightened public anger. When pressed on whether the ministry could intercept AI-assisted cheating, the Education Minister remarked, "Given my age... I don't think I can rely entirely on myself to get it perfect." Critics condemned the comment as an admission of helplessness. Analysts also point out that the Lee Jae-myung administration's strategy relies primarily on heavy penalties after cheaters are caught, rather than investing in physical equipment to block devices at test center entrances.

This leaves the administration facing a difficult political dilemma. Postponing the nationwide exam would be seized upon by opponents as an admission of executive incompetence, causing immense disruption for students. However, holding the exam under questionable security risks a severe crisis if another AI cheating scheme comes to light post-exam. With frustration mounting across both conservative opposition circles and ruling party supporter communities, the issue has transformed into a critical test of public trust in government competence.
