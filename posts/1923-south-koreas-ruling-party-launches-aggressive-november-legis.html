South Korea's Ruling Party Launches Aggressive November Legislative Drive

November 04, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party has initiated an intense legislative push for November to back President Lee Jae-myung's policy agenda, prioritizing a special counsel bill to investigate past prosecutorial abuses and key livelihood issues despite fierce opposition from the conservative People Power Party.

  • Special Counsel Push: The Democratic Party has adopted a bill targeting evidence manipulation and overzealous prosecutions during the tenure of impeached former leader Yoon Seok-yeol, aiming for a vote in a plenary session.
  • Crypto Tax Delay: Lawmakers have formally proposed a two-year postponement on taxing virtual assets to protect the country's estimated 7 million retail investors and stabilize the market.
  • Livelihood Reforms: The party is pushing for year-end processing of a retirement age extension and the introduction of direct elections for the head of the National Agricultural Cooperative Federation.
  • Tightened Schedule: To counter potential filibusters by the People Power Party, the ruling party has scheduled plenary sessions every Thursday for three weeks, while maintaining a strict target to finalize the national budget by December 2.

Pushing Through Legislative Gridlock

The Democratic Party's strategy centers on demonstrating legislative competence by tackling both high-profile political reforms and immediate economic concerns. The decision to fast-track the special counsel bill is framed as a crucial step toward restoring judicial integrity following the turbulent era under Yoon Seok-yeol, who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment. However, the People Power Party has vowed to use every available tool, including filibusters, to block the legislation, labeling it a political shield for the administration.

Beyond politics, the ruling party is pivoting toward pragmatic economic management. The push to delay cryptocurrency taxation acknowledges that the current administrative infrastructure is not yet ready to handle the market without causing undue friction for younger investors. Similarly, advancing the agricultural cooperative reforms and the retirement age extension reflects an effort to shore up support among key voting blocs while addressing South Korea's rapidly aging society.

The Budget Battle Ahead

With the ruling party committing to weekly Thursday plenary sessions to bypass opposition stalling tactics, November is shaping up to be a defining test of legislative strength. The ultimate showdown will likely center on the December 2 statutory deadline for the national budget. While the opposition prepares significant spending cuts, the Democratic Party is holding firm on safeguarding welfare and job creation initiatives designed to jump-start President Lee's economic priorities.
