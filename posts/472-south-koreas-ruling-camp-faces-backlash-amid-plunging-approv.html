South Korea's Ruling Camp Faces Backlash Amid Plunging Approval Ratings

September 08, 2026

  • Senior Presidential Secretary Hong Ik-pyo's recent media appearance backfired, fueling anger among supporters as poll numbers slide toward the 20% range.
  • Critics slam the administration for using a "false equivalency" argument that blurs the lines between victims of political attacks and aggressive pro-government commentators.
  • The controversy highlights growing anxiety within the ruling circle over potential upcoming political defeats, including mayoral by-elections.

South Korea's ruling administration is facing a deepening credibility crisis as plunging approval ratings expose widening rifts between the Blue House and its core supporters. Rather than addressing the root causes of voter dissatisfaction, senior officials have increasingly resorted to deflection and evasive rhetoric.

A prime example came when Senior Presidential Secretary for Political Affairs Hong Ik-pyo appeared on a popular political broadcast to defend the administration. Instead of offering policy shifts or genuine self-reflection, Hong leaned heavily on false equivalencies—suggesting that both government critics and aggressive online factions share the blame for current political toxicity. Observers and supporters alike condemned the framing as a cowardly attempt to excuse bad behavior by equating abusers with the abused.

Compounding the frustration is a perceived lack of internal accountability under President Lee Jae-myung. Critics note parallels to previous conservative administrations, where controversial policies—such as sweeping prosecutorial office reforms—are dropped on the public just before the president departs on overseas diplomatic trips, leaving aides to absorb the political fallout at home. With key pro-government commentators remaining silent on sensitive issues like overseas troop deployments, many progressives fear the administration is drifting into an isolated, top-down style of governance that ignores grassroots dissent.
