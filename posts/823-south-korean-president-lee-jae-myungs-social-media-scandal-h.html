South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s Social Media Scandal Highlights Governance Risks

September 20, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is facing a significant political backlash after he publicly endorsed a social media post from a supporter who frequently uses extreme, derogatory language toward government officials and political rivals.

  • The Incident: President Lee quoted an X (formerly Twitter) post praising his administration’s reform agenda, replying, "Thank you. It is a great comfort."
  • The Fallout: It was soon revealed that the original author is a well-known extremist who routinely directs vitriolic abuse at members of the ruling Democratic Party, exposing a failure in the presidential office's vetting processes.
  • The Response: President Lee deleted the post and issued a "conditional apology," stating he was unaware of the author’s history and claiming that quoting someone does not equate to endorsing their entire worldview.
  • Political Impact: The incident has exacerbated internal friction within the Democratic Party and accelerated a decline in public approval ratings, which are reportedly nearing the 20% threshold.

A Crisis of Communication and Accountability

The President's reaction has been criticized as a classic example of "evasive rhetoric." By framing his apology with the caveat, "if anyone was hurt by the author's other writings," Lee effectively avoided taking direct responsibility for his own lapse in judgment. Critics argue that instead of acknowledging a failure in his staff’s vetting system, the President opted for emotional, defensive appeals, leaving many voters frustrated by the lack of accountability.

This episode is symptomatic of a deeper, more structural issue within the current administration. While the President frequently calls for "national unity" and "responsible governance," his tendency to rely on the support of polarized, extreme fanbases—and his reluctance to distance himself from their toxic rhetoric—creates a glaring contradiction. The public is increasingly viewing this as a sign of an administration that talks about reform but is unwilling to conduct the necessary internal house-cleaning to achieve it.

The Trap of Polarized Fandom

The core of the issue lies in the President's relationship with his most fervent supporters. By calling these individuals "friends," Lee has effectively provided them with a veneer of legitimacy, even as they engage in the systematic harassment of internal party dissenters. When a leader fails to draw a clear line between healthy critique and abusive, targeted harassment, they risk alienating the moderate public.

For the administration, the path forward is narrow. To recover, Lee must move beyond social media damage control and address the growing disillusionment among voters who are tired of "us vs. them" politics. If the President continues to prioritize the validation of his hardline base over genuine, bipartisan outreach, he risks losing the political capital required to govern effectively for the remainder of his term. The "fingertip revolution" of social media may have helped him win power, but it is proving to be a fragile foundation for sustaining it.
