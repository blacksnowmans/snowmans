The Democratic Party’s Strategy Crisis Under Kim Min-seok

October 09, 2026

  • Falling Support: Despite promising to turn around public approval ratings within 100 days, Democratic Party (DP) leader Kim Min-seok faces mounting skepticism as his party’s popularity continues to slide.
  • Internal Discord: High-profile political commentator Rhyu Si-min recently warned that the party’s current strategy is heading toward "inevitable failure," triggering a backlash from party loyalists.
  • Failed Outreach: The party’s attempt to engage voters through its official YouTube program, "Minty 07," has floundered, marked by low viewership and the departure of its lead writer after only one month.
  • Misspent Resources: Controversy has erupted over the Prosecution Reform Task Force, which spent over $700,000 (1 billion won) in taxpayer money—including $90,000 (130 million won) on consultant fees—without producing a final policy proposal.

The Democratic Party (DP), South Korea's governing party, finds itself in an increasingly precarious position. Barely a year into President Lee Jae-myung’s term, the party is struggling with a widening gap between its ambitious legislative goals and the harsh reality of public sentiment. Critics and even some party supporters are calling the current leadership's strategy "amateurish," noting that the party seems more focused on internal power dynamics than on addressing the bread-and-butter issues facing the electorate.

The frustration is perhaps best exemplified by the failed push to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. In an attempt to pressure the judiciary, the party leadership championed an impeachment move that experts quickly labeled a political misstep. The tactic not only failed to sway the court but also alienated moderate voters who viewed the move as an assault on the independence of the judiciary, further damaging the party's credibility.

Internal Dissent and Media Failures

The party’s inability to handle constructive criticism has created an echo chamber. When veteran commentator Rhyu Si-min suggested that President Lee’s leadership and his team’s planning capabilities were dangerously lacking, he was met with vitriol from party officials rather than policy reflection. Senior party figures dismissed his warnings as "curses" and "delusions," reinforcing the perception that the DP is currently allergic to dissenting voices.

This insular approach extends to the party's digital strategy. The "Minty 07" YouTube channel, intended to be a flagship platform for communication, has become a symbol of the party’s disconnect. By relying on aggressive, exclusionary rhetoric that mocks political opponents rather than inviting broader public discourse, the show has failed to gain traction. The departure of its lead writer after just 30 days is widely seen as a symptom of a project built on poor planning and a misunderstanding of how to reach voters beyond the party's hardcore base.

Ultimately, the DP is paying the price for favoring internal solidarity over effective governance. With approval ratings among voters in their 30s in Seoul falling to 70% negative, the party is running out of time to pivot. Unless the leadership stops prioritizing party defense and starts addressing the practical failures—like the fiscal waste of its reform committees—the upcoming local elections could serve as a grim referendum on their tenure.
