Seoul Apartment Sales Stall Near Zero Growth as Higher Rates and Tax Burdens Cool Buyers

November 27, 2026

Seoul’s apartment sales prices effectively ground to a halt in late November with a meager 0.01% weekly gain, as rising mortgage rates and property taxes pushed price declines from wealthy southern districts into the broader Han River belt.

  • What happened: Seoul's weekly apartment price growth slowed for a sixth consecutive week to 0.01%, with nine districts turning negative—led by Songpa District, which plunged 0.50% in its sharpest weekly fall in nearly four years.
  • Key drivers: A recent rate hike by the Bank of Korea, South Korea's central bank, pushed commercial mortgage rates toward 5%, while nearly 730,000 property owners faced comprehensive real estate holding taxes, driving Seoul's for-sale apartment inventory up 45.6% compared to early this year.
  • Market divergence: While prospective buyers pulled back amid high borrowing costs, Seoul's apartment jeonse (lump-sum lease deposit) prices rose 0.14% due to a worsening shortage of rental listings.
  • Government response: The Lee Jae-myung administration pointed to the slowing figures as evidence of market stabilization, while highlighting plans to recruit applicants for 56,000 public housing units in the capital area in 2026.

A Shift in Buyer Leverage

The sharp drop in Songpa District—where asking prices at the major complex Helio City fell by as much as 400 million KRW (about $290,000)—signals that Seoul’s premium housing defense is weakening. For nearly two years, sellers held firm on high asking prices. However, with for-sale listings accumulating rapidly and holding costs mounting from taxes and 5% mortgage rates, power is shifting toward buyers who prefer to wait out market volatility. The downward pressure that began in high-end southern hubs like Gangnam, Seocho, and Songpa has now spread to Han River belt areas like Gwangjin and Yeongdeungpo Districts.

The Jeonse Squeeze

A slowdown in transaction prices does not necessarily relieve housing stress for residents. As high mortgage rates deter home purchases, many prospective buyers are choosing to stay in rental housing instead, driving Seoul's jeonse prices up 0.14% in a single week. This widening gap between stagnant sales prices and rising rental costs leaves tenants facing higher living expenses, even as the government highlights cooling sales growth. Until new public housing supply begins absorbing this demand, Seoul renters remain caught between high interest rates and tightening rental supply.
