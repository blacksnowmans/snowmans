South Korea’s November Childcare Briefing: Community Charity and Public Infrastructure

November 06, 2026

Across South Korea, local daycare centers are turning charitable activities and expanded public infrastructure into a pillar of community welfare, aligning with the current Lee Jae-myung administration’s focus on state-led childcare.

  • Grassroots Philanthropy: Centers nationwide are donating proceeds from charity bazaars to support vulnerable neighbors, with individual donations ranging from roughly $500 to over $1,200 (1.32 million to 1.63 million KRW).
  • Public Expansion: Local governments are aggressively establishing new state-run childcare facilities to bridge the urban-rural divide and ensure consistent, high-quality care.
  • Transparency Efforts: The growth of "Open Daycare Centers"—where parents actively participate in daily operations and monitoring—is fostering greater trust and accountability.
  • Holistic Growth: Beyond basic care, programs are integrating historical education, agricultural field trips, and intergenerational activities with local senior centers.

The current push toward "state-responsible childcare" marks a clear departure from the market-oriented approach seen during the administration of the now-imprisoned former president, Yoon Seok-youl. By prioritizing public access and parental participation, the government is attempting to stabilize childcare environments that were previously neglected or left to private market fluctuations. These local initiatives, such as charity bazaars that serve as practical economic education, demonstrate a shift toward treating childcare as a community-wide responsibility rather than a private burden.

Strengthening the Public Safety Net

The expansion of state-run facilities and the institutionalization of "Open Daycare Centers" are critical to maintaining public trust. In Ulsan, for example, the number of designated Open Daycare Centers has surged to 56, signaling a systemic effort to curb childcare-related issues through transparency. Similarly, new facilities planned for rural areas like Asan, South Chungcheong Province, address the historic infrastructure deficit in the countryside.

However, the sector remains cautious about administrative hurdles. While the policy direction under the Lee Jae-myung administration is widely viewed as a move toward stability, the focus must remain on the welfare of childcare workers and the quality of care provided. Without fair and transparent management of these facilities, the infrastructure expansion risks becoming a hollow gesture. As the nation grapples with low birth rates, ensuring that these centers act as true community hubs—where teachers are respected and children are nurtured—is essential for long-term social recovery.
