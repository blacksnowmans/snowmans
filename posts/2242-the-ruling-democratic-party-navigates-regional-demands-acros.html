The Ruling Democratic Party Navigates Regional Demands Across Korea’s Political Divides

November 12, 2026

The leadership of South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party (DP) has launched a series of high-stakes regional engagements, promising massive infrastructure investments in its southern heartlands while entertaining rare cross-party cooperation with opposition-led provinces.

  • Securing the Base: Party leaders visited Gwangju to promise a new semiconductor cluster and the relocation of a local military airport to spur regional growth.
  • Bipartisan Overtures: The DP leadership met with conservative leaders from the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main opposition, to discuss a proactive review of the Daegu-Gyeongbuk (TK) New Airport and a regional administrative merger.
  • Medical Tensions: Protests erupted in the southwestern region over demands for a dedicated national medical school, highlighting the friction between local needs and central party planning.
  • Labor Push: DP local councilors in Daegu are pushing for the city's first "living wage" ordinances—policies that set pay above the legal minimum—in a direct challenge to the opposition-held local government.

Infrastructure Promises and Grassroots Friction

During a high-level meeting in Gwangju, the DP leadership pledged to mobilize the full weight of the ruling party to ensure the success of a semiconductor cluster in the Honam region, the party's traditional political stronghold. The plan includes the "Mega Project" of relocating the Gwangju military airport and drafting legislation for a "Super-Regional Special Zone." These moves are intended to transform the regional economy through legislative and budgetary support. However, the visit was not without controversy. Outside the meeting hall, residents of the southwestern region protested what they called "deceptive" plans to build hospital branches instead of a full-scale national medical school, an issue that remains a sensitive point for the Lee Jae-myung administration’s rural healthcare policy.

Meanwhile, the DP is attempting to expand its influence in the conservative-leaning Daegu area. Currently, all nine districts in Daegu have zero "living wage" ordinances, a sharp contrast to DP-led regions. By coordinating a joint proposal for these labor protections, DP local councilors are trying to create a "social safety net" for public sector workers, using labor rights as a wedge issue in an opposition-dominated territory.

Strategic Cooperation with the Conservative Heartland

In a notable display of pragmatism, DP leadership also held talks with regional leaders from the opposition PPP, including North Gyeongbuk Governor Lee Cheol-woo and other high-ranking officials. The conservative delegation requested urgent support for the Daegu-Gyeongbuk New Airport and a proposed administrative merger between the two regions. Given that the DP holds a decisive majority in the National Assembly, these conservative strongholds recognize that their "long-cherished" local projects cannot proceed without the ruling party's blessing.

The DP’s response—a "proactive review" of these requests—suggests a strategic willingness to trade cooperation on regional development for smoother passage of the national budget. By entertaining the opposition’s infrastructure needs, the DP positions itself as a party of national governance rather than just a regional faction. However, this cross-party cooperation has limits; local DP branches in the Gyeongbuk area have already criticized the provincial government for excluding certain districts from "basic income" pilots, signaling that even as high-level cooperation occurs, the ground-level political battle remains fierce.
