South Korea's New Political Thriller Sparks Controversy Over Historic Truths

October 02, 2026

The upcoming film "All About Crime" (tentative title) has become a flashpoint for debate in South Korea, as director Hur Jin-ho suggests that powerful "shadow forces" are working to suppress the movie’s release due to its unflinching look at dark chapters in the nation’s modern history.

  • The Premise: The film investigates the 1979 assassination of President Park Chung-hee, specifically focusing on the mystery of the "sixth gunshot"—an extra bullet fired beyond the official record—as a metaphor for state-sponsored cover-ups.
  • The Production: The movie is produced by Hive Media Corp, the studio behind massive historical hits like "12.12: The Day" (Seoul's Spring) and "Inside Men," both known for exposing systemic corruption and the fragility of power.
  • The Resistance: Director Hur alleges that certain factions are actively trying to hinder the film's success, fearing that its popularity will embolden further public scrutiny of the ruling class’s historical origins.

Unearthing the "Sixth Bullet"

Director Hur Jin-ho recently clarified that while the film draws on intense historical speculation—including the mystery of how six shots were heard when only five were officially accounted for—it is not merely an exercise in conspiracy theories. Hur emphasizes that he intentionally avoided suggesting President Park orchestrated his own demise, aiming instead to balance historical fact with the perspectives of those who lived through the era.

The film shifts focus from the political elite to the journalists who risked their lives to track down the truth. By highlighting these reporters, the film acts as a tribute to an era when the press faced physical violence from "political hoodlums" to maintain their integrity. Hur noted that he specifically hopes current journalists—even those at conservative outlets—watch the film to reflect on the legacy of their predecessors who fought for the freedom of the press.

A Reflection of Modern Tensions

The controversy surrounding the film’s production highlights a deeper anxiety within South Korean society: the fear that acknowledging past state violence might threaten the standing of today's establishment. Because Hive Media Corp has a proven track record of turning historical trauma into blockbuster successes, its latest project is being treated by some as a direct threat to the status quo.

Ultimately, "All About Crime" serves as more than just a historical thriller. It is a mirror held up to contemporary South Korea, questioning whether the nation has truly moved past the era of state-sanctioned cover-ups or if the struggle for transparency is simply shifting form. The "shadow forces" allegedly working to kill the film’s momentum prove that the history the movie explores remains as volatile and politically sensitive as ever.
