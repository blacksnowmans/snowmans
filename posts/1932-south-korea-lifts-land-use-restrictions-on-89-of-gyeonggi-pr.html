South Korea Lifts Land Use Restrictions on 89% of Gyeonggi Province

November 04, 2026

South Korean authorities have announced a major reversal in regional land policy, lifting "land transaction permit zone" designations across 324 square kilometers (approximately 125 square miles) of Gyeonggi Province—the area surrounding Seoul. This move, which took effect immediately, scraps restrictions on 89% of the land previously under government oversight.

  • What happened: The government effectively ended the restrictive permitting process for most of Gyeonggi Province, which had been locked down since mid-August to curb speculation.
  • Why it matters: The policy aims to restore property rights for residents in areas where development was not actually occurring, ending a period where owners needed government permission just to buy or sell land.
  • Who is affected: While most property owners in the province regain control over their land, residents in high-demand zones like Gimpo, Guri, Hanam, and Uijeongbu remain under tight government control due to ongoing infrastructure and housing supply projects.

A Shift Toward Market Autonomy

For months, the "land transaction permit zone" policy—which requires residents to live in or use the property they purchase—drew heavy criticism for being an administrative sledgehammer. By treating non-development zones the same as high-speculation areas, the government essentially froze the property rights of ordinary citizens. The decision to lift these restrictions is a rare admission that the blanket, "one-size-fits-all" approach to market regulation was causing more harm than good.

This pivot reflects the current strategy of the Lee Jae-myung administration, which is attempting to balance the need for housing supply with the realities of a stagnant market. By clearing the regulatory clutter, the government hopes to stimulate local economies that were left in the cold by the previous, overly rigid policies enacted during the tenure of the incarcerated former leader, Yoon Seok-yeol. However, the move is as much a political necessity as an economic one; the public frustration over the inability to conduct basic property transactions had reached a boiling point.

The "Hot Zones" That Remain Locked

Despite the widespread deregulation, the government has kept its grip on 39 square kilometers of strategic land. Areas like Hanam—specifically the Gamil, Gambuk, and Misa neighborhoods—have been hit with a three-year renewal of these strict controls. These regions are central to the current government's aggressive housing supply expansion, and officials are clearly terrified that any loosening of the rules would trigger a fresh wave of speculative capital.

This creates an uneven landscape. While most of Gyeonggi Province is now free to trade, the remaining "hot zones" are likely to face increased pressure as investors eye the few remaining areas with guaranteed development upside. For the residents trapped in these designated zones, the government’s next step is crucial: without a clear, transparent timeline for when these restrictions will eventually be lifted, these neighborhoods risk becoming either permanent targets for speculation or centers of legal, drawn-out disputes over property rights.
