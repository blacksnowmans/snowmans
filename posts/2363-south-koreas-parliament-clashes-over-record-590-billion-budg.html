South Korea's Parliament Clashes Over Record $590 Billion Budget as Political Standoff Deepens

November 16, 2026

South Korea's National Assembly faces a severe deadlock over the government's proposed 821 trillion won (about $590 billion) budget for 2025, as fierce partisan battles over special counsel bills and outdoor protests threaten to push deliberations past the constitutional deadline.

  • What happened: The parliamentary budget subcommittee began line-item reviews of the record 821 trillion won budget with less than two weeks remaining before the December 2 statutory deadline.
  • The central policy clash: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea is fighting to preserve the package—including a flagship 162 trillion won ($116 billion) fund for future technology—while the main opposition conservative People Power Party vows deep cuts against what it calls wasteful spending.
  • The political obstruction: Parliamentary negotiations have stalled as the People Power Party stages street rallies against controversial judicial legislation, prompting the Democratic Party to accuse the opposition of dereliction of duty.
  • What it changes: Crucial state support for South Korea’s semiconductor industry, artificial intelligence infrastructure, and social safety nets remains in limbo as the November 19 parliamentary plenary session approaches.

A $116 Billion Tech Fund at the Center of the Budget War

At the core of the financial dispute is the President Lee Jae-myung administration’s proposed 162 trillion won ($116 billion) "Future Response Fund." The ruling Democratic Party of Korea argues this allocation is essential to secure national competitiveness in key industries like semiconductors, artificial intelligence, and renewable energy, while distributing tech-driven economic gains to small businesses and vulnerable citizens. However, the opposition People Power Party views the fund as a loosely defined "slush fund" that threatens fiscal stability, promising a rigorous audit to slash unnecessary expenditures.

Political Scandals and Street Protests Derail Deliberations

Budget reviews are being heavily disrupted by broader political warfare. The People Power Party has taken to the streets—holding major rallies in cities like Busan—to protest a controversial special counsel bill targeting alleged prosecutorial abuses, while demanding the resumption of trial proceedings for President Lee. In response, the Democratic Party condemned the street demonstrations as an abandonment of legislative duty during a critical budget review season. Tensions also remain elevated over legal proceedings stemming from the aftermath of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's impeachment and imprisonment, with the ruling party warning against the release of key figures involved in the past insurrection crisis.

With the November 19 plenary session serving as a critical threshold, parliament has very little time before the December 2 constitutional deadline to pass the 2025 budget. Unless both sides find common ground between fiscal restraint and strategic economic investment, South Korea risks missing its deadline, leaving essential economic and welfare programs in limbo for the coming year.
