Packing for the Camino: Why Buying Your Gear in Spain Beats Pre-Trip Prep

September 07, 2026

  • The core rule: Keeping your backpack under 7kg (about 15 lbs) is essential, as packs exceeding this weight dramatically spike your risk of injury within the first three days.
  • The strategy: Skip expensive full-kit purchases at home; picking up budget-friendly gear at local European outposts like Decathlon saves baggage weight and lets you dial in the exact fit.
  • The gear priorities: A 40-liter pack with a proper hip belt, weather-appropriate layering, and two-sizes-too-big hiking boots matter far more than brand names or extra luxuries.
  • The takeaway: Santiago de Compostela pilgrimage routes punish overpacking, and true comfort on the trail comes from listening to your body rather than filling your bag with "just in case" items.

The 40-Liter Sweet Spot and Local Sourcing

Preparing for a 40-day trek across Spain is less about anticipation and more about survival logistics. The single biggest mistake pilgrims make is overloading their packs. Statistically, the moment a backpack tips past 7kg, the probability of suffering knee or ankle injuries skyrockets to 90%. That is not an exaggeration—it is the hard reality faced by countless walkers who limp off the trail within the first week.

Buying gear locally in Madrid at Decathlon—a ubiquitous French sporting goods retailer popular across Europe—proves far more efficient than hauling heavy equipment through international flights. A standard 40-liter pack (costing roughly €70, or about $75) hits the exact threshold required for long-distance walking. Anything smaller leaves you short; anything larger invites dangerous overpacking. Using Decathlon's in-store pickup and sizing checks ensures that the hip belt actually rests on your pelvis rather than your waist, transferring the load safely to your legs instead of crushing your shoulders.

Footwear, Layering, and the Reality of the Trail

On the Camino, your feet dictate your fate. Seasoned walkers universally advise buying hiking boots two sizes larger than normal to accommodate inevitable swelling over 7 to 8 hours of daily trekking. Paired with thick wool socks and a generous coating of Vaseline to prevent friction, proper footwear separates a spiritual journey from a miserable forced march. Leave sandals as a backup camp shoe; trying to navigate Spain's punishing stone and gravel paths without proper sole support is a rookie error.

Weather management also demands a minimalist, functional approach. Mornings start freezing, while afternoons bake under the Spanish sun. Packing a tiny, fist-sized packable down vest (around €15) alongside a lightweight, water-resistant windbreaker solves the temperature swing without adding bulk. Ditch heavy cotton shirts in favor of cheap, fast-drying synthetic layers that can be rinsed out in a pilgrim hostel sink and dried by morning. Ultimately, the trail strips away non-essentials, proving that the best gear is the weight you refuse to carry.
