Cracks in the Foundation: Progressive Leaders Abandon President Lee Jae-myung

October 06, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung’s administration is facing a deepening political crisis as key intellectual supporters publicly withdraw their backing, citing a fundamental loss of the reformist values that brought the current government to power.

  • High-profile defection: Professor Choi Bae-geun, a prominent voice in the liberal camp, has formally retracted his support for President Lee, accusing the administration of abandoning its reform mandate.
  • Plummeting approval: Recent polling from Realmeter shows disapproval hitting 59.8%, with approval stuck at 37.4%, signaling a collapse in core support.
  • Internal unrest: Disillusionment is spreading through online political communities, with supporters increasingly expressing a sense of "betrayal" over the administration's policy failures.
  • Widening rift: The divergence between the administration’s social media-based communication strategy and the expectations of the base is eroding the unity of the Democratic Party, South Korea's main liberal opposition party.

A Crisis of Credibility

The defection of Professor Choi—who once vowed to be the first to criticize the president if promises were not kept—is more than a symbolic blow. It represents a "cracking of the facade" within the progressive camp. For many, the sense of betrayal stems from the belief that the president has fundamentally changed his direction since taking office. The feeling among long-term supporters is not merely disappointment in specific policies, but a existential realization that the "reform" they campaigned for is no longer on the table.

The administration’s reliance on social media signals—such as the president retweeting videos from figures like author Rhyu Si-min—is being received by the public as an out-of-touch response to a real-world emergency. By failing to address the substantive grievances of their base, the president’s team is effectively isolating itself. The polling data, particularly the low "very satisfied" rating among core Democratic Party supporters, suggests that the administration has moved beyond a temporary slump into a structural crisis of confidence.

The Road Ahead: A Reckoning

As the National Assembly prepares for upcoming government audits, the administration is running out of time to pivot. Critics argue that the time for diagnostics and internal debates has passed; the current situation demands an acknowledgment of failure and a drastic shift in strategy.

The danger for the Democratic Party is that this internal fracture could lead to broader political irrelevance. When core supporters, who once acted as a shield for the party, begin to call for the search for "new leaders" rather than blindly defending the status quo, the electoral consequences could be severe. The upcoming legislative sessions will be a litmus test for whether the administration can reclaim its reformist identity or if it will continue its slide into political isolation.
