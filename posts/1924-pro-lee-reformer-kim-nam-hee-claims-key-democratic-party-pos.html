Pro-Lee Reformer Kim Nam-hee Claims Key Democratic Party Post in Internal Election Sweep

November 04, 2026

First-term lawmaker Kim Nam-hee has been elected head of the National Women’s Committee of the Democratic Party of Korea, South Korea’s ruling liberal party, solidifying President Lee Jae-myung’s grip on internal party power.

  • What happened: Rep. Kim Nam-hee defeated two-term lawmaker Moon Jung-bok in a high-profile internal vote to lead the Democratic Party’s women’s committee.
  • Broader sweep: Elections concluded for 10 national committee chairs, placing five active lawmakers—including Rep. Kim Dong-ah for youth and Rep. Moon Geum-ju for farmers and fishermen—into top organizational posts.
  • Factional shift: Kim’s victory over Moon, who was backed by factional ally Rep. Jung Chung-rae, marks a clear triumph for figures closely aligned with President Lee over traditional seniority and internal networks.
  • What it changes: The election outcome tightens ruling party unity behind the administration and increases committee influence over candidate nominations ahead of upcoming local elections.

Consolidating Party Unity Behind President Lee

The election was widely viewed within South Korea as a proxy battle between factions directly loyal to President Lee Jae-myung and those tied to prominent party figure Rep. Jung Chung-rae. Kim’s victory as a first-term lawmaker underscores a clear decision by party members to prioritize reform credentials and direct alignment with President Lee’s governing philosophy over traditional seniority. By securing key posts across internal organizations, pro-Lee figures have reinforced a unified party structure behind the presidential office.

With sitting lawmakers claiming half of the 10 national committee positions—including Rep. Min Byung-dug taking the small business committee and Rep. Im Mi-ae leading the senior citizens committee—the Democratic Party has elevated these posts into policy-driving roles. This structure is intended to tie party grassroots organizations directly to parliamentary activity, accelerating legislative support for the administration's key priorities.

Focus on Reform Agenda and Election Readiness

As the newly elected Women’s Committee Chair, Kim Nam-hee has committed to building a system that expands women's political participation and strengthens policy implementation for vulnerable groups. The newly formed committee leadership will also hold significant influence over candidate nomination rules for the 2026 nationwide local elections, ensuring that the party apparatus remains aligned with President Lee's core platform.

This internal consolidation comes as the ruling party works to counter political attacks from the main opposition conservative group, the People Power Party. With the party focused on dismantling the policy remnants of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who was impeached and is currently imprisoned—a disciplined and unified organization under President Lee gives the ruling party the momentum needed to pursue its legislative and social reform agenda.
