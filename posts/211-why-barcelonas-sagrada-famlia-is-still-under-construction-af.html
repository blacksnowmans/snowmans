Why Barcelona’s Sagrada Família Is Still Under Construction After 140 Years

September 07, 2026

Antoni Gaudí’s masterwork in Barcelona challenges the modern obsession with efficiency, remaining unfinished more than a century after construction began in 1882.

  • The timeline: While officials eye 2026 for structural completion to mark the centenary of Gaudí's death, full exterior detailing is expected to drag on until 2036.
  • The bureaucracy: The basilica operated without official city building permits for 137 years before finally settling the violation with local authorities.
  • The scale: The monument drew over 4.87 million visitors last year, fueling a booming tourism economy that simultaneously strains the site's original purpose.
  • The takeaway: The enduring appeal of the Sagrada Família lies not in rapid output, but in the uncompromising patience of its creation.

Defying the Cult of Efficiency

We live in an era obsessed with deadlines, performance bonuses, and quick returns. Against that backdrop, the Sagrada Família stands as a deliberate provocation. Started in 1882, the basilica was conceived on a human scale that stretches far beyond a single lifetime, echoing Gaudí’s famous philosophy that his client—God—was not in a hurry. The irony that this monumental achievement operated as an illegal building for nearly 140 years highlights the tense gap between rigid modern municipal codes and visionary art.

Yet behind the romance of the stonework lies a heavier human toll. The soaring spires and stained-glass forests rely on the daily sweat of artisans and stonecutters, raising uncomfortable questions about whose sacrifices subsidize architectural beauty. Furthermore, surging visitor numbers threaten to turn a sacred sanctuary into a crowded tourist trap, trading quiet contemplation for endless lines and camera clicks.

The True Legacy of Antoni Gaudí

Gaudí’s life ended in tragic obscurity when he was struck by a tram in Barcelona, dressed so poorly that passersby mistook him for a vagrant. He poured every resource into a building that would outlive him, leaving behind a monument shaped by gravity rather than straight lines. In an age of quick policy fixes and rushed infrastructure, the unfinished basilica offers a rare counter-narrative: that the integrity of the process matters far more than a hurried finish line.
