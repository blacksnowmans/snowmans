South Korea’s Supreme Court Sparks Political Storm Over Fast-Tracked Relief for Ex-PM Han Duck-soo

November 20, 2026

South Korea’s Supreme Court has triggered fierce backlash from legal analysts and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea after partially overturning the insurrection charges against former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo in an unprecedented nine-day review.

  • The Ruling: Under Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, the Supreme Court remanded Han's case with partial acquittal intent, reasoning that recognizing martial law as unconstitutional does not automatically prove an intent to commit insurrection.
  • The Legal Dispute: Legal critics, including attorneys Park Pan-gyu and Jeong Pil-seung, argue the verdict fractures legal precedent established during the 1996 trial of former military dictator Chun Doo-hwan by fragmenting unified, coordinated acts into isolated procedural violations.
  • Impeachment Push: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), led by figures such as Representative Park Ju-min, is pursuing the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho, citing the rushed verdict, failure to fulfill duties regarding justice nominations, and personal expense card misuse.
  • Broader Fallout: The ruling establishes a restrictive standard that critics warn will soften upcoming insurrection trials of other senior officials from the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, such as former interior minister Lee Sang-min and former justice minister Park Sung-jae.

Judicial Self-Preservation Under Scrutiny

Critics cited in progressive and legal circles contend that the Supreme Court set an impossibly narrow evidentiary standard to shield the judiciary from broader culpability. Shin Jang-sik, representative of the minor opposition Rebuilding Korea Party, argued the bench constructed a convenient exit: conceding that the emergency decree was illegal, yet claiming officials failed to recognize it as an insurrection. Commentators have also pointed to stark disparities in judicial rigor, contrasting the comprehensive evidence accepted in the four-year sentence handed to Kim Keon-hee for stock manipulation against the fractured standards applied to Han and the recent acquittal of political broker Myung Tae-kyun.

Clash Over Court Reform and Executive Oversight

The swift nine-day decision has galvanized the DPK to target structural overhauls of the judiciary. Party leaders are linking the Chief Justice’s conduct—including alleged ethics violations regarding luxury spending—to long-standing grievances over the National Court Administration, the powerful administrative body governing court personnel and budgetary power. With the ruling party preparing to push reform legislation and an impeachment vote ahead of an upcoming national judges' conference, the decision has escalated from an appellate review into an existential clash over accountability and judicial independence in post-crisis Korea.
