Why Autonomous Vehicles Will Redesign South Korea’s Cities

September 07, 2026

As South Korea pushes toward fully driverless vehicles by 2027, the real revolution won’t be on the highway — it will be the way our cities are physically rebuilt around the human scale.

    What happened: South Korea is advancing rapidly toward Level 3 and Level 4 autonomous vehicle commercialization, with autonomous tech moving from a driver-assist feature to a core driver of urban planning. The spatial shift: Self-driving and shared mobility require much narrower lanes than traditional car infrastructure, potentially freeing up enormous ground-level space in cities like Seoul, where roads currently take up about 13% of total land area. The human stakes: Experts argue that reclaiming these spaces for pedestrians and green zones offers a vital tool to combat depopulation, build "compact cities" with 15-minute neighborhood access, and fix car-dominated urban designs.

For decades, urban design has been dictated by the spatial demands of the automobile, sacrificing ground floors to parking lots and dividing communities with elevated infrastructure. Autonomous vehicles offer a rare chance to reset this hierarchy. By reducing lane widths and pushing parking underground, cities can return valuable land to everyday public life. Dense, vertical cities like Busan — whose compressed topography has long fascinated architects — are uniquely positioned to leverage smart mobility, provided planners focus on human-centric spaces rather than just technological speed.

Yet, rushing into a driverless future without robust social infrastructure risks deepening mobility divides. As seen in global testing data, autonomous systems remain imperfect, and local streets are notoriously unpredictable. Relying solely on insurance reforms or technological optimism misses the point. The ultimate goal of autonomous transit shouldn't simply be getting people somewhere faster, but slowing down our immediate environments so that spaces once lost to traffic can be reclaimed for human connection.
