Veteran Korean Actress Shim Hye-jin Reflects on 1,450 Kilometers of Pilgrim Trails

September 07, 2026

Veteran South Korean actress Shim Hye-jin has shared profound personal insights after walking a combined 1,450 kilometers across Spain's Camino de Santiago and the Portuguese Way, trading her familiar daily routine for a grueling test of endurance in her late 50s.

  • The challenge: Shim walked the 800km Camino de Santiago and 650km Portuguese Way, carrying a heavy backpack weighing up to 20kg filled with "just in case" items.
  • The takeaway: Physical exhaustion forced her to drop unnecessary gear, mirroring how people carry excessive psychological anxiety through daily life.
  • The human element: The journey reshaped her views on relationships, teaching her the value of quiet companionship, non-verbal empathy, and breaking down social prejudices by simply talking to strangers.
  • The philosophy: She concluded that kindness and letting go of perfectionism are essential tools for a happier life, rather than mere strategies for surviving a long trek.

The Heavy Burden of Over-Preparation

When Shim first packed for her pilgrimage, she fell into the trap of textbook preparation, letting anxiety dictate her luggage. Hauling a 15-to-20-kilogram pack left her struggling with muscle pain and physical exhaustion while other travelers breezed past with lighter bags. It was only when physical limits forced her to start shedding items that she realized her physical load was an exact metaphor for her mental worries. The trial taught her that the pain we experience is rarely external; rather, it is self-inflicted by the weight of unwarranted anxieties we refuse to put down.

This minimalist epiphany extended directly into how she handled human connection on the road. Surrounded by fellow travelers, Shim discovered the art of healthy boundaries—learning that a simple pair of earbuds could signal a need for solitude without rejecting human warmth. In a hyper-connected modern world where we fear isolation, the pilgrimage reminded her that true relationships thrive only when individual pacing and personal space are fully respected.

Confronting the Self and Practicing Kindness

Walking hundreds of miles alone ultimately forced Shim to confront her own internal darkness and take responsibility for interpersonal friction instead of blaming others. By stepping outside her comfort zone and initiating conversations—such as boldly greeting a high school junior she recognized on the trail—she realized that fear of rejection often builds invisible walls around us. Language barriers faded into the background, replaced by universal gestures like smiles, hugs, and a supportive pat on the shoulder.

Ultimately, Shim’s grueling trek transformed into an exercise in radical self-reflection and proactive kindness. Her desire to walk the trails again stems from a simple, hard-earned truth: kindness is not merely something you give to others, but an essential mechanism to make your own life happier. For readers weighed down by the stresses of daily life, her journey serves as a timely reminder to open up the heavy backpack of modern anxieties, drop what isn't necessary, and embrace a lighter pace.
