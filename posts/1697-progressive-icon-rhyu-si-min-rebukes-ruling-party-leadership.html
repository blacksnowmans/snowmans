Progressive Icon Rhyu Si-min Rebukes Ruling Party Leadership, Urging Angry Base Not to Jump Ship

October 29, 2026

Influential progressive author and commentator Rhyu Si-min has delivered a sharp warning to the leadership of South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party, cautioning that procedural arrogance and factional maneuvering are alienating the party's own core base.

  • What happened: Grassroots outrage erupted after party leader Kim Min-seok oversaw controversial, last-minute convention rule changes to introduce preferential voting, only for a non-mainstream challenger to capture a stunning 46% of the vote in silent protest.
  • The data: A sentiment analysis by public opinion firm WinG Korea Consulting revealed that negative mentions of Kim outnumbered positive ones by roughly five to one, driven largely by traditional liberal online communities accusing him of prioritizing personal ambitions over state affairs.
  • The risk: Deepening internal friction has sparked talk of mass defections to minor progressive alternatives, most notably the Rebuilding Korea Party founded by former justice minister Cho Kuk.
  • The takeaway: Rhyu urged disaffected members to stay inside the Democratic Party and wait for their leverage to return, calling the party the only vessel capable of sustaining a ruling majority.

A Backlash Against Machine Politics

The controversy surrounding Party Leader Kim Min-seok goes far beyond routine factional friction. When party brass manipulated convention rules ahead of the vote, they severely damaged their procedural legitimacy. Despite the tilted playing field, rank-and-file members handed nearly half the ballots to an outsider candidate, delivering an unmistakable reprimand to the party elite.

Kim’s subsequent performance has done little to soothe these tensions. Public tracking data shows his attention index spiking to 86 while his favorability plunged to 17, dragged down by combative feuds with the conservative opposition People Power Party and an ill-conceived public assault demanding the resignation of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. Rather than advancing structural legal and social reforms, the leadership has generated noisy headlines that core supporters worry are eroding the standing of President Lee Jae-myung's administration.

Holding the Line Inside the 'Ocean Liner'

Rhyu’s intervention aims to stop a panic at the exits. To those considering jumping ship, Rhyu drew a sharp distinction between niche progressive groups and the governing party: while outfits like the Rebuilding Korea Party can serve as useful "icebreakers" or emergency lifeboats, only the Democratic Party is an ocean liner large enough to steer national policy and win national mandates. Burning the ship simply because one dislikes the current captain is, in his view, political self-defeat.

Crucially, Rhyu paired his appeal for patience with a blunt warning about the nature of executive power. Democratic leaders must not confuse dissent with treason or substitute private irritation for public governance. The rigorous democratic standards once used to hold the government accountable during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's term in office—which culminated in Mr. Yoon's historic impeachment, ouster, and current imprisonment—must apply with equal force to the ruling camp today. For rank-and-file members, Rhyu argues, the best path forward is to keep their party cards, hold the leadership accountable, and prepare for the moment their votes will once again set the course.
