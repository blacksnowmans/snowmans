South Korean Politics Descends Into Mud-Slinging Over Revived Covid-19 Lobbying Tapes

September 09, 2026

South Korean conservative politician Han Dong-hoon has reignited a political firestorm by releasing selective transcripts regarding an old, legally cleared lobbying scandal, sparking fierce partisan warfare.

    What happened: Han Dong-hoon, a high-profile conservative figure and former ruling party heavyweight, released audio transcripts from a 2021 investigation into alleged lobbying for a Covid-19 treatment trial involving a pharmaceutical firm. The tapes target opposition Democratic Party politicians. What it changes: The release has derailed political discourse, turning parliamentary confirmation hearings and judicial reviews into a bitter partisan brawl. Prosecutors have slapped a travel ban on the central lobbyist, while the conservative People Power Party pushes to summon witnesses to grill the opposition. Who is affected: Opposition lawmakers accused in the transcripts face reputational damage, the ruling and opposition blocks are locked in an escalating war of narratives, and the South Korean public is left wading through exhausted political theater instead of substantive governance.

The Anatomy of a Manufactured Scandal

The core of the controversy centers on a 2021 investigation into a failed Covid-19 drug developer and a lobbyist named Yang, who allegedly bragged about his political connections to push clinical trial approvals. Courts have already cleared key figures associated with the investments, ruling that financial transactions cited by accusers were legitimate business operations rather than broker kickbacks. Yet, critics note that political actors have conveniently discarded these legal acquittals, pulling out weaponized snippets of audio to cast broad aspersions on their rivals.

This tactic relies on what local critics call "salami tactics"—slicing up raw, unverified boasts from a self-aggrandizing lobbyist and feeding them piecemeal to the media to demonize opponents. The lobbyist in question did not limit his networking to a single side; records show he actively courted conservative politicians as well, receiving appointments from the conservative camp ahead of the 20th presidential election. Reducing a bipartisan opportunist into a partisan weapon exposes the superficiality driving modern political combat in Seoul.

Beyond the Tapes: The Cost of Perpetual Grievance Politics

What makes this episode particularly exhausting is its timing. At a time when economic pressures and policy challenges demand the full attention of lawmakers, political elites are fighting proxy wars over historical transcripts that failed to yield criminal indictments even under previous conservative administrations. Prosecutors who failed to secure charges years ago are now scrambling to justify past inaction, while politicians treat the public square as an arena for revenge.

Ultimately, this saga highlights a deepening sickness in South Korea's political culture. When personal ambition and strategic demonization replace policy debate, democracy itself pays the price. The public is left with manufactured scandals, bruised reputations, and a profound cynicism toward the political class.
