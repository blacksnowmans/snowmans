President Lee Jae-myung Appoints Civilian Justice Minister to Accelerate Prosecution Reform

November 07, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung has nominated Lee Yong-cheol, the current head of the state arms procurement agency, as South Korea’s new Minister of Justice, signaling a decisive move to remove the influence of career prosecutors from the ministry.

  • Lee Yong-cheol, currently head of the Defense Acquisition Program Administration (DAPA), is a former human rights lawyer and veteran of the judicial reforms under the late President Roh Moo-hyun.
  • The appointment is part of a broader reshuffle that includes naming former lawmaker Choi In-ho as Senior Secretary for Political Affairs and Ahn Deok-su as Commissioner of the National Tax Service.
  • The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main conservative opposition, has denounced the move as a "defense project" for the President, citing the nominee's past ties to the President's administration in Seongnam city.
  • The nomination aims to finalize the restructuring of the national criminal justice system, specifically the separation of investigative and prosecutorial powers.

A Strategic Break from Prosecutorial Tradition

The choice of Lee Yong-cheol is a deliberate departure from the tradition of appointing career prosecutors to lead the Ministry of Justice. By selecting a civilian administrator who belongs to Minbyun (Lawyers for a Democratic Society, a prominent liberal legal group), President Lee is doubling down on his promise to "civilianize" the ministry. This is a direct response to the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, whose presidency—ended by impeachment and subsequent imprisonment—was often criticized for functioning as a "prosecutorial republic."

Lee Yong-cheol’s background is unique; he has spent the recent months managing defense exports and procurement at DAPA. Moving from arms procurement to justice may seem unconventional, but the administration views him as an "administrative expert" capable of managing a resistant bureaucracy. His primary mandate is to ensure the stable implementation of the new investigative framework, which seeks to strip the Prosecution Service of its century-long monopoly on both investigating and indicting crimes.

Political Warfare and the 'Seongnam Line'

The opposition PPP has immediately signaled a scorched-earth policy for the upcoming confirmation hearings. They label the nominee as part of the "Seongnam Line," referring to the President’s former aides from his time as the Mayor of Seongnam. The opposition claims Lee was a "hidden architect" of the Daejang-dong land development project, a controversial real estate deal that has been a focal point of political and legal battles for years. They argue the appointment is a tactical move to have a loyalist oversee the withdrawal of pending charges against the President.

However, the administration frames the reshuffle as a push for institutional stability. Alongside Lee, the appointment of Choi In-ho—a veteran politician with deep ties to the liberal establishment—is intended to smooth over relations with the National Assembly. Meanwhile, the promotion of Ahn Deok-su at the National Tax Service suggests a desire for professional continuity in financial oversight. For President Lee, this trio represents the "final lineup" needed to cement his domestic reform agenda and protect his administration from what his supporters call "politically motivated" judicial overreach by remnants of the old guard.
