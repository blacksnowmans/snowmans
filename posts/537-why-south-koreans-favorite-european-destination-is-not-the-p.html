Why South Koreans' Favorite European Destination Is Not the Paradise It Seems

September 10, 2026

Spain has overtaken Switzerland as South Korea's top-rated overseas travel destination, driven by relatively reasonable prices and familiar culinary comforts, but beneath the romantic Mediterranean facade lies a harsh reality of soaring housing costs, petty crime, and exploited labor.

  • Cost over Scenery: Spain's lower price index compared to Switzerland has made it a top pick for budget-conscious Korean travelers facing economic pressure.
  • The Barcelona Housing Crisis: Local residents face extreme financial strain, with median monthly rents reaching about $1,400 (₩2 million) against average incomes of about $2,100 (₩3 million).
  • Rampant Petty Crime: Legal loopholes that treat thefts under about $430 (400 euros) as misdemeanors have made Barcelona a notorious hotspot for professional pickpockets.
  • Invisible Labor: The city's gig economy relies heavily on undocumented migrants trading hundreds of dollars a month to rent delivery accounts on apps like Glovo.

For years, Swiss Alps and pristine nature served as the ultimate European dream for South Korean tourists. But with Switzerland's exorbitant cost of living draining wallets, travelers have pivoted heavily toward Spain for its relative value, vibrant street life, and rich food culture that resonates with Korean palates. Yet, peel back the Instagram-friendly photos of the Sagrada Família and sun-drenched plazas, and you find a city grappling with the raw edges of modern European capitalism.

That friction is most visible in the housing market. While tourists debate whether a 21-euro seafood paella is worth the price, locals are caught in a grueling squeeze. Barcelona's housing prices and rents are among the highest in Spain, forcing many adults into shared housing. It has also birthed strange phenomena like Okupa—a legal gray area where unauthorized occupants can claim rights to a property if they go undetected for 48 hours—reflecting deep societal frustration with a housing system that leaves ordinary people behind.
