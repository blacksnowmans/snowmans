US Midterms on a Knife-Edge as Early Voting Surge Threatens Trump's Grip

October 29, 2026

  • Surging early voter turnout is boosting Democratic hopes and panicking Republicans, with the party struggling to defend even its safest seats and facing doubts over holding 51 Senate seats.
  • Donald Trump has signaled he may reject the election results unless he deems them fair, setting the stage for potential challenges and laying groundwork to protect his political survival.
  • Global right-wing ties are under the spotlight as Trump defends former Brazilian President Jair Bolsonaro following his defeat and departure to the US.

With the US midterm elections fast approaching, Washington politics are descending into chaos. What was once expected to be a comfortable Republican sweep has turned into a desperate fight for core districts, driven by a wave of early voting that has energized the Democrats. As the political establishment holds its breath, the stakes extend far beyond American borders, carrying direct implications for global markets, security, and South Korea's diplomatic posture under the current Lee Jae-myung administration.

True to form, Trump has leaned into aggressive rhetoric, doubling down on fiery attacks to rally his base while casually floating provocative ideas, such as suggesting Venezuela could become America's 51st state. Yet behind the bluster lies deep anxiety. Analysts view his conditional acceptance of the vote and attacks on the electoral system as preemptive armor against a devastating political defeat. Meanwhile, geopolitical flashpoints—including warnings of a potential strike on Iran and Trump's public boasting over his ties to Israeli leadership—illustrate how domestic political survival is once again dictating high-stakes foreign policy.

Global Ripples and Lessons for Seoul

For South Korea, the turbulence in Washington is a stark reminder of the fragile nature of international alliances when superpowers turn inward. As the US political landscape fractures ahead of the midterms, markets face maximum uncertainty, and foreign policy shifts risk rattling regional security. The spectacle of leaders like Bolsonaro fleeing prosecution only to be embraced by Trump highlights a troubling consolidation of global right-wing populism that rejects democratic norms when convenient.

In Seoul, the administration must navigate this shifting power dynamic with strategic independence. Whether Washington leans left or right, the core priority remains safeguarding South Korea's economic and security interests without relying blindly on shifting American political winds. As history and recent events—including the disgraced downfall of imprisoned former leader Yoon Suk-yeol—have shown, democratic resilience begins from within, requiring steady institutions capable of weathering external storms.
