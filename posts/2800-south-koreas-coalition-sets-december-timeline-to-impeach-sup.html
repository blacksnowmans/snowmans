South Korea's Coalition Sets December Timeline to Impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice

November 27, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea and the allied Rebuilding Korea Party plan to introduce an impeachment motion against Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae on December 3, targeting a final vote before the regular parliamentary session ends on December 9.

  • What is happening: Progressive lawmakers are pushing to impeach Chief Justice Cho, citing his conduct and decisions from two years ago during Yoon Suk-yeol's presidency.
  • The legislative squeeze: National Assembly rules mandate a vote between 24 and 72 hours after an impeachment bill is reported to the plenary session, overlapping directly with the December 2 statutory deadline for passing the national budget.
  • Friction inside the courts: While senior court officials decry the motion as an attack on judicial independence, rank-and-file judges are divided, highlighted by a senior judge at Changwon District Court who publicly voiced opposition to collective pushback by court leadership.
  • Who is driving it: Key lawmakers on the National Assembly’s Legislation and Judiciary Committee, including Park Joo-min and No Jong-myeon of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea and Kim Hyeong-yeon of the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party.

A Tight Legislative High-Wire Act

Introducing the motion on December 3 carries deliberate political symbolism by targeting Chief Justice Cho's actions from two years ago. However, symbolic dates come with strict procedural risks under National Assembly law. Once reported to the plenary floor, the 72-hour voting clock begins immediately. Managing this rigid timeframe alongside final negotiations over the national budget—which faces a statutory deadline of December 2—creates an intense scheduling crunch. If the coalition stumbles on legislative procedures or fails to hold the vote before the regular session closes on December 9, the drive for judicial accountability risks losing momentum to a strong counter-offensive from the main opposition People Power Party.

Institutional Fractures Inside the Bench

The impeachment drive has exposed severe cracks within South Korea’s legal establishment. Court leadership and chief judges have attempted to mobilize collective opposition under the banner of defending judicial independence. Yet, resistance from front-line magistrates demonstrates that the bench is far from united. The pushback against court administrators reflects growing self-reflection among judges over lost public trust stemming from compromised rulings during Mr. Yoon's tenure. For proponents of impeachment, removing Cho is not merely about holding one official accountable, but a critical prerequisite for dismantling systemic elitism and reforming the court administration.
