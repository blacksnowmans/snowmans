South Korea's Opposition Mobilizes 10,000 in Daegu, Escalating Clash Over President and Judiciary

November 22, 2026

According to a political briefing by Korean blog Blacksnowman, South Korea's main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), rallied roughly 10,000 supporters in its stronghold of Daegu on November 21 to demand President Lee Jae-myung's impeachment and oppose ruling-party bills targeting the judiciary and prosecution.

  • The conservative show of force: Rally organizers reported that 24 of the 25 lawmakers representing the Daegu and North Gyeongsang (TK) region attended, where PPP lawmaker Jang Dong-hyuk declared "impeachment is the only answer" and called for forcibly removing President Lee from the Blue House, South Korea's presidential office.
  • The ruling party's pushback: Lawmaker Han Byung-do of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) condemned the event as a stage for "crude rhetoric" that ignores people's livelihoods, while the DPK maintained that its proposed special counsel bill on "fabricated indictments" is needed to correct abuses of prosecutorial power.
  • High stakes for the courts: The PPP labeled the DPK's special counsel bill an unconstitutional "jailbreak law" for Lee and warned that the ruling party's push to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae amounts to an "insurrection" that threatens judicial independence.
  • Moving the fight to Seoul: Following the Daegu protest, PPP leaders and members marched toward the Government Complex Seoul, signaling a shift from parliamentary negotiation to a sustained street campaign aimed at pressuring the administration in the capital.

Competing Visions of Justice

At the heart of the standoff are directly conflicting views of legal accountability. The PPP insists that President Lee's suspended court trials must resume immediately, framing its street campaign as a defense of constitutional order. Conversely, the DPK contends that past prosecutorial indictments were manipulated, portraying both its special counsel bill and the proposed impeachment of Chief Justice Cho as legitimate steps toward judicial reform.

What This Means for Seoul Politics

Moving this legislative battle onto the streets carries clear political risks for both camps. For the PPP, rallying in Daegu reliably energizes traditional conservative supporters, yet reports of booing and shouting within the crowd point to internal unease over whether hardline rhetoric could alienate moderate and Seoul-area voters.

For the DPK, advancing a special counsel bill alongside an impeachment drive against the nation's top judge keeps its base mobilized, but exposes the administration to charges of legislative overreach. As the source commentary noted, this escalating cycle of impeachments and street protests leaves little room for parliamentary compromise—sidelining everyday livelihood issues while putting institutional stability to the test.
