Seoul Mayor and Conservative Opposition Join Forces in Budget Battle Against Lee Administration

November 07, 2026

The conservative People Power Party and the Seoul Metropolitan Government have declared a "one-team" alliance to challenge President Lee Jae-myung’s national budget proposal, signaling a fierce legislative battle over fiscal spending and local autonomy.

  • Strategic Coordination: Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon and leadership from the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition party, met on November 6 to synchronize their demands for the 2025 fiscal year.
  • Fiscal Gap: The PPP is advocating for a massive 925 trillion won (about $665 billion) national budget, significantly higher than the 821 trillion won (about $591 billion) proposed by the Lee Jae-myung administration.
  • Aggressive Rhetoric: Mayor Oh criticized the central government as "fiscal Nolbu"—a reference to a legendary greedy folk character—accusing the administration of starving the capital of necessary funds while offloading costs.
  • Local Burden: Seoul officials claim the city has been forced to absorb 3.6 trillion won (about $2.6 billion) in additional costs due to central government-led welfare expansion without corresponding federal support.

A Tactical Alliance Against the Blue House

The alignment between Mayor Oh Se-hoon and the PPP leadership is more than a routine administrative meeting; it is a calculated political maneuver to frame the Lee Jae-myung administration as fiscally irresponsible. By meeting three times in a single week, the two sides are building a narrative that the ruling Democratic Party is neglecting urban stability—specifically in housing and infrastructure—in favor of populist cash-transfer programs. For the PPP, Seoul’s administrative data provides the ammunition needed to attack the government’s budget during upcoming National Assembly audits.

Mayor Oh is particularly vocal about a 1 trillion won (about $720 million) shortfall between what Seoul requested and what the central government actually allocated in its proposal. By utilizing the "Nolbu" label—a character known for his greed and cruelty toward his family—he is tapping into a cultural trope of a wealthy, stingy entity that hoards resources. This rhetoric serves to shield the city government from blame regarding potential project delays, effectively shifting the political cost of fiscal strain onto the President’s desk.

The Road to the Next Election

Beyond the immediate budget figures, this "one-team" declaration highlights the shifting power dynamics within the conservative camp. With the conservative movement still reeling from the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, Mayor Oh Se-hoon has emerged as a primary conservative figurehead. His ability to command the party’s legislative support for Seoul’s budget reinforces his standing as a pragmatic leader capable of challenging the Lee administration’s policy direction.

However, this strategy carries risks. While the PPP criticizes the government's fiscal management, it is simultaneously demanding even higher total spending. This tension between calling for fiscal discipline and requesting more local funding may lead to accusations of inconsistency. For now, the opposition appears content to use the budget process as a platform for political resistance, prioritizing the weakening of the Lee administration’s momentum over bipartisan compromise.
