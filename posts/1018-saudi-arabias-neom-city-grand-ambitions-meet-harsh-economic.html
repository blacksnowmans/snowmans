Saudi Arabia’s Neom City: Grand Ambitions Meet Harsh Economic Realities

October 12, 2026

Saudi Arabia’s ambitious plan to build a futuristic, car-free desert metropolis is buckling under the weight of ballooning costs and logistical hurdles, casting doubt on the future of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman’s signature “Vision 2030” reform program.

  • What happened: Massive construction contracts for components of the Neom project—including the linear city "The Line" and the mountain resort "Trojena"—have been terminated, with projects scaled back significantly.
  • The cost: Initial budget estimates of $500 billion have proved wildly optimistic; internal projections suggest the cost for "The Line" alone could exceed $1 trillion.
  • The impact: International investors are growing wary as internal rates of return have plummeted, forcing Riyadh to shift focus from "prestige" projects to more practical industrial zones.

A Vision Under Pressure

At the heart of the crisis is the disconnect between Saudi Arabia’s top-down political structure and the realities of global finance. Because Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman personally chairs the Public Investment Fund (PIF) and oversees the Neom board, there is little room for local experts or foreign partners to suggest course corrections without challenging his authority. This centralization has led to "megaproject fatigue," where the state is struggling to balance the Neom budget while simultaneously funding the 2030 Riyadh Expo and the 2034 World Cup.

Construction giants like South Korea's Samsung C&T (the construction and trading arm of the Samsung conglomerate) and Hyundai E&C (a major Korean builder) were initially brought on for key infrastructure work. However, as the geopolitical climate remains volatile and oil revenues have failed to keep pace with the massive spending requirements, the Saudi government has had to prioritize survival over splendor.

From Fantasy to Feasibility

The project is not being abandoned, but it is undergoing a painful pivot. Riyadh is increasingly shifting its focus toward Oxagon, an industrial and logistics port city, which offers a more realistic path to economic return compared to the futuristic but economically questionable "The Line." Analysts note that while the dream of a post-oil economy remains the official goal, Saudi Arabia must now prove it can build functional industrial ecosystems rather than just headline-grabbing desert monuments.

Despite the setbacks, the "Vision 2030" strategy has achieved some success, with the non-oil sector now accounting for a record 54.8% of Saudi GDP. Whether this momentum can survive the financial hangover of the Neom experiment remains the defining question for the Kingdom’s economic future. The days of unlimited spending are over; Saudi Arabia is now entering a phase of cold, hard cost-benefit analysis.
