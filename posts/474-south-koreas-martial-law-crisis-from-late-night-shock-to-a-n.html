South Korea’s Martial Law Crisis: From Late-Night Shock to a New Presidency

September 30, 2026

South Korea’s democracy survived its most severe test in decades between late 2024 and mid-2025, moving from a sudden presidential martial law declaration to impeachment, removal from office, and a snap election.

    What happened: Then-President Yoon Suk Yeol declared emergency martial law on December 3, 2024, sending armed troops into the National Assembly, before lawmakers unanimously voted it down hours later. The fallout: After an initial failed impeachment vote and massive nationwide protests, a second impeachment motion passed easily on December 14, 2024. The resolution: The Constitutional Court unanimously upheld the impeachment and removed Yoon in April 2025, paving the way for a presidential election in June won by opposition leader Lee Jae-myung. Who is affected: Foreign investors and international observers watching South Korea's political stability, as well as citizens navigating a deeply polarized domestic landscape.

A Midnight Crisis and the Resilience of the National Assembly

The sequence of events that began on the night of December 3, 2024, stunned a country that thought it had long moved past authoritarian rule. When Yoon announced martial law under the pretext of eradicating "anti-state forces," military helicopters landed on the grounds of the National Assembly and armed soldiers attempted to enter the building. In response, ordinary citizens rushed to the legislature, scaling walls alongside lawmakers to block the military.

That immediate pushback proved decisive. Under the leadership of National Assembly Speaker Woo Won-shik, lawmakers broke through the blockade and unanimously passed a resolution demanding the immediate lifting of martial law. Facing total political isolation and mounting public pressure, Yoon backed down within hours and rescinded the order. Yet the attempt left deep scars, immediately triggering widespread demands for accountability and formal impeachment proceedings.

From Impeachment to a New Political Era

The path to removing Yoon from office was not immediate. A first impeachment vote on December 7, 2024, collapsed when ruling party lawmakers boycotted the vote, failing to meet the quorum. That failure brought millions of protesters into the streets in sub-freezing temperatures, from Seoul to regional hubs like Daegu, Busan, and Gwangju, demanding the president's resignation. Realizing the political cost of inaction, the legislature returned on December 14 and overwhelmingly passed a second impeachment motion.

The constitutional process culminated on April 4, 2025, when the Constitutional Court issued a unanimous ruling officially removing Yoon from office, ruling that his martial law decree was a grave violation of the constitution. In the subsequent snap presidential election held on June 3, 2025, liberal opposition candidate Lee Jae-myung won with 49.42% of the vote and was sworn in the next day. The crisis demonstrated both the fragility of executive power under South Korea's constitutional framework and the immense resilience of its public in defending democratic norms.
