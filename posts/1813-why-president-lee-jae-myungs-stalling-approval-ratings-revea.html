Why President Lee Jae-myung’s Stalling Approval Ratings Reveal a Deep Crisis for South Korea’s Progressives

November 01, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s stalling approval ratings and the ruling Democratic Party’s political dilemma stem from a fundamental miscalculation: confusing a fortuitous stock market boom and a mistaken quest for centrist appeal with genuine political skill.

  • What happened: Despite coming to power following the removal of former President Yoon Suk Yeol, President Lee secured only 49.42% of the vote in the snap election—failing to cross the 50% majority threshold.
  • The market illusion: A massive global semiconductor and AI boom sent the country's KOSPI stock index soaring by 60% to 70%, which the administration mistakenly credited to its own pragmatic policies rather than external luck.
  • Who is affected: Long-standing core progressive and working-class supporters, who have watched key reform promises get sidelined while the administration pivots toward conservative-leaning market tactics and populism.

The Dangerous Myth of Centrist Expansion

Winning power in the wake of the political turmoil surrounding Yoon Suk Yeol’s ouster, the liberal Democratic Party expected a sweeping mandate. Yet President Lee’s 49.42% share of the vote was barely higher than his 2022 election performance and failed to top the combined opposition tallies. Rather than recognizing this as a lingering warning from working-class voters who have seen little structural change over the past two decades of liberal rule, the administration diagnosed the problem as a failure to woo moderate and conservative voters.

This diagnosis led to a fateful tactical shift. Convinced that an economic bounce driven by the global tech and semiconductor cycle was proof of successful centrist policymaking, the administration put crucial reform pledges—including prosecutorial overhauls and steps to dismantle entrenched inequality—on the back burner. Believing that their core base had nowhere else to go, leaders sidelined the very people who brought them to power, leaving working-class families feeling betrayed and taken for granted.

Abandoning Core Values for Populist Temptations

As approval ratings stagnated and political momentum slowed down, the administration began leaning into troubling political tactics. In an effort to court younger voters, policy has become hyper-focused on boosting the stock market, while broader labor conditions and the struggles of middle-aged working families are overlooked. More alarmingly, critics note that the administration has occasionally flirted with anti-immigrant and exclusionary messaging to recapture disaffected demographics—borrowing pages directly from the far-right playbook.

This is a profound departure from the foundational progressive ideals of protecting the vulnerable and challenging the powerful. When a reformist government trades its core principles for short-term political maneuvering and transactional pragmatism, it risks hollowing out its own political identity. Without a return to genuine welfare reforms and solidarity with society's weakest, the current administration risks alienating its foundational base completely and opening the door for conservative opposition forces to make a political comeback.
