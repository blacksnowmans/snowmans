South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party Fractures as Leaked Audio Triggers Factional Warfare

November 15, 2026

An internal ethics probe into ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) supreme council member Choi Min-hee over leaked comments regarding President Lee Jae-myung’s past stance on impeaching Yoon Suk-yeol has exploded into an all-out factional war within the ruling party.

  • What happened: Leaked audio revealed Choi claiming President Lee once opposed impeaching Yoon Suk-yeol; Choi called it a slip of the tongue, but pro-Lee factions immediately demanded her expulsion from the party.
  • Internal escalation: Three reformist supreme council members boycotted official leadership meetings and held a joint press conference demanding an ethics investigation into DPK leader Kim Min-seok instead, accusing him of conducting a targeted purge.
  • Opposition offensive: The main opposition People Power Party (PPP), led by Han Dong-hoon, seized on the ruling party’s infighting and mutual allegations of religious group ties to demand a special counsel investigation.
  • Who is affected: The ruling party's highest decision-making body has been effectively paralyzed, causing the DPK's public approval rating to fall by nearly five percentage points.

A Flashpoint Over Impeachment Legitimacy

The controversy began when audio surfaced showing Choi Min-hee claiming at a local party meeting that President Lee Jae-myung had initially opposed the impeachment of Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president who was ousted from power and subsequently imprisoned. Choi swiftly backtracked, stating she had accidentally misspoken while trying to explain the party's past strategy regarding former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo. However, misrepresenting President Lee's role in the impeachment of Yoon Suk-yeol—the foundational event that paved the way for the current administration—struck a deep nerve. The pro-Lee mainstream, spearheaded by external groups like the Deohminjoo Gyeonggi Innovation Council, launched fierce protests outside Choi's constituency office. The rift deepened further after President Lee reportedly expressed his displeasure late at night, remarking that "arrows flying from behind hurt the most."

Leadership Paralysis and Opposition Exploitation

Rather than containing the damage, DPK leader Kim Min-seok's decision to launch an ethics inspection into Choi pushed the leadership into open revolt. Three reformist supreme council members—Choi Min-hee, Han Min-soo, and Lee Seong-yun—boycotted official proceedings and launched a scathing counterattack. They demanded an ethics probe into Kim himself while both sides traded toxic, unverified allegations regarding ties to controversial religious groups like Shincheonji and the Unification Church. This public spectacle has handed the opposition PPP a massive political opening, allowing them to push for special prosecutor probes into the ruling party's internal disclosures. With internal trust broken and public approval dropping rapidly, the DPK faces a severe leadership crisis that threatens to derail its legislative agenda.
