South Korea’s Housing Market Signals Downturn as Ruling Party Fractures Over Real Estate Taxes

November 13, 2026

South Korea’s housing market is showing clear signs of an impending downturn as inventory piles up and sales slump, triggering sharp political fractures within President Lee Jae-myung’s administration over real estate tax reform.

  • What happened: Land, Infrastructure and Transport Minister Hong Ji-sun declared that surging property listings paired with plunging transaction volumes mark a clear turn toward a falling market, with price drops already expanding across Seoul's affluent Gangnam, Seocho, and Songpa districts.
  • What it changes: Growing voter backlash has ignited a policy rift inside the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, where metropolitan lawmakers are demanding relief on major property taxes against the wishes of party leadership.
  • Who is affected: Homeowners face declining property valuations amid high interest rates, while Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon of the conservative People Power Party, South Korea's main opposition party, is stepping in with city-level subsidies for young renters.

A Stalled Market Masked as Stabilization

Land Minister Hong Ji-sun frames the combination of growing property listings and frozen transaction volumes as evidence that heavy regulatory targeting and elevated interest rates are cooling down the market. However, a drop in transaction volume is not the same as genuine market stabilization. With prospective buyers priced out by high borrowing costs and sellers slow to lower asking prices on mid-tier homes, the market is experiencing structural stagnation rather than improved affordability for average citizens.

Political Fractures and Opposition Offensives

This stagnation has turned real estate into an urgent political liability for President Lee Jae-myung. Lawmakers within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea who represent competitive metropolitan districts are breaking ranks with leadership, calling for revisions to the Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax and the Reconstruction Excess Profit Recapture System. Though Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok has attempted to quash the rebellion in the name of policy consistency, voter frustration is already impacting public opinion. In a recent National Barometer Survey (NBS), the opposition People Power Party pulled ahead of the ruling party in housing policy trust (34% to 29%), while President Lee’s approval rating slipped to 44%.

Capitalizing on the discord, Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon of the People Power Party labeled the central government’s strict mandatory residency rules a "policy catastrophe" that choked off rental supply and drove up costs. By pledging municipal subsidies for youth rent and utility bills, Mayor Oh is positioning a supply-side alternative against the administration's regulation-heavy approach, setting up housing as a decisive battleground ahead of upcoming political contests.
