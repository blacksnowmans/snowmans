Korean Jazz Vanguard: The Boundary-Pushing Sound of Soojin Coloris Trio

October 16, 2026

South Korean jazz outfit Soojin Coloris Trio is redefining contemporary instrumental music in Seoul, blending rigorous composition with unconventional techniques that challenge mainstream conventions.

  • The unit: Formed around 2019 by drummer-composer Seo Soo-jin alongside pianist Kang Jaehun and bassist Kim Younghoo—all veterans of US jazz programs—the group gained early recognition abroad before winning Best Jazz Crossover and Best Performance at the Korean Music Awards, the country's leading critic-led music honors.
  • The technique: Rather than serving as a standard backing rhythm section, Seo expands the drum kit's tonal palette into melodic territory—even applying an arco string bow directly to percussion—while Kang and Kim anchor the complex arrangements.
  • The repertoire: Their recent showcase pairs tension-heavy conceptual pieces like "UNCERTAINTY III – BEGINNINGS AND ENDINGS," written during the COVID-19 pandemic to mirror life's lack of clear resolution, with "TEAR DOWN A WALL (FOR BRION)," a lush tribute to American film composer Jon Brion (known for Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind and Lady Bird).
  • The trajectory: With seven years of collaboration behind the core members, the trio demonstrates that South Korea's niche instrumental scene can produce uncompromising art that earns critical acclaim without diluting its complexity.

Deconstructing Tradition with Bows and Brushes

The standard jazz piano trio is one of the most thoroughly explored formats in modern music, making genuine innovation difficult. Soojin Coloris Trio bypasses predictable formulas by treating the trio not as soloist-plus-rhythm, but as an integrated, breathing organism. Seo's deliberate recruitment of Kang and Kim centered on finding musicians capable of sharing a micro-level sense of time while tolerating structural ambiguity. When Seo replaces drumsticks with an arco bow or brushes, the percussion sheds its purely rhythmic duties and assumes a textural, almost orchestral weight.

From Existential Tension to Cinematic Romance

The contrast between the trio’s flagship compositions highlights their technical range. In "UNCERTAINTY III," sharp rhythmic fractures from the piano and aggressive shifts between pizzicato and bowed bass force the listener to abandon conventional expectations of melodic resolution. The piece intentionally avoids clear beginnings and clean finishes—a deliberate reflection of post-pandemic precarity where audiences are invited to ride the wave of the groove rather than intellectualize it.

By contrast, their Jon Brion tribute demonstrates that technical complexity does not preclude emotional warmth. Switching to brushed snare and sweeping harmonic progressions reminiscent of an upscale rooftop lounge, the group captures the melancholy, vintage lyricism characteristic of Brion's film scores. In an era dominated by algorithmic pop production, Soojin Coloris Trio offers a compelling reminder of the power of raw, human interplay on the Korean music landscape.
