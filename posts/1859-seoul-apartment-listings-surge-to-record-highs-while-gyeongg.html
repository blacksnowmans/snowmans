Seoul Apartment Listings Surge to Record Highs While Gyeonggi Tech Belt Booms

November 02, 2026

Seoul’s apartment inventory has jumped nearly 30% to over 78,000 listings as high asking prices and temporary leasing gluts cool buyer demand, creating a sharp divide with the booming semiconductor hubs in neighboring Gyeonggi Province.

  • What happened: Apartment listings across Seoul grew 29.5% between early August and late October to exceed 78,000 properties, with outlying districts like Seongbuk-gu seeing inventory surges as high as 55%.
  • What it changes: Leverage in Seoul has shifted decisively to buyers as a wave of 6,000 new housing completions in prime Gangnam softened lease prices, triggering price cuts of up to 1 billion KRW (about $740,000) in ultra-luxury complexes like Apgujeong.
  • Who is affected: While Seoul cools, tech workers in Gyeonggi Province cities like Gunpo and Uiwang are driving weekly price gains above 0.5%, fueled by record semiconductor earnings and generous corporate housing loans.

The Great Divergence: Freezing Capital vs. Booming Tech Hubs

Data from Asil, a Korean real estate big-data platform, highlights a sudden shift in market momentum across the Seoul capital region. In Seoul, seller panic is mounting as inventory piles up across 19 of the city's 25 districts. The chill began in high-end Gangnam—where 6,000 newly completed apartments flooded the lease market and knocked down rental rates—and quickly cascaded outward to mid-tier and peripheral neighborhoods. Budget-friendly districts such as Geumcheon and Dobong are the rare exceptions, where an acute shortage of jeonse (Korea's unique lump-sum lease deposit system) is forcing frustrated renters into "survival buying."

Just south of the capital, a completely different market is unfolding. Cities along Gyeonggi Province's semiconductor corridor—such as Gunpo and Uiwang—are logging weekly price surges near 0.5%, an annualized pace of over 24%. This rally is backed by massive corporate liquidity. Memory giants SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics have generated massive quarterly profits, and Samsung now offers its staff internal housing loans at a 1.5% interest rate for up to 500 million KRW (about $370,000), exempt from standard debt-service ratio (DSR) caps. This corporate backing has created a localized bull market completely detached from Seoul's stagnation.

Policy Shifts and Strategic Buyers

The current correction reflects broader policy shifts under President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, which is focusing on long-term supply stabilization and industrial support, steering away from the market-wide deregulation seen during Yoon Suk-yeol's term. Because new public housing initiatives will take years to deliver actual units, analysts view Seoul’s inventory pileup as a temporary breather caused by localized lease surpluses rather than a structural collapse.

For prospective buyers, gridlock in Seoul offers rare leverage to negotiate terms and select prime units without facing bidding wars. Once Gangnam's temporary lease surplus is absorbed, underlying supply deficits in the capital are likely to reassert control over pricing.
