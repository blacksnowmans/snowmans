The Art of Packing for the Camino de Santiago

September 07, 2026

For many, the Camino de Santiago is a spiritual odyssey; for those preparing to walk it, it is a high-stakes logistics operation where every gram counts. A recent video from a Korean content creator preparing for the 30-day trek reveals that the true challenge of the pilgrimage begins not at the airport, but in the grueling, analytical process of packing.

  • The Weight Dilemma: The creator’s pack weighed in at nearly 12kg (about 26 lbs), far exceeding the recommended threshold for long-distance walking.
  • Survival Essentials: Foot care is treated with surgical precision, featuring toe-socks and high-performance footwear like the Hoka Kaha 2 ($225, or about 300,000 KRW).
  • Digital Compliance: New South Korean aviation regulations now require all lithium batteries to be packed in clear plastic bags for security screening.
  • Cultural Comforts: The inclusion of Korean staples like chili paste (gochujang) and instant ramen seasoning highlights the psychological need for a taste of home.

The Physics of a Light Pack

The creator’s approach highlights a recurring tension in travel: the struggle between functionality and the "what if" anxiety that leads to overpacking. By opting for "all-in-one" soap and solid toothpaste, the traveler prioritizes minimalism, yet still concedes that carrying luxuries—like specific Korean seasonings—is a calculated risk. The consensus among experienced trekkers is that "if you can live without it, leave it behind," as every extra kilogram exerts a heavy toll on the knees and ankles over the uneven terrain of the Spanish countryside.

What makes this preparation particularly insightful is the shift from "fashion" to "utility." The selection of technical gear, such as moisture-wicking joggers and specialized windbreakers, is designed for a climate where four seasons can occur in a single day. The creator emphasizes that items like down jackets aren't just for warmth, but function as makeshift sleeping bag liners in drafty albergues (pilgrim hostels).

Digital Discipline and Human Connection

Modern pilgrims cannot entirely disconnect, but they must manage their tech with care. Beyond the strict compliance with airline battery safety rules, the creator’s decision to pack a wired pair of earphones as a backup to Bluetooth devices serves as a poignant reminder: in the digital age, analog redundancies are essential. This is not just about keeping a phone charged for navigation; it’s about ensuring that the digital tools meant to facilitate the journey don't become sources of stress.

Ultimately, the video suggests that the Camino is a mirror for life’s burdens. Whether it is the weight of one’s backpack or the "weight" of expectations and worries, the act of thinning out one's possessions is a cathartic exercise. As the traveler prepares to step onto the trail, the message is clear: the most successful pilgrims are those who learn to lighten their load—both physical and mental—long before they reach the starting line.
