Seoul’s "Hangang Bus" Project Mired in Corruption Allegations and Evidence Tampering Claims

October 21, 2026

Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s flagship "Hangang Bus" project—a high-profile initiative to launch water taxis on the Han River—is facing intense scrutiny over allegations of illegal commissions and a systematic cover-up within the Seoul Metropolitan Government. Recent reports suggest that large sums of public money were funneled through dubious channels, while whistleblowers within the city administration allege they were targeted for retaliation after attempting to report the irregularities.

  • Ghost Commissions: Approximately $230,000 (320 million KRW) was paid out as "brokerage fees" for ship construction, despite no such provision appearing in the official contracts.
  • Evidence Tampering: Internal whistleblowers claim they were ordered by superiors to destroy documents related to the project before any formal audit or investigation could take place.
  • Attacking the Messenger: Mayor Oh and the city administration have characterized the whistleblowers as disgruntled employees with personal vendettas, a tactic critics describe as a blatant attempt to discredit the source to bury the scandal.

The "Ghost Fee" and Questionable Contracts

The controversy centers on payments made to Gadeok Heavy Industries, the primary partner for the ferry construction. Legal experts have noted that the contract lacks any clause for "consulting" or "brokerage fees." Yet, an additional 2% of the project cost—an amount significantly higher than industry standards—was paid out without a clear audit trail. When questioned during a National Assembly audit, Mayor Oh defended the payment as a "brokerage fee," but his inability to provide a contractual basis has fueled speculation that the money was a kickback or slush fund.

Further deepening the suspicion is the conduct of the Seoul Metropolitan Government. Instead of cooperating with inquiries, city officials reportedly directed staff to destroy internal records. Evidence has emerged, including a memo dated September 12, explicitly instructing staff to "maintain strict security" and "adjust the timing" of reporting to law enforcement to minimize political fallout. This suggests that the administration was aware of the irregularities and actively sought to manage the narrative rather than ensure fiscal transparency.

Retaliation Against Whistleblowers

The city’s response to those who spoke up has been sharp and punitive. Whistleblowers have been subjected to what they describe as "second-wave victimization," with the Mayor’s office publicly labeling them as problem employees involved in interpersonal conflicts. This strategy—denigrating the messenger to distract from the message—is a classic tool of administrative suppression.

The timing of personnel changes also raises alarms. A key official who began raising concerns in March was transferred to a new department on July 30, a move widely interpreted by staff as a retaliatory action intended to isolate the individual and prevent further disclosure. Under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, which has emphasized a policy of transparent governance, the behavior of the Seoul municipal government appears increasingly out of step with the current political climate.

Ultimately, the Han River Bus project has become a symbol of the very "closed-door" administration that Mayor Oh previously criticized. With officials reportedly resorting to password-protecting documents to keep them hidden from the administration's own purge, it is clear that the project’s internal management has devolved into a state of deep distrust. For a project aimed at revitalizing public transport, the foundation of its administration is currently anything but transparent.
