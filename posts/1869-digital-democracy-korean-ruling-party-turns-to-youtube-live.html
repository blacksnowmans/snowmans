Digital Democracy: Korean Ruling Party Turns to YouTube Live to Unify Base

November 02, 2026

The decision to live-stream a high-profile political talk event on Park Si-young TV, a prominent pro-government YouTube channel, highlights the growing role of digital platforms in managing internal fractures within South Korea’s ruling party.

  • Organizers pivoted a private recording session with Jung Chung-rae—a senior leader of the Democratic Party (DP), South Korea’s current ruling party—into a public YouTube live-stream following overwhelming demand from grassroots supporters.
  • The event served as a "political therapy" session aimed at healing emotional divides among party members that emerged during the recent national convention.
  • Participants moved beyond online grievances to establish concrete goals, such as a "membership doubling campaign" and the revitalization of local regional chapters to support the Lee Jae-myung administration.
  • The broadcast functioned as a counter-narrative to sensationalist media reports that had characterized the party retreat as a scene of internal chaos.

From Private Meetings to Virtual Town Halls

In the current political climate, the Democratic Party is increasingly bypassing traditional media to communicate directly with its most fervent supporters. The transition of the Jung Chung-rae talk from a closed recording to a live broadcast reflects a strategic embrace of "party member sovereignty." By opening the doors to a national audience, the DP and its allied media creators are attempting to transform passive viewers into active political agents. This shift is particularly vital as the party seeks to solidify its base following the tumultuous impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

The atmosphere of the event, as described by attendees and participating lawmakers like Kim Young-hwan, was less about venting anger and more about "rational deliberation." While the transition to a live format presented technical risks, it created a virtual public square where members felt their voices were central to the party’s future. This emotional connection is being leveraged to move the base toward practical local organizing, which the party views as essential for maintaining its governing momentum under President Lee Jae-myung.

Combating Media Sensationalism Through Transparency

The event also served as a defensive maneuver against legacy and niche media outlets. When Pyeonghwa Namu (Peace Tree), a progressive-leaning news site, framed the retreat with headlines suggesting the party was "in an uproar," the live-stream allowed the DP to project an image of "dignified debate" directly to the public. By showing the actual proceedings—including group discussions and policy brainstorming—the party aimed to neutralize claims of internal instability or "secretive" political maneuvering.

Ultimately, this reliance on YouTube channels like Park Si-young TV signals a permanent change in how Korean politics functions. For the ruling party, these platforms are no longer just mirrors of public opinion; they are the primary tools for mass mobilization. As the government works to restore systems damaged during the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the ability to bypass traditional gatekeepers and speak directly to a "sovereign" membership remains the Democratic Party’s most potent strategy for internal unity and long-term political survival.
