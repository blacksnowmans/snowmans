South Korea's Ruling Democrats Kill Property Tax Cuts to Defend Policy Consistency

November 12, 2026

The leadership of South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea has officially shut down internal proposals to soften the country's landmark Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax, holding the line on strict market regulation under President Lee Jae-myung.

  • The decision: Party leader Kim Min-seok rejected calls from within the party to raise the single-homeowner tax threshold from 1 billion won (about $750,000) to 1.2 billion won (about $900,000), ruling out any tax rollbacks or exemptions.
  • The rationale: Leadership warned that conceding on property taxes would create a domino effect, inevitably triggering demands to ease mortgage lending limits and fueling speculative buying.
  • Who is affected: Upper-middle-class homeowners in Seoul and the surrounding metropolitan region will not receive tax relief on rising valuations, ensuring property revenues remain earmarked for public housing and regional welfare programs.

A Firm Line Against Policy Drift

The internal debate emerged as some lawmakers in the Democratic Party grew anxious over pushback from middle-class voters in the capital area, where rising official property assessments have swept more single-home owners into the high-bracket tax bracket. Lawmakers representing Seoul constituencies floated raising the exemption floor by 200 million won (about $150,000) to appease owner-occupiers feeling the pinch.

Party leader Kim Min-seok and key allies, including lawmakers Han Byung-do and Kim Byung-joo, moved decisively to crush the proposal during internal leadership dinners and supreme council meetings. They argued that carving out exceptions for the top slice of homeowners would hollow out the tax's core purpose: placing a social levy on the wealthiest 2% of property owners to fund balanced national development. Kim Byung-joo cautioned that loosening tax rules would puncture the entire regulatory framework, inviting uncontrollable demands for looser lending rules and household debt expansion.

Defending the Core of Lee's Economic Philosophy

This decision draws a sharp contrast with the aggressive deregulation pursued during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's time in office, which the current administration argues exacerbated wealth disparities and destabilized the housing ladder. By holding firm, the Democratic Party is signaling to financial markets and property speculators that real estate policy under President Lee will not vacillate for short-term electoral gain.

While the choice risks alienating some swing voters in affluent districts of Seoul, the leadership calculated that policy predictability is worth the immediate political blowback. By keeping property taxation intact, the government preserves a vital source of revenue for its signature social agenda—such as expanding public housing—while clearly differentiating itself from the conservative opposition, the People Power Party.
