Architect of Lee Jae-myung’s Economic Vision Turns on the South Korean President

October 19, 2026

Professor Choi Bae-geun, the progressive economist who designed South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s flagship campaign policies, has publicly broken with the administration, accusing Lee of running an increasingly autocratic presidency enforced by online mob tactics.

  • The rift: In an October 19 live broadcast, Choi revealed that after the presidential office solicited his recommendations for the next Bank of Korea governor, selective leaks twisted his private advice into an alleged demand for the top post, triggering a smear campaign against him.
  • Online mob rule: Choi accused President Lee of using his personal X (formerly Twitter) account to direct hardcore online supporters—whom he termed "digital Red Guards"—to harass and silence internal critics within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK).
  • The factional purge: The clash highlights an intensifying effort by the presidential office to displace the party’s traditional base loyal to former presidents Roh Moo-hyun and Moon Jae-in in favor of an uncritical "New Lee" loyalist faction.
  • Budget contradictions: The dispute comes alongside broader governance controversies, including a 27% cut (roughly $210 million) to local fire safety subsidies citing falling tobacco revenues, even as the government swiftly disbursed about $5 million (6.83 billion won) in emergency reserves to set up a new Major Crimes Investigation Agency.

The Weaponization of Digital Fandom

Choi’s public denunciation carries unique weight because he was not a conservative rival, but the intellectual architect of Lee's signature economic platform. By publicly apologizing to the nation for endorsing Lee's candidacy, Choi validated long-simmering accusations that the administration governs through social-media coordinate attacks rather than institutional procedures. When presidential posts drop dog whistles against reformist figures like former Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae or allied minor parties, coordinated digital swarms rapidly target the dissenters' reputations to create an aggressive chilling effect.

Factional Realignment and Institutional Strain

This infighting reflects a deliberate campaign to overhaul the ruling party's internal demographics. By attempting to force out traditional progressives, the presidential circle is seeking an unassailable majority loyal solely to Lee. The unprecedented appointment of a former National Tax Service chief as presidential chief of staff has further alarmed party veterans, who view the move as a tacit threat of state audits against political challengers.

For outside observers, the fallout reveals severe fractures within South Korea’s governing coalition. When a president relies on personal social media followings to settle domestic policy debates, institutional governance suffers. With key fiscal pledges under strain and central bank appointments politicized, ideological discipline is increasingly taking precedence over transparent policy execution.
