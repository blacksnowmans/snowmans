Packing for the Camino de Santiago: Lessons in Minimalism

September 07, 2026

Preparing for the 800km Camino de Santiago pilgrimage is less about physical training and more about a brutal exercise in "the art of letting go." For the experienced trekker, the primary challenge is keeping your backpack under a manageable threshold, as every extra kilogram acts as a physical tax on your body over hundreds of thousands of steps.

  • The Golden Rule: Experts recommend keeping your pack weight at or below 10% of your body weight to prevent chronic knee and lower back injuries.
  • The "Just in Case" Trap: Over-packing stems from anxiety, but on the trail, excess weight is a direct liability to your health and stamina.
  • Strategic Minimalism: Success depends on choosing high-utility gear and accepting that you will be washing clothes daily in the albergues (pilgrim hostels).
  • Essential Comforts: While weight is critical, items that support mental well-being or prevent injury—such as compression stockings or familiar comfort foods—are non-negotiable exceptions to the weight-cutting rule.

Mastering the 6.3kg Pack

For this journey, I have finalized my pack weight at 6.3kg. Achieving this required rigorous editing: I opted for a 38-liter Osprey Kyte pack, a proven choice for long-distance walking. Clothing is strictly limited to one primary set plus minimal layers, such as a lightweight puffer jacket for chilly mornings and functional moisture-wicking tops. The philosophy is simple: if it doesn't dry overnight after a quick hand-wash, it doesn't belong in the bag.

However, I refuse to compromise on health and hygiene. My 900g toiletry pouch—containing skincare essentials like Cica cream and sunblock—remains. While some might call this excess weight, maintaining a basic self-care routine is a vital psychological anchor during the grueling 800km trek. Similarly, I am prioritizing injury prevention over weight savings by packing multiple knee and ankle braces; the weight of a brace is far less than the cost of a forced exit due to injury.

Beyond the Gear: A Mental Shift

Packing for the Camino reveals a profound truth about our daily lives: we are accustomed to carrying far more than we actually need. By stripping my life down to 6.3kg, I am not just preparing for a hike; I am de-cluttering my existence. This process forces you to identify what is truly essential, separating the items that provide genuine utility from those that merely satisfy a fear of the unknown.

Ultimately, the weight of your pack is a physical manifestation of your priorities. A 10kg pack might have felt manageable in past experiences, but the "lean" 6.3kg setup I’ve crafted represents a mature understanding of the trail’s demands. It is a balance between necessary survival equipment and the small, personal comforts—like a few packets of Korean ramen seasoning—that keep the spirit alive when the legs are tired. The goal is to reach Santiago de Compostela not just with a lighter bag, but with a lighter perspective on what we truly need to move forward.
