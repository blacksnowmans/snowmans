Court Upholds Prison Sentence for Yoon Suk-yeol, Threatening $28 Million Blow to Opposition

November 05, 2026

An appellate court has upheld a prison sentence for the imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol for spreading false information during the 2022 presidential campaign, a ruling that could bankrupt the country’s main conservative opposition party.

  • The Verdict: The Seoul High Court maintained the original sentence of 18 months in prison, suspended for three years, for violating the Public Official Election Act.
  • The Crimes: Judges ruled that Mr. Yoon intentionally lied about his ties to a shaman and his role in a legal scandal involving a former tax official to deceive the electorate.
  • Financial Fallout: If finalized by the Supreme Court, the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's conservative opposition, must return approximately $28 million (39.7 billion won) in state-reimbursed election expenses.
  • Democratic Integrity: The court emphasized that in an election decided by a razor-thin 0.73% margin, the candidate's "deliberate lies" constituted a severe threat to the foundations of democracy.

A Verdict on Shamanism and Deception

The court’s decision centers on two specific lies told by Mr. Yoon during the 2022 race. First, he denied introducing a lawyer to Yoon Woo-jin, a former head of the Yongsan Tax Office, despite evidence proving the connection. Second, he claimed he had no relationship with Jeon Seong-bae—a shaman known as "Geonjin-beopsa"—and that his wife, Kim Keon-hee, had never met the man. The court found these to be calculated falsehoods designed to suppress the "shamanism risk" that haunted his campaign. The presiding judge, Baek Seung-hyeop, dismissed the defense's claim that these were mere mistakes, noting that as a former prosecutor, Mr. Yoon was a legal expert well aware of the weight of his words.

This ruling is a significant moment for South Korean jurisprudence, as it refuses to categorize campaign lies as "white lies" or political hyperbole. In the eyes of the court, Mr. Yoon’s actions were an attempt to obstruct the voters' ability to make an informed choice. By maintaining the 18-month sentence, the judiciary is signaling that even powerful political figures cannot escape accountability for misleading the public. For Mr. Yoon, who is already serving time following his impeachment and removal from office, this adds yet another layer to his legal collapse.

The $28 Million "Financial Bomb" for the PPP

While the personal consequences for Mr. Yoon are severe, the institutional consequences for the People Power Party (PPP) are existential. Under the Public Official Election Act, if a winning candidate is later convicted of a serious election crime, the nominating party must return all campaign subsidies provided by the state. The $28 million (39.7 billion won) bill is a staggering sum for the PPP. Although the party holds significant assets, most are tied up in real estate. To pay the debt, the party may be forced to sell its headquarters or take out massive private loans, potentially crippling its ability to compete in future elections.

This case also highlights the contrast between the current state of Korean and global politics. While the U.S. Democratic Party recently celebrated a "Blue Tsunami" in the midterms by focusing on institutional reform and accountability, South Korea’s political landscape remains mired in the legal fallout of the previous administration. The ruling Democratic Party (DP), currently led by President Lee Jae-myung, must now navigate its own internal divisions while the opposition PPP faces financial ruin.
