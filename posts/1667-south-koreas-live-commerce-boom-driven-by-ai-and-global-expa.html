South Korea's Live-Commerce Boom Driven by AI and Global Expansion

October 29, 2026

  • AI takes the wheel: Korean tech firms like Buzzni and Featuring are deploying AI tools to slash marketing production costs and automate influencer matching.
  • Global push: Logistics giant Hanjin is leveraging partnerships with platforms like TikTok to help South Korean sellers break into the US and Japanese markets.
  • Market polarization: While major creator commerce firms are scaling past 3 trillion won (about $2.2 billion) in transactions, independent micro-sellers face intense competition and algorithm dependency.

South Korea's live-commerce sector is undergoing a rapid structural shift, moving far beyond simple real-time video sales. Local tech companies are aggressively embedding artificial intelligence into everyday retail operations—automating everything from influencer casting to content generation—while domestic brands and creators look overseas to escape a saturated home market.

Yet this hyper-efficient, tech-driven expansion has a sharp downside. As platforms and capitalized agencies consolidate power, smaller independent sellers and creators are increasingly squeezed by shrinking margins and algorithmic pressure. Even as major logistics initiatives like Hanjin's "One-Click Connect" clear the path for international shipping to North America and Japan, the human cost behind the flashy sales milestones—ranging from precarious creator incomes to overworked micro-influencers—remains largely unaddressed in the rush for growth.
