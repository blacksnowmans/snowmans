South Korea Market Briefing: Kospi Reclaims 7,000 as Won Strengthens Ahead of Long Weekend

October 03, 2026

  • Kospi Rebound: South Korea's benchmark Kospi index recovered to the 7,000 level ahead of a five-day holiday, driven by institutional and retail buying, while SK Hynix steadied the market.
  • Bond Market Relief: Stronger-than-expected tax revenues allowed the South Korean government to reduce treasury bond issuance by about 5 trillion won (approx. $3.7 billion), easing domestic credit fears.
  • Semiconductor Rally: Driven by soaring memory and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) pricing forecasts, domestic semiconductor supply-chain stocks surged 20% to 30% over the past month.
  • Tech Debt Risks: Global AI infrastructure expansion is increasingly fueled by heavy leverage, highlighted by Amazon reportedly planning an $80 billion loan backed by Nvidia AI chips.

Kospi Recovers as Won Leads Asian Currencies

South Korean markets closed on a resilient note ahead of a long holiday weekend, with the Kospi finishing at 7,003.74. While tech giant Samsung Electronics saw a slowdown, SK Hynix and major financial stocks provided a sturdy floor. Foreign selling eased significantly as US yields declined, leaving foreign net sales at about 140 billion won (approx. $104 million), while retail investors scooped up 1.7 trillion won (approx. $1.26 billion) in equities. However, slowing purchases from other corporate entities hint that Samsung's ongoing share buyback program may be winding down, shifting focus toward smaller cap names.

In the foreign exchange market, the South Korean won emerged as one of Asia's strongest performers. The dollar-won exchange rate dipped near 1,346, easing import cost pressures on a trade-dependent economy. Meanwhile, domestic bond yields fell notably—with the 10-year treasury yield dropping from 4.55% to 4.35%—providing much-needed relief to local credit markets as government borrowing needs shrank due to healthy tax receipts.

Semiconductor Optimism and Hidden Leverage Dangers

The semiconductor sector remains red-hot. Taiwan-based TrendForce projects double-digit price hikes for DRAM and NAND flash through the fourth quarter, with upcoming HBM4 memory chips expected to command significantly higher prices than their predecessors. This boom has propelled domestic material, parts, and equipment (often called "Sobujang") exchange-traded funds up by 20% to 30% recently. Analysts warn, however, that widespread media enthusiasm and surging valuations mirror past speculative peaks, demanding caution against chasing overcrowded trades.

At the same time, the massive capital requirements of the artificial intelligence boom are raising structural questions. Beyond South Korea's borders, massive data center investments are relying on steep leverage—evidenced by reports that major tech giants are pledging expensive AI hardware as collateral for multi-billion-dollar credit facilities. As domestic refiners enjoy a jump in profit margins due to shifting export dynamics and renewable energy infrastructure accelerates, investors must weigh short-term cyclical wins against the mounting weight of global debt.
