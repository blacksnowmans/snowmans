South Korea Goes Back to School: Local Governments Lead a Lifelong Learning Boom

October 26, 2026

South Korea’s push for adult education has shifted from a personal hobby to a critical survival strategy, with local governments across the country rolling out civic festivals, AI training, and specialized community programs.

    Jangseong County joins the fold: The rural county officially received its new designation as a certified lifelong learning city at a national festival, launching age-inclusive education programs. Massive festival turnout: Cities like Uiwang and Goyang hosted large-scale outdoor learning festivals, drawing 35,000 attendees to Uiwang's lake park alone. Bridging the digital divide: Urban centers are integrating practical AI and digital literacy courses into community centers to help older and marginalized residents keep pace with technology. Public-private synergy: Moving away from the fragmented, laissez-faire approach seen during the administration of jailed former leader Yoon Suk-yeol, the current administration under President Lee Jae-myung has encouraged local authorities and regional universities to restore public education infrastructure.

Beyond Hobbies: Lifelong Education as Social Infrastructure

What is happening across South Korea's towns and cities is much more than casual night classes. Local governments are stepping in to position education as a primary tool for community survival, particularly for smaller regions facing population decline. By linking adult learning with local welfare and job training, cities are attempting to build safety nets that prevent elderly residents and marginalized groups from falling behind.

This localized push represents a distinct shift in tone from the previous conservative administration, which critics note left regional educational ecosystems underfunded and isolated. Under the current government, municipalities are partnering with local institutions—such as Baekseok University in Cheonan—to turn campuses into community learning hubs. These programs offer everything from vocational training to specialized support for developmental disabilities, reframing education as a fundamental civic right rather than a luxury.

Tackling the AI Digital Divide

As artificial intelligence reshapes the modern economy, local authorities are racing to ensure that technological progress does not translate into social exclusion. Cities like Goyang and Anyang have introduced practical digital literacy courses—spanning image editing, e-book creation, and AI tools—designed specifically for adult learners and tech-novice seniors.

Without deliberate public intervention to lower the barrier to entry for digital skills, the rapid advancement of AI risks widening existing class divides. By embedding technology education into community lifelong learning programs, South Korea's local governments are attempting to ensure that older generations and vulnerable populations remain active, contributing members of a changing society.
