Former MBC Announcer Im Hyun-joo Refutes Rumors Over Childcare Leave

November 18, 2026

Former Munhwa Broadcasting Corporation (MBC) announcer Im Hyun-joo publicly pushed back on November 17 against viral accusations that she resigned immediately after exhausting 21 months of childcare leave, revealing she actually took a single 10-month leave and forfeited remaining time off to quit.

  • The rumor vs. the record: Online critics and early headlines alleged Im took two separate leaves totaling one year and nine months before abruptly leaving MBC, one of South Korea's major public broadcasters, while Im clarified this year marked her first-ever childcare leave.
  • Prior work history: Im stated she anchored live broadcasts until full term during both of her pregnancies and returned to the studio three months after each birth, pushing back against harsh accusations that she freeloaded off colleagues.
  • Rapid media shift: The dispute generated 27 Korean media headlines between 10:34 a.m. and 6:50 p.m. on November 17, pivoting from initial outrage over her resignation to reporting her exact timeline and issuing corrections.
  • Regional parallel: Coverage by South Korean sports and entertainment outlet MHN Sports noted that a similar dispute erupted in Japan around a female announcer leaving her broadcaster, underscoring shared tensions over parental leave and resignation in both countries.

What the Backlash Reveals About Korean Workplaces

What made the backlash against Im travel so fast was its fake precision. Rather than vague gossip, critics circulated concrete numbers—two leaves and 21 months—which made an unverified claim look like established fact and gave online commenters permission to accuse her of exploiting coworkers.

Even if someone used every day of legally guaranteed childcare leave before moving on, they would be exercising a statutory right, not committing misconduct. Yet in South Korea, working mothers who resign after taking leave frequently face suspicion that they stayed on payroll solely to collect benefits, regardless of their prior contributions.

Im managed to turn the news cycle around within hours only because she released specific dates and reminded the public that she worked live broadcasts right up to delivery. Most ordinary office workers do not have a public platform to litigate their attendance records—and forcing parents to prove they worked hard enough before leaving a job only deepens hesitation around using parental leave in the first place.
