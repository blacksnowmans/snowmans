South Korea Braces for Parliamentary Shutdown Over Special Counsel and $593 Billion Budget

November 04, 2026

South Korea’s National Assembly is headed for a total legislative stalemate as the ruling party pushes for a special counsel probe into prosecutorial misconduct while the opposition threatens a marathon filibuster.

  • The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the ruling party supporting President Lee Jae-myung, plans to pass a "Fabricated Prosecution Special Counsel Act" during the November 5 plenary session.
  • The People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, has vowed to block all pending legislation using a filibuster (unlimited debate) to prevent what they call a "judicial shield" for the current administration.
  • The standoff puts the 821 trillion KRW (roughly $593 billion) national budget at risk, with the PPP demanding cuts of at least 30 trillion KRW (about $21.7 billion).
  • Key regional development and climate change bills, such as the Future Response Fund and the Mega-City Special Act, remain stalled in committees due to the partisan divide.

A Battle Over Judicial Justice and Political Survival

The DPK’s push for a special counsel is framed as a necessary measure to "normalize" the prosecution. The act targets alleged evidence fabrication and illegal investigative tactics used by prosecutors during the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the impeached former president who is currently imprisoned. To the ruling party, these reforms are a prerequisite for a fair society; they argue that "livelihood politics" cannot exist on a foundation of corrupted justice. By investigating past abuses, the DPK aims to dismantle what it describes as the weaponization of the state’s investigative powers.

The opposition PPP sees the move as a direct assault on the judiciary intended to protect the current administration from its own legal risks. They have characterized the special counsel bill as an attempt to "bury" legitimate trials and have signaled a scorched-earth strategy in response. By threatening to filibuster every single bill on the docket, the PPP is effectively holding the legislative schedule hostage. This "all-or-nothing" approach reflects a deep-seated fear within the conservative camp that a special counsel probe will further expose the failings of the previous Yoon era.

The $593 Billion Ideological War

Beyond the legal drama, the fight over the 821 trillion KRW budget represents a fundamental clash of economic philosophies. President Lee Jae-myung’s administration has proposed an expansionary budget focused on social safety nets, support for small business owners, and regional revitalization. The DPK views this spending as vital "investment" to prevent a deeper economic slump. However, the PPP has labeled it "ultra-inflationary" and "populist," demanding massive cuts under the banner of fiscal responsibility—a stance critics find ironic given the significant tax revenue deficits recorded during Mr. Yoon’s tenure.

The human cost of this political gridlock is high. While the two parties trade barbs over prosecutorial reform and fiscal health, essential bills for regional autonomy and future-proofing the economy against demographic shifts are gathering dust. The "Future Response Fund," intended to address the climate crisis and the aging population, has become a political football, with the opposition framing it as a debt burden for future generations rather than a necessary insurance policy. As the November 5 deadline approaches, the National Assembly looks less like a deliberative body and more like a battlefield where the public's needs are secondary to partisan survival.

Our View: The opposition’s threat to paralyze the entire legislature over a special counsel probe is a dangerous escalation. If the PPP is confident that the prosecutions under Mr. Yoon were handled fairly, they should welcome an independent investigation to clear their names rather than obstructing the state's budget and livelihood bills.
