South Korea's Budget Battles, Ferry Rebate Allegations, and Tax Debate

November 10, 2026

South Korean politics was dominated by fierce clashes in the National Assembly over a massive future-investment fund, alongside widening investigations into religious groups and transit project graft allegations.

  • Budget showdown: Ruling and opposition parties locked horns over a 162 trillion won (about $115 billion) government future-readiness fund, with the ruling party backing it as vital productivity investment and the conservative opposition slamming it as debt-fueled pork.
  • Graft and church probes: Fresh controversies erupted as the opposition called for a full investigation into an alleged systematic infiltration of political parties by the Shincheonji religious sect, while graft allegations surfaced over Seoul's Han River bus transit project.
  • Special counsel clash: The main conservative opposition party boycotted legislative committee sessions and protested outside the presidential office against a special counsel bill targeting past prosecutions.
  • Regional cooperation: Local governments across the political divide pushed for joint special legislation and vital regional budgets, including funding for a proposed aerospace complex.

Budget Wars and the Reality of Fiscal Policy

The core of the current parliamentary stalemate lies in the 162 trillion-won future fund. The Democratic Party, South Korea's main liberal party and the current ruling force under President Lee Jae-myung, argues that aggressive spending on artificial intelligence, robotics, and green transition is essential for national survival. Meanwhile, the conservative People Power Party, South Korea's main opposition, has attacked the fund as irresponsible populist spending that saddles future generations with debt while bypassing proper legislative checks.

Beyond fiscal disputes, government officials moved quickly to quell doubts surrounding major international projects. The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy pushed back firmly against rumors that a multi-billion-dollar Czech nuclear power plant export deal could fall through, insisting that South Korea's "Team Korea" consortium remains fully on track to complete the project.

Graft Allegations and Judicial Showdowns

Political tensions intensified further with explosive claims from former politicians regarding religious groups infiltrating party ranks. At the same time, the opposition targeted Seoul Mayor Oh Sehun over allegations of a 1.5 billion won (about $1.1 million) rebate tied to the city's new Han River water-bus project—a claim the city government pledged to investigate thoroughly.

On the judicial front, conservative lawmakers staged intense street protests against a special counsel bill aimed at investigating past prosecutorial decisions made during the administration of imprisoned former leader Yoon Seok-youl. The ruling camp maintains that clearing up past abuses of prosecutorial power is a necessary step for judicial reform, while the opposition labels the bill a political shield to protect the current administration.
