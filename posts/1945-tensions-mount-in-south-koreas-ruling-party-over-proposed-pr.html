Tensions Mount in South Korea's Ruling Party Over Proposed Progressive Merger

November 04, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea is locked in a fierce internal feud as party leadership resists a proposed merger with the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party—a unification initiative aligned with President Lee Jae-myung's political vision.

  • What happened: Democratic Party Leader Kim Min-seok and his allies blocked internal procedures and polling regarding a merger with the Rebuilding Korea Party, defying a strategic proposal launched in January by lawmaker Jung Chung-rae and backed by senior presidential aides.
  • Distorted polling: Opponents of the merger are accused of manipulating public sentiment by incorporating voters from the conservative People Power Party, South Korea's main opposition party, to artificially inflate anti-merger figures.
  • Base support for unity: Polling from STI shows strong appetite for unification among progressives, with 64% of Rebuilding Korea Party supporters and 50.1% of Democratic Party voters favoring the move.
  • Rising fallout: Party Leader Kim faces severe pressure over plummeting approval ratings—even in traditional liberal strongholds like the Honam region—and backlash over unverified accusations of cult infiltration within party ranks.

Fractures Between the Presidency and Party Leadership

The rift within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) highlights a deepening divide between President Lee Jae-myung’s broader political strategy and the party’s executive leadership under Kim Min-seok. While President Lee has sought to consolidate progressive strength ahead of upcoming local elections, Kim and his inner circle have effectively stonewalled democratic voting and debate on the issue. Critics charge that party leadership is prioritizing factional self-preservation and local constituency security over the president's governing vision.

To justify rejecting the merger, party leadership previously claimed that public sentiment was overwhelmingly hostile. However, detailed survey data revealed that anti-merger totals were heavily padded by opposition conservative voters. By treating these skewed figures as a mandate from the party base, Kim's faction has alienated core supporters who favor a unified progressive front with the Rebuilding Korea Party.

Desperate Tactics and a Leadership Crisis

Kim Min-seok's political standing has deteriorated rapidly as a result. Disapproval ratings for the party leader now outstrip those of opposition figures, eroding support across every major region. Meanwhile, support for the Rebuilding Korea Party continues to rise, signaling that liberal voters are growing impatient with internal friction inside the ruling establishment.

Facing calls to step down, Kim has drawn further controversy by alleging that thousands of members from the Shincheonji religious group secretly infiltrated the party. The shifting details and lack of clear evidence behind these claims have invited comparisons to McCarthy-style political distraction. With local elections fast approaching, the ruling party's inability to resolve this internal dispute risks fracturing its coalition and stalling the momentum of President Lee's legislative agenda.
