Democratic Party Crisis: Leadership Vacuum Fuels Polling Freefall

October 12, 2026

  • Leadership failure: The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung, is facing a severe drop in public support, with internal critics blaming the current leadership under Chairman Kim Min-seok for being passive and directionless.
  • Rise of the Rebuilding Korea Party: Disaffected DPK supporters are increasingly turning to the Rebuilding Korea Party (a progressive minor party), which is now polling in the high single digits—a threshold that would grant it significant leverage in the National Assembly.
  • The "Chung Chung-rae Effect": Many party supporters argue that the DPK’s approval ratings were only stable when Rep. Chung Chung-rae was actively leading the defense of the administration; his current relative silence is viewed as a major contributing factor to the party's loss of momentum.

A Party Adrift

The Democratic Party is currently gripped by a sense of urgency that transcends mere numbers. Critics within the party have labeled Chairman Kim Min-seok’s leadership as "historically incompetent," arguing that his reliance on excessive caution is testing the patience of the base. While some party insiders invoke a sense of "political duty" to stand by the current leadership, the reality is that the party’s primary mission—supporting the Lee Jae-myung administration—is faltering as the administration’s momentum fades.

The DPK finds itself paralyzed, unable to decide whether to compete with or collaborate with the Rebuilding Korea Party. This indecision has created a vacuum, as progressive voters searching for a clear, combative opposition voice are finding it elsewhere. If the Rebuilding Korea Party sustains its support above 10%, the DPK risks losing its status as the sole, undisputed leader of the progressive bloc, turning a once-loyal ally into a potential "kingmaker" that could dictate parliamentary outcomes.

Missing the "Chung Chung-rae" Factor

There is a growing narrative that the party's decline is directly linked to the sidelining of Rep. Chung Chung-rae. During the early days of the Lee Jae-myung presidency, Chung served as a fierce shield for the administration, consistently setting the agenda on issues like judicial reform. His aggressive leadership style provided a focal point for the base and acted as a catalyst for high approval ratings.

Currently, the party is filled with "New Lee Jae-myung" loyalists who appear to lack the political instinct that defined the party’s previous success. While Rep. Chung remains silent out of a sense of political decorum following his loss in the party leadership race, this silence has left a void. Without a clear, fiery figure to command the narrative, the DPK has devolved into a defensive, reactionary organization. The party’s survival may depend not on waiting for a ten-day turnaround in the polls, but on a decisive return to its core identity—a shift that requires the "silent majority" of lawmakers to finally speak up before the current leadership leads them to further isolation.
