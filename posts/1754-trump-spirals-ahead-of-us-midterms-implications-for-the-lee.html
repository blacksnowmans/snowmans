Trump Spirals Ahead of U.S. Midterms: Implications for the Lee Jae-myung Administration

October 31, 2026

Former U.S. President Donald Trump has abruptly shifted to remote campaigning amidst health rumors and growing controversy over verbal attacks on the press, signaling a volatile finish to the U.S. midterm elections just three days away.

  • Campaign Volatility: Trump canceled physical rallies in Republican strongholds to switch to remote appearances, sparking rumors about his physical stamina and leading to a high-profile verbal assault on a reporter.
  • Shifting Strongholds: Recent polling shows a surprising shift in Texas, a state that has served as a Republican "Red Wall" for over 30 years, forcing the Trump camp into defensive and increasingly extreme rhetoric.
  • Election Denialism: The former president has begun labeling unfavorable polls as "fabricated" and has refused to commit to a peaceful acceptance of the election results, raising fears of renewed civil unrest.
  • Korean Economic Stakes: The Lee Jae-myung administration, South Korea's current liberal government, is closely monitoring the outcome as shifts in U.S. trade protectionism could directly impact Korean battery and automotive exports.

A Leadership Crisis in the Republican Camp

The atmosphere surrounding the Trump campaign is reportedly turning gloomy as internal data suggests his personal appearances may be alienating moderate voters. Reports from Washington indicate that the campaign is increasingly insular, staffed by loyalists who shield the former president from negative polling data. Amidst this isolation, Trump’s reaction to a reporter’s question about his health—calling the journalist "disgusting" and launching a minute-long personal tirade—has been viewed by critics as a sign of deep-seated insecurity regarding his age. Following the outburst, his team took the drastic step of revoking the reporter’s press credentials, a move seen by many as a direct assault on the freedom of the press.

The instability is further highlighted by Trump’s recent rhetoric regarding the voting system. In an attempt to preemptively challenge the results, he has made unsubstantiated claims about the complexity of ballots in states like Alaska, suggesting they are designed to be "too difficult" for average voters to understand. This strategy of sowing distrust in the democratic process mirrors the lead-up to the January 6th Capitol riot, leaving both American and international observers concerned about the stability of the U.S. political system.

Strategic Challenges for the Lee Jae-myung Government

For Seoul, the midterm results are a matter of urgent national interest. President Lee Jae-myung has prioritized a strategy of "practical diplomacy," but a U.S. Congress dominated by protectionist "America First" sentiment could jeopardize the tariff benefits currently enjoyed by Korean firms that have invested billions in U.S. soil. If the U.S. Democratic Party retains control of the legislature, they are expected to launch a series of intense investigations into the Trump administration’s past conduct, including the mishandling of classified documents and private financial gains. Such a "legislative war" would likely paralyze Washington, complicating Korea’s efforts to coordinate on regional security and trade.

The current political climate in the U.S. carries echoes of Korea’s own recent history. The descent into extreme partisanship and the rejection of democratic norms are reminiscent of the period leading up to the impeachment and imprisonment of Yoon Suk-yeol-ssi (the former president currently serving a prison sentence). As the U.S. approaches the "Red Scream" of election day, the Lee administration must navigate a path that protects Korean economic interests regardless of which party seizes the gavel in Washington. The risk of a fractured America remains the single greatest uncertainty for Korea’s global trade and security calculus.
