Seoul's Nodeul Island Reopens as a 24/7 Waterfront Green Escape

September 19, 2026

  • Seoul has reopened Nodeul Island, a previously underused island in the middle of the Han River, as a redesigned waterfront cultural and ecological space following a major renovation.
  • The newly unveiled area features themed zones—including an ecological garden, a play garden, a grass garden, and pocket gardens—connected by walking paths designed to blend with the natural river landscape.
  • The outdoor spaces are open to the public 24 hours a day, offering urban residents and visitors round-the-clock access to walking trails, sunset views, and night walks along the river.

For years, Nodeul Island sat in the Han River as an awkward parcel of land that struggled to find a clear identity amid Seoul's dense concrete landscape. Its latest makeover shifts the focus entirely away from commercial or showy architecture, leaning instead into what urbanites need most: breathing room. By carving out interconnected gardens and accessible paths right over the river, city planners have turned a forgotten waypoint into a functional sanctuary.

What makes this renewal stand out is its commitment to everyday accessibility rather than grand, one-off spectacles. The decision to keep the outdoor spaces open 24 hours a day acknowledges the realities of life in a fast-paced metropolis where stress doesn't punch out at 5 PM. Whether visitors drop by for a quiet morning stroll or an evening wind-down after a long workday, the island provides a reliable escape without requiring a trek out to the city's rural outskirts.

Navigating the New Nodeul Island

Ecological Gardens and Round-the-Clock Access

The core appeal of the renewed Nodeul Island lies in its thoughtful integration of plant life and river views. Walking trails wind past wild grasses and shaded pockets of greenery designed to mimic natural riverbanks rather than manicured corporate lawns. Because the outdoor spaces operate around the clock, evening visitors can catch the sunset over the Han River bridges and walk safely under well-maintained lighting.

While the grounds are always open, indoor facilities operate on a more structured schedule. The island's cultural and indoor spaces are generally open from 10:00 AM until 9:00 PM on weekdays, and extend until 10:00 PM on weekends and public holidays. Visitors planning to combine an outdoor walk with indoor exhibitions should time their arrival accordingly, and prepare for strong sun during daytime visits or chilly river breezes after dark.

A Blueprint for Urban Public Spaces

In a city where commercial developments often crowd out genuine green spaces, Nodeul Island's redesign serves as a welcome shift in priorities. It demonstrates that public land can be successfully returned to citizens as a place for quiet reflection rather than commercial exploitation. If Seoul continues to prioritize human-centric urban design like this, stressed-out city dwellers will have far fewer reasons to pack up and flee to the countryside every weekend just to find some peace.
