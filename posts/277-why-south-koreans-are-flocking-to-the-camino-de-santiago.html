Why South Koreans Are Flocking to the Camino de Santiago

September 07, 2026

  • Thousands of South Koreans are abandoning rigid corporate structures and social pressures to hike the 800-kilometer (approx. 500-mile) Camino de Santiago pilgrimage across Europe.
  • South Korea consistently ranks among the top countries for pilgrim participation, reflecting a deep cultural exhaustion with hyper-competition and societal expectations.
  • The surge in popularity has brought unintended consequences, sparking local complaints about overtourism and noise in historic European villages.

For many hyper-connected, overworked South Koreans, the antidote to a life dictated by social milestones and constant evaluation isn't a luxury vacation—it's 40 days of agonizing physical exertion. Setting off from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port in southern France, pilgrims strap on backpacks, collect their traditional pilgrim credentials, and embark on a grueling foot-journey toward Santiago de Compostela in Spain. In a society where every career move, meal, and lifestyle choice feels heavily scrutinized, the sheer simplicity of the trail—where the only mandate is to walk forward and find a place to sleep—offers a rare psychological release.

Yet, this mass exodus exposes a modern contradiction. While travelers seek inner peace and freedom from judgment, their sheer numbers are transforming quiet European hamlets into hotspots of overtourism. Local residents increasingly push back against early-morning noise and trash left behind by visitors, forcing a reckoning with the ethics of modern travel. True pilgrimage, critics note, shouldn't come at the expense of the communities hosting it.

Ultimately, the enduring appeal of the Camino for South Koreans lies in its absolute lack of rankings, grades, and corporate ladders. When your only daily goals are managing blisters and putting one foot in front of the other, the anxiety of tomorrow fades. It is a harsh, beautiful reminder that self-reliance and the simple right to rest matter more than any societal benchmark.
