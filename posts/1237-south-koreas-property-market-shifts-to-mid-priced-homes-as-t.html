South Korea's Property Market Shifts to Mid-Priced Homes as Tax and Loan Rules Bite

October 16, 2026

Strict loan regulations and tax reform proposals under President Lee Jae-myung are driving South Korea’s real estate market into extreme polarization, shifting buyer demand away from Gangnam’s ultra-luxury high-rises and into homes priced under 1.5 billion KRW (about $1.1 million) in outer Seoul and neighboring Gyeonggi Province.

  • What happened: While price growth in Seoul’s wealthy Gangnam districts has paused after surging over 40% in recent years, outer Seoul districts and Gyeonggi satellite cities have seen apartment prices jump between 18% and 26%.
  • What changes: Wealthy property owners are downsizing from multi-million-dollar homes to secure cash before tighter capital gains tax rules take effect, driving intense competition in mid-tier housing brackets.
  • Who is affected: Middle-class buyers face a shrinking inventory of entry-level homes in the 400 million to 600 million KRW ($290,000–$440,000) range, while provincial property markets outside the greater Seoul area face severe stagnation.

Statistical Illusion vs. Suburban Migration

Recent statistics showing minor price dips in Gangnam—Seoul’s wealthiest southern district—have led some observers to predict a broader market downturn. However, a drop of 0.1% following a multi-year surge of over 40% represents a temporary breather rather than a market collapse. The underlying momentum remains strong, but strict lending caps have redirected capital away from high-end luxury properties toward homes under the 1.5 billion KRW ($1.1 million) regulatory threshold.

This shift has triggered rapid price growth in outer Seoul and neighboring Gyeonggi Province. Outer districts like Seongbuk-gu saw prices rise 18.36% over nine months, while satellite hubs such as Dongtan in Hwaseong recorded gains of 26.45%. Driven by value-seeking buyers and expanding transport links, suburban apartments are currently outperforming core Seoul properties in percentage gains, leaving lower-priced units in short supply.

Policy Pressure Drives Wealthy Downsizing

The migration is accelerated by policy adjustments under the Lee Jae-myung administration aimed at tightening real estate taxation and curbing leverage—a market structure previously unsettled by deregulation during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s presidency. High-net-worth homeowners, including those holding units in Gangnam's iconic Eunma Apartment redevelopment complex, are increasingly listing their properties. Anticipating a proposed 1 billion KRW ($730,000) cap on capital gains tax exemptions, these owners are selling high-value assets to downsize into mid-priced primary residences while holding cash.

This liquidity reallocation exacerbates a stark regional divide. While the greater Seoul area absorbs capital flowing out of prime luxury real estate, provincial markets outside the capital region remain frozen, with transactions limited strictly to a tiny selection of top-tier local developments. For buyers, navigating South Korea's current housing landscape requires looking beyond headline figures to focus on specific price brackets and regional infrastructure corridors.
