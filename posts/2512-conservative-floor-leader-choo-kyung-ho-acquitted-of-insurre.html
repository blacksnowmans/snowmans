Conservative Floor Leader Choo Kyung-ho Acquitted of Insurrection Charges

November 19, 2026

On November 18, the Seoul Central District Court acquitted Choo Kyung-ho, the floor leader of South Korea's main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP), of charges that he aided an insurrection by attempting to obstruct a parliamentary vote to end emergency martial law.

  • The Ruling: The court cleared Choo of playing an essential role in insurrection, citing insufficient evidence that he directly conspired with Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol or acted on direct orders to disrupt the National Assembly.
  • The Quorum Argument: Judges ruled that because rival lawmakers held enough seats to pass the resolution lifting martial law regardless of the PPP’s boycott, Choo’s actions did not physically prevent the legislature from functioning.
  • Conservative Counterattack: The People Power Party hailed the acquittal as a "triumph of common sense" and signaled plans to file criminal complaints against Special Counsel Cho Eun-seok for alleged abuse of prosecutorial authority.
  • Ruling Party Outrage: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), aligned with the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, condemned the decision as "treason against history" and demanded an immediate appeal.

Judicial Scrutiny and Deepening Division

The centerpiece of the court’s decision lies in its strict evidentiary standard: while Choo’s calls for his lawmakers to avoid the assembly hall drew severe ethical criticism, prosecutors failed to produce a definitive "smoking gun" tying him directly to Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s chain of command. More controversially, the court adopted an outcome-based rationale regarding quorum. By concluding that the vote succeeded anyway through the presence of other parties, the ruling invites sharp criticism from legal observers who argue it sets a dangerous precedent—implying that attempts to disrupt constitutional proceedings escape criminal liability so long as opposing forces manage to salvage the process.

Politically, the acquittal hands the conservative opposition a potent weapon to push back against ongoing special counsel investigations, which they have long characterized as partisan retaliation by the Lee Jae-myung government. While third-party voices such as Reform Party lawmaker Lee Jun-seok criticized both major parties for turning governance into a race to jail opponents, the ruling has fundamentally widened South Korea's partisan gulf. Rather than resolving the constitutional fallout from martial law, the verdict has energized right-wing supporters, intensified government skepticism toward the judiciary, and ensured that the upcoming appellate battle will be fought with even higher political stakes.
