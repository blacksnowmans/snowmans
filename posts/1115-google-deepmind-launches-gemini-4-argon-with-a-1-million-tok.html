Google DeepMind Launches Gemini 4 Argon with a 1-Million Token Context Window

October 14, 2026

  • What happened: Google DeepMind introduced Gemini 4 Argon, a new AI model designed for heavy enterprise workflows, featuring a 1-million token context window (about 750,000 words).
  • What it changes: Users can now input entire books, long meeting videos, or massive codebases without losing context, while benchmark tests show significantly reduced hallucination rates.
  • Pricing and access: The model is currently available through Gemini Pro and Ultra subscriptions, with developer API pricing set at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens until prices double in December.
  • Who is affected: Enterprise users, software developers, and businesses relying on deep document processing and multi-step AI agents.

Google has officially expanded its artificial intelligence lineup with the release of Gemini 4 Argon, introduced by Google DeepMind Senior Vice President and Chief AI Architect Koray Kavukcuoglu. Rather than rushing out an incomplete model due to industry FOMO, Google has taken its time to target complex enterprise workflows, complex coding tasks, and real-world physical integration, such as robotic learning.

The standout feature of Gemini 4 Argon is its massive 1-million token capacity, allowing it to process massive volumes of text and data in a single prompt while cutting hallucination rates down to roughly 15% of previous levels. In practical tests, the model excelled at generating detailed SVGs, building physics simulations with real-time slider controls, and organizing complex scheduling constraints into clear tables. However, users still encountered interface stalls where backend processing finished successfully even though the frontend froze—a frustrating bug that highlights the gap between execution and user experience.

While competitors like Anthropic and OpenAI continue to push the boundaries of creative aesthetics and raw intelligence, Google's true advantage lies in its deeply integrated ecosystem. From Workspace tools like Gmail and Google Calendar to unified developer credit systems, Argon is designed to function less like a standalone chatbot and more like a practical enterprise agent. As API prices are set to double in December, developers and businesses have a narrow window to test the waters and optimize their workflows before costs scale up.
