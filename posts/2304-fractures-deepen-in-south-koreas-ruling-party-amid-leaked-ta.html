Fractures Deepen in South Korea's Ruling Party Amid Leaked Tapes and Ethics Feud

November 14, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has descended into open factional warfare following leaked audio recordings of leadership members, retaliatory ethics probes, and failed merger overtures with minor political allies.

  • What happened: Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee faces an ethics investigation after leaked recordings showed her making controversial statements regarding President Lee Jae-myung's past political strategies, hurling insults at colleagues, and calling reporters "hyenas."
  • Factional retaliation: Lawmakers aligned with party leader Jung Cheong-rae boycotted leadership meetings after Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok initiated the ethics probe, demanding Kim himself be investigated instead.
  • Failed coalition talks: Overtures to merge with the minor opposition Cho Kuk Innovation Party collapsed after its leader, Cho Kuk, rejected rumors of talks as a "political show."
  • Who is affected: President Lee Jae-myung’s administration faces a paralyzed ruling party leadership unable to effectively support its legislative agenda.

Leaked Audio Sparks Internal Warfare

The dispute within the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) escalated on November 13 following the release of private audio recordings featuring Choi Min-hee, a member of the party's supreme decision-making body. In the tapes, Choi discussed past internal deliberations, claiming President Lee Jae-myung had consistently pushed for the impeachment of Yoon Suk-yeol—who was later impeached, removed from office, and imprisoned—and sought to partner with Prime Minister Han Duck-soo to induce Yoon Suk-yeol's resignation. Beyond political claims, Choi sparked outrage by alleging that the Unification Church was backing key party figures, branding colleagues "opportunists," and calling journalists "hyenas," which prompted a dignity violation complaint to the National Human Rights Commission.

When Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok directed the party's ethics tribunal to investigate Choi, the party's internal divisions erupted. Lawmakers allied with party chief Jung Cheong-rae launched a collective boycott of leadership meetings, accusing Kim of mounting a targeted strike against rival factions and demanding that Kim himself face an ethics probe. With pro-Lee Jae-myung lawmakers calling for Choi’s outright expulsion, the leadership apparatus of the ruling party has effectively reached a standstill.

Stalled Merger Overtures and Governance Risks

Attempts to pivot away from internal strife by floating a potential merger with the minor Cho Kuk Innovation Party only compounded the ruling party's troubles. Cho Kuk, the party’s leader, forcefully rejected the proposal, stating that integration discussions had not progressed "even a single millimeter" and criticizing DPK figures for using merger rumors for domestic political maneuvering. The initiative also lacks a clear mandate from the party's base; a Gallup poll cited on November 13 showed DPK supporters almost evenly split, with 44% approving a merger and 43% opposing it.

With Supreme Council meetings boycotted and key figures trading public demands for expulsions, the ruling party faces severe leadership turmoil. As the economic climate demands legislative action, the DPK's internal strife threatens to drain the political momentum needed to back President Lee's national policy priorities.
