Political Memo Smear Targets Progressive Commentators Under Lee Administration

October 14, 2026

A circulating anonymous political dossier alleging that influential progressive commentator Kim Ou-joon demanded top cabinet positions from President Lee Jae-myung’s inner circle has ignited fierce pushback among supporters, who view it as a coordinated attempt to undermine systemic prosecutorial reform.

  • The leak: An anonymous political memo (locally termed jjirasi) dated October 13 alleges that Kim demanded key roles—including Prime Minister, Justice Minister, and head of the newly created Serious Crimes Investigation Agency—and retaliated politically after being rejected.
  • The targets: Prominent pro-government media voices, particularly Kim and former minister Rhyu Si-min, who command massive progressive followings and drive public backing for judicial restructuring.
  • The establishment backlash: The dossier follows sharp criticism from progressive commentators against institutional power brokers, including Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de.
  • The broader risk: Stalling the rollout of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency, which was established to permanently strip the state prosecution service of direct investigative powers.

Anatomy of a Fabricated Personnel Scandal

The circulating document reads less like genuine political intelligence and more like an attempt to engineer factional discord within the ruling bloc. Accusing Kim Ou-joon of demanding appointments to run the Justice Ministry or the Senior Presidential Secretariat ignores his historical operating style; even during the Moon Jae-in administration, when he had direct personal access to the presidential office, he pointedly avoided seeking patronage or official titles. Fabricating claims of backroom horse-trading is a well-worn tactic in South Korean politics designed to alienate reform-minded voters from key media figures.

The timing is deliberate. Kim recently leveled pointed public critiques at Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de, arguing that the conservative judicial leadership continues to treat state politics as territory under its personal control. Unable to rebut these critiques on their merits, entrenched judicial and conservative circles appear to be leaning on shadowy whisper networks to damage the credibility of outside commentators who spotlight institutional resistance.

The Battle Over Prosecutorial Reform

The real battle behind this smear campaign centers on the fate of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency. The new agency is the cornerstone of structural reforms designed to ensure that the sweeping investigatory and indictment powers once concentrated in the prosecutor's office can never be weaponized again—an abuse South Koreans witnessed firsthand during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure before his impeachment and imprisonment. Hobbling the agency at its inception is the primary vehicle for those hoping to stage an institutional comeback for the state prosecution.

Discrediting major communication hubs like Kim and Rhyu is intended to sever the communication pipeline between the Lee Jae-myung administration and the grassroots public. If the public becomes disillusioned by rumors of self-serving power grabs, the political capital needed to steer the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency through its fragile early phase will evaporate. For reformers, this memo is not mere political gossip, but an early warning that the judicial establishment has no intention of ceding ground quietly.
