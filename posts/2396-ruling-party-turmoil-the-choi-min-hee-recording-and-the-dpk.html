Ruling Party Turmoil: The Choi Min-hee Recording and the DPK Factional War

November 16, 2026

Kim Min-seok, a senior leader within the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), has ordered an internal ethics investigation into fellow leadership member Choi Min-hee following a leaked recording of her criticizing President Lee Jae-myung’s past political decisions.

  • The Scandal: A leaked recording from an October private meeting shows Choi Min-hee, the DPK’s Chief Supreme Council member, suggesting that President Lee Jae-myung has made strategic errors in the past, specifically regarding the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • The Stakes: If the investigation leads to a suspension of party rights, Choi—an elected official—would be stripped of her leadership post, potentially triggering a power vacuum within the ruling party’s top brass.
  • The Counter-Attack: Supporters of Choi argue the recording was maliciously edited and have retaliated by exposing a private group chat where rival party members allegedly used violent rhetoric and sexual insults against her.
  • The Context: The dispute centers on how the party handled the 2024 "insurrection" and the subsequent imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, revealing deep-seated tensions between the party’s moderate and hardline factions.

Context vs. Character Assassination

The recording in question stems from an October 16 private meeting with party members. In the clip, Choi refers to the period surrounding the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol (the former president currently serving a prison sentence). She noted that President Lee—then the party leader—initially hesitated to push for full impeachment, preferring a "voluntary resignation" path to win over centrist voters. Choi's comment that "even the President’s political judgment isn't always right" has been seized upon by the "Chin-seok" faction (loyalists to Kim Min-seok) as an act of political betrayal.

However, a closer look at the full transcript suggests Choi was making a nuanced point about the Chief Justice, Jo Hee-dae. She was explaining to frustrated supporters why certain impeachments are legally difficult, using President Lee’s past caution as an example of how strategy evolves through debate. Supporters argue that stripping the context makes a defense of the President’s deliberative style look like a personal attack. They point to news archives from December 4, 2024, which confirm that the DPK leadership did indeed initially call for Mr. Yoon's resignation before pivoting to impeachment, validating the factual basis of Choi's remarks.

The Group Chat and Double Standards

The feud escalated significantly when the news outlet Sisa Tapa exposed a "members-only" KakaoTalk group chat (South Korea’s dominant messaging app). Unlike public forums, this chat required real-name verification and an invitation. Leaked screenshots show participants—allegedly including figures linked to Kim Min-seok’s circle—using extreme language, including calls to "kill" Choi and various sexual insults. This has sparked a "double standard" row: why is a nuanced private discussion by a congresswoman being met with an official ethics probe, while violent and misogynistic rhetoric within the party’s inner circles goes unpunished?

The outcome of this investigation will serve as a bellwether for the DPK’s internal stability. If Choi is ousted, it signals a consolidation of power by the Kim Min-seok faction and a crackdown on internal dissent. If she survives, it may embolden the party’s grassroots reformist wing, who view the current leadership's tactics as "old-school" political maneuvering. For President Lee, the optics are poor; a party focused on internal purges over a "slip of the tongue" risks appearing distracted while the People Power Party, the main conservative opposition, looks for any sign of weakness in the ruling administration.
