The People Power Party’s Civil War: Scandals and Savior Complexes

November 03, 2026

The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, is descending into a chaotic leadership struggle characterized by media-driven hype and a failure to address the party’s historical baggage.

  • Leadership Maneuvers: High-profile figures like Han Dong-hoon, the former justice minister, and Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon are being positioned by conservative media as party saviors, despite deep-seated personal animosity among the rank and file.
  • Educational Scandals: Rep. Kang Deuk-gu of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) has filed a formal complaint against Han Dong-hoon, alleging his daughter’s volunteer records and an app development project for the Apple Store were fabricated for university admissions.
  • Internal Fragmentation: Lawmaker Jang Dong-hyuk has signaled a run for the party chairmanship, challenging the dominant Han-Oh narrative and suggesting a potential shake-up in local government leadership.
  • The Shadow of Impeachment: The PPP remains unable to fully decouple itself from the legacy of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president currently in prison after being removed from office.

The Mirage of Unity

Conservative media outlets are currently working overtime to manufacture a narrative of "saving the party" by promoting a unified front between Han Dong-hoon and Oh Se-hoon. However, this is largely a media construct. In reality, the opposition camp is a hornet's nest of mistrust. For instance, the rumored "Hun-Hun Alliance" between Han and veteran politician Ahn Cheol-soo is a fiction; Ahn has been vocally critical of Han, as has Lee Jun-seok, the former PPP leader. These men are not cooperating to help the public; they are sharpening knives for a leadership seat that grants them control over the party's future. Mayor Oh, meanwhile, appears more focused on using his position in Seoul as a springboard for national power than on governing the capital.

The tragedy of this internal warfare is the complete abandonment of "minsaeng," or the people's livelihood. While voters struggle with economic hardship, the PPP’s potential leaders are busy calculating their chances for the next election cycle. Even solemn events, such as visits to funeral halls, are being staged and sold by the press as "pivotal political meetings." This disconnect between the political elite in Yeouido (Seoul’s political district) and the reality of the working class is reaching a breaking point.

A Crisis of Credibility

The most damaging blow to the PPP’s brand is the intensifying scrutiny of Han Dong-hoon’s family. The allegations—that his daughter’s volunteer hours were forged and her software projects were ghost-written—strike at the heart of the educational meritocracy that South Korean voters hold dear. It is deeply ironic that a figure who rose to prominence by championing "law and principle" now finds himself mired in accusations of "parental chance." If these claims of forged credentials prove true, it would represent a total collapse of Han's political moral authority.

Ultimately, the PPP is failing because it is looking backward. While the current government under President Lee Jae-myung moves forward with its agenda, the conservatives are still litigating the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. By catering to fringe elements who still hold protests against Mr. Yoon’s imprisonment or claim "election fraud," the party is alienating the moderate voters it needs to survive. Without a total purge of its "old guard" mindset and a genuine focus on economic stability, the PPP risks becoming a permanent fixture of the political fringe rather than a viable government-in-waiting.
