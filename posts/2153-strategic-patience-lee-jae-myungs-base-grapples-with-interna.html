Strategic Patience: Lee Jae-myung’s Base Grapples with Internal Rifts

November 09, 2026

Supporters of South Korean President Lee Jae-myung are calling for strategic patience and absolute unity as internal factions and legal pressures threaten to fracture the liberal base.

  • Internal Schism: A growing "New Lee Jae-myung" faction is accused of "gaslighting" the liberal base by attacking the legacies of former presidents and demanding that moderate supporters leave the movement.
  • Coalition Tensions: Hardliners are specifically targeting those who support cooperation with the Rebuilding Korea Party, a smaller liberal party led by former Justice Minister Cho Kuk.
  • Legal Pressure: The recent search and seizure of Lee Jong-won, CEO of the pro-government media outlet Sisa Tapa, has sparked outrage among supporters who view it as an Echo of the heavy-handed tactics used during the previous administration.
  • The "Ripening" Strategy: Leadership voices are urging the rank-and-file to avoid premature mobilization, arguing that they must wait for the political situation to "ripen" before launching a decisive counter-offensive.

The "New Lee" Faction and the Threat of Division

The Democratic Party (DPK), South Korea’s ruling liberal party, is currently facing a "divisionist" movement from within. Certain influencers and theorists, self-identifying as the "New Lee Jae-myung" faction, have begun purging those who do not adhere to their strict hardline stance. Figures such as influencer Oh Yun-hye have come under fire for telling supporters of the Rebuilding Korea Party to "get out," a move critics describe as "gaslighting" that only serves the interests of the People Power Party (PPP), the conservative opposition.

This internal friction is more than a simple disagreement over tactics; it is a battle for the party’s soul. The author argues that the DPK’s strength is rooted in a continuous lineage of liberal leaders, from the late Roh Moo-hyun to Moon Jae-in and now President Lee Jae-myung. Attempting to "erase" these predecessors to make room for a narrower "New Lee" identity is viewed as a dangerous gamble that isolates the president and weakens his mandate. The intellectual weight of this new faction is also being questioned, with critics arguing their rhetoric relies on emotional outbursts rather than the strategic depth historically provided by figures like Lee Hae-chan, the DPK’s veteran strategist.

Waiting for the "Pimple" to Ripen

For many liberal voters, the current political atmosphere feels stifling. However, the prevailing advice to the base is to treat this frustration as "time accumulated for victory." Using a vivid local analogy, the author compares the political climate to a ripening pimple: if you squeeze it too early, it only causes pain and scarring; you must wait until it is "yellow" and ready to burst. This "wait-and-see" approach suggests that the administration's enemies—specifically the remnants of the ousted administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—are acting out of desperation because they have been pushed to the brink.

The briefing concludes that the liberal base remains overwhelmingly stronger than the vocal minority of "divisionists." The strategy moving forward is one of "stillness in motion"—maintaining daily support for the government while conserving energy for a massive, singular mobilization when the political "inflection point" is reached. By protecting key media voices like Lee Jong-won and maintaining the alliance between the president and the party’s elder statesmen, the administration aims to weather current provocations and emerge with a unified front.
