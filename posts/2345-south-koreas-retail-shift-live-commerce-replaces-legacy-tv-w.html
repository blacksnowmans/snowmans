South Korea's Retail Shift: Live Commerce Replaces Legacy TV with Star Power and Farm Directs

November 15, 2026

South Korea’s live-commerce ecosystem is transitioning from simple mobile simulcasts into an end-to-end retail model where creators participate directly in product development and traditional television networks scramble to pivot online.

  • Legacy home shopping networks are ditching the TV screen: Squeezed by declining television viewership and rising transmission fees, South Korea's three major home shopping broadcasters are aggressively pivoting toward 24-hour mobile live streams and short-form video.
  • Creators and A-listers are taking over production: Influencers and top celebrities—including global star Lee Jung-jae—are driving hundreds of thousands of dollars in single-broadcast sales, shifting their roles from simple pitchmen to equity partners and product planners.
  • Rural producers and regional schools embrace direct streaming: Local institutions like Kyungbuk Science College are running practical streaming academies for smallholders, allowing farmers to bypass wholesale middlemen and ship fresh produce, like peppers, straight from the harvest field.
  • The model expands across Southeast Asia: Similar mobile shifts are transforming markets like Vietnam, where government-backed regional specialty programs (such as OCOP) combine media production training with direct-to-consumer live broadcasts in urban hubs like Hanoi.

From Passive Pitching to Full-Stack Collaboration

South Korean retail is no longer treating live commerce as an auxiliary sales channel. Historically, brands contracted show hosts or internet personalities simply to read scripts in front of a camera. Today, retailers are building collaborative tripartite models with manufacturers and creators who maintain passionate, highly engaged fanbases. These creators participate in early product ideation, packaging design, and fulfillment strategy, often operating on revenue-sharing terms rather than one-off appearance fees.

The consumer psychology here is straightforward: direct participation builds immediate trust. When top-tier actors like Lee Jung-jae appear on mobile live platforms, the events blur the line between a flash sale and an interactive fan meeting. Viewers use live chat to interact with stars and hosts in real time, driving immediate checkout impulses that traditional TV commercials cannot replicate. By slicing these live broadcasts into viral short-form clips post-show, retailers capture an extended secondary promotional cycle across social platforms.

Survival Pivot for Home Shopping and Rural Producers

For South Korea's traditional television shopping operators, the shift to mobile is an existential imperative. Skyrocketing cable transmission fees coupled with cord-cutting have broken the economics of the traditional 60-minute cable broadcast. In response, networks are adapting their high-end studio production talent into fast-paced, 1-to-2-minute mobile commerce formats to attract younger buyers.

Meanwhile, the democratization of live streaming is redefining regional agriculture. By cutting out multilayered distribution networks, rural farmers are keeping full margins while offering verified freshness straight from the field. As local municipalities and academic institutions fund digital broadcast training, the barrier to entry continues to drop. Whether in South Korean agricultural counties or urban trade centers across Southeast Asia, the merchant who controls the live mobile feed now holds the leverage in Asian retail.
