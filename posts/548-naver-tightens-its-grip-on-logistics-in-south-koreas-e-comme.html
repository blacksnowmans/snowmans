Naver Tightens Its Grip on Logistics in South Korea's E-Commerce Wars

September 10, 2026

South Korea's dominant web portal Naver is locking its early-morning delivery behind its paid membership wall, shifting from a hands-off marketplace intermediary into a direct logistics controller to challenge rival Coupang.

  • The shift: Starting late November, Naver will restrict its early-morning delivery service exclusively to subscribers of its Naver Plus Membership.
  • The playbook: The move mirrors the paid-membership, speed-driven logistics model perfected by Coupang, South Korea's largest e-commerce player.
  • The impact: Small merchants face tougher delivery requirements and higher fees to maintain search visibility, while Naver gains tighter control over supply chain data.
  • The partnership: Naver has also teamed up with upscale grocery delivery platform Kurly for fresh food delivery, seeing transactions surge tenfold since launch.

From Loose Alliance to Direct Control

For years, Naver relied on an "asset-light" strategy, avoiding the massive capital expenditures and labor risks associated with owning warehouses and fulfillment centers. Instead, it built the Naver Fulfillment Alliance in 2021—a cooperative network linking third-party logistics providers and startups to handle deliveries for sellers on Naver Smart Store. While this approach helped drive a 15.5% jump in second-quarter open-market transactions, it created a structural flaw: inconsistent delivery speeds and varied pricing among partners prevented Naver from matching the uniform customer experience offered by Coupang.

Now, Naver is dismantling that horizontal alliance. The company is rapidly scaling direct contracts with sellers, aiming to route half of all "N-Delivery" volume through these direct agreements by the end of the year. This shift gives Naver complete pricing power, the ability to enforce strict service-level agreements like 7-day and midnight-cutoff deliveries, and direct access to valuable inventory and return data. By reshaping contract structures rather than owning physical warehouses, Naver is seizing the reins of its logistics network.

The Squeeze on Independent Sellers

As Naver ties search visibility directly to fast-delivery badges, merchants find themselves caught in a high-stakes dilemma. Appearing at the top of search results now depends on carrying the N-Delivery badge—usage of which surged 76% year-over-year by June—rather than simply running ads. To earn that badge, sellers must meet brutal fulfillment schedules, including night and weekend dispatches.

This puts immense financial pressure on small-scale merchants. Entering Naver's designated fulfillment network incurs steep packaging, storage, and handling costs—often running around 3,500 won (about $2.50) per small item, significantly higher than standard courier rates. Merchants face a painful trade-off: join the system and watch profit margins get eaten by rising logistics expenses, or stay out and risk losing search visibility entirely. While Naver is simply adopting the playbook used by Coupang and its Rocket Growth service, the transition threatens to turn independent sellers into mere cogs within a rigid corporate machine.
