Investigative Reporter Challenges Authorities Over Polling Manipulation Scandal

October 24, 2026

South Korean investigative journalist Chang In-soo is openly daring law enforcement to raid his premises, threatening in return to expose suppressed evidence of a 255-member group chat that allegedly orchestrated primary poll manipulation.

  • The threat: Law enforcement agencies appear poised to execute search-and-seizure warrants against Chang following intrusive interrogations regarding his reporting, according to colleagues at independent media outlets.
  • The counter-allegation: Chang claims authorities are deliberately ignoring "The Hana," a 255-member chatroom involving key political figures—such as Kim Yong, former deputy head of the Democratic Party's think tank, the Institute for Democracy—that coordinated party members to pose as ordinary citizens to skew election polling.
  • The apparent double standard: The police's Anti-Corruption and Public Crimes Investigation Unit recently raided the home of Lee Jong-won, head of the YouTube channel Sisa Tapa, over spoken commentary during a party convention, while leaving the coordinated chatroom untouched.
  • The wider fallout: The standoff adds fuel to an escalating confrontation between President Lee Jae-myung's administration and the conservative-led judiciary under Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.

Coordinated Interference and Selective Enforcement

The controversy underscores persistent grievances over selective justice in South Korea's legal apparatus. While authorities moved aggressively against Lee Jong-won for on-air political commentary, they have stalled on explicit evidence of organized polling fraud. Leaked screenshots from "The Hana" reveal members circulating manuals instructing partisans who had already voted to answer public surveys by falsely claiming they were not party members. Internal messages demonstrate participants knew this cross-over voting scheme was illicit, openly fretting about leaks while coordinating their ballots.

The presence of prominent figures like Kim Yong elevates the matter from fringe online activism to potential systemic election meddling. Chang’s public challenge—daring prosecutors to see if they can handle the fallout of raiding him—signals that he holds documentation tracing the illicit polling directives directly to political insiders.

Judicial Clashes and the Battle for Reform

This fight plays directly into a broader clash between the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) and the judiciary. Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae has drawn heavy fire from the ruling camp for bypassing consultation with the presidential office when recommending Supreme Court justices, prompting senior DPK lawmakers to accuse him of staging a "judicial coup." That tension is aggravated by recent rulings, including the appellate acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon—a key figure in the main opposition People Power Party—over allegations that a third-party supporter paid roughly 21 million won (about $15,000) for private campaign surveys conducted by an election broker.

To critics, the current situation echoes past abuses, from state-backed online opinion rigging under earlier conservative administrations to aggressive media crackdowns during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure before his impeachment and imprisonment. By daring authorities to move against him, Chang is turning an individual investigation into an urgent test of whether President Lee's administration can rein in entrenched prosecutorial and judicial privileges.
