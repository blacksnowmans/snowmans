South Korea Briefing: Polling Contradictions, Martial Law Sentencing, and Market Stability

September 23, 2026

  • Polling Discrepancies: South Korean presidential approval ratings swing by over 10 percentage points depending on whether data is collected via live telephone interviews or automated response systems (ARS), driven by high-turnout partisan respondents on automated lines.
  • Martial Law Sentences: A Seoul court handed down prison sentences ranging from 10 to 18 years to top military commanders for a 2024 martial law conspiracy, citing seized phone memos outlining fabricated border crises.
  • US Investment Pressures: President Lee Jae-myung's administration faces tough US negotiations over massive energy and nuclear investments, as Washington attempts to strip out "commercial rationality" safety clauses.
  • Market Recovery: The Kospi stock index rebounded past the 7,000-point mark, easing investor anxiety ahead of the Chuseok autumn harvest festival.

Polls, Protests, and Policy Missteps

South Korean public opinion polling is experiencing a credibility crisis. Depending on the methodology used, the same presidential press conference can yield a positive rating of 50.5% (via live phone interviews) or a negative rating of 53.3% (via automated ARS systems). This massive gap stems from response bias: phone interviews capture ordinary, moderate citizens who answer out of politeness, while ARS surveys—notoriously plagued by low response rates—are dominated by highly politically engaged, partisan extremes.

This statistical noise coincides with real political friction. President Lee's administration has seen a notable drop in support among moderates, driven by policy ambivalence over constitutional reform and prosecutorial overhauls. Combined with shifting housing markets—where super-luxury property prices in Seoul's Gangnam district face intense tax pressures while broader gains flatten—the ruling bloc is struggling to maintain its reform momentum. Chief of Staff Kang Hoon-shik has offered his resignation, signaling that a major staff reshuffle is underway in the presidential office.

Military Conspiracies and Economic Relief

In the judiciary, the Seoul Central District Court delivered landmark 1st-trial verdicts against former military commanders for their roles in a 2024 martial law plot. Seized mobile phone memos revealed chilling plans by commanders to provoke North Korean military incidents to manufacture a security crisis, justifying the suspension of parliament. The court's harsh sentences—ranging up to 18 years for former Defense Counterintelligence Commander Yeo In-hyung—serve as a severe warning against attempts to subvert constitutional order.

On the economic front, local markets have offered rare good news. The Kospi index climbed back to the 7,000 mark as worries over AI semiconductor valuations and interest rate hikes subsided, providing a welcome psychological boost to retail investors ahead of the Chuseok holiday. As South Korea navigates high-stakes diplomatic friction ahead of President Lee's upcoming UN General Assembly address and a state visit to Mexico, the domestic mood remains a fragile balance between institutional anxiety and everyday resilience.
