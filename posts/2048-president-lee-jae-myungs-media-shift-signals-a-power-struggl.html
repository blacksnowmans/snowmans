President Lee Jae-myung’s Media Shift Signals a Power Struggle in Korea’s Liberal Landscape

November 06, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung has declared he is not "trapped in algorithms," publicly discarding a protective narrative spun by star podcaster Kim Eo-jun and signaling a major recalibration of power within South Korea's liberal media ecosystem.

  • What happened: President Lee launched a personal YouTube broadcast and explicitly rejected claims that he is shielded from online criticism, directly refuting a long-standing defense framed by influential liberal podcaster Kim Eo-jun.
  • What it changes: The move shifts the center of media authority away from independent commentators and directly into the presidential office and a rival network of aggressive pro-Lee YouTubers.
  • Who is affected: Veteran commentator Kim Eo-jun faces potential isolation, while emerging pro-Lee media channels gain leverage over the ruling Democratic Party of Korea’s digital base.
  • The underlying dynamic: President Lee is shedding his legacy narrative as a persecuted political outsider to consolidate direct command over public messaging.

Ditching the Shield of Independent Podcasters

During his years in the opposition enduring intense political pressure under Yoon Suk-yeol—the former president who was later impeached and imprisoned—Lee Jae-myung relied heavily on allied commentators like Kim Eo-jun to rally his supporters. Influential podcasters routinely constructed defensive shields for Lee, arguing that negative press or political missteps were merely the result of distorted information algorithms isolating him. By publicly declaring on his new YouTube broadcast that he personally monitors harsh online criticism and insults, President Lee has dismantled that shield, signaling that his administration will no longer rely on external commentators to manage his image.

Consolidating Power Over the Liberal Media Ecosystem

This shift directly aligns with an ongoing initiative by hardline pro-Lee YouTube channels, such as those associated with commentator Lee Dong-hyung, to dismantle Kim Eo-jun’s long-held dominance over South Korea's left-wing media. As prominent political writer Rhyu Si-min previously observed, Lee has completed his transition from the nation's most targeted political outsider to its supreme power holder—making reliance on defensive victimhood narratives fundamentally obsolete. By publicly distancing himself from Kim's framing, President Lee has given green-light authority to rival pro-administration YouTubers to discipline the broader liberal media landscape, forcing podcasters to align directly with the presidential office or risk being branded as opposition figures.
