South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party Fractures Over Referral of Lawmaker Choi Min-hee to Ethics Board

November 16, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party has descended into acute internal turmoil following leadership's rapid decision on November 15 to refer lawmaker Choi Min-hee to the party's Ethics Tribunal over her recent controversial public remarks.

  • What happened: Democratic Party Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok referred lawmaker Choi Min-hee to the party's Ethics Tribunal just three days after initiating an internal inspection into her aggressive public statements.
  • Factional fallout: The swift disciplinary move triggered an open power struggle, pitting loyalists of President Lee Jae-myung demanding Choi’s expulsion against figures aligned with lawmaker Jung Chung-rae who warn against targeted purges.
  • Legislative reaction: The party’s freshman lawmaker coalition held an emergency meeting late on November 15, agreeing on the severity of the crisis while delaying a formal statement until the next day.
  • Legal escalation: A civic group filed a criminal complaint with the police against Choi on November 15, transforming an internal party dispute into a dual political and legal crisis.

Escalating Factional Warfare Inside the Ruling Camp

The leadership's decision to bypass political mediation and push Choi’s case directly to the Ethics Tribunal underscores how severely her remarks have rattled party discipline. Pro-Lee Jae-myung faction members insist that anything short of heavy punishment or expulsion will damage public trust and derail the ruling party's reform agenda. In contrast, lawmakers aligned with lawmaker Jung Chung-rae fear the ethics probe is being weaponized to purge internal dissent, even as Jung himself maintains public silence.

The rift has mobilized the party's broader legislative base. "Deomincho," an association of first-term Democratic Party lawmakers, convened an urgent gathering on November 15 to debate collective action. While consensus was reached on the gravity of Choi's conduct, deep divisions over the appropriate severity of punishment forced the group to push its final statement to November 16. The tribunal's pending verdict—ranging from a simple warning to full expulsion—is set to test leadership's control over the party.

Mounting Legal Risks and Opposition Strategy

The controversy extended beyond parliament on November 15 when a civic group filed an official police complaint against Choi over her statements. Facing dual pressures from a police investigation and internal disciplinary procedures, Choi’s legal troubles threaten to strip the ruling party of its political momentum during crucial parliamentary proceedings.

Meanwhile, the main conservative opposition People Power Party is eager to exploit the crisis. Although the opposition remains embroiled in its own internal feud regarding former emergency committee chief Han Dong-hoon's potential reinstatement, the ruling party's self-inflicted chaos provides a prime opportunity for conservative counteroffensives during ongoing legislative and budget negotiations.
