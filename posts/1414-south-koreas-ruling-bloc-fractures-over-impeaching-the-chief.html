South Korea’s Ruling Bloc Fractures Over Impeaching the Chief Justice

October 23, 2026

South Korea’s ruling progressive bloc is clashing internally over whether to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, as the push for sweeping judicial and prosecutorial reforms reaches a volatile flashpoint.

  • Factional divide in the ruling camp: While hardliners within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) and the allied minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party demand Cho’s immediate impeachment, DPK leadership figures, including Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok, are urging tactical restraint to prevent a political backlash.
  • Conservative opposition unites: The People Power Party, South Korea’s main conservative opposition, and the minor conservative Reform Party declared joint resistance, accusing the government of attempting to intimidate and subordinate the judiciary.
  • Audit fireworks over prosecutorial reform: In the final stretch of the National Assembly’s annual audit, lawmakers traded sharp barbs over dropping past criminal charges brought against President Lee Jae-myung and transferring prosecutorial authority to a new indictment agency.
  • Economic battles overshadowed: High-stakes policy decisions—including a delay in taxing cryptocurrency gains and a contentious 3 trillion won (about $2.2 billion) proposal to merge the nation’s service academies—face paralysis amid the escalating institutional confrontation.

Judicial Reform Meets Coalition Friction

The push to remove Chief Justice Cho represents a high-risk gamble for the DPK. Riding a wave of support from voters demanding a purge of entrenched judicial and prosecutorial networks, hardline lawmakers argue that hesitation will squander momentum. That pressure is amplified by the Rebuilding Korea Party, whose aggressive reform stance has brought it to the verge of double-digit polling, boxing the DPK into a corner where moderation risks looking like retreat.

Yet senior DPK leaders are wary of overreach. An impeachment of the nation's top judge requires clear, undeniable evidence of constitutional or statutory violations. Without transparent proof, the ruling bloc risks handing the opposition a potent weapon: the accusation that the ruling party is dismantling judicial independence to protect President Lee from remaining legal entanglements.

A Lifeline for the Conservative Opposition

For the fractured conservative opposition, the DPK’s impeachment push provides an unexpected rallying point. Still reeling from the political fallout surrounding the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the People Power Party and the Reform Party have found common ground in framing themselves as defenders of constitutional checks and balances. Their emerging alliance threatens to plunge parliament into prolonged gridlock.

This escalating battle comes at an inconvenient moment for the presidential office. While President Lee attempts to spotlight economic revival, and the cabinet scrambles to deliver on pledged urban housing for young families, the political oxygen in Seoul is once again being consumed by retribution and institutional warfare. If the ruling camp proceeds with the impeachment without decisive public buy-in, it risks turning a mandate for governance into an exhausting battle of attrition.
