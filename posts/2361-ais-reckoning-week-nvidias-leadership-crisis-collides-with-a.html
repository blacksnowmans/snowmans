AI’s Reckoning Week: Nvidia’s Leadership Crisis Collides with Anthropic’s $87 Billion IPO

November 16, 2026

A pivotal week for global artificial intelligence has arrived as Nvidia faces a severe leadership vacuum ahead of its November 18 earnings report while generative AI pioneer Anthropic prepares for a massive Nasdaq debut on November 19.

  • What happened: Nvidia is reeling from the sudden resignation of CEO Jensen Huang amid workplace abuse allegations and tightening US export controls, while Anthropic is set to list on the Nasdaq at an estimated valuation of 120 trillion won (about $87 billion) despite posting a $104 billion net loss over the past nine months.
  • What it changes: US regulators are closing proxy routes by banning remote cloud access for Chinese firms, while Anthropic successfully ported its models to AMD hardware in just three days, cutting infrastructure costs from $40 billion to $20 billion per gigawatt and cracking Nvidia’s long-standing CUDA software monopoly.
  • Who is affected: Global markets are experiencing extreme liquidity shifts—with Bitcoin dropping 15.6% to $62,000 as capital drains into Anthropic’s IPO pool—while South Korean chipmaker SK Hynix faces supply chain uncertainty and SK Telecom stands to gain from its early equity stake in Anthropic.

Nvidia’s Stumble Opens Doors for Hardware Competition

Nvidia’s dominance is facing unprecedented strain following Jensen Huang’s resignation after reports surfaced of internal workplace abuse. The fallout comes at a precarious time: Nvidia’s $34 billion data center expansion in Southeast Asia is under review over concerns that Chinese firms were using the region to circumvent US export controls. With a 4 trillion won (about $2.9 billion) data center project in China frozen and US lawmakers moving to ban remote cloud access for foreign adversaries, Nvidia’s 213 trillion won ($153 billion) circular financing ecosystem is taking a direct hit just as the company prepares to release its quarterly results without its long-time chief executive.

This leadership vacuum has created a rare opening for rivals. Anthropic recently demonstrated that its large language models could be fully ported to AMD’s accelerator architecture in only three days. By proving that AI developers do not need to rely exclusively on Nvidia’s proprietary CUDA software layer, Anthropic has signaled that hardware costs can be slashed nearly in half. As a result, the AI chip landscape is shifting from a single-player monopoly toward a multi-vendor market.

Capital Drain and the Impact on South Korean Tech

Meanwhile, Anthropic’s impending IPO has become a liquidity black hole. Retail investor orders have exceeded six times the available shares, drawing roughly $64.4 billion out of alternative risk assets and causing Bitcoin to drop to $62,000 amid rising 10-year US Treasury yields. Under strict US regulatory mandates, institutional investors from China and Hong Kong have been entirely barred from participating in the offering, underscoring how deeply geopolitical tensions now dictate AI capital flows.

In South Korea, where the benchmark KOSPI index is striving to regain momentum, the shift in AI power dynamics presents mixed outcomes. SK Hynix, South Korea's second-largest chipmaker and a critical supplier of High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) chips to Nvidia, is monitoring Nvidia's earnings and leadership crisis closely. Conversely, telecommunications provider SK Telecom expects significant valuation gains from its early strategic investment in Anthropic, while tech giant Samsung Electronics and IT firm Samsung SDS stand to benefit as model developers increasingly diversify their hardware and multi-cloud infrastructure choices.
