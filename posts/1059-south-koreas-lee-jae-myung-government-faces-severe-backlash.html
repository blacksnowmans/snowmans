South Korea's Lee Jae-myung Government Faces Severe Backlash and Plummeting Approval Ratings

October 13, 2026

  • South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is facing a sharp drop in support, driven by the loss of his core progressive base rather than standard political fluctuations.
  • Key progressive figures, including Professor Choi Bae-geun and political commentator Rhyu Si-min, have publicly broken ties with the administration amid brewing scandals and looming exposés.
  • Critics point to controversial appointments—such as Kim Ji-yong as head of the Serious Crimes Investigation Office—and a preference for loyal yes-men over competent reformers as central drivers of the crisis.
  • Struggling under economic pressures, particularly housing and tax policies, the administration is seeing its reform credibility evaporate among the very voters who brought it to power.

Cracks in the Progressive Coalition and the Politics of Loyalty

South Korea's progressive camp is fracturing at an astonishing speed. Just months after a triumphant election, President Lee Jae-myung is locking horns with the very intellectuals and activists who built his political coalition. Professor Choi Bae-geun formally withdrew his support, accusing the administration of abandoning promised reforms. Instead of engaging with the criticism, the presidential office dismissed the dissent as bitterness from someone angling for a government job—a defensive reflex that alienated allies and signaled a deep intolerance for internal critique.

This insular governance style is further highlighted by commentator Rhyu Si-min's outright refusal to hold a private meeting with the president. Rhyu warned that the administration's current trajectory on key issues like prosecutorial reform amounts to deceiving the public. Rather than surrounding himself with sharp, independent minds who might challenge policy, President Lee appears to favor compliant figures who ask few questions. The controversial appointment of Kim Ji-yong to head the Serious Crimes Investigation Office encapsulates this trend, trading administrative competence and reformist grit for unquestioning loyalty.

A Freefall in Public Trust and the Erosion of Core Support

The political fallout is showing up clearly in the numbers. Approval ratings have plunged from their post-election highs into volatile territory, setting new lows across major polling firms. Observers note that this is not the usual dip caused by minor policy missteps; it is an abandonment by the core base that previously rallied behind the administration as an alternative to the imprisoned former leader Yoon Seok-youl.

Economic anxiety, particularly around mishandled real estate policies and taxation frames, has left voters cold. When combined with an administration that acknowledges communication gaps but changes no underlying behaviors, public cynicism deepens. For an administration that promised sweeping reform, treating dissent as betrayal and stacking key agencies with political operators risks gutting the government's momentum entirely. If the core coalition continues to walk away, the consequences for upcoming legislative battles will be severe.
