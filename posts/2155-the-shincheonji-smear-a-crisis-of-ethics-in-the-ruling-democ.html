The "Shincheonji" Smear: A Crisis of Ethics in the Ruling Democratic Party

November 09, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party is facing a backlash from within its own ranks over allegations that leadership figures used a fabricated "cult connection" scandal to sideline rivals during its last national convention.

  • The Accusation: High-ranking officials in the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) claimed a "triple secret alliance" existed between internal rivals and Shincheonji, a controversial religious sect widely viewed as a cult.
  • The Evidence: Despite a 100-page internal investigation, President Lee Jae-myung has admitted the claims were groundless, and suspicious voters accounted for only 0.4% of the party base.
  • The Cover-up: The party leadership has refused to make the full report public, allowing lawmakers only 30 minutes of supervised viewing, which critics call an attempt to hide a "successful political maneuver" based on lies.
  • The Shift: Amidst this internal rift, the party is pivoting its policy toward relaxing the Comprehensive Real Estate Tax (a wealth tax) for homes worth up to 1.4 billion KRW (about $1 million).

A "Successful" Maneuver Built on Lies

The scandal centers on the "triple secret alliance" theory—a claim that anti-leadership factions teamed up with Shincheonji members to infiltrate the party’s voting bloc. Kim Min-seok, a member of the Supreme Council (the party’s top decision-making body), recently offered a vague apology for the "concerns" raised. However, skeptics point out that the numbers don't add up: only about 3,300 suspicious voters were identified out of 1.5 million members. Critics within the party, including Supreme Council member Lee Sung-yoon, now label the incident a "successful political maneuver," where false accusations were used to secure victory and are now being swept under the rug in the name of "party unity."

Lee Sung-yoon even drew a provocative parallel to the 1990s, when prosecutors argued that a "successful coup cannot be punished." He warned that by justifying a win through fabrication and "black propaganda," the DPK is adopting the very tactics it once condemned in far-right groups. The refusal to publish the full report has led to accusations of "cabinet politics," where the leadership hides its mistakes to avoid accountability under the Party Act, which strictly prohibits spreading false information during internal elections.

Identity Crisis and Tax Populism

This internal ethical decay coincides with a controversial rightward shift in economic policy. The DPK is currently pushing the government to raise the tax-exemption threshold for the Comprehensive Real Estate Tax to 1.4 billion KRW (about $1 million). This would effectively shield wealthy non-resident homeowners—those owning high-value property they do not live in—from the wealth tax. For a party that brands itself as the champion of the working class and "tax justice," this move toward pro-wealthy populism is raising eyebrows.

By shielding the rich from taxes while simultaneously using "cult-baiting" tactics against internal rivals, the ruling party is moving away from the progressive values of its predecessors. The current leadership appears to be using "people's livelihoods" as a shield to deflect from internal corruption. If the DPK cannot clean its own house and provide transparency regarding the Shincheonji smear, it risks losing the moral high ground it has long claimed over the main opposition, the People Power Party. In the era of President Lee Jae-myung, the party’s greatest threat may not be the imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, but its own willingness to sacrifice principles for political survival.
