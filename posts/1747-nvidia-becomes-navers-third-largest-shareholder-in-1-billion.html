Nvidia Becomes Naver’s Third-Largest Shareholder in $1 Billion AI Alliance

October 31, 2026

Nvidia is investing $1 billion (approximately 1.4 trillion KRW) in Naver, South Korea’s dominant search portal and tech giant, to anchor a massive regional AI infrastructure project.

  • The Deal: Nvidia will become Naver’s third-largest shareholder through a third-party share issuance, with the capital injection scheduled for completion on November 6.
  • The "AI Factory": The funds will jumpstart a $10 billion (about 13.5 trillion KRW) initiative to build "AI Factories"—high-performance data centers designed to "manufacture" artificial intelligence for B2B clients.
  • Strategic Shift: The partnership moves Naver beyond a domestic search engine into a regional provider of "Sovereign AI," offering localized AI infrastructure to Asian markets and governments.
  • Market Reaction: Despite the historic scale of the investment, Naver’s stock price initially dipped as investors weighed long-term growth against short-term share dilution.

A Pivot from Search to AI Infrastructure

This is not a simple procurement contract; it is a capital alliance that fundamentally alters the Korean tech landscape. For years, critics questioned if Naver could survive the global AI arms race against Silicon Valley titans who spend tens of billions annually on hardware. By bringing Nvidia—the world’s primary gatekeeper of AI chips—directly onto its cap table, Naver has secured both the capital and the guaranteed supply of GPUs needed to remain relevant.

The core of this deal is the "AI Factory" blueprint. Unlike traditional data centers that merely store data, these facilities are designed as intelligence powerhouses capable of running massive Large Language Models (LLMs) and "Physical AI" for robotics and autonomous driving. This aligns with the Lee Jae-myung administration’s current push for national digital transformation. While similar digital initiatives stalled during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure, this private-sector alliance provides the actual industrial muscle to make "AI sovereignty" a reality rather than just a political slogan.

The Price of Survival and the Road to 400,000 KRW

To secure this future, Lee Hae-jin, Naver’s founder and Global Investment Officer (GIO), has made a calculated sacrifice. By allowing Nvidia to take a major stake, Lee is accepting the dilution of his own control. In the context of Korean corporate culture, where founders often prioritize absolute control over external growth, this is a rare and pragmatic "bet-the-company" move. He is choosing to own a smaller piece of a global AI contender rather than 100% of a fading domestic search engine.

However, the market remains skeptical. While analysts are busy raising price targets to 400,000 KRW (about $290), retail investors are frustrated by the immediate share price stagnation. The "sell on news" phenomenon reflects a cold reality: the market has priced in the hype and is now waiting for receipts. For the "Nvidia Alliance" to be viewed as a success, Naver must now prove it can convert this $10 billion infrastructure plan into tangible B2B revenue. The technical foundation is now in place; the next challenge is proving that the world wants to buy what Naver’s new factories are producing.
