Four New Korean Films Are Turning Cinema into Political and Historical Resistance

October 28, 2026

  • A wave of politically charged new South Korean films—including Assassination Plot 1974 and The Meryl Streep Project—is using cinema as a tool for historical memory and social activism.
    •
  • Assassination Plot 1974 unearths dark chapters of modern Korean history, including political collusion with Japanese yakuza tied to the 1965 Korea-Japan Normalization Treaty.
  • Survival drama Heart of the Beast and documentary A Flowing Journey draw parallels between physical isolation, state secrecy, and the fight for women's solidarity in post-2016 South Korea.
  • Critics argue that supporting these films goes beyond leisure, functioning as a vital civic defense against political erasure and historical amnesia.

Unearthing Dark History and Demanding Accountability

In South Korea, going to the theater has increasingly become an act of political alignment. A new crop of independent releases is directly challenging audiences to confront uncomfortable truths about state power, corruption, and systemic erasure. At the forefront is Assassination Plot 1974, a film digging into the authoritarian past, specifically examining how South Korean political elites and intelligence figures worked alongside Japanese organized crime networks. Alongside a matching book-funding campaign on Aladdin (a major South Korean online bookstore), the project aims to preserve historical records that officials would rather leave buried.

Yet, these films face an uphill battle. As box-office screening times dwindle, the physical books and records tied to them risk disappearing from public view. Observers note that when historical records fade, political corruption becomes easier to justify. By pairing theatrical screenings with public discussions featuring directors and historical witnesses, these filmmakers are trying to turn passive audiences into active participants in a broader struggle for historical truth.

Survival, Solidarity, and the Politics of Information

The trend extends beyond historical exposés into metaphors of modern alienation. Heart of the Beast uses a brutal, 1-man-and-dog wilderness survival setup to mirror a different kind of extreme environment: information isolation. In a society grappling with opaque state information and political cover-ups, the grueling effort required to extract hidden truths feels intimately connected to the physical hardships on screen. Meanwhile, A Flowing Journey and The Meryl Streep Project trace the lineage of South Korea's feminist movement, mapping the ripple effects of the watershed 2016 Gangnam Station murder case onto personal journeys of artistic pursuit and collective solidarity.

Ultimately, these works reject the idea that cinema is mere escapism. In a polarized political landscape where critical art often faces targeted framing attacks, choosing to buy a ticket for these projects is being framed as an intentional civic declaration. As these films fight to keep difficult conversations alive, they remind viewers that forgetting the past is a luxury a democracy cannot afford.
