Democratic Party Leadership Faces Backlash Over Shincheonji Infiltration Probe

November 04, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party of Korea is facing growing internal friction after party leadership attempted to abruptly settle a months-long investigation into alleged mass sign-ups by a controversial fringe religious group with a brief apology rather than releasing a full public report.

  • What happened: An internal panel led by Rep. Park Sun-won concluded that rumors of coordinated mass sign-ups by Shincheonji, a controversial religious sect, were groundless, but party leader Kim Min-seok restricted full access to the report and offered a quick apology to close the matter.
  • The controversy: Lawmakers within the party, including Rep. Jung Chung-rae, criticized the leadership for sweeping the findings under the rug and relying on contradictory logic to avoid taking formal political responsibility.
  • Who is affected: Members and supporters of the Democratic Party of Korea, South Korea's ruling liberal party, who are questioning internal transparency and governance under President Lee Jae-myung's administration.
  • The legal catch: Asserting that Shincheonji members joined the party without verifiable evidence creates a dilemma, as acquiring official church rosters is restricted under South Korea’s personal information protection laws.

A Patchwork Solution to an Internal Crisis

For over two months following the Democratic Party's national convention, rumors circulated that members of Shincheonji had systematically joined the party to exert political influence. When an internal investigation led by Rep. Park Sun-won concluded that these allegations of coordinated mass enlistment were factually unfounded, party leadership had an opportunity to clear the air. Instead, party leader Kim Min-seok limited access to the findings to private viewings by lawmakers and attempted to wrap up the scandal with a brief apology in the name of party unity.

This decision has backfired, sparking accusations of a cover-up. Critics such as Rep. Jung Chung-rae argue that claiming "Shincheonji members existed but there was no third-party collusion" creates a self-contradictory narrative. Because South Korea’s Personal Information Protection Act strictly guards religious membership records, asserting that church members enrolled en masse without verifiable proof raises troubling questions: either the leadership obtained private data through unauthorized means, or it relies on unverified claims to manage internal politics.

Political Maneuvering Over Transparency

The core issue troubling party supporters is not just the original rumor, but how party leadership chose to handle it. Rather than demonstrating the transparency expected of a governing party, the leadership's top-down handling of the probe feels like an attempt to suppress internal debate and neutralize political rivals. Using high-volatility religious controversies as political tools threatens to erode trust among the party's own base.

If the ruling party hopes to maintain public trust following the political shifts that led to the removal of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol and the election of President Lee Jae-myung, it must move past evasive compromises. True unity requires laying out facts transparently, fixing structural vulnerabilities, and prioritizing open governance over short-term political maneuvering.
