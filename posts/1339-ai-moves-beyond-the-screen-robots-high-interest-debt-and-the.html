AI Moves Beyond the Screen: Robots, High-Interest Debt, and the "Shadow AI" Dilemma

October 20, 2026

The AI landscape is shifting from digital chatbots to "Physical AI" and autonomous agents, triggering a scramble for capital and new social responsibilities among global tech giants.

  • Anthropic, a leading AI safety and research company, is pledging a 5% stake to a public interest fund to address job displacement, as its entry-level hiring rate plunged from 27% to 9%.
  • OpenAI, the creator of ChatGPT, is expanding its footprint in South Korea with a developer event in Seoul on October 22, while announcing plans to launch humanoid robots by 2027.
  • LG Electronics, South Korea's dominant home appliance manufacturer, has signaled a pivot toward "Physical AI," integrating intelligence directly into hardware and living spaces.
  • Anthropic is reportedly seeking roughly $8.7 billion (12 trillion KRW) in junior bonds with interest rates exceeding 10% to fund massive infrastructure and chip procurement.
  • Corporate South Korea is grappling with "AI cost bombs" and "Shadow AI"—the unauthorized use of external AI models—leading to new security protocols and "digital employee" management systems.

The Hardware Pivot: From Chatbots to Humanoids

The era of the simple chatbot is ending as AI moves into the physical world. OpenAI’s decision to target 2027 for humanoid robot production marks a transition from software to hardware, a move that directly intersects with South Korea’s industrial strengths. LG Electronics is already rebranding its 68-year history of appliance manufacturing around "Physical AI," envisioning homes that understand and act on behalf of residents. For a manufacturing powerhouse like Korea, this transition is existential; it is the bridge between a hardware heritage and a software-defined future. Local governments and agencies under the Lee Jae-myung administration are already moving to establish "Physical AI" hubs in regions like North Jeolla and Daegu to secure a foothold in this new ecosystem.

Simultaneously, the technology is evolving into "agents" that don't just talk, but act. New iterations of ChatGPT can now navigate Chrome tabs to fill out forms, while Adobe has integrated tools to edit PDFs directly within AI chat interfaces. This shift moves the locus of work from human fingers to AI reasoning engines. However, this convenience brings the risk of "Shadow AI"—where employees use unapproved tools—exposing sensitive corporate data. In response, Korean firms and public institutions like the Korea Investment Corporation are beginning to issue "digital ID cards" to AI agents to manage their access levels and security permissions.

Financial Strain and Social Costs

The financial reality behind these advancements is increasingly stark. Anthropic’s attempt to raise billions at double-digit interest rates highlights the "capital fitness test" currently straining the industry. Even with massive revenue growth, the astronomical costs of electricity and high-end semiconductors are forcing companies to move from "romantic" experimentation to "ruthless" pragmatism. Many businesses are now facing "cost bombs" because employees use high-end models for simple tasks. Consequently, the market is shifting toward sLLMs (small Large Language Models) that prioritize cost-efficiency over raw power.

Perhaps most concerning is the proven impact on the labor market. Anthropic’s internal data shows that AI is already cannibalizing entry-level roles, which have dropped by two-thirds in their recent hiring cycles. While the company’s 5% equity pledge for a public fund is a significant gesture of corporate social responsibility, it underscores a grim reality: the AI revolution is no longer a future threat to jobs—it is a present one. As tech elites in Silicon Valley see their wealth explode, the challenge for the Democratic Party of Korea and global policymakers will be ensuring that the "Physical AI" revolution doesn't leave the junior workforce behind.
