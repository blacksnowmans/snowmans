Record Profits, Sinking Stocks: Why South Korea's Semiconductor Boom Isn't Reaching Retail Investors

October 27, 2026

  • The disconnect: South Korea's chip giants, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, are headed toward a combined quarterly operating profit approaching 185 trillion won (about $135 billion), yet their stock prices languish near global financial crisis levels.
  • The shareholder pushback: SK Hynix is attempting to reverse market sentiment with a massive 40 trillion won (about $29 billion) share buyback and cancellation plan, putting pressure on Samsung to follow suit.
  • Retail fallout: Over 11 million retail shareholders, alongside speculative global products like a recently liquidated US-listed SK Hynix leveraged ETF, are grappling with severe market volatility despite the fundamental memory super-cycle.
  • Global headwinds: The rally depends heavily on upcoming US Big Tech earnings and Federal Reserve decisions, leaving South Korea's chipmakers vulnerable to broader macroeconomic currents.

The Great Disconnect Between Record Earnings and Stagnant Shares

South Korea's semiconductor sector is posting numbers that defy imagination. Analysts project that the combined operating profit for memory titans Samsung Electronics (the world's largest memory chip maker) and SK Hynix will clear 185 trillion won this quarter. Yet, opening a brokerage account tells a grim story. While corporate scorecards dazzle, stock prices remain heavily depressed compared to their historic peaks, leaving investors wondering where the fruits of the boom have vanished.

The core issue isn't corporate fundamentals; it's a bottleneck of market sentiment and capital flows. Retail investors feel the trickle-down effect has completely stalled, pointing to structural reliance on foreign capital and institutional mood swings. A vivid symbol of this precariousness played out when a US-listed SK Hynix leveraged ETF was liquidated just three months after its launch—a sharp reminder of how violently sentiment can turn against Korean tech heavyweights when short-term speculative money flees.

Can Aggressive Shareholder Returns Heal the Rift?

In response to the mounting pressure, SK Hynix has dropped a mega-ton card: a plan to cancel 40 trillion won worth of treasury shares to appease the market. For a stock market where corporate earnings have historically benefited controlling families and corporate reserves rather than everyday shareholders, this move represents a critical shift toward sharing profits. However, whether a single headline-grabbing figure can mend the deep-seated frustration of South Korea's 11 million retail investors remains to be seen.

Meanwhile, Samsung Electronics has remained relatively quiet, drawing intense scrutiny from shareholders who demand a comparable value-enhancement strategy. As the two giants fiercely contest the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) market—crucial components for artificial intelligence systems—their massive capital expenditures continue to weigh on short-term sentiment. Ultimately, whether this semiconductor super-cycle translates into sustained equity value will depend less on raw production numbers and more on whether management can prove they are building long-term value for the everyday investors bearing the risk.
