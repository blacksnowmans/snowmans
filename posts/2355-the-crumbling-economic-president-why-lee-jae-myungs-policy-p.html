The Crumbling "Economic President": Why Lee Jae-myung’s Policy Pivot is Backfiring

November 15, 2026

The Lee Jae-myung administration’s self-styled image as a "competent economic presidency" is collapsing under the weight of a stock market rout and worsening inequality, signaling a fundamental crisis of trust in the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK).

  • Market Incompetence: The KOSPI, South Korea’s benchmark index, has faced "artificial disasters" due to government passivity and the introduction of high-volatility financial products like single-stock leverage ETFs.
  • Stagnating Livelihoods: Real wages have stalled at roughly $2,900 (4.06 million KRW) while the wage gap between large corporations and small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) has widened to its worst level in years.
  • The Growth Illusion: Massive semiconductor investments, including an $81 billion (113 trillion KRW) project by Samsung Electronics, are being criticized as "jobless growth" that fails to produce the "trickle-down" effects promised to the working class.
  • Political Alienation: By adopting conservative "growth-first" rhetoric, the administration is alienating its core base, drawing parallels to the decline of the Roh Moo-hyun administration in the early 2000s.

The Failure of "Trickle-Down" in the AI Era

President Lee Jae-myung’s recent pivot toward "Mega Projects" and corporate-led growth marks a departure from his original "Great Transformation" agenda. While the administration touts a "Second Miracle on the Han River" through massive semiconductor investments, the structural reality is grim. Unlike the labor-intensive heavy industries of the 1970s, the modern chip sector has a negligible employment inducement effect. When Samsung or SK Hynix profit, the gains are increasingly concentrated among high-income shareholders and a tiny elite of tech workers, leaving the broader domestic economy cold.

The disconnect between export numbers and the "pockets" of the people is stark. Despite high semiconductor exports, domestic consumption has remained stagnant for nearly a decade. Small business owners are facing a "tsunami of bad debt," with many unable to service loans taken out during the previous administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. By prioritizing the expansion of the "economic pie" over equitable distribution, the DPK is effectively recycling the same conservative economic logic it once criticized, leaving the lower 50% of the population to face the brunt of inflation and asset deflation.

A Market Left to Rot

The administration’s handling of the stock market has been particularly damaging to public trust. While regional rivals like Taiwan have actively used national stabilization funds to cushion market shocks, the Lee government has largely remained a bystander, citing "market principles." This passivity was compounded by the ill-timed introduction of risky leverage products that doubled Korea's market volatility compared to its neighbors. When the KOSPI plummeted 17% during recent geopolitical tensions—far exceeding Taiwan’s 4.5% dip—the administration offered little more than bureaucratic shrugs.

Looking ahead, the horizon is darkened by a looming "AI bubble burst" predicted for 2027. Experts warn that as global big tech companies struggle to monetize their massive AI investments, South Korea—highly leveraged to the semiconductor cycle—will be the first to feel the pain. Without a fundamental shift back to "people-centered" economics, the Lee administration risks not just a market crash, but a "Lost 10 Years" for the Korean progressive movement. The current trajectory suggests a "political suicide," as the ruling party clears a red carpet for the return of the conservative People Power Party in the 2028 elections.
