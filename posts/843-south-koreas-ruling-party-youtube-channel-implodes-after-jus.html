South Korea’s Ruling Party YouTube Channel Implodes After Just One Month

October 09, 2026

The sudden departure of the main producer behind "Minty 07," the official YouTube channel of South Korea's ruling Democratic Party, exposes deep internal fractures and a growing crisis of public trust over partisan media echo chambers.

  • What happened: The lead producer, recruited from a popular political commentary show with high expectations, quit less than a month after the channel's launch, citing production constraints and burnout.
  • What it changes: The exit shatters the channel's image as a polished media operation, exposing friction between the party's strategic messaging and the realities of daily digital broadcasting.
  • Who is affected: The Democratic Party and its core faction—often dubbed the "New Lee Jae-myung" loyalists—face mounting criticism as viewership plummets and the channel narrows into an insular echo chamber.

A High-Profile Launch Ends in a Fast Exit

When the Democratic Party launched Minty 07 as a current-affairs variety show, it leaned heavily on its marquee hire: a veteran producer from the hit independent show *Maebul Show*. The pitch was simple—bring independent digital savvy to South Korea's ruling party. Instead, the channel quickly devolved into a narrow mouthpiece for pro-government factions, sidelining broad policy debates in favor of internal party jockeying and personality cults.

While the producer officially blamed a grueling daily production schedule and health reasons, broadcasting insiders view the exit as an inevitable collision between ambitious political branding and an unworkable internal culture. When a key creative lead abandons ship after thirty days, it signals that the channel's core philosophy is either broken or entirely uncommunicated to the people running it.

Echo Chambers and the Politics of Scarcity

The meltdown of Minty 07 reflects a broader malaise gripping progressive and conservative political YouTube alike: falling viewership driven by audience fatigue. As the ruling faction tightens its inner circle, excluding dissenting voices and labeling internal critics as "traitors," the political spoils and media attention to go around are shrinking. Without room for genuine deliberation or broader public appeal, these digital platforms risk becoming costly liabilities for the administration rather than effective communication tools.
