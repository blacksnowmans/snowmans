The Democratic Party’s YouTube Experiment Collapses Under Arrogance

October 08, 2026

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—the country’s ruling liberal party—is facing a public relations disaster as its ambitious new YouTube channel, “Minty07,” suffers from plummeting viewership and the sudden resignation of its lead writer just one month after launch.

  • What happened: The program’s lead writer, a veteran producer from the popular political show Maebulshow, quit abruptly due to burnout and grueling production conditions.
  • The numbers: The show’s premiere garnered about 670,000 views, but by the fourth episode, that figure had dropped to 290,000. Current live viewership has stalled at approximately 20,000.
  • The strategy: The DPK intended for Minty07 to be the “official” voice of the party, aiming to overshadow independent political influencers—most notably the omnipresent commentator Kim Ou-joon—by setting the party's own agenda.

The failure of Minty07 highlights a fundamental disconnect between the party’s desire for control and what the public actually wants. The DPK leadership, led by figures like Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok, treated the channel as a “textbook” to instruct supporters, viewing independent YouTube channels as mere “workbooks” that lack authority. This top-down approach ignores why people watch political YouTube in the first place: they crave unfiltered analysis, catharsis, and the willingness of hosts to challenge even their own preferred politicians.

Instead of fostering an open space for debate, the DPK has turned its official channel into a propaganda megaphone. By framing internal dissent or criticism of government appointments as “disruptive” or lacking “self-restraint,” the party is mirroring the same stifling atmosphere that characterized the tenure of the now-imprisoned former president, Yoon Suk-yeol. The irony of the current ruling party demanding “restraint” from its own members while simultaneously failing to tolerate any internal critique has not been lost on the public.

A Government Out of Touch

The administration’s struggle to manage its image extends to the presidential office. When confronted with criticism regarding President Lee Jae-myung’s excessive and controversial social media activity, the administration’s response was to defend him by comparing his behavior to that of foreign leaders—even citing far-right Japanese politicians. This defensive, “whataboutism” approach fails to address the qualitative impact of the President’s online presence on national diplomacy and domestic policy.

The DPK is currently operating like a car with a broken engine that blames the warning sensors for signaling a malfunction. Rather than listening to the plummeting viewership numbers or the warnings from party elders, leadership is choosing to lash out at “internal disruption.” If the party continues to prioritize the control of its narrative over genuine communication, it will find that no amount of production value can save them from the political consequences at the ballot box. They are building a fortress of silence, and in politics, such fortresses eventually become prisons.
