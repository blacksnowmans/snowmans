Walking the Camino de Santiago: How to Shed Weight with South Korea’s "Daiso" Economy

September 07, 2026

For those preparing to walk the Camino de Santiago, the weight of your backpack is directly proportional to the health of your knees—and seasoned pilgrims have found that a strategic trip to Daiso, a popular South Korean dollar-store chain, is the secret to keeping that load light.

  • The "Daiso" Strategy: Instead of expensive, heavy gear, pilgrims are opting for cheap, lightweight, and disposable items that perform specific, vital tasks on the trail.
  • Weight vs. Utility: The core philosophy is to prioritize items that are light enough to be discarded at the end of the trip without financial regret, or functional enough to solve common trail hazards like blisters and sleepless nights.
  • Essential List: Experts recommend a minimalist kit featuring bike lights, basic skincare like petroleum jelly, and organizational tools like laundry nets—all costing just a few dollars each.

Smart Gear for a Long Road

The Camino is notorious for dark, pre-dawn starts. While many pilgrims pack heavy, high-tech headlamps, veterans suggest a 2,000 won (about $1.50) clip-on LED bike light. It is silicone-based, weighs next to nothing, and clips easily onto a backpack strap, offering sufficient visibility without the bulk of batteries. Similarly, a 1,000 won (about $0.75) whistle and a multi-purpose carabiner—specifically one that doubles as a smartphone stand—provide essential safety and utility in sparse, rustic hostels known as albergues.

Physical maintenance is equally critical. Blisters are the primary cause of injury on the trail, often stemming from friction and moisture. The solution isn't heavy medical kits, but a simple 2,000 won (about $1.50) tub of petroleum jelly (Vaseline). Applying it between the toes acts as a preventative barrier, saving hikers from the debilitating pain that can ruin a journey.

Minimalism as a Philosophy

The broader lesson from these pilgrims is that "professional" gear is not always the best gear. Often, the best items are those that acknowledge the realities of trail life: the need to navigate communal snoring with cheap 1,000 won earplugs, or the necessity of a 1,500 won (about $1.15) laundry net to keep clothes organized in shared washing machines.

Ultimately, the "Daiso method" is about shifting your mindset from accumulation to subtraction. By focusing on what you can leave behind—rather than what you need to add—you make your journey easier. As many pilgrims note, your gear should be a tool to support your movement, not an obstacle to overcome. Whether it is a tiny nail clipper for foot care or a cooling towel for the Spanish sun, the most effective items are the ones you won't even notice you are carrying.
