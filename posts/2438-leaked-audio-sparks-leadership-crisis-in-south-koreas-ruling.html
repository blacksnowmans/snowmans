Leaked Audio Sparks Leadership Crisis in South Korea’s Ruling Party

November 17, 2026

A partially leaked audio recording from a closed-door party meeting has ignited a fierce disciplinary and factional conflict inside South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), pitting party leader Kim Min-seok and the presidential office against a rival leadership faction.

  • What leaked: On November 12, the South Korean weekly magazine Sisa Journal published 45 to 50 minutes of audio from a 90-minute closed-door session held on October 16 in Namyangju by DPK Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee, in which she appeared to say President Lee Jae-myung had opposed the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, cited unverified tips that the Unification Church and Shincheonji religious groups backed Kim Min-seok’s leadership bid, and labeled officials like Agriculture Minister Song Mi-ryeong "yes-men."
  • Conflicting accounts: Choi apologized three times between November 12 and 16, explaining she had misspoken by confusing Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol with former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo, while allies including DPK lawmaker Kim Young-hwan argued the partial leak stripped away the meeting’s broader message of rallying behind President Lee.
  • Swift disciplinary action: President Lee’s office immediately dismissed the remarks as groundless, and Kim Min-seok ordered an internal ethics probe on November 12 before referring Choi to the party’s Ethics Tribunal for formal disciplinary review on November 15.
  • Factional backlash and pressure: Pro-Jung Chung-rae Supreme Council members—a faction aligned with former party chief Jung Chung-rae—boycotted a November 13 leadership meeting to accuse Kim of selective targeting, while pro-reform party networks and first-term lawmakers demanded Choi’s expulsion and the resignation of Supreme Council members Lee Sung-yoon and Han Min-soo.

Power Struggle Beyond a Verbal Slip

The rapid escalation from a local party gathering to a full-blown leadership standoff shows how fragile factional trust remains inside the DPK. What Choi’s defenders framed as an informal, selectively edited conversation has been treated by Kim Min-seok’s camp and activist party groups—such as the Gyeonggi and National Innovation Councils—as an attack on both the party leader’s legitimacy and President Lee’s governance.

President Lee Jae-myung weighed in indirectly on X on November 14 and 15, warning against "arrows flying from behind" and public figures who obstruct reform in reform’s name. Supporters of disciplinary action read those posts as a clear rebuke of Choi and the boycotting council members, whereas Choi’s defenders maintained that the original Namyangju meeting had been intended to help the president overcome political challenges.

What Happens Next

How the dispute resolves now depends on the DPK’s independent Ethics Tribunal, which under party rules can impose heavy sanctions or close the case with no penalty once it weighs the full context of the 90-minute recording. Meanwhile, calls to oust pro-Jung Supreme Council members Lee Sung-yoon and Han Min-soo have triggered debate over whether further resignations could collapse the leadership majority and force an emergency committee, leaving the ruling party’s stability hanging in the balance.
