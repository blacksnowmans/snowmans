Why Surging US Bond Yields and Sticky Inflation Threaten Tech Stocks

August 31, 2026

Global financial markets are facing a reality check as sticky inflation and rising US bond yields collide with booming artificial intelligence infrastructure spending, forcing investors in export-driven economies like South Korea to rethink their exposure to tech heavyweights.

  • The Trigger: Remarks from former Federal Reserve officials at the Jackson Hole symposium shattered hopes of near-term rate cuts, emphasizing that price stability remains the central bank's sole priority over employment.
  • The Tech Divergence: While AI infrastructure spending is generating explosive revenue—with OpenAI and Anthropic hitting a combined $100 billion in token-based sales—broader tech valuations are being squeezed by higher-for-longer interest rates.
  • The Korean Impact: South Korean chipmakers like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix face immediate pressure from a strong US dollar, rising Treasury yields, and volatile foreign capital flows.
  • What to Watch: Rather than chasing lagging indicators like corporate capital expenditure announcements, analysts advise tracking leading metrics such as Amazon Web Services (AWS) quarterly revenue growth rates to gauge true semiconductor demand.

The core vulnerability for tech investors right now is a fundamental misunderstanding of the macroeconomic cycle. Markets spent months pricing in aggressive rate cuts, assuming the Federal Reserve would rush to rescue equities at the first sign of trouble. Instead, a resilient US domestic economy and stubborn inflation readings—with personal consumption expenditures remaining high—have left the Fed with little reason to pivot. When interest rates stay elevated while valuations are stretched, multiple compression is inevitable.

At the same time, dismissing the AI boom as a pure bubble ignores the hard data coming out of companies like SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic, where capital input is rapidly converting into tangible cash flow and record-shattering usage. Yet even the most revolutionary technology cannot entirely decouple from a macro environment defined by soaring US 10-year and 30-year bond yields, a robust dollar index, and potential currency unwind risks from Japan. For investors watching the Korean stock market, navigating this volatility requires patience, strict attention to leading indicators like cloud revenue growth rather than headline-grabbing spending pledges, and a willingness to step back until the macro storm clears.
