South Korea Polling Shock: Ruling Democrats Fall Behind Conservatives as Strongholds Wobble

November 16, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party has fallen behind the conservative opposition beyond the margin of error, as President Lee Jae-myung's approval rating remains trapped in the mid-30s despite improving macroeconomic headlines.

  • Opposition surges ahead: In the latest survey by pollster Realmeter, the main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) climbed to 43.0%, opening an 8.4-percentage-point lead over the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), which slid to 34.6%.
  • Stronghold alert: While President Lee’s national approval sat essentially flat at 36.4%, automated phone polls revealed a sharp 10.1-percentage-point plunge in Honam, the traditional southwestern bedrock of South Korean liberalism, driven by frustration over delayed regional mega-projects.
  • Progressive vote splinters: The minor, more uncompromising Rebuilding Korea Party surged to 8.7%, absorbing disaffected liberal voters who feel the ruling party has dragged its feet on judicial reform.
  • Bipartisan leadership crisis: Public cynicism has engulfed both establishments; polling by Kuki News showed negative ratings above 60% for both DPK leader Kim Min-seok (67.4% negative) and PPP leader Jang Dong-hyeok (61.5% negative).

Macroeconomic Gains Overshadowed by Everyday Anxiety

On paper, President Lee’s administration should be gaining ground: semiconductor exports have rebounded sharply, and the government recently rolled out a major investment blueprint for the central Chungcheong region. Yet none of this has translated into political momentum. Everyday voters remain squeezed by grocery inflation and volatile domestic stock markets, making headline export statistics feel entirely abstract.

Compounding this bread-and-butter unease is a series of political self-inflicted wounds. Mixed messaging over property tax revisions, fierce parliamentary showdowns over a special prosecutor bill, and lingering intra-party factional disputes have dominated the news cycle. When automated polling removes the filter of live interviewers, voter frustration surfaces immediately—most visibly in Honam, where local voters are signaling that their historical loyalty cannot be taken for granted without rapid delivery on promised regional development.

A Fragile Conservative Lead Driven by Backlash

The conservative PPP's breakout lead is less an endorsement of their platform than a protest against the status quo. The party has rallied its base around the prospect of high-profile lawmaker Han Dong-hoon formally rejoining its ranks, but deeper metrics show widespread public skepticism. A separate survey by Gallup Korea found the PPP’s favorability at just 26%—meaning roughly three out of four Koreans still view the conservatives unfavorably. Rather than genuine enthusiasm, the PPP is largely capitalizing on ruling-party voters staying home or refusing to answer pollsters.

At the same time, public sentiment on economic governance reveals deeply rooted market conservatism that complicates the Lee administration's reform agenda. Even though South Korea's comprehensive real estate holding tax applies to roughly 3% of property owners, nearly half the electorate (48.5%) supports easing it. Combined with 65.7% of respondents wanting minimal government intervention in equity markets, the administration faces an uphill battle: unless it can clearly demonstrate tangible financial relief for ordinary households, its broader reform drive risks running out of steam.
