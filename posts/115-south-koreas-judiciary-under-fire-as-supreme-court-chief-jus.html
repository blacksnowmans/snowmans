South Korea's Judiciary Under Fire as Supreme Court Chief Justice Faces Audit Summon and Expense Scandals

September 29, 2026

South Korea is caught in a political storm following moves to compel the country's top judge to testify before parliament, alongside fresh revelations of dining expense falsifications and tense foreign policy spats.

  • Chief Justice Summoned: The parliamentary legislation committee voted to call Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae as a general witness for the upcoming national audit, breaking decades of tradition where the head of the judiciary only delivered a brief opening remark.
  • Expense Falsification Allegations: Investigative outlet Newstapa reported that a 525,000 won ($390) lunch at an upscale restaurant attended by seven people was logged as an 11-person event to skirt anti-graft meal limits.
  • Diplomatic Friction: Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's public mention of defecting North Korean prisoners being sent to South Korea drew conservative criticism of the South Korean government, despite the arrangement being agreed upon as confidential for safety reasons.

The decision to summon Chief Justice Cho marks a major escalation in the standoff between the liberal ruling bloc and the judiciary. While the ruling party justifies the move by citing unaddressed judicial appointments that violate constitutional norms, critics argue it infringes on the separation of powers. However, the judiciary's traditional shield of immunity is wearing thin, particularly as it faces compounding controversies over internal management and executive spending.

Compounding these institutional blows, the expense report scandal hits at the core of the judiciary's moral authority. When the country's top legal official is accused of manipulating receipt logs to fit anti-graft caps while dining with legal media executives, it transcends a mere administrative error and feeds public cynicism. Meanwhile, opposition parties continue to weaponize sensitive foreign policy and military incidents—such as a DMZ landmine explosion and North Korean prisoner transfers—for political leverage, threatening national security and deflecting from systemic reform.
