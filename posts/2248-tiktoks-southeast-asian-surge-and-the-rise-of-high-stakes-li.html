TikTok’s Southeast Asian Surge and the Rise of High-Stakes Live Shopping

November 12, 2026

South Korea’s live commerce industry is rapidly evolving from a platform for cheap impulse buys into a sophisticated global ecosystem where consumers are now comfortable purchasing high-ticket services like bathroom renovations through their smartphone screens.

  • TikTok Shop reached a 45% market share in Vietnam this September, signaling a massive shift toward "discovery-based" shopping that is now aggressively challenging regional leader Shopee.
  • Ace Hardware (operated in Korea by Yujin Home Center) drew 450,000 viewers to a single livestream for bathroom remodeling, proving that high-involvement, expensive home services can thrive on mobile platforms.
  • KT Alpha, the T-commerce (TV-based retail) subsidiary of telecom giant KT, reported a third-quarter operating profit of 11.5 billion won (about $8.2 million), highlighting the steady profitability of integrating TV and mobile shopping.
  • K-beauty brands are successfully penetrating the conservative Japanese market by partnering with local TikTok influencers, with some campaigns generating over 300 million won (about $215,000) in immediate sales.

From Discounts to High-Value Services

The success of Ace Hardware’s bathroom remodeling stream is a watershed moment for the industry. Traditionally, live commerce was reserved for "low-involvement" goods—snacks, basic cosmetics, or clothing—where the risk of a bad purchase is minimal. By attracting nearly half a million viewers to a renovation package that included installation and after-sales care, the platform demonstrated that real-time interaction can bridge the "trust gap" necessary for major household investments. Consumers are moving past mere price hunting; they are looking for expert consultations and one-stop solutions delivered in an entertaining format.

This shift is also reflected in corporate earnings. Pond Group’s "Clickmate" recently hit a daily order peak of 2.3 billion won (about $1.65 million), while KT Alpha saw its transaction volume grow by over 15%. However, the thin margin of profit growth—just 1.5% for KT Alpha—suggests that while the market is getting bigger, it is also becoming more expensive to compete in. Platforms are now forced to use data-driven targeting to ensure that the high cost of production leads to actual sales rather than just "empty" views.

The Global Pivot and the AI Reality Check

The geography of Korean commerce is also expanding. With the domestic market saturated, companies like APR (a leading beauty-tech firm) are aggressively hiring marketing talent for the U.S. and Europe. Meanwhile, TikTok has become the essential gateway for Korean brands entering Japan and Southeast Asia. The strategy has shifted from traditional search-based ads to "content-driven" commerce, where a viral short-form video acts as the storefront. Tech providers like VCAT, which automates video production, are now targeting the Japanese B2B market to feed this insatiable demand for content.

Interestingly, the industry is reaching a consensus on artificial intelligence. While "AI Transformation" (AX) is streamlining video editing and background music, TikTok’s own analysis suggests that AI cannot yet replicate the "raw" human emotion or the specific "vibe" that triggers Gen Z to buy. The future of the market likely lies in a hybrid model: using AI for efficiency and ChatGPT-like conversational interfaces for product discovery, while relying on human creators to provide the authenticity and "fun" that prevents shoppers from hitting the exit button. In this new era, the platform that provides the best story, rather than just the lowest price, will likely dominate the "Discovery Commerce" landscape.
