South Korea’s Main Opposition Party Implodes over Leader Jang Dong-hyeok’s Refusal to Reintegrate Rival Han Dong-hoon

November 28, 2026

South Korea’s main opposition People Power Party (PPP) has descended into severe turmoil as party leader Jang Dong-hyeok vowed to block former leader Han Dong-hoon’s reinstatement and announced a boycott of his own party’s general assembly.

  • What happened: PPP leader Jang Dong-hyeok publicly rejected former party chief Han Dong-hoon's demand to rejoin the party, telling him to "solve it legally" and threatening to immediately re-discipline him even if courts order Han's return.
  • Leadership paralysis: Jang announced he will boycott a scheduled December 2 general assembly of party lawmakers, claiming he has "no reason to go and listen" to internal criticism from fellow representatives.
  • Rising factional hostility: Lawmakers aligned with Han, including Representative Bae Hyun-jin, denounced Jang’s leadership as "rogue," while allegations emerged that party ethics proceedings against Han were rigged.
  • Distraction tactics: Facing mounting pressure from conservative lawmakers like Yoon Sang-hyun and Woo Jae-jun, Jang attempted to divert focus by calling for President Lee Jae-myung of the ruling Democratic Party to be jailed.

A Party Meltdown Driven by Power Struggles

The feud inside the People Power Party has escalated far beyond routine factional friction into an all-out survival battle over future party leadership and presidential nominations. By threatening to abuse internal rules to bar Han Dong-hoon—and signaling that even court rulings would be bypassed through immediate re-disciplinary measures—Jang Dong-hyeok is leveraging party machinery to neutralize a key political rival. This weaponization of internal ethics committees has triggered severe backlash, with critics accusing leadership of staging rigged disciplinary proceedings to purge opposition within the party.

Jang's refusal to attend the party’s lawmakers’ assembly marks an extraordinary breakdown in democratic governance within the main opposition party. When a party leader refuses to engage with his own parliamentary caucus because he fears confrontation, effective political leadership ceases to exist. Instead of presenting a coherent policy platform or holding President Lee Jae-myung’s administration accountable, the PPP is burning its political capital on personal attacks, nomination feuds, and mutual sabotage.

Distraction Through Extremism

To deflect from his failing internal authority, Jang has resorted to hyper-partisan rhetoric, declaring that President Lee Jae-myung belongs in prison and demanding accelerated judicial trials. However, this attempt to rally the conservative base through external attacks fails to mask the party's core vulnerabilities. With key party figures publicly questioning Jang's strategic competence and pro-Han lawmakers threatening further confrontation, the PPP risks completely alienating mainstream voters and forfeiting its credibility as a functional opposition force.
