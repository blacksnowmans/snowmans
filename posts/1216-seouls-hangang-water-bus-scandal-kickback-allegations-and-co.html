Seoul's Hangang Water Bus Scandal: Kickback Allegations and Cover-Up Claims Hit Mayor Oh Se-hoon

October 16, 2026

Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon is facing mounting political fallout after a parliamentary audit revealed his administration shelved an internal investigation into an alleged 1.5 billion won (roughly $1.1 million) kickback scheme tied to his flagship Hangang River commuter ferry project.

  • The Alleged Kickback: A city audit uncovered suspicious fund transfers from vessel builder Gadok Heavy Industries through an intermediary broker, with findings suggesting a senior official at the municipal builder Seoul Housing & Communities Corporation (SH) received kickbacks during contractor selection.
  • The Cover-Up Allegations: Lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party revealed that municipal auditors drafted a formal police referral following an in-person briefing to Mayor Oh in September 2025, but the document was subsequently discarded and buried to keep the ferry launch on schedule.
  • Amnesia as a Defense: Questioned during the audit, Mayor Oh—a prominent conservative from the People Power Party—insisted he had "no recollection" of the criminal referral and dismissed the corruption findings as unverified "rumors."
  • Compounding Safety Hazards: The scandal follows repeated groundings of river vessels in shallow waters, which led City Hall to issue administrative directives instructing agencies not to use the alarming Korean word for "grounding" or "shipwreck" (jwach’o), swapping it for the softer technical term "stranding" (jwaju).

A Signature Initiative Derailed by Institutional Secrecy

The water bus project was designed to be a centerpiece of Mayor Oh’s long-standing waterfront urban development vision. However, the revelation that municipal officials drafted—and then apparently purged—a criminal referral strongly suggests that political milestones took priority over basic governance. Pushing ahead with passenger operations while actively burying an internal corruption inquiry leaves City Hall vulnerable to charges of willful malfeasance, especially given city regulations that mandate record retention even when a referral is dropped.

Mayor Oh's attempt to deflect the scrutiny by claiming memory lapses and pointing to a dismissed audit petition to the Board of Audit and Inspection (BAI), South Korea's state watchdog, is falling flat. An administrative dismissal by the national auditor should have triggered an immediate police referral if the city was serious about clearing its name. By letting the matter idle until lawmakers forced it into the open, the mayor’s office appears far more interested in running out the clock than establishing accountability.

Semantic PR Cannot Conceal Navigation Risks

The financial irregularities are made far worse by immediate public safety concerns. With two major groundings already stranding hundreds of passengers near Jamsil and the Banpo Bridge, the project's technical viability on the shallow Hangang River remains under heavy doubt. That City Hall’s bureaucratic response to near-disasters was semantic policing—ordering staff to sanitize official incident reports—demonstrates an administration preoccupied with image control over commuter welfare. As legislative pressure intensifies, calls for an independent criminal probe will be nearly impossible for the mayor to resist.
