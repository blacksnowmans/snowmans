Packing for the Camino de Santiago: A Pragmatic Guide to Surviving 500 Miles

September 07, 2026

  • The Golden Rule: Your backpack weight should never exceed 10% of your body weight. For a 60kg (132lb) person, that means keeping the pack under 6kg (13lbs).
  • Essential Gear: Invest in high-quality trekking shoes (waterproof Hoka brand is recommended), a reliable 35-48L backpack (Osprey is a top choice), and specialized items like a poncho and a lightweight "Naturehike" sleeping bag.
  • What to Skip: Avoid "just in case" items like heavy locks, multiple hair styling tools, or heavy electronics. Most pilgrims find these to be unnecessary dead weight.
  • Survival Tips: Use trekking poles and knee braces to protect your joints, and pack Vaseline in a lip-balm-style tube to prevent blisters without the mess.

Packing for the Camino de Santiago—the legendary 800km (approx. 500-mile) pilgrimage across Spain—is less about preparation and more about survival. Beginners often treat their backpack like a suitcase, filling it with comforts of home. However, every gram counts when you are walking 20-25km (12-15 miles) every day for over a month. An overstuffed bag doesn't just make the trek difficult; it makes it a grueling, painful experience.

The secret to a successful trek is ruthless minimalism. Items like large hair dryers, bulky camera equipment, and unnecessary security gear like chains or six separate padlocks are routinely abandoned by pilgrims along the way. Instead, prioritize multi-purpose gear. A simple poncho can double as wind protection, and a single bar of soap can handle everything from laundry to showers. If you are worried about digital connectivity, remember that 10-20GB of data is plenty for navigation; you likely won't need the massive 50GB plans some travelers erroneously purchase.

Protecting Your Body on the Road

The biggest threats to completing the Camino aren't the steep climbs, but blisters and knee injuries. Seasoned pilgrims swear by simple hacks to keep their bodies moving. Because sweat and friction cause blisters, keeping your feet dry and lubricated is critical. Applying Vaseline to your feet before you start walking is a standard practice, and using a lipstick-style container makes this possible without getting your hands messy at a roadside stop.

Equally important is the protection of your joints. Even for those in good shape, the constant downhill strain on paths like the Pyrenees will eventually take its toll. Trekking poles are not an accessory; they are a necessity for distributing your weight and relieving pressure on your knees. Pairing these with knee braces will help you avoid the common pitfalls that force many travelers to cut their journey short. Ultimately, the Camino is a test of endurance where your gear should act as an extension of your body, not an obstacle to your progress.
