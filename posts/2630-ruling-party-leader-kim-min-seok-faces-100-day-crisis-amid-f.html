Ruling Party Leader Kim Min-seok Faces 100-Day Crisis Amid Falling Ratings and Party Feuds

November 23, 2026

Just two days ahead of his 100th day in office, Kim Min-seok, leader of South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), is facing severe headwinds as party approval ratings dropped by 5 percentage points amid escalating factional infighting.

  • What happened: The ruling party's approval rating fell 5 percentage points, driven by public fatigue over internal squabbles such as disciplinary proceedings against lawmaker Choi Min-hee and open power struggles among senior party figures.
  • What it changes: While Kim retains top support among core DPK loyalists as a leading progressive presidential contender, his failure to deliver concrete economic policies has limited the party's appeal among moderate and youth voters.
  • Who is affected: The ruling party leadership and President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, whose legislative initiatives risk losing momentum if internal rifts continue to erode public trust.

Rallying the Base with Hardline Moves

Despite weakening overall support, Kim maintains solid backing from core party loyalists, keeping him at the top of presidential preference polls among progressive voters. To stem his broader political decline, party leadership has floated aggressive political moves, including officially discussing the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de. While such hardline tactics energize hardcore supporters, they risk alienating moderate voters who view the move as political conflict rather than a focus on economic stability.

Internal cohesion is further strained by deep factional divisions. Disciplinary actions surrounding representative Choi Min-hee have sparked communication breakdowns among lawmakers, while veteran figures like Choo Mi-ae and Jung Chung-rae are stepping into the spotlight to expand their own influence. This perceived leadership vacuum has allowed factional jockeying to play out publicly, undermining party unity.

Policy Alignment vs. Internal Disunity

On executive coordination, Kim’s leadership receives positive marks for maintaining smooth alignment with President Lee Jae-myung’s administration and helping advance key government agendas in parliament. However, smooth government coordination is offset by internal dysfunction. Meanwhile, Kim's public outreach to young voters has drawn criticism for offering rhetorical gestures rather than practical solutions for youth housing, job growth, and living costs.

As Kim reaches the 100-day mark, his leadership faces a crucial test. To reverse the slide in public approval, the DPK chair must move beyond political maneuvering to resolve internal strife and deliver tangible economic policies that resonate beyond his core base.
