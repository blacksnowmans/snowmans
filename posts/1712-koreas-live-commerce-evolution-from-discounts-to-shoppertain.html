Korea’s Live Commerce Evolution: From Discounts to "Shoppertainment"

October 30, 2026

South Korean retail giants are launching a high-stakes November shopping war by merging live-streamed sales with K-pop fandom and creator-led content to overcome a persistent consumer spending slump.

  • CJ OnStyle, the home shopping and e-commerce arm of media giant CJ ENM, successfully concluded its first "Creator Commerce Festival Korea" (CCFK), where 65.6% of attendees reportedly generated direct sales revenue on the spot.
  • SSG.com, the online mall for the Shinsegae Group retail conglomerate, has prepared 100 exclusive products for its 10-day "Sseuk Day" mega-sale, signaling the start of the year-end retail season.
  • Olive Young, South Korea’s dominant health and beauty retailer, is leveraging K-pop fandom by selling girl group NMIXX albums through its dedicated live commerce channel, "Ol-Young Live."
  • State-run Public Home Shopping (Gongyoung) is supporting small businesses by offering about $14,500 (20 million KRW) in rewards as part of the "Korea Grand Festival" sales event.

The Rise of the Creator Economy and Exclusive Content

The traditional model of live commerce—essentially a mobile version of home shopping where a host shouts about discounts—is being replaced by a creator-centric ecosystem. At CJ OnStyle’s recent festival, the high conversion rate of visitors into sellers demonstrates that the infrastructure for influencers to monetize their personal brands is now fully mature. Instead of relying solely on advertising fees, mid-sized creators are now acting as boutique distributors, curating products that resonate with their specific niche audiences.

This shift is also visible in the aggressive marketing for November, Korea's equivalent to Black Friday. Gmarket, a major e-commerce platform owned by Shinsegae, has hired veteran actor So Ji-sub for its "Big Smile Day" campaign. Meanwhile, platforms like Reju are launching variety-show-style golf programs to sell equipment. The goal is to keep viewers engaged with entertainment so that the eventual purchase feels like a natural extension of the content rather than a hard sell.

Fandom Loyalty and the Digital Transition of Local Markets

Perhaps the most significant trend is the weaponization of K-pop fandom. By selling artist albums and exclusive merchandise via live streams, retailers like Olive Young are tapping into a demographic that values real-time interaction and "exclusive" perks over simple price cuts. Fandom-driven commerce provides a level of engagement and loyalty that traditional discount-hunters cannot match, turning a sales broadcast into a digital fan meeting.

However, this high-tech retail boom has its challenges. While large platforms celebrate record-breaking viewership, small-scale sellers often struggle with high commission fees and marketing costs. Furthermore, as seen in the rapid growth of TikTok Shop in Vietnam—where quarterly sales reached nearly $6 billion (150 trillion VND)—the explosion of live commerce often brings risks regarding counterfeit goods and quality control. For the Korean market to remain sustainable, the focus must shift toward ensuring that the "shoppertainment" trend benefits local producers and small merchants, not just the massive conglomerates and top-tier influencers.
