Lower Body Fitness, Diabetes Control, and the High Cost of Korean Healthcare Treatments

October 16, 2026

Maintaining lower body and spine strength—evidenced by South Korean football star Son Heung-min—is crucial for managing chronic conditions like diabetes, even as patients in South Korea face steep out-of-pocket costs for clinical treatments.

  • Athletic inspiration: Football icon Son Heung-min’s peak athletic performance demonstrates how rigorous lower-body and spinal conditioning drive peak endurance and metabolic health.
  • High treatment expenses: Diabetes patients in South Korea using weekly injectable therapies face monthly out-of-pocket costs of about 400,000 won (around $300), driven up by strict coverage exclusions from private insurance policies.
  • Charitable advocacy: Football commentator Park Moon-sung, a six-year ambassador for Korea Food for the Hungry International (a major relief NGO), highlights how foundational physical health enables long-term community service.
  • Preventative routines: Health experts advocate simple daily home workouts—such as a 10-second single-leg balance test and targeted squats—to regulate blood sugar, build core stability, and reduce fall risks.

Systemic Gaps in Chronic Disease Care

While athletic role models like Son Heung-min demonstrate the power of physical conditioning, everyday South Koreans managing metabolic conditions face significant financial hurdles. Weekly injectable treatments have emerged as effective tools for blood sugar control, but South Korea's private indemnity insurance (known locally as silson insurance) and regulatory frameworks often restrict coverage or limit reimbursements by treating these drugs similarly to cosmetic treatments. This leaves patients paying 400,000 won ($300) or more each month out of pocket, highlighting calls for the Lee Jae-myung administration to expand subsidy frameworks for essential chronic disease care.

Managing diabetes solely through strict diet control often causes fatigue and psychological frustration. While enjoying food remains vital for quality of life, physical movement serves as the primary mechanism for burning excess glucose. Because lower-body muscles act as the body's largest glucose sink, building lower-body strength is an essential medical strategy rather than a cosmetic luxury.

Daily Routines as Preventative Medicine

To complement clinical care, simple home exercises offer a practical line of defense against functional decline. A standard 10-second single-leg balance test—performed with eyes closed—serves as an indicator of ankle stability and neurological health, with studies showing that poor performance on balance tests correlates with higher mortality risks from fall-related injuries. Incorporating short squat sessions and core rotation routines into daily habits strengthens the hamstrings, glutes, and spinal muscles, helping sedentary individuals maintain mobility and metabolic control without expensive equipment.
