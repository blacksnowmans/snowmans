TikTok Eclipses YouTube Shorts as the Practical "Side Hustle" for Korean Creators

November 01, 2026

TikTok’s lower barrier to entry and significantly higher payout per view are driving a surge of South Korean creators away from YouTube Shorts toward the short-form video pioneer.

  • Drastically lower thresholds: TikTok requires only 10,000 followers and 100,000 views within 30 days to start earning, whereas YouTube Shorts demands a daunting 10 million views over 90 days.
  • Superior monetization: TikTok’s "Creator Rewards Program" offers roughly 1 KRW per view (about $0.0007), nearly ten times the average rate of YouTube Shorts in the Korean market.
  • AI-driven production: Creators are using tools like Typecast, a Korean AI voice synthesis service, and CapCut to build profitable "faceless" channels without expensive cameras or studios.
  • The 61-second rule: Savvy creators produce videos exactly 61 seconds long to qualify for TikTok rewards, then trim them to 59 seconds for YouTube Shorts to maximize reach across both platforms.
  • Affiliate integration: Creators are bypassing TikTok's link restrictions by using bio-link services to drive traffic to Coupang Partners, the affiliate program for Coupang, South Korea’s dominant e-commerce platform.

From "Dance Challenges" to Serious Cash Flow

The perception of TikTok in South Korea is shifting. Once dismissed as a platform for teenagers to share dance covers, it has evolved into a sophisticated engine for "information curation." For the average Korean office worker looking for a "pipeline" (a popular local term for passive income), TikTok offers a much faster path to the first dollar than YouTube. While only about 8% of YouTube creators manage to meet the platform's harsh monetization requirements, TikTok’s entry barrier is effectively 100 times lower.

This shift is facilitated by a localized tech stack. Creators often "benchmark" successful scripts from popular YouTube videos—such as clips from the hit Netflix show Culinary Class Wars—and use AI tools like Typecast to generate natural-sounding Korean narration. By focusing on high-retention "information" content rather than personal vlogs, creators can generate significant revenue without ever showing their faces. One documented case saw a creator earn about $640 (870,000 KRW) in a single day by combining TikTok views with Coupang affiliate commissions.

The Efficiency of "One-Source, Multi-Use"

The most successful Korean creators aren't choosing between platforms; they are leveraging the "1-minute-1-second" rule. TikTok’s rewards program only pays for videos longer than 60 seconds, while YouTube Shorts is capped at 60 seconds. By producing a 61-second video, a creator can satisfy TikTok's payout algorithm and then, with a simple two-second trim, have a perfectly formatted YouTube Short. This "One-Source, Multi-Use" (OSMU) strategy mitigates the risk of being at the mercy of a single platform's shifting algorithm.

However, this new "gold rush" comes with a warning regarding originality. TikTok’s monitoring systems have become increasingly adept at flagging "re-upload" channels that simply steal broadcast footage. To survive, Korean creators are learning to add "interpretive value"—adding their own commentary or unique editing to existing clips. Furthermore, as these earnings are typically paid via PayPal and not taxed at the source, local creators are being advised to prepare for Global Income Tax filings in May to avoid legal friction with the National Tax Service.

"In an era where the value of labor feels like it is shrinking against inflation, the difference between those who consume content and those who produce it is the difference between being a data point and being a business."
