Top Investigation Agency Nominee Under Fire for Concealing Details on Past Sex Crime Cases

October 14, 2026

Kim Ji-yong, the nominee to lead South Korea's Serious Crimes Investigation Agency, faces severe backlash at his parliamentary confirmation hearing after submitting vague records that obscure critical details of his past defense work in three separate sexual assault cases.

  • What happened: At an October 14 confirmation hearing, lawmaker Jeong Chun-saeng revealed that government-submitted vetting records concealed the specifics of Kim's legal career, substituting generic offense categories like "fraud" or "indecent assault" while omitting victim profiles and statutory sub-clauses.
  • The cases in question: Vetting materials showed Kim represented clients in three sexual offense cases between August 2024 and November 2025, including two indecent assault cases and a charge under the Act on Special Cases Concerning the Punishment of Sexual Crimes.
  • Why it matters: The Serious Crimes Investigation Agency is charged with handling sensitive offenses against vulnerable groups, including child abuse, sexual violence, stalking, and domestic abuse, making the agency head's ethical stance on gender-based crime paramount.
  • The pushback: Lawmakers demanded Kim immediately resubmit comprehensive records, rejecting the notion that professional attorney confidentiality can be used as a shield against public scrutiny for high-ranking government posts.

Confidentiality Shield vs. Public Vetting

Legal professionals transitioning to state appointments in South Korea frequently face scrutiny over past casework. While attorney-client privilege (ACP) and the constitutional right to defense are legitimate legal safeguards designed to protect defendants from state overreach, conflating those protections with a nominee’s duty of transparency in a public hearing undermines the vetting process. High-profile nominees who built private practices defending severe criminal offenses cannot expect blanket immunity from ethical evaluations once they seek public power.

Institutional Mandate and Moral Fitness

The controversy strikes directly at the credibility of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency. Entrusted with protecting the most vulnerable members of society, the agency's leadership requires rigorous sensitivity to victims' rights. Whether Kim defended perpetrators accused of assaulting minors or offenses involving severe familial violence is not a trivial detail—it is fundamental to determining his fitness to direct state investigative power. Masking specific charges under broad legal headings merely deepens public skepticism over whether elite appointees are held to meaningful standards of accountability.
