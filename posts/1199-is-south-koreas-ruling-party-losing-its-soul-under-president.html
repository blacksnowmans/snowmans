Is South Korea’s Ruling Party Losing Its Soul Under President Lee Jae-myung?

October 16, 2026

South Korea’s progressive camp is facing a severe internal crisis as tribalist politics, blind loyalty to President Lee Jae-myung, and a drift away from traditional democratic values threaten to consume the liberal Democratic Party.

  • The danger of blind loyalty: Die-hard supporters hide behind the slogan "protect the president" to shut down all criticism, effectively isolating President Lee and stifling internal debate.
  • High-profile media influence: Influential progressive commentators, such as Kim Eo-jun, are pushing a harsh binary worldview that frames political disagreements in moralistic terms, accelerating the alienation of moderate voters.
  • Administrative and appointment scandals: The controversial appointment of Kim Ji-yong as head of the newly established Investigation Bureau exposed dishonesty within the presidential office, drawing sharp comparisons to past conservative administrations.
  • Abandoning the legacy of humility: Observers note that the "humble power" championed by former presidents Kim Dae-jung, Roh Moo-hyun, and Moon Jae-in has been replaced by self-absorbed achievement-ism and aggressive self-defense.

The Trap of Partisan Tribalism and Moral Blind Spots

The Democratic Party, South Korea's main liberal party, was built on the ideals of human rights, reform, and a willingness to accept criticism. Today, however, dissent is treated as betrayal. When supporters declare that they must protect President Lee at all costs, reasonable doubts and policy critiques are instantly written off as hostile acts. This defensive shell has turned the administration inward, blinding it to public discontent and eroding the checks and balances vital to a healthy democracy.

Compounding this problem is the rhetoric of prominent progressive media figures like Kim Eo-jun, whose black-and-white framing reduces complex policy issues to existential struggles. By painting critics as reactionaries or ideological foes, these prominent voices narrow the space for compromise, driving away independent voters and poisoning the democratic well from within.

Arrogance of Power and the Distortion of Reform

The administration's handling of key appointments—most notably the opaque process and subsequent falsehoods surrounding the appointment of Kim Ji-yong—has severely damaged its moral authority. Rather than admitting mistakes, the presidential office has repeatedly dismissed criticism as partisan attacks. Critics argue that prosecutorial reform, a cornerstone of the progressive agenda, has been warped from a systemic fix into a tool for self-preservation and political score-settling.

This dynamic marks a profound departure from the legacy of past progressive leaders. While former leaders like Kim Dae-jung and Roh Moo-hyun prided themselves on self-restraint and accountability, the current posture leans heavily on personal achievements and defensive posturing. Unless the ruling camp re-embraces transparency, genuine public communication, and a willingness to police its own missteps, it risks replicating the very overreach it once fought so hard to dismantle.
