South Korea’s Semiconductor Sector Rallies on Major Supply Deals and Nvidia Alliances

November 11, 2026

South Korea’s semiconductor industry saw a sharp market recovery led by tech giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, alongside a wave of major equipment contracts and structural leadership shifts.

  • Market Value Rebound: Shares of Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix surged 4.7% and 6.7% respectively, restoring their combined market capitalization to 2,000 trillion won (around $1.45 trillion) and pushing the benchmark KOSPI back above 5,200.
  • Equipment Order Surge: Semiconductor equipment maker KCTech clinched a 21.1 billion won ($15.2 million) supply contract with SK Hynix, while toolmaker SEC secured a deal to supply high-bandwidth memory (HBM) inspection equipment to U.S.-based Micron.
  • Nvidia Technical Alliance: Samsung and SK Hynix revealed a joint advance with Nvidia that cuts semiconductor virtual simulation times by 4,000 times, reducing testing from 24 hours down to just one minute.
  • Ecosystem & Leadership Shifts: Equipment maker Hanwha Semitech nominated 37-year-old former consultant Kim Jin-woo as CEO to stem financial losses, while AI chip startup DeepX earned a CES 2027 Innovation Award for its 2-nanometer NPU chip scheduled for production with Samsung Foundry.

Deepening Global Alliances and Supply Chain Gains

The latest market rally highlights how essential South Korean chipmakers remain to global artificial intelligence infrastructure. By accelerating virtual simulation testing 4,000-fold alongside Nvidia with 99% accuracy, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are positioning themselves as core technological co-creators rather than basic memory suppliers. Concurrently, specialized toolmakers like KCTech and IMT—which is co-developing solutions for HBM yield bottlenecks with SK Hynix—are turning persistent AI demand into solid order backlogs, restoring confidence among domestic retail investors following a month of heavy selling.

Ecosystem Modernization Meets Internal Friction

Beneath the stock recovery, South Korea’s chip industry is undergoing a broader operational modernization. Hanwha Semitech’s decision to appoint a 37-year-old chief executive breaks with industry convention to aggressively fix profitability in the high-stakes HBM equipment market. Meanwhile, DeepX’s award-winning 2-nanometer AI chip demonstrates expanding capability among domestic fabless designers beyond traditional conglomerate dominance. However, execution risks remain: Samsung’s super-enterprise union is demanding mandatory prior agreements over staff relocations for a planned semiconductor megaproject in South Korea's southern Honam region. Even as Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron align to standardize automated robot charging across their global plants, managing labor relations will prove just as critical to sustaining momentum as manufacturing upgrades.
