Apple's Big Fall Event: iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro, and AirPods 5

September 10, 2026

Apple’s latest fall product showcase delivered a major hardware leap, headlined by the company's long-awaited entry into the foldable market and sweeping performance upgrades across its flagship lineups.

  • iPhone Duo: Apple's first foldable phone arrives with a passport-sized closed form, a virtually crease-free 7.6-inch inner screen using custom nano-texture glass, and a starting price of about $2,300 (3.29 million KRW).
  • iPhone 18 Pro & Pro Max: Powered by the new A20 Pro chip, these models feature variable aperture main cameras, massive battery life improvements (up to 45 hours on the Pro Max), and starting prices around $1,400 to $1,550 (1.99 million to 2.19 million KRW).
  • AirPods 5: The open-ear wireless earbuds now include Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) as a standard feature, priced at about $140 to $160 (200,000 to 230,000 KRW).
  • Apple Watch Series 12 & Ultra 4: Upgraded with a redesigned traffic sensor for faster heart rate tracking every 5 seconds, an audio intelligence "Live Rewind" transcription feature, and tougher ceramic shielding.

The Foldable Pivot and the Price of Innovation

Apple is notoriously late to emerging product categories, but the strategy is usually to let rivals stumble through the growing pains before perfecting the execution. With the iPhone Duo, that playbook is on full display. By tackling the persistent issue of screen creases with custom nano-texture finishes and AI-optimized hinge engineering, Apple has bypassed the clumsy transition phase that defined early foldable devices. The software seamlessly adapts when unfolding, supporting multitasking and Apple Pencil Pro integration in a way that makes standard smartphones suddenly feel rigid.

Yet, this level of engineering comes with severe friction, primarily a punishing price tag that crosses well past the $2,000 mark. While tech enthusiasts will praise the hardware wizardry, a 254-gram weight and exorbitant cost mean foldables remain an elite luxury rather than an accessible mainstream shift. Apple has proven that it can build a vastly superior folding phone, but the real test will be whether it can ever make such technology approachable for everyday consumers.
