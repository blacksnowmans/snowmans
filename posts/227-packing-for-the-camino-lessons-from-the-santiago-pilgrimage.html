Packing for the Camino: Lessons from the Santiago Pilgrimage

September 07, 2026

The Camino de Santiago—the centuries-old pilgrimage network spanning over 800 kilometers (approx. 500 miles) across Spain—is not merely a test of faith, but a brutal physical trial where every ounce in your pack dictates your ability to survive the trail.

  • The Golden Ratio: A properly packed bag should distribute 70% of its weight to your hips via a waist belt, leaving only 30% on your shoulders.
  • The Injury Factor: Over 80% of trail-related injuries involve the feet; neglect here is the fastest way to end a pilgrimage prematurely.
  • The Bedbug Reality: High-traffic hostels (albergues) make a personal sleeping bag your only reliable barrier against parasites and poor hygiene.
  • Avoid Minimalism Traps: While weight reduction is vital, extreme "ultralight" tactics often lead to hypothermia or injury by sacrificing necessary gear for warmth or safety.

The Physics of Survival

For the pilgrim, a backpack is not just luggage; it is an extension of the body. Beginners often prioritize a "light" bag, but experienced trekkers know that torso length—the measurement from your collarbone to your pelvic bone—is the true metric for success. A pack that does not fit your frame forces the shoulders to carry the load, transforming the pilgrimage from a spiritual journey into a grueling endurance test. Investing in a pack with proper ventilation is equally vital; a damp back leads to chafing, cold-induced illness, and misery.

Survival on the Camino is essentially a game of managing variables. Weather in regions like the Pyrenees fluctuates from winter-like mornings to desert-like afternoons, making functional, layered clothing a non-negotiable requirement. Similarly, foot care is a daily ritual. Taping toes and using lubricants like Vaseline are not mere "hacks"—they are essential maintenance protocols for the only tools you have: your feet.

Beyond the Gear

The most important realization for any aspiring pilgrim is that the Camino is an exercise in subtraction. Most people arrive with 12-kilogram (approx. 26 lbs) packs, only to realize by the third day that they are carrying items they don't need. The true test of a pilgrim isn't what they bring, but what they are prepared to leave behind.

While the goal is to travel light, do not treat the journey as a competition to carry the least. Being cold because you chose a sleeping bag too thin to save a few ounces is a mistake that ruins your recovery time. Ultimately, the Camino is a process of checking your own ego. You will likely discard half your toothpaste and extra clothes within the first week—and in doing so, you will find that the road is as forgiving as it is demanding.
