Far-Right Surge Across Western Democracies Delivers a Warning Signal to Seoul

October 31, 2026

Rising inflation, economic stagnation, and political fragmentation are fueling a dangerous wave of far-right populism across Europe and the Middle East, highlighting how vulnerable modern democracies remain when mainstream leadership fails.

  • France and Germany: Marine Le Pen leads French presidential polling at 33 percent, while Germany’s far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) remains resilient despite a massive internal email leak exposing neo-Nazi links.
  • UK and Israel: Nigel Farage’s Reform UK faces severe moral scandals after losing a local vote by just two votes, while Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu failed to secure a parliamentary majority.
  • Youth Disillusionment: Over 100,000 young Europeans have taken to the streets to protest soaring housing costs, inflation, and shrinking economic opportunities.
  • Democratic Vulnerability: The global drift toward division mirrors South Korea’s own recent experience with severe political polarization before constitutional checks restored order.

Extremism Capitalizes on Mainstream Failures

Across Europe, middle-of-the-road political coalitions are buckling under economic pressure and scandal. In France, former Prime Minister Édouard Philippe faces investigations over the misuse of public funds while third-quarter growth contracted by 0.1 percent, allowing far-right figurehead Marine Le Pen and radical left leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon to dominate the political field. In Germany, AfD continues to stoke anti-immigrant sentiment despite internal leaks exposing Moscow connections and extremist ties, demonstrating that institutional legal hurdles alone cannot curb populist momentum.

Britain offers a similar cautionary tale of divisive rhetoric masking governance failures. Nigel Farage’s Reform UK party—recently categorized as an authoritarian group in European cooperation studies—is embroiled in controversy after Farage's personal bodyguard was convicted of rape and party associates targeted Conservative MP Kemi Badenoch with racist slurs. When mainstream political elites fail to fix basic living conditions, populist forces readily convert public frustration into targeted hostility against minorities.

A Sobering Lesson for South Korea

This global resurgence of hate politics carries deep resonance for South Korea. The country recently endured its own severe trial of democratic backsliding, marked by extreme ideological division and governance breakdown during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's term in office. South Korea managed to steer itself back onto a stable constitutional track by removing Mr. Yoon through impeachment and establishing President Lee Jae-myung's administration.

Yet, conspiracy theories and fringe extremist rhetoric still linger within the domestic political landscape. As demonstrated across Europe, whenever young people face severe housing shortages, stagnant wages, and narrowing employment prospects, demagogues step in to offer easy scapegoats. Defending democratic norms requires more than just holding elections; it demands that mainstream leaders deliver tangible economic relief and actively rebuild civic solidarity.
