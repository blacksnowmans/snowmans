Double Standards and Corruption Allegations Shake Korea's Legal and Municipal Leadership

October 19, 2026

Allegations of political favoritism in law enforcement and corruption in municipal projects are sparking renewed public outcry over equal justice and accountability in South Korea.

  • What happened: Recent disclosures highlight sharp contrasts between civil rights advocates who expanded assembly rights and recent high-level law enforcement decisions, including lenient probes into Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's wife, Kim Keon-hee, and credential-padding allegations involving the daughter of former People Power Party leader Han Dong-hoon.
  • Municipal scandal: Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon faces mounting criticism over a suspect 320 million won (about $230,000) broker fee in his "Hangang Bus" water transit project, accompanied by allegations that city officials ordered internal audit files destroyed.
  • Judicial backlash: Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae faces investigation over the alleged misuse of official discretionary funds, shaking trust in the nation's highest court.
  • Who is affected: South Korean taxpayers and ordinary citizens, as public confidence in prosecutors, local government procurement, and judicial neutrality hits a critical low.

Prosecutorial Double Standards

During Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s presidency, prosecutors faced persistent criticism for shielding key administration figures while aggressively targeting political opponents. Special investigation teams probing Kim Keon-hee over luxury gift reception and stock manipulation allegations conducted controversial off-site interrogations at private security facilities, surrendering their phones and ultimately declining to file charges. Similarly, law enforcement repeatedly skipped search warrants and failed to pursue international judicial cooperation regarding paper-ghostwriting and credential allegations surrounding the daughter of Han Dong-hoon, former leader of the main opposition People Power Party.

These passive probes stand in stark contrast to the legacy of civil rights attorneys like Park Joo-min—now a lawmaker with the ruling Democratic Party—who spent years on the front lines challenging state overreach. Park notably led the legal fight that overturned the blanket ban on nighttime outdoor assemblies in the Assembly and Demonstration Act, establishing the constitutional protections that enabled major peaceful protests. The growing perception of selective justice for political elites continues to undermine public faith in the rule of law.

Seoul's Water Bus Controversy and Judicial Inquiries

At the local government level, Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon of the People Power Party is under fire over his flagship "Hangang Bus" passenger ferry project. Parliamentary audits revealed that a primary shipbuilder transferred 320 million won (about $230,000) to a short-lived shell company under the guise of brokerage fees. Internal whistleblowers allege that after Seoul City auditors uncovered evidence of executive rebates, city leadership ordered the destruction of investigation records and submitted a watered-down document to the Board of Audit and Inspection.

Compounding this administrative crisis, Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae and Senior Judicial Administrator Noh Kyung-pil are now under investigation by special prosecutors over allegations of abuse of power and misuse of official entertainment funds. Together, these legal and municipal scandals highlight urgent calls for structural reform and strict accountability across South Korea’s governance and judicial institutions.
