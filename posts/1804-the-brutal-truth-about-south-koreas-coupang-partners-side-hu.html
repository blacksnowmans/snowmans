The Brutal Truth About South Korea's Coupang Partners Side Hustle Culture

November 01, 2026

If you are hoping to get rich quick with zero capital through South Korea's exploding affiliate marketing trend, prepare for a harsh reality check: your first three months will likely net you less than $10 (about 10,000 won) a day.

    What happened: New data reveals that the median net profit for a beginner on Coupang Partners—South Korea's dominant e-commerce affiliate program run by retail giant Coupang—is a meager $12 (about 17,000 won) for their entire first month. The mechanics: Creators embed affiliate links in short-form videos on platforms like YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, earning a roughly 3% commission whenever viewers click and buy. Who is affected: Over 90% of hopeful beginners quit within the first 90 days, burned out by the invisible algorithm and abysmally low initial returns. The pivot: Those who survive past six months stop acting like pushy salespeople and instead treat themselves as "traffic drivers," relying on the platform's 24-hour cookie window where any purchase a user makes after clicking a link earns a commission.

Shedding the "Get Rich Quick" Illusion

The internet is flooded with self-proclaimed marketing gurus charging upwards of $1,500 to $2,200 (2 million to 3 million won) for masterclasses promising guaranteed monthly incomes. Yet, seasoned creators who clawed their way up from zero note that these expensive courses are largely unnecessary. The secret isn't a hidden algorithmic cheat code; it is sheer, grinding consistency—spending two to three hours every single day editing and uploading videos without a single day off.

Success requires mastering a two-track video strategy. The first track relies on "viral curiosity"—showcasing bizarre or fascinating gadgets that make viewers stop and comment. The second focuses on "problem-solving," presenting everyday household frustrations with neat, impulse-buy solutions. Because social media algorithms reward user retention above all else, creators who optimize for watch-time rather than hard selling are the ones who ultimately survive Coupang's punishing initial learning curve.

Navigating Platform Realities and Regulatory Pressures

Operating within South Korea's digital economy also means dealing with the whims of massive tech platforms. Coupang itself faces intense regulatory scrutiny; the Korea Fair Trade Commission recently slapped the company with a hefty $380,000 (500 million won) fine for deceptive advertising regarding its paid WOW membership discounts—a stark reminder that platforms often prioritize their own lock-in strategies over users.

For independent side-hustlers, staying compliant is just as crucial as chasing views. South Korea's Fair Trade Commission strictly requires clear disclosure text stating that posts involve affiliate commissions, and failing to do so can result in instant account bans. Ultimately, while automated tools and recycled content promise shortcuts, they usually end up flagged as low-quality spam. In a hyper-competitive market, only relentless execution and authentic audience engagement translate into a steady secondary income.
