Political Chaos in Seoul: Ruling and Opposition Parties Face Internal Meltdown

November 13, 2026

Internal fractures within both South Korea's ruling and main opposition parties have paralyzed the National Assembly, shifting the focus from public policy to aggressive internal policing and factional warfare.

  • The ruling Democratic Party (DP) has launched an urgent ethics investigation into Representative Choi Min-hee after she controversially claimed that President Lee Jae-myung opposes the past impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, is descending into civil war over allegations that internal disciplinary actions against party leader Han Dong-hoon were fabricated.
  • Legislative progress on sensitive issues, such as the Comprehensive Real Estate Tax (a heavy tax on high-value property owners), has stalled as party leaders prioritize silencing dissenters over passing bills.
  • Hardline members of the ruling DP are threatening to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae of the Supreme Court, fearing the potential release of Han Duck-soo, a high-profile figure from the previous administration currently in custody.

The Ruling Party’s Battle for Discipline

The Democratic Party is currently gripped by an "internal cleanup" following a series of verbal blunders by Representative Choi Min-hee. Choi caused a firestorm by suggesting that President Lee Jae-myung was privately against the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, a statement that directly contradicts the party’s official stance and the sentiments of its core supporters. Her additional disparaging remarks toward journalists further fueled public backlash, forcing the party leadership to initiate a swift ethical probe to prevent a further slide in approval ratings, which currently hover around 35%.

This crackdown reflects a deeper anxiety within the Blue House (the South Korean presidential office). While President Lee Jae-myung attempts to project an image of national unity, rogue comments from senior lawmakers are creating "strategic noise" that the opposition is quick to exploit. The internal friction is not limited to rhetoric; the party is also split on policy, with leadership figures like Kim Min-seok and Jung Chung-rae clashing over how to reform property taxes without alienating middle-class voters. For a ruling party that should be focused on the budget, these distractions are increasingly costly.

The Opposition’s Existential Strife

Meanwhile, the opposition People Power Party is facing a crisis of legitimacy. The "party message board scandal"—an ongoing dispute involving online criticism of party leadership—has escalated into accusations of "political purges." Representative Jang Dong-hyeok recently lambasted party leader Han Dong-hoon, calling his leadership "immoral" and a "seed of division." The situation turned explosive when an ethics committee member admitted that disciplinary measures against Han were based on manipulated information, leading Han to claim he is the victim of a coordinated political hit.

The PPP’s internal warfare has left it unable to function as an effective check on the government. With its approval ratings languishing at 25%, the party is struggling with a geographical and ideological divide between its traditional base and its more moderate wing. Instead of capitalizing on the ruling party’s missteps, the PPP is bogged down in lawsuits and mutual recriminations. As long as the opposition remains focused on its own survival and the upcoming race for floor leader, the legislative agenda in Seoul will remain in a state of total gridlock.

"Politics that is stained by internal sniping and verbal slips cannot win the trust of the people."
