NVIDIA Posts Massive Q3 Revenue, Rejects Jensen Huang's Return

November 19, 2026

NVIDIA defied market anxiety over the departure of its long-time leader, reporting a stellar $115 billion (155 trillion KRW) in third-quarter revenue and double-digit growth in both sales and operating profit.

  • Record Performance: Despite fears that the absence of Jensen Huang would stifle growth, the company’s strong earnings triggered a rebound in global tech stocks and the Nasdaq.
  • Leadership Shakeup: NVIDIA’s board officially rejected Huang’s bid to return as CEO, signaling a shift toward a new era of independent management.
  • Market Impact: The news helped South Korea’s benchmark index, the KOSPI, reclaim the 5,000-point level, buoying major tech stocks like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix.
  • Regulatory Hurdles: NVIDIA faces mounting pressure, including a new investigation by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), a cessation of AI chip supplies to China, and scrutiny over past business practices involving Alibaba.

A Test of Systems Over Personality

The most striking development of November 18 was not just the revenue figures, but the board’s firm refusal to rehire Jensen Huang. For a technology giant to publicly shut the door on its legendary founder suggests an immense level of confidence in its current order backlog and operational systems. By distancing itself from the "Huang era," NVIDIA is attempting to prove that its growth trajectory is driven by institutional momentum rather than individual charisma.

However, while strong financials provide temporary cover, they do not resolve the structural risks now facing the company. The combination of an SEC probe, the total suspension of China-bound chip shipments, and fresh scrutiny regarding the company’s past role in connecting Chinese partners like Alibaba to specific entities is a significant liability. The company is now navigating a complex "leadership vacuum" that will test whether its new management can maintain its market-leading position under intense regulatory scrutiny.

Supply Chain and Regional Resilience

In South Korea, the ripple effects were immediate. The KOSPI’s return to the 5,000-point mark confirms that demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) from local players like SK Hynix remains robust. NVIDIA further solidified its ties to the South Korean tech ecosystem by revising its component supply contracts with Samsung Electro-Mechanics and partnering with Yonsei University to open-source over 100 billion data points for 6G and AI-RAN (Radio Access Network) research.

While these moves shore up NVIDIA's hardware infrastructure, they serve as a double-edged sword for the South Korean tech sector. While current demand is secure, the volatility surrounding NVIDIA’s future leadership and its strained relationship with China mean that local suppliers may need to brace for sudden shifts in supply chain requirements and trade conditions. The transition away from Huang is not just an internal corporate matter—it is a global pivot that forces every partner in the AI supply chain to recalibrate their dependence on the chip giant.
