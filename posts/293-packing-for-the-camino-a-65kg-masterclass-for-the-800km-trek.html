Packing for the Camino: A 6.5kg Masterclass for the 800km Trek

September 07, 2026

Four years after COVID-19 grounded a planned departure just days before takeoff, a Korean traveler is finally packing their bags for a 45-day, 800km journey along the Camino de Santiago.

  • The core challenge: Balancing ultralight packing with essential gear, keeping total backpack weight at a manageable 6.5kg (including a laptop).
  • Smart gear choices: Utilizing a 30-liter backpack, a multi-use water-purifying bottle, and pre-broken-in hiking boots to prevent injuries.
  • Financial safety net: Distributing funds across four different travel cards and foreign currency accounts to guard against loss.
  • The mindset: Approaching the pilgrimage not as a glamorous vacation, but as a test of endurance, community, and personal resilience.

The Philosophy of the 6.5kg Pack

The golden rule of the Camino is that a backpack should never exceed 10% of your body weight. Striking the right balance between minimalism and survival is crucial. While ultralight enthusiasts preach absolute austerity, experienced hikers know that certain items—like knee braces, blister treatments, and proper electronics—are non-negotiable investments in safety rather than dead weight. Strategic redundancy, such as carrying multiple travel payment cards and pre-purchasing local SIM cards, eliminates friction on the road.

Walking as a Metaphor for Modern Life

Just as modern life forces us to extract meaning from mundane routines—much like finding comfort in a budget buffet—the Camino strips away the noise of daily existence. The 45-day trek across Spain’s unpredictable spring weather is less about sightseeing and more about enduring physical discomfort while building quiet camaraderie with fellow travelers. It is a deliberate exercise in shedding excess and discovering what remains when everything non-essential is left behind.
