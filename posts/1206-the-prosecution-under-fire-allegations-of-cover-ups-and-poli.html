The Prosecution Under Fire: Allegations of Cover-ups and Political Manipulation

October 16, 2026

South Korea’s legal establishment is facing a reckoning as serious allegations of misconduct emerge against senior prosecutors. The current scrutiny focuses on Kim Ji-yong, a chief prosecutor, and Lee Hee-dong, a high-ranking prosecutor, over claims that they systematically covered up sex crimes and manipulated evidence in politically charged cases during the previous administration.

  • The Allegations: Kim Ji-yong is accused of intentionally stalling investigations into sex crimes during his time as an inspection chief, while Lee Hee-dong is suspected of falsifying investigation reports to bury the "prosecutorial complaint-peddling" scandal.
  • The Impact: These revelations have deepened public distrust in the Prosecutor General’s Office, further fueling the push for radical judicial reform under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung.
  • The Core Issue: Critics argue these are not isolated incidents but symptoms of an insular "prosecutorial cartel" that prioritizes organizational protection and political alignment over the rule of law.

A Culture of Complicity

The accusations against Kim Ji-yong center on his tenure as an inspection chief, where he allegedly ignored reports of sexual misconduct involving powerful figures. Specifically, during the scandal involving former prosecutor Ahn Tae-geun, Kim is said to have avoided formal investigations under the pretext that the victims could not be clearly identified. This behavior is being characterized by legal observers not as administrative incompetence, but as a calculated, structural cover-up designed to protect "one of their own" at the expense of justice.

For a public already disillusioned by the era of the impeached and imprisoned former president, Yoon Suk-yeol, these revelations are a chilling reminder of how the prosecution once operated as a law unto itself. The perception is that for many years, the path to career success within the prosecution was paved by silence regarding internal misconduct and the suppression of inconvenient truths.

"Complaint-Peddling" and the Erosion of Integrity

Equally damaging are the claims against prosecutor Lee Hee-dong regarding the infamous "complaint-peddling" case, where prosecutors were accused of inciting political allies to file legal complaints against opponents. Investigations suggest that reports were finalized without conducting actual witness interviews or gathering evidence, effectively turning the prosecutorial process into a tool for political engineering rather than legal inquiry.

The scandal highlights a breakdown in the internal control systems of the prosecution. When senior officials can draft and sign off on fictitious investigation reports without consequence, it confirms that the organization’s primary function had drifted far from its mandate. Under the current Lee Jae-myung administration, the pressure is mounting to move beyond mere personnel changes and enact deep-seated structural reforms.

Ultimately, the crisis within the prosecution is one of legitimacy. If the institutions charged with upholding the law are viewed as the primary architects of its subversion, the social contract is severely damaged. For the reform movement, the goal is clear: strip away the culture of impunity, treat the "prosecutorial cartel" with the same scrutiny as any other offender, and restore the judiciary as a neutral protector of human rights rather than a political instrument.
