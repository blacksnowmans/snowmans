President Lee Jae-myung's Approval Ratings Slide: What the Numbers Actually Mean

October 11, 2026

  • Recent polls show President Lee Jae-myung's disapproval ratings hitting a stark 61.4%, with his approval hovering at around 37% just six months into his term.
  • The Democratic Party, South Korea's liberal ruling party, also saw its party support drop by 5 percentage points in tandem with the presidential slump.
  • While polls heavily reflect the disproportionate anger of highly politicized core activists, they also signal deep-seated frustration and fragmentation within the ruling bloc's traditional base.

South Korean politics is flashing warning lights for the progressive camp. Just six months after taking office, President Lee Jae-myung—leader of the liberal Democratic Party—is facing unprecedented public pushback. Recent surveys from Straight News and JoWon C&I place his disapproval rating at 61.4%, while approval sits trapped at roughly 37%. For a government that rode a wave of high expectations for reform, these figures feel like a jarring reality check.

A superficial look at the data suggests a government in freefall. However, reading these polls requires looking past the raw percentages. The respondents answering lengthy political surveys during this fatigue-heavy climate tend to be hyper-engaged political partisans eager to voice their frustrations, while moderate and neutral voters largely hang up. At the same time, the broader reform coalition is fracturing, with core supporters increasingly alienated by policy missteps and a lack of tangible economic relief.

The danger for the administration is not just sliding numbers, but self-inflicted isolation. As the ruling party attempts a clumsy pivot toward centrist voters, it risks alienating its base without actually winning over conservatives—who remain deeply opposed to President Lee regardless of policy shifts. With a fierce parliamentary audit season approaching, President Lee's late-night social media outreach and frantic communication efforts will ring hollow unless backed by systemic overhaul and real improvements to everyday livelihoods.
