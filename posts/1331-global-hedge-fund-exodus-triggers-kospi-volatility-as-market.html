Global Hedge Fund Exodus Triggers KOSPI Volatility as Markets Seek a "W-Shaped" Bottom

October 19, 2026

A historic sell-off in global semiconductor stocks, driven by massive hedge fund liquidations of "AI baskets," has pushed the KOSPI into a volatile search for a technical floor.

  • The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 15.7% over eight consecutive trading days, a streak of losses comparable only to the 2008 global financial crisis.
  • Major hedge funds aggressively offloaded AI-related holdings, slashing their exposure from 50% of their portfolios to roughly 28% in a single week to rotate into defensive sectors.
  • Panic has been fueled by rumors regarding the liquidation of the "VARA Fund," a massive entity managing approximately $40 billion (about 55 trillion won) that held concentrated positions in AI infrastructure.
  • Hana Securities, a major South Korean brokerage, forecasts a "W-shaped" recovery for the KOSPI, identifying a potential final support level near 5,300 points.

Liquidity, Not Technology, is the True Culprit

The recent carnage in the tech sector—impacting global leaders like Nvidia and Micron—is less about a sudden loss of faith in Artificial Intelligence and more about a brutal "money game" on Wall Street. Institutional investors are facing rising borrowing costs and margin calls, forcing a mechanical liquidation of their most profitable "AI basket" positions. This "invisible hand" of fund rebalancing explains why stock prices collapsed even without negative fundamental news from the companies themselves. With hedge fund exposure now nearing historical norms of around 25%, the worst of this forced selling pressure has likely peaked.

Market sentiment was further damaged by a "Situational Awareness" panic, reminiscent of previous hedge fund collapses where aggressive long-short positions went wrong. When prominent commentators like Jim Cramer of CNBC suggest that the market is currently "clearing out" hedge fund liquidations, it confirms that the current volatility is a systemic plumbing issue rather than a tech bubble bursting. For investors, the takeaway is clear: the current drop is a liquidation event, and selling into this panic often provides the "cheap entries" that institutional "hyenas" seek.

Navigating the Lee Jae-myung Administration’s Macro Challenges

For the Lee Jae-myung administration, the immediate task is managing a domestic market caught in a global macro pincer. While the KOSPI showed resilience by bouncing off recent intraday lows, a "V-shaped" vertical recovery is unlikely. Analysts expect a "W-shaped" pattern where the market tests a second low—potentially 2% lower than the first—to confirm a solid base. In this scenario, the 5,300 level is viewed as the ultimate "buy zone" rather than a reason for further panic.

The return of foreign capital, which is essential for a sustained rally, depends on three macro triggers: international oil prices stabilizing below $89 per barrel, US 10-year Treasury yields cooling toward 4.5%, and the won-dollar exchange rate dropping below 1,419 won. Until these indicators align, the Democratic Party of Korea (the ruling party) and the Bank of Korea (South Korea's central bank) will likely see continued volatility. Investors are advised to look beyond the crowded semiconductor trade and consider rotation into "low PBR" (price-to-book ratio) sectors such as secondary batteries, automotive, and defense, which are currently favored by institutional buyers looking for safety.

Market Note: While the market expects the Bank of Korea to hold interest rates steady in its upcoming meeting, the long-term pressure of high global rates continues to weigh on domestic "young-kkul" borrowers (those who stretched their finances to buy assets).
