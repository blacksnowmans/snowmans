Jensen Huang’s Sudden Exit Shakes Korean Chipmakers and Sparks Export Probe

November 14, 2026

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has abruptly stepped down after 33 years at the helm amid expanding US federal investigations and sensational allegations linking him to an alleged Chinese intelligence operative, sending immediate shockwaves through South Korea’s semiconductor sector.

  • Market hit: SK Hynix fell 3.0% and Samsung Electronics slipped 0.96%, pulling South Korea’s benchmark Kospi index down 0.98% to close at 5,290.
  • Espionage and sanctions probe: Taiwanese media reported that Huang’s alleged romantic partner, Alice Huang, is tied to a Chinese Ministry of State Security operation targeting semiconductor secrets, while US lawmakers and Malaysian authorities investigate whether advanced chips circumvented export bans through Southeast Asia.
  • Leadership vacuum: As an 11-member board deliberates on Huang’s successor, Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress has emerged as a central figure, pointing toward a more fiscally conservative operational era.
  • Seoul's emergency review: South Korean authorities and the National AI Strategic Committee convened an emergency meeting, confirming that Nvidia's planned delivery of 260,000 graphics processing units (GPUs) to Korea remains secure.

The HBM Alliance Faces a New Reality

For Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, the foundational demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) remains insulated from immediate collapse. Acute global shortages of advanced AI memory mean Nvidia cannot alter its dependence on Korean suppliers without disrupting its own product rollouts. However, the end of Huang's dominant one-man leadership removes a predictable, visionary counterpart who had personally managed relationships with Seoul's chip executives.

Should CFO Colette Kress take the reins, analysts anticipate a tighter grip on capital expenditures and a more aggressive push to diversify Nvidia’s supply chains. That shift is set to intensify the direct contest between SK Hynix, which seeks to defend its early-mover advantage in high-end HBM, and Samsung Electronics, which is positioning itself to capture additional custom HBM design orders under a more cost-sensitive Nvidia management.

Geopolitical Fallout and the Push for Domestic NPUs

The geopolitical ramifications carry greater systemic risk. Allegations that advanced chips leaked through third-party hubs like Malaysia will give Washington fresh momentum to tighten export controls across Asia. While Chinese buyers may scramble for alternative components from Korean suppliers, the tightening American regulatory net will narrow the leeway Korean semiconductor firms previously navigated between Washington and Beijing.

At home, the shock has spurred South Korea’s government to accelerate its campaign for technological self-reliance. Highlighting the vulnerability of leaning entirely on a single foreign GPU supplier, officials and tech conglomerates—including Samsung SDS and SK Telecom—are throwing heavier support behind local fabless startups such as Rebellions and FuriosaAI to advance homegrown neural processing units (NPUs) as viable long-term substitutes.
