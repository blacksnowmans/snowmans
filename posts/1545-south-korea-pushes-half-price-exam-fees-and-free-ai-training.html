South Korea Pushes Half-Price Exam Fees and Free AI Training to Ease Youth Job Crunch

October 27, 2026

  • What happened: The South Korean government rolled out a new public support package offering young job seekers a 50% discount on professional certification exam fees and free artificial intelligence training courses.
  • What it changes: Job hunters can now access government-backed tech training and fee subsidies directly through the unified "Gov24" digital portal, bypassing fragmented bureaucratic channels.
  • Who is affected: Young adults facing high living costs and steep credentialing fees, alongside adult learners navigating rapidly shifting labor markets driven by AI and automation.
  • Broader policy shift: A presidential advisory council has proposed a radical "National Activity Allowance" providing up to $1,100 (1.54 million won) per month for 24 months to workers undergoing AI-driven career transitions.

Tackling Job Market Stagnation With Direct Relief and AI Upskilling

South Korea's job market has turned into a high-stakes obstacle course where obtaining basic professional credentials and keeping pace with digital transformation demands hundreds of thousands of won per month. For a generation caught between soaring living costs and tightening employment barriers, the state's decision to cut certification exam fees in half and provide free public AI training is a necessary intervention. Rather than leaving young people to shoulder the soaring costs of private academies, the public sector is finally building a baseline safety net for career entry.

This pragmatic, field-focused approach marks a distinct shift from the previous administration under disgraced former President Yoon Suk-yeol, whose cuts to youth budgets left many feeling abandoned to survive on their own. Under current President Lee Jae-myung, policy design is pivoting toward easing the immediate financial pain of credentialing while integrating modern tech literacy into public services. By consolidating these benefits into the Gov24 platform, the administration is lowering administrative friction so that support reaches applicants before they burn through their savings.

Rethinking Social Safety Nets for the Age of Artificial Intelligence

Beyond entry-level job hunting, South Korea is beginning to confront the structural displacement that artificial intelligence brings to legacy industries. The presidential National Economic Advisory Council's proposal for a monthly living allowance during career retraining signals that policymakers recognize traditional unemployment insurance is no longer sufficient. When workers need up to two years to transition out of shrinking sectors and into digital roles, guaranteeing basic income security during retraining is the only way to prevent widespread economic fallout.

At the same time, the private-public divide in education is blurring as edtech firms move aggressively into government-backed vocational training markets. Yet, regional disparities remain a glaring vulnerability—as seen in Incheon, where a long-awaited disabled workers' training center was abruptly canceled after eight years of planning due to land-use disputes. If South Korea's ambitious push toward lifelong learning and AI readiness is to succeed, administrative competence must match political ambition, ensuring that public infrastructure protects society's most vulnerable rather than leaving them behind in bureaucratic limbo.
