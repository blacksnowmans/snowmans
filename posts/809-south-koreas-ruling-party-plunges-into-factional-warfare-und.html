South Korea's Ruling Party Plunges Into Factional Warfare Under President Lee Jae-myung

October 07, 2026

South Korea's progressive ruling camp is facing a severe internal crisis as critics accuse President Lee Jae-myung's administration of weaponizing the state apparatus to silence political opponents and purge rival factions.

  • Targeting families: A liberal civic group filed criminal charges against attorney Lee Jong-geun, husband of opposition-critical lawmaker Park Eun-jung, drawing fierce backlash for using family members as political collateral—a tactic reminiscent of the previous conservative administration.
  • Internal purges: Observers note a systematic effort by pro-Lee factions within the Democratic Party, South Korea's main liberal party, to sideline veteran mainstream figures tied to former liberal presidents Moon Jae-in and Roh Moo-hyun.
  • Rising dissent: Party heavyweights like Kim Min-seok are positioning themselves as potential counter-weights, signaling growing fractures within the ruling bloc as presidential approval ratings slide.
  • Broader fallout: Critics argue that the administration is squandering its reformist mandate by mimicking the aggressive prosecutorial politics it once vowed to dismantle, threatening long-term party unity.

The Lee Jong-geun Investigation and the Politics of Retribution

The controversy erupted when a progressive civic group filed complaints against attorney Lee Jong-geun, alleging abuse of power and professional misconduct related to a 2020 prosecutorial misconduct case. While framed as anti-corruption enforcement, critics view the move as a thinly veiled proxy attack on his wife, Lawmaker Park Eun-jung, who has openly criticized the current administration's policies.

For many veteran supporters of South Korea's liberal movement, seeing the ruling party deploy prosecutorial pressure against the family of a political critic brings an uncomfortable sense of deja vu. Observers point out that these tactics mirror the aggressive investigative strategies used against former justice ministers Cho Kuk and Choo Mi-ae during the administration of the imprisoned former leader Yoon Suk Yeol. When a reform-minded government adopts the very methods it once condemned, it alienates its core base and damages its moral authority.

Erosion of Party Roots and the Drive for Total Control

Beyond individual investigations, the ruling party is undergoing a profound internal realignment aimed at cementing President Lee Jae-myung's absolute control over the Democratic Party. Long-standing factions tracing their lineage back to former presidents Kim Dae-jung, Roh Moo-hyun, and Moon Jae-in are increasingly marginalized, replaced by loyalists and pro-Lee figures.

This centralization of power has triggered fierce jockeying among ambitious politicians eyeing the next presidential race. Figures within the party are quietly drawing battle lines, fearing that the administration's heavy-handed approach will alienate mainstream voters. Unless the ruling camp returns to its founding principles of horizontal democracy and institutional checks, it risks turning a hard-won democratic mandate into a fragile house of cards.
