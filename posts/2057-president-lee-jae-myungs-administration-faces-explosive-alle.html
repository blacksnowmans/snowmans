President Lee Jae-myung’s Administration Faces Explosive Allegations of Judicial Tampering

November 06, 2026

A shocking allegation has emerged suggesting that a sitting lawmaker and high-ranking officials from President Lee Jae-myung’s administration coordinated an unofficial task force to obstruct the arrest of a major fugitive in exchange for favorable legal testimony.

  • Rep. Park Sun-won of the ruling Democratic Party is accused of blocking the formation of a police "capture team" for Bae Sang-yoon, the former chairman of KH Group, who is currently an Interpol Red Notice fugitive facing breach of trust charges involving about $300 million (400 billion won).
  • The allegations, first reported by Energy Economy News, suggest a secret task force involving the Democratic Party, the Presidential Office, and the National Intelligence Service (NIS)—South Korea's main spy agency—met in August to negotiate judicial leniency for Bae in exchange for testimony favorable to President Lee.
  • Lee Kwang-bum, a prominent lawyer and legal counsel for President Lee, was recently appointed as an outside director for Bit & Electron, a company linked to the fugitive’s business group, further fueling suspicions of a backroom deal involving an estimated $11 million (15 billion won) in lobbying funds.
  • If proven true, the mobilization of state agencies like the NIS for the President’s private legal defense would constitute a grave constitutional violation and potential grounds for impeachment.

The Privatization of State Power

The core of this scandal is the alleged "mercenary" use of public institutions. The NIS is legally prohibited by Article 4 of the National Intelligence Service Act from engaging in domestic political affairs or private legal matters. If agents from the NIS and the Presidential Office indeed met in an "unofficial TF" to trade the freedom of a major financial criminal for political testimony, it represents a total collapse of the rule of law. This is particularly jarring for an administration that rose to power by criticizing the "lawless" governance of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment.

The silence from the accused parties is deafening. Despite the severity of the claims—which include allegations of illegal campaign funds being funneled through KH Group affiliates—neither Rep. Park Sun-won nor attorney Lee Kwang-bum has offered a substantial rebuttal. In South Korea's hyper-reactive political environment, where even minor slights are met with immediate legal threats or press releases, this prolonged silence suggests a high level of discomfort with the evidence being brought to light.

Trading Justice for Testimony

The deal allegedly centered on the "North Korea remittance case," a long-running investigation into illegal money transfers to Pyongyang. Prosecutors believe Bae Sang-yoon is a key link in this case. By allegedly offering Bae a "no-arrest" guarantee or a suspended sentence, the administration is accused of trying to "clean" the testimony of a primary witness to protect the President's political future. This mirrors similar allegations raised earlier this year regarding attempts to have the President's indictments withdrawn through unofficial channels.

The human and institutional costs of such a deal are immense. While ordinary citizens struggle with inflation and economic stagnation, the spectacle of the ruling elite allegedly negotiating with organized crime-linked businessmen to bypass the judiciary creates a profound sense of public betrayal. For international investors and observers, these allegations raise serious questions about the independence of the South Korean justice system under the current leadership. If the state’s investigative power is being used as a bargaining chip for the President’s personal legal risks, the very foundation of South Korean democracy is at stake.
