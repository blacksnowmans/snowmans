South Korea Bets Big on "Physical AI" as Robotics Meets Real-World Industry

November 14, 2026

South Korea is aggressively pivoting from software-bound chat interfaces to "Physical AI"—artificial intelligence embedded in physical robots that can navigate factories, hospitals, and city streets to perform real-world tasks.

  • Commercial momentum: Industrial AI firm MakinaRocks posted about $10 million (14.3 billion won) in cumulative sales for the third quarter, proving that physical AI is generating actual manufacturing revenue rather than just research lab results.
  • Academic-industrial alliances: Major players like startup XYZ and machinery maker T-Robotics are partnering with the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST) to co-develop embodied AI solutions and robotics hardware.
  • Government push: The administration of South Korean President Lee Jae-myung has announced a national roadmap to develop 10 customized AI semiconductor types for cars, home appliances, and robots by 2030.
  • Global and local infrastructure: Tech giants like Intel and SanDisk are rolling out specialized on-device AI storage chips, while local municipalities like Seongnam are building medical robot integration centers.

Moving From Screens to Factories

The core shift in South Korea's tech sector is the transition from "brains in a box" to embodied intelligence. This is no longer about answering text prompts; it's about machines that control physical actuators and sensors in real time. Industry leaders like LG CNS are building out "Physical AI operating systems" to manage fleet logistics, while smaller firms test the waters with novelty projects—such as high-paying robot-dog-walking gigs that double as urban data-collection trials.

At the same time, this rush to physical automation brings thorny ethical and labor questions. As robots enter public spaces and factory floors, companies are scrambling to set up safety protocols and liability frameworks. Whether these machines end up easing human labor or simply reducing workers to data-shuttles remains the defining tension of the physical AI era.
