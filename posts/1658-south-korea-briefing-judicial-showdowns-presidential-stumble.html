South Korea Briefing: Judicial Showdowns, Presidential Stumbles, and a $2.6 Million Secret Poll Controversy

October 29, 2026

South Korean politics hit a boiling point as a planned parliamentary probe into Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae collided with President Lee Jae-myung’s controversial remarks on foreign labor and fierce blowback over the presidential office's secretive polling expenditures.

    What happened: The liberal ruling Democratic Party pushed ahead with a parliamentary investigation into Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, while President Lee sparked criticism from the progressive camp over comments questioning multicultural family support policies. Meanwhile, reports revealed the presidential office spent about $2.6 million (3.6 billion won) across 181 opinion polls while keeping the subjects and polling agencies strictly confidential. What it changes: The aggressive legislative push against the judiciary tests the limits of the separation of powers, while Lee's outreach to conservative online communities has unexpectedly fractured his own progressive base. Who is affected: The judiciary faces unprecedented legislative pressure, foreign and multicultural workers find themselves at the center of a sudden political debate, and the ruling party grapples with mounting public fatigue over backroom political squabbling.

Judicial Battles and Secret Polls

The legislative pressure on South Korea's judiciary reached new heights as the liberal ruling bloc insisted on a parliamentary probe into Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, drawing fierce pushback from the conservative opposition People Power Party and Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, who labeled the move an assault on judicial independence. Tempers also flared on the ground during a parliamentary audit involving millions of disputed ballots, where shouting matches drew police intervention and heightened political tensions.

Compounding the administration's headaches, transparency advocates and opposition lawmakers slammed the presidential office for running 181 internal opinion polls totaling about $2.6 million (3.6 billion won) without disclosing the specific research topics or polling agencies used. Critics argue that hiding the data behind a veil of secrecy undermines the basic public trust required for effective governance, turning what should be public data into opaque political maneuvering.

Presidential Outreach and Conservative Fractures

President Lee Jae-myung attempted a novel outreach strategy by posting directly on a conservative online community, where he touched on foreign labor issues and suggested reviewing preferential treatment policies for multicultural families. While some welcomed the direct engagement, the comments drew sharp rebukes from progressive minor parties and the Rebuilding Korea Party, who criticized the remarks as insensitive to marginalized communities. Even as Lee hosted a lengthy dinner with senior ruling-party lawmakers to urge unity and a focus on public livelihoods, the political landscape remained fixated on internal power struggles.

Within the conservative opposition, a proxy leadership battle has emerged between Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon and former party leader Han Dong-hoon, further complicating conservative realignment efforts as parties ready themselves for upcoming legislative fights, including the ruling party's push for a special counsel bill targeting alleged prosecutorial evidence fabrication.
