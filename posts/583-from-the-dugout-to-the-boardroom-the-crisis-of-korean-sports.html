From the Dugout to the Boardroom: The Crisis of Korean Sports Management

September 10, 2026

The latest episode of Sports Factory, a popular Korean sports commentary show, has reignited a fierce debate about the state of professional sports in South Korea. While the banter between veteran pundits was lighthearted, the underlying message was clear: whether on the baseball diamond or the soccer pitch, South Korean sports are being held back by archaic management and a lack of long-term vision.

  • The "Temp" Culture: The show highlighted the recurring trend of teams and associations relying on "stopgap" solutions—temporary managers and quick fixes—rather than building sustainable systems.
  • Baseball's Stagnation: Persistent struggles from teams like the Lotte Giants and Hanwha Eagles are no longer seen as simple "bad luck" but as failures of front-office management.
  • Soccer's High-Stakes Gamble: The South Korean national football team has appointed a Spanish manager, Moreno (a former analyst under Luis Enrique), but with a controversial six-game "probationary" contract.
  • Fans vs. Bureaucrats: The gap between modern, data-savvy fans and the Korea Football Association’s (KFA) 1990s-style bureaucratic decision-making has reached a boiling point.

The Failure of Front-Office Systems

The conversation on Sports Factory—hosted by political commentator Kim Ou-joon, soccer expert Park Moon-sung, and broadcaster Im Yong-soo—cut through the usual post-game fluff. The panelists pointed out that the misery of lower-tier baseball teams is a systemic issue. When a team fails to perform, the response is almost always a robotic apology from the front office, followed by the vague promise of "rebuilding."

The critique is that sports in Korea have become a "litmus test" for the country's broader professional frustrations. Fans are no longer satisfied with "trying their best." They are demanding accountability, data-driven decision-making, and transparency—all of which are currently missing from organizations that seem to rely on nepotism or agent influence rather than clear, merit-based strategies.

A Risky Gamble for the National Team

The appointment of Moreno as the new national football team manager is a perfect case study in KFA dysfunction. While Moreno’s tactical analysis background is respected, his lack of experience as a head coach is a massive red flag. The KFA’s decision to put him on a "six-game trial" is being widely criticized as a cowardly move: if he fails, the KFA can point to his "lack of ability," and if he succeeds, they claim credit for their "visionary hiring."

By treating the national team like an internship program, the KFA is failing to provide the stability and authority a manager needs to succeed. It is a classic case of Korean sports administration prioritizing short-term liability protection over long-term strategic growth. As long as the "top brass" continues to hide behind short-term contracts and bureaucratic jargon, the talent on the field will continue to be overshadowed by the incompetence in the boardroom.
