The AI Capex Mirage: Why Tech Booms While the Broader Market Bleeds

October 12, 2026

An unprecedented surge in artificial intelligence infrastructure spending is single-handedly keeping headline stock benchmarks near record highs, masking a punishing liquidity squeeze across small caps, consumer sentiment, and export-reliant economies like South Korea.

  • Capex driving earnings: Massive data center buildouts are creating a powerful industrial supply chain ripple—Japanese machine tool order backlogs have topped ¥1 trillion (about $6.5 billion), helping push projected S&P 500 earnings growth toward nearly 30%.
  • Extreme market concentration: The headline rally obscures severe weakness underneath, with only about 30% of S&P 500 stocks trading above their 50-day moving average and high-yield junk bond rates spiking to 15%.
  • Korean equities hit hard: Lacking the broad buffer of Wall Street's mega-cap tech giants, Seoul's markets have absorbed the full brunt of high interest rates, with the tech-heavy junior Kosdaq index erasing its annual gains after a sharp 5.3% weekly tumble.
  • Supply chain inflation hitting hardware: Soaring component costs have triggered a 21% plunge in global PC shipments, pushing retail computer prices in Korea from under 3 million won to past 4.5 million won (about $3,350) and feeding broader consumer strain.

Capital Concentration in the Data Center Funnel

The persistence of high equity valuations despite interest rates exceeding 5% defies traditional market models, but it does not signal broad macroeconomic health. Instead, global capital is concentrating into a single physical asset: the AI data center. While cloud margins remain lucrative, private credit and mega-cap balance sheets are cornering computing hardware and energy capacity, leaving smaller enterprises to struggle under double-digit financing costs.

This dynamic creates a precarious divergence between paper wealth and the real economy. As U.S. 10-year Treasury yields hover near multi-year highs and consumer sentiment indicators remain near historic lows, soaring power demand from server farms is compounding energy bottlenecks. If inflation expectations force central banks to prolong restrictive monetary policies, highly leveraged corporate borrowers and consumer-facing sectors face deepening insolvency risks.

The Cost for Export-Heavy Korea

For South Korea, this global capital reallocation is a double-edged sword. While upstream semiconductor manufacturers capture essential revenue from the data center buildout, the broader domestic market is bearing the domestic cost of prolonged global inflation and a pressured won trading near 1,340 per dollar. With high interest rates sapping local liquidity, small- and mid-cap stocks have been left vulnerable, leaving ordinary retail investors disconnected from the gains enjoyed by foreign tech conglomerates.
