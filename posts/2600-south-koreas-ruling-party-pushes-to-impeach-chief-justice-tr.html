South Korea’s Ruling Party Pushes to Impeach Chief Justice, Triggering Constitutional Clashes

November 22, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party is coalescing around a plan to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, sparking a fierce backlash from the opposition and the legal community over judicial independence.

  • What happened: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea is preparing to introduce an impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho, targeting December 3 to bring the measure forward over dissatisfaction with recent top court rulings.
  • The opposition's reaction: Han Dong-hoon, leader of the main opposition People Power Party, denounced the drive as an "institutional coup" and a "confession of insurrection" meant to subjugate the court system.
  • Legal sector pushback: South Korean bar associations issued formal statements demanding the ruling party halt the initiative, warning that impeaching a top judge over court rulings destroys the constitutional separation of powers.
  • The broader impact: The high-stakes feud threatens to completely paralyze year-end legislative business, including critical budget negotiations, while deepening institutional hostility across state branches.

A Fierce Escalation Between the Ruling Party and Opposition

The Democratic Party's push reflects growing pressure from its hardline faction. Lawmakers such as Representative Choo Mi-ae have called for immediate execution without calculating political risks, while allied figures like Representative Shin Jang-sik of the minor opposition Rebuilding Korea Party urged a swift vote. However, internal questions remain: while the motion stems from political anger over recent judicial decisions, reports indicate that specific controversial rulings have been excluded from the current draft grounds for impeachment to withstand scrutiny.

The conservative People Power Party has declared all-out warfare against the move. Opposition leader Han Dong-hoon openly challenged the ruling party to proceed on December 3, warning that those orchestrating the motion will ultimately face criminal accountability. PPP lawmakers blasted the attempt as "legislative lynching" and a political "suicide bomb," accusing the ruling party of trying to force compliance from the judiciary.

Why Legal Experts Are Sounding the Alarm

The controversy has sent shockwaves through the legal establishment. Bar associations across the country issued statements warning that using impeachment to express dissatisfaction with judicial verdicts allows the legislature to act as a pseudo-appellate court. Legal scholars emphasize that subjecting judges to political retaliation undermines the rule of law and deters members of the bench from ruling according to conscience and legal precedent.

Regardless of whether the Democratic Party ultimately passes the motion, the dispute is taking a heavy toll on constitutional norms. With both sides taking uncompromising stances, the confrontation over the judiciary threatens to consume South Korean politics for the remainder of the year.
