South Korea's August 13 Real Estate Policy Stokes Market Chaos

September 01, 2026

South Korea's latest real estate package has sparked widespread criticism, as analysts and prospective homebuyers argue the measures do little to ease an acute housing shortage while fueling confusion through contradictory lending rules.

  • What happened: The South Korean government unveiled the August 13 real estate measures, promising new housing supply through greenbelt releases and new town developments alongside adjustments to mortgage and lease financing.
  • The supply illusion: Out of 27,000 planned housing units announced, only about 1,500 can be supplied immediately, with major new developments requiring eight to 10 years before residents can move in.
  • Mixed signals on debt: Sudden shifts in lending regulations and policies that permit certain homeowners to access key-money deposit loans (*jeonse*) have rattled buyers and aggravated rental shortages.
  • Who is affected: Prospective homebuyers, young couples, and renters facing vanishing *jeonse* (lump-sum deposit) listings and skyrocketing housing anxiety.

A Supply Crunch Disguised as Long-Term Promises

The core grievance with the August 13 policy is its staggering disconnect from reality. When officials tout plans for 20,000 new homes via third-generation new towns and Seoul's Seocho District, they ignore a basic timeline: building these neighborhoods takes nearly a decade. For a renter squeezed out of a home today, a prospective apartment key a decade away is an empty promise. Compounding the frustration, the vast majority of the government's grand 27,000-unit blueprint consists of unbuilt or distant projects, leaving immediate housing needs unmet.

This "hope-torture" approach amplifies market uncertainty. Without clear guidelines on pre-sale prices or exact timelines, buyers are left navigating a chaotic landscape blindfolded. Critics argue that half-measures and timid rollouts only intensify public panic, turning the housing search into a guessing game.

Policy Whiplash and the Rental Squeeze

Financial rules are creating just as much friction. The government's erratic handling of mortgage and loan regulations—alternating between sudden clampdowns and questionable allowances for existing homeowners—has transformed the market into an unpredictable casino. Sudden curbs leave genuine homebuyers stranded without adequate transition periods, while looser rules for certain asset-holders risk inflating prices further.

Meanwhile, the rental market bears the brunt of these missteps. As tax shifts and regulatory pressures push landlords to convert *jeonse* units into monthly rentals or withhold listings altogether, affordable housing options for newlyweds and first-time buyers are vanishing. Observers note that prioritizing political logic over straightforward housing stability has left everyday citizens to fend for themselves in an unforgiving market.
