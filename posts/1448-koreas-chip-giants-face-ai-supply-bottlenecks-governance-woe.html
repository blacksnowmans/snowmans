Korea’s Chip Giants Face AI Supply Bottlenecks, Governance Woes, and a Rising China

October 25, 2026

As the global artificial intelligence boom strains hardware supply chains, South Korean chipmakers and component suppliers face a double-edged sword of soaring AI-driven demand alongside severe supply bottlenecks and aggressive pressure from Chinese competitors.

  • Major Nvidia partnership: Samsung Electro-Mechanics, the electronic component arm of Samsung Group, finalized a multi-year deal with Nvidia to supply next-generation AI package substrates and secured 3.7 trillion won (about $2.7 billion) in combined funding from Tesla and Nvidia for dedicated production lines.
  • Pivot to custom AI memory: Industry leaders Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, South Korea's top two chipmakers, are moving away from standardized memory to focus on custom-designed HBM4 (high-bandwidth memory) tailored to tech giants' bespoke AI chip architectures.
  • Shareholder pushback on governance: SK Hynix scrapped plans for a controversial subsidiary dual-listing following intense backlash from retail investors concerned about corporate governance and stock value dilution.
  • Surging Chinese competition: State-backed Chinese memory makers ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) and Yangtze Memory Technologies Corp (YMTC) are making rapid gains, with CXMT launching a 39 trillion won (about $28 billion) expansion plan that has drawn interest from Nvidia.

The Hardware Bottleneck and Valuation Disconnect

While AI server builds are fueling bright profit forecasts for Korean tech giants, physical supply chain constraints are emerging as a major bottleneck. Multilayer ceramic capacitor (MLCC) lead times at Samsung Electro-Mechanics have stretched to 61 weeks—nearly triple normal levels—causing component prices to jump 23%. Yet despite record earnings projections in the AI segment, South Korean chipmakers remain trapped in low stock valuation multiples. Investors remain skittish due to deep slumps in traditional consumer tech markets, where "chipflation" helped drive a 21% drop in global PC shipments, alongside lingering local governance risks that continue to weigh on Korean market valuations.

Customization and China's Rapid Chase

To defend their market share, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are transforming memory semiconductors from a bulk commodity business into a high-end custom engineering service. The next primary battlefield is custom HBM4 memory, where foundry integration allows memory chips to be directly optimized for custom tech-firm silicon. Samsung is also showing signs of life in its non-memory design division, with its upcoming Exynos 2700 mobile processor outperforming Qualcomm chips in internal testing. However, South Korea's window of technical dominance is narrowing as Chinese rivals leverage massive state support to capture legacy memory markets, even as domestic infrastructure hurdles—such as power grid supply conflicts at the planned national semiconductor cluster in Yongin—threaten to slow down South Korea's own facility expansion plans.
