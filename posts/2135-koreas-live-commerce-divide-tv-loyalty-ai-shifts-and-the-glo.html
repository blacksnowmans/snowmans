Korea's Live Commerce Divide: TV Loyalty, AI Shifts, and the Global Push

November 09, 2026

South Korea's live commerce and retail sectors are seeing a sharp divergence in strategy and financial results, as traditional TV home shopping proves stubbornly resilient against premature mobile-first pivots even while operators turn to artificial intelligence and platforms like TikTok Shop for future growth.

  • The TV-mobile split: GS Shop defended its earnings during the consumer downturn by doubling down on core TV broadcasts, while rival CJ OnStyle saw performance stall after aggressively pushing an early "post-TV" transition toward mobile livestreams and short-form video.
  • Global expansion: Kurly, the South Korean e-commerce platform known for pioneer dawn grocery delivery, is taking its private-label beauty products overseas by setting up shop on Amazon and TikTok Shop to tap social commerce demand.
  • AI integration: Broadcasters are rolling out AI recommendation engines and automated late-night virtual hosts to cut studio production costs and parse live chat comments in real time.
  • Seller fee squeeze: Small and mid-sized merchants are raising alarms over platform commission rates exceeding 34%, which threaten merchant profitability once logistics and broadcast production expenses are factored in.

The Perils of Abandoning TV Too Soon

The contrasting third-quarter fortunes of GS Shop and CJ OnStyle offer a sharp lesson in the limits of digital transition. While mobile live commerce represents the obvious long-term direction, South Korea's deep-pocketed consumers in their 50s and 60s remain firmly anchored to legacy cable TV. By cutting back on its television core to chase mobile users, CJ OnStyle incurred heavy marketing overhead without immediately capturing replacement revenue. GS Shop, by contrast, relied on traditional broadcasts to generate stable cash flow.

This dynamic is reshaping executive strategy across Korean retail conglomerates. Shinsegae Group recently reassigned veteran executive Lee Seok-goo—known for turnarounds at Starbucks Korea and Shinsegae Live Shopping—to oversee department store operations ahead of an impending corporate affiliate separation. The move reflects an industry-wide push to blend physical flagship store experiences directly into live mobile streams rather than treating digital and legacy retail as separate businesses.

Chasing Scale Through Algorithms and Foreign Feeds

With domestic consumer spending soft, Korean retailers are using live video to bypass saturated home markets. Kurly's bet on TikTok Shop in North America and Southeast Asia reflects a broader transition among K-beauty sellers away from conventional search-based shopping toward video-led impulse buying. Meanwhile, entertainment giant CJ ENM is integrating content production with global checkout pipelines to monetize foreign viewers directly through live video formats.

Yet the ecosystem's expansion faces structural headwinds at home. With headline platform fees climbing past 34% on certain domestic and international channels, vendors report that promotional live streams increasingly generate vanity volume rather than operating profit. As platforms automate late-night broadcasting with AI avatars to keep feeds running around the clock, surviving will depend on whether operators establish sustainable commission terms before merchant margins collapse completely.
