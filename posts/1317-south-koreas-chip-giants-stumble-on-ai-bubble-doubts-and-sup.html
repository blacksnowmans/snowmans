South Korea's Chip Giants Stumble on AI Bubble Doubts and Supply-Demand Distortion

October 19, 2026

The steep global sell-off hitting South Korean chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix reflects panic over AI monetization and aggressive short selling rather than broken semiconductor fundamentals.

  • Global tech rout hits Seoul: Foreign investors offloaded over 6.2 trillion won (about $4.6 billion) in domestic stocks across two days, concentrating 87% of the barrage on semiconductors.
  • Divergent plays on key players: Even while suppressing indices through futures, foreign funds net purchased 181.8 billion won (about $136 million) in Samsung Electronics spot shares near book-value support, while selling 74.3 billion won of SK Hynix.
  • AI infrastructure jitters: Sharp drops in GPU cloud rental firms CoreWeave (-11.9%) and Nebius (-12.2%), combined with 10-year US Treasury yields topping 5.06% and looming 2026 debt roll-overs, spurred fears of slowing AI capex.
  • Underlying demand remains intact: Conventional memory fundamentals remain solid, with third-quarter DRAM contract prices up 18–22% and server flash memory facing structural supply shortages.

Market Sentiment Clashes with Robust Memory Pricing

The current turbulence resembles an intentional shakeout rather than an industry downturn. Sinking prices across the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index—plunging nearly 4% as major names fell 5% to 6%—have pushed technical indicators into deeply oversold territory. Yet the panic regarding "neocloud" providers ignores the reality that these firms constitute under 10% of the aggregate AI data center footprint. Meanwhile, proof of real software revenue is emerging, highlighted by AI firm Anthropic delivering $11.5 billion in preliminary revenue and reaching its first profitable quarter.

In Seoul, market pricing has detached from underlying corporate earnings. Combined operating profit estimates for Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix approach an eye-watering 190 trillion won, driven by surging DRAM and NAND pricing. SK Hynix, despite trading down significantly from recent highs, continues to benefit from severe server memory shortages. For retail investors navigating an environment whipsawed by short-selling hedge funds, trying to pinpoint the absolute bottom is a trap; maintaining cash reserves and pacing gradual purchases over weeks offers the only consistent hedge.

Governance Hurdles and Corporate Transparency

Beyond macroeconomic swings, domestic tech bellwethers face growing friction with retail shareholders over corporate stewardship. Investors in SK Hynix have grown vocal over potential US listing plans for its NAND subsidiary, Solidigm. While navigating geopolitical friction between the US and China is critical for South Korean chipmakers, management has yet to clarify how proceeds from a separate listing would benefit existing shareholders rather than diluting long-term corporate value.

Retail investors are increasingly demanding structural shareholder return policies like aggressive buyback cancellations, similar to those seen in Japan, or the shareholder-friendly practices recently adopted by select domestic auto parts makers like PHA. As long as Korean tech leaders treat balance sheet actions as corporate prerogatives detached from shareholder returns, the lingering "Korea Discount" will amplify downward volatility whenever external tech cycles wobble.
