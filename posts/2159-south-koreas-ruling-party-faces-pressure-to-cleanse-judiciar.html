South Korea’s Ruling Party Faces Pressure to Cleanse Judiciary and Prosecution

November 10, 2026

  • Grassroots activist groups linked to the ruling Democratic Party have launched a formal parliamentary petition calling for the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.
  • Critics argue the ruling party moved too slowly on a special counsel bill targeting fabricated prosecutions from the previous administration, even as the legislation passes the National Assembly.
  • President Lee Jae-myung has nominated Lee Yong-chul as Minister of Justice, setting up a showdown over judicial reform and potential dismissals of politically motivated indictments.

South Korea’s political battlegrounds have shifted firmly toward the judiciary. Following the impeachment and imprisonment of former leader Yoon Suk-yeol, activists and ruling-party supporters are demanding an immediate reckoning with the conservative legal establishment that they accuse of shielding past abuses.

The push to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae through a public petition process marks a escalation in tensions. Because the petition crossed the threshold required for formal legislative review, it must now be sent to the parliamentary legislation and judiciary committee, setting the stage for high-profile hearings. Critics argue that under Cho, the Supreme Court has repeatedly favored conservative interests and dragged its feet on accountability.

Confronting the Legacy of Politically Motivated Prosecutions

At the same time, the special counsel bill targeting fabricated prosecutions has cleared the National Assembly, though internal party critics have slammed leadership for failing to push the measure through immediately after party conventions. Opposing conservative lawmakers have attempted to block the move by boycotting budget deliberations, but the ruling bloc maintains legal avenues—including committee votes and direct submission by the National Assembly speaker—to pass state budgets on schedule.

The newly nominated Justice Minister, Lee Yong-chul, is expected to spearhead efforts to reform the prosecution service. A long-time progressive figure, Lee faces the immediate and controversial task of deciding whether to drop politically driven indictments brought during Yoon's tenure. Supporters view the moves as essential justice, while opponents warn of executive overreach.
