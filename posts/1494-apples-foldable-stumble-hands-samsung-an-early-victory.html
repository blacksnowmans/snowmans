Apple’s Foldable Stumble Hands Samsung an Early Victory

October 26, 2026

Apple’s first foldable smartphone, the iPhone Duo, has triggered widespread backlash over sudden screen failures and unoptimized software, sparking a global re-evaluation of Samsung Electronics’ hardware dominance.

  • The "Crackgate" Outcry: Priced at roughly $2,400 (3.29 million won), the iPhone Duo faces mounting reports of center-crease fractures within hours of unboxing, compounded by brutal teardowns from tech reviewers showing severe dust ingress and screen death.
  • Astronomical Repair Costs: An out-of-warranty internal display repair is listed at $999, forcing buyers to shoulder roughly one-third of the device’s total cost for a single hardware failure.
  • Unfinished Software: Everyday applications from Line to Pokémon GO are launching with massive black borders and hidden user interface buttons, exposing a rushed developer rollout that caught app makers off guard.
  • The Beneficiary: South Korea's Samsung Electronics and its Galaxy Z Fold 8 are seeing an unexpected surge in praise, as global reviewers re-examine the durability and software maturity built over eight generations of foldables.

Fragile Glass and Rushed Software

Apple entered the foldable race promising premium durability with internal titanium reinforcement, but real-world usage has told a different story. Prominent tech outlets and torture tests have demonstrated that hinge pressure transfers directly into the foldable panel, leaving the display vulnerable to debris and reverse bending. Combined with a steep $999 repair fee, consumers are finding themselves holding a fragile, high-risk luxury item rather than a reliable daily driver.

The software experience has proved equally unready. Instead of leveraging the expanded canvas, the phone frequently boxes legacy apps into standard smartphone dimensions flanked by empty black margins. Developers report having had as little as a month to adapt to the new aspect ratios, resulting in obscured navigation menus and broken tablet workflows. For a company whose reputation rests on seamless user experience, the rough launch represents an uncharacteristically clumsy platform debut.

Samsung’s Eight-Year Moat

In South Korea and abroad, the Duo’s struggles have served as an inadvertent showcase for Samsung’s engineering resilience. The Korean tech giant weathered its own humiliating launch delay with the original Galaxy Fold in 2019, spending the subsequent seven years and eight hardware iterations perfecting micro-brush hinge seals, alloy support plates, and flexible multitasking interfaces. When recent stress tests showed the Fold 8 surviving severe bend tests while the iPhone Duo broke down, consumer sentiment shifted rapidly.

Hardware scale cannot be improvised overnight. While Apple's entry legitimizes the foldable category, it also proves that brand power cannot bypass the physical trials of flexible engineering. Samsung's head start has built a deep industrial moat—giving Korean manufacturers a distinct edge in ultra-thin materials, precision hinges, and specialized user interfaces that competitors are still struggling to replicate.
