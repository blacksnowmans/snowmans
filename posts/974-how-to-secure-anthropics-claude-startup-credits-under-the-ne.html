How to Secure Anthropic's Claude Startup Credits Under the New Rules

October 11, 2026

  • Anthropic has overhauled its "Claude Startups" program criteria, moving past the era of automated approvals to aggressively filter out non-startup applicants.
  • Despite official "limit exceeded" notices on the website, founders are still successfully getting approved—provided they meet strict verification standards.
  • Applicants must now use corporate domain emails, verifiable websites, and exact organization setups to claim up to $45,000 in total value, including a free year of the Claude Team plan and $1,000 (about 1.4 million won) in API credits.
  • The crackdown reflects rising anxieties over AI safety, forcing developers to balance rapid innovation against strict platform compliance.

Navigating the New Application Gatekeepers

Anthropic's popular startup support program has become significantly harder to crack following an October overhaul. While the company's official landing page still warns that benefit limits have been exceeded, this serves largely as a speed bump to deter casual applicants. In reality, re-evaluations and re-approval notices are rolling out for genuine organizations that were caught in the initial automated backlog.

Getting past the screening process now requires total operational transparency. Using a personal Gmail or Outlook address instead of a company domain name (`@company.com`) is an instant disqualifier. Furthermore, startup websites can no longer hide behind vague jargon like "AI-powered solutions." Reviewers are demanding specific use cases, explicit pain points, and proof that the applicant is an active business entity rather than an opportunistic hobbyist.

Maximizing Your Benefits Safely

Once approved, speed and caution are equally critical. Founders must immediately check their console to ensure the billing total drops to $0, as unclaimed approvals risk being revoked. API credits carry a strict six-month expiration date and work exclusively within the Claude Console—third-party platforms like Amazon Bedrock or Google Vertex AI are excluded.

Beyond the technical hurdles, Anthropic's tightening grip highlights the broader tension between AI democratization and tight risk control. As the current administration under President Lee Jae-myung navigates the rapid growth of domestic tech, policymakers and creators alike are grappling with safety reports warning of AI misuse in biological weapons and cyberattacks. For founders, navigating these new rules is less about free handouts and more about proving that your organization can use powerful tools responsibly.
