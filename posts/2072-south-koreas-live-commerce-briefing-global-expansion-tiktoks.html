South Korea's Live-Commerce Briefing: Global Expansion, TikTok's Push, and the Trust Deficit

November 07, 2026

Live-streaming retail in South Korea has evolved past simple shopping broadcasts into a high-stakes arena for global expansion, short-form marketing, and cross-border logistics.

  • CJ OnStyle, a major South Korean home-shopping and e-commerce channel, is holding global seminars to help small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) master short-form marketing and creator strategies for overseas markets.
  • TikTok has launched standalone TikTok Shop apps in four Southeast Asian countries, integrating content consumption with direct in-app purchasing and threatening traditional e-commerce models.
  • GS Shop, another major home-shopping network, faced a severe backlash after a perfume featured in a live broadcast and touted as authentic became embroiled in counterfeit allegations.
  • Shinsegae Group brought back 77-year-old retail veteran Lee Suk-goo—famous for introducing Starbucks Korea's Siren Order—to head its department store unit as retail groups race to overhaul their digital strategies.

The Push for Global Scale and Transparent Settlements

Korean e-commerce players are increasingly looking outward, but structural problems at home and abroad are forcing a rethink of how deals are processed. As platforms scale up, the lack of robust financial tracking has long plagued the industry. To counter this, live-streaming platform Larastation has partnered with Samil PricewaterhouseCoopers to build a transparent global settlement and ranking system for its upcoming project, tackling historical issues of ranking manipulation and payment opacity.

At the same time, Korean brands are finding success through targeted overseas exposure. Adapt's beauty brand A-pe jumped to Tier 6 on the US TikTok Shop after scoring 13 million views via a video with influencer Loren Gray, seeing daily sales multiply by 15. Meanwhile, local government-backed initiatives—such as Gangwon Tourism Organization partnering with Kakao Shopping Live to offer steep travel discounts—show how public resources are increasingly leveraging major chat and platform apps to stimulate local spending.

Technology, Trust, and Political Live Streams

As retail technology grows more sophisticated, consumer trust remains the ultimate currency. While fashion app The Beige introduced a "watch replay" feature to curb the fleeting nature of live broadcasts and boost conversions, GS Shop's counterfeit perfume controversy serves as a stark reminder that flashy production cannot substitute for rigorous product verification. Inspecting goods must come before chasing hype.

Politics is also following the live format. President Lee Jae-min has begun communicating via YouTube channels, weighing real-time broadcasts to connect directly with citizens—a stark contrast to the closed-door approach of jailed former leader Yoon Suk-yeol. Whether in e-commerce or governance, the demand for unfiltered, direct digital engagement is reshaping how Korean institutions operate.
