Can You Change Your Personality? What Psychology Really Says

September 08, 2026

Contrary to the popular belief that people cannot change, modern psychology suggests that while your core temperament is biologically hardwired and largely fixed, your "character"—the way you express that temperament—can evolve significantly throughout your life.

  • Temperament vs. Character: Your basic personality traits (like introversion or emotional sensitivity) are innate and stable, but your character is a dynamic construct shaped by your daily choices.
  • The MBTI Illusion: The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) is not a fixed diagnostic tool; it is a fluid measure of your "social mask" and how you have adapted to your environment over the last few years.
  • The Power of Openness: The defining factor between those who become wiser with age and those who become bitter is "Openness"—the willingness to accept new information and challenge one’s own beliefs.

The Biology of Your "Default Settings"

Many people use the phrase "people don't change" to justify personal limitations or social frictions. However, psychology views personality as a biological framework. Traits like extroversion, neuroticism, and cognitive speed are often established in early development, influenced by fetal hormone exposure and neurological patterns. Trying to force yourself to be a diametrically different person is often an exercise in futility; instead, the key to a smoother life is recognizing these "default settings" as your starting line rather than a defect to be "repaired."

Psychologists categorize these traits using the BIG 5 model (Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism). Furthermore, the HEXACO model—a framework involving South Korean researchers—adds a sixth critical dimension: Honesty-Humility. This dimension is a vital lens for understanding integrity and greed in social settings. Rather than judging yourself by these metrics, the goal should be to lean into your natural strengths while using the "character" you build to compensate for your blind spots.

Why Character Outgrows Temperament

While your temperament is like a mountain—immovable and vast—your character is like pottery, constantly being reshaped by your actions. The most effective strategy for self-improvement is not to waste energy "fixing" your weaknesses, but to cultivate your unique strengths. An introvert’s deep focus can be a massive asset, while an extrovert’s social energy can build bridges; neither is superior. When we stop trying to change our fundamental nature and start focusing on developing a mature character, we reduce the friction in our relationships.

Finally, there is a physical, often overlooked component to personal growth: sleep. In high-pressure cultures like South Korea, sleep is often sacrificed for productivity. However, research proves that sleep deprivation destroys our ability to manage our "social mask," leading to irritability and poor interpersonal judgment. If you want to be a more mature, thoughtful person tomorrow, the most radical step you can take today is to put down your phone and get a full night's rest. By managing your physical health, staying open to new experiences, and maintaining healthy boundaries, you can ensure that aging remains a process of growth rather than stagnation.
