South Korea’s Ruling Party Passes Special Counsel Bill Targeting Prosecutorial Abuses, Triggering Street Revolts

November 08, 2026

South Korea's National Assembly has passed a special counsel bill aimed at investigating alleged politically motivated and fabricated prosecutions during the previous administration, igniting fierce street protests from the opposition.

  • What happened: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) pushed a special counsel bill through parliament to investigate alleged targeted investigations, evidence manipulation, and abuse of prosecutorial power conducted during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's former administration.
  • Opposition reaction: The main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP), led by Han Dong-hoon, staged massive street rallies near Gyeongbokgung Station in central Seoul, denouncing the law as an unconstitutional "privilege bill" to protect President Lee Jae-myung and openly calling for presidential impeachment.
  • Broader judicial push: DPK leadership is also considering a parliamentary audit of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae over judicial bias and trial delays, despite internal cautions from DPK lawmaker Kwak Sang-eon regarding legal precision and constitutional risks.
  • Who is affected: South Korea's legal system, senior prosecutors facing independent scrutiny, and the political landscape as parliamentary debate spills over into partisan street confrontations.

Opposition Resistance and Street Protests

The passage of the special counsel bill marks a major effort by the DPK, South Korea's ruling liberal party, to hold prosecutors accountable for what it characterizes as abusive targeted investigations under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's presidency. However, the opposition PPP responded by boycotting normal legislative channels and rallying supporters in downtown Seoul. PPP leader Han Dong-hoon publicly attacked the measure as an unprecedented "one-man privilege law" designed to shield President Lee Jae-myung, while PPP lawmaker Jang Dong-hyeok escalated the rhetoric by calling for President Lee's immediate impeachment.

This turn toward street politics highlights the intense polarization dominating South Korea's political landscape. PPP lawmakers such as Jeong Jeom-sig have labeled the legislation an "instructional coup" and accused the ruling party of attempting to drop valid indictments. The DPK has countered that the opposition's street demonstrations are an attempt to distract the public from past investigative misconduct and shield former authorities from legitimate accountability.

Judicial Reform and Internal Party Debates

The conflict is expanding beyond the prosecution into the judiciary itself. DPK members are actively debating whether to launch a parliamentary audit into Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, citing concerns over trial delays and political impartiality within the courts. For the ruling party, establishing checks on both prosecutors and judges is seen as essential for restoring public faith in the rule of law.

At the same time, the ruling party is navigating internal debate over procedural rigor. DPK lawmaker Kwak Sang-eon raised concerns regarding potential constitutional vulnerabilities in the bill's scope, urging lawmakers to ensure the special counsel's mandate can withstand legal scrutiny. How the ruling party balances aggressive judicial reform with constitutional integrity will determine whether this special counsel delivers real accountability or remains bogged down in endless partisan warfare.
