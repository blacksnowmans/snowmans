South Korea’s Judicial Justice Under Fire: Leaked Documents and Elite Academic Cartels

October 21, 2026

South Korea is reeling from fresh allegations involving leaked confidential prosecution files and corporate-sponsored academic "spec-stacking" for the children of the political elite, sparking calls for the impeachment of the nation's top judge.

  • Rep. Park Ji-won of the Democratic Party (the current ruling party) exposed that Han Dong-hoon, a prominent lawmaker for the conservative People Power Party, allegedly obtained internal prosecution indictment plans that should have remained strictly confidential.
  • The Rebuilding Korea Party, a minor progressive party, is formally discussing the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae for allegedly stalling judicial proceedings against Kim Keon-hee, the wife of imprisoned former President Yoon Suk-yeol 씨.
  • New evidence suggests a "parental perk" connection between Han’s daughter and 3M Korea, where an executive’s school ties allegedly facilitated corporate sponsorship for an academic competition won by the lawmaker's child.
  • These scandals highlight the persistent influence of a "vested interest cartel" that critics say continues to undermine the reforms of the current Lee Jae-myung administration.

The Prosecution Leak and the 3M Connection

The controversy surrounding Han Dong-hoon centers on the "unauthorized outing" of prosecution secrets. During a recent audit, Rep. Park Ji-won questioned Kim Hyang-yeon, head of the Seoul Western District Prosecutors Office, about how internal indictment plans and investigation transcripts ended up in a politician's hands. Kim admitted these documents "must remain inside the prosecution office" and acknowledged that their external release could constitute a criminal leak of official secrets. This suggests that despite the imprisonment of Yoon Suk-yeol 씨, "pipelines" within the prosecution still feed sensitive data to conservative political figures.

Parallel to the leak is the "genius daughter" scandal. Han’s daughter reportedly won a top prize in an English essay competition while in high school, but the records were abruptly deleted from the organizer's website once scrutiny began. Investigations revealed that 3M Korea sponsored the event with laptops and funding, and the company’s legal executive happens to be a university classmate of Han and his wife. This "alumni network" is viewed by the public as a classic example of the elite using corporate resources to manufacture academic credentials—a practice that leaves ordinary students feeling deeply marginalized.

Judicial Stalling and the Impeachment of Chief Justice Cho

The Rebuilding Korea Party is now weaponizing the ultimate constitutional tool: impeachment. They argue that Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae is intentionally delaying cases related to Kim Keon-hee, particularly those involving stock manipulation allegations. Critics call this "judicial defensive play," a tactic where the courts use procedural delays to shield members of the former ruling elite from accountability. By refusing to fast-track these high-profile cases, Cho is accused of turning the judiciary into a fortress for the remnants of the Yoon era.

For the Lee Jae-myung government, these issues are more than just legal disputes; they are the front lines of a battle against a deeply entrenched cartel of prosecutors, judges, and major corporations. If secret prosecution files are treated as political trophies and the Chief Justice is perceived as a shield for the former First Lady, the fundamental promise of "justice for all" remains unfulfilled. The push for impeachment signals that the legislative majority is no longer willing to wait for the judiciary to reform itself from within.
