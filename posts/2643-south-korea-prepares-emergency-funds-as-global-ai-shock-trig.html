South Korea Prepares Emergency Funds as Global AI Shock Triggers KOSPI Sell-Off

November 23, 2026

South Korean financial regulators are preparing emergency intervention measures after a sharp global tech sell-off wiped out all year-to-date gains on the benchmark KOSPI index.

  • What happened: A 5.28% single-day plunge in the Nasdaq sent shockwaves across Asian markets, hitting South Korea hard after key AI names like Nvidia collapsed and new market entrant Anthropic stumbled post-IPO.
  • Who is affected: Highly leveraged South Korean retail investors face severe losses, with forced broker liquidations from unmet margin calls rapidly approaching 300 billion won (about $215 million).
  • The decoupling: Domestic semiconductor behemoths Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix reported record-breaking export figures, but stock prices continue to drop as macro rate fears override solid business fundamentals.
  • Policy response: President Lee Jae-myung's administration is preparing to activate the Stock Market Stabilization Fund to bolster equities, though market observers warn against panic-driven retail buying.

The AI Valuation Shockwave

The current market turmoil stems from a sudden rupture in the global artificial intelligence narrative. Nvidia's steep decline—driven by U.S. regulatory scrutiny and executive governance rumors—alongside a disappointing post-IPO performance from Anthropic, triggered automated sell programs across major global hedge funds. SoftBank suffered heavy collateral damage as its massive $65 billion AI investment portfolio unraveled, signaling a market-wide shift from buying into future narratives to demanding immediate earnings execution.

Fundamentals Versus Panic in Seoul

In South Korea, retail traders spent over 14.7 trillion won (about $10.5 billion) trying to buy the dip, pushing margin debt to dangerous highs. This aggressive buying clashes sharply with economic reality: while domestic semiconductor exports reached a record $38.5 billion in early November and chipmakers forecast historic operating profits, equity markets remain trapped in a feedback loop of anxiety over elevated U.S. Federal Reserve interest rates.

Historical precedent offers a prospective timeline for recovery. Across four previous major financial crashes in South Korea, the KOSPI averaged an 87% rebound from its trough within 18 months. However, retail investors relying on heavy leverage face immediate liquidation long before any structural recovery can take hold. Disciplined risk management, rather than impulsive dip-buying, will determine who survives the current downturn.
