South Korea’s Ruling Party Locked in Internal War Over Cult Membership Investigation

October 26, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea is embroiled in a fiery internal feud over whether to publicly disclose an investigation into party members allegedly affiliated with Shincheonji, a controversial fringe religious group.

  • What happened: Key figures within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) are publicly attacking each other over an internal probe examining whether members of Shincheonji infiltrated the party.
  • The core conflict: Factional rivals are demanding that DPK Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok provide hard evidence for his past claims of cult ties, while lawmakers like Jung Chung-rae push for full public disclosure of the probe's findings.
  • What it changes: The chaotic public spat is eroding party discipline and diverting political momentum away from President Lee Jae-myung’s legislative priorities.
  • Who is affected: The DPK leadership faces a severe credibility test, while the main conservative opposition People Power Party is capitalizing on the governing party's internal strife.

Accusations and Escalating Factional Strife

The controversy escalated after DPK Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok previously raised explosive claims regarding potential links between party elements and Shincheonji. However, when Kim failed to present concrete proof, prominent lawmakers like Han Min-soo and Jung Chung-rae turned on him, accusing Kim of irresponsible public threats. Representative Jung sharpened the attack with the blunt warning that "whoever tries to cover this up is the culprit," placing heavy pressure on the leadership to release the full investigation rather than quietly shelve it.

The dispute has exposed a complete breakdown in party messaging. Other lawmakers, including Choi Min-hee and Park Sun-won, issued conflicting statements over whether the party ever intended to keep the findings private. Seizing on the friction, Kim Jae-seob, a lawmaker from the main opposition People Power Party (PPP), publicly criticized the ruling party as being run by "outdated politicians." While such attacks carry an ironic edge given the PPP's own history under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who was impeached and is currently in prison—the DPK’s self-inflicted chaos has handed the opposition easy political momentum.

Factional Calculus Over Policy Momentum

At its core, this struggle is less about procedural transparency and more about factional maneuvering for control within the governing party. By turning an internal inquiry into a battle over party leadership and future candidate nominations, rival camps are exhausting valuable political capital. The endless loop of media attacks has effectively drowned out discussion of major government policies and reform initiatives.

For the ruling party, attempting to sweep the investigation under the rug will only fuel further suspicion among supporters and the public. If the leadership wants to restore credibility and support President Lee Jae-myung's national agenda, it must publish the full investigation transparently, hold accountable those with improper ties, and sanction those who make unfounded public accusations.
