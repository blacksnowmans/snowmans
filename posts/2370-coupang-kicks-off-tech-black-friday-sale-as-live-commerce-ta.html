Coupang Kicks Off Tech Black Friday Sale as Live Commerce Takes Center Stage in South Korea

November 16, 2026

South Korean e-commerce leader Coupang has launched its 14-day Appliance and Digital Black Friday promotion, concentrating its steepest discounts and promotional perks into a targeted five-day live-streaming window.

  • What happened: Coupang launched its year-end digital sale running from November 16 to November 29, featuring flagship products from Samsung Electronics, LG Electronics, Intel, and JBL, including devices like the Galaxy Tab S12.
  • The peak discount window: While the promotion spans two weeks, the platform is locking its deepest savings, stackable live-only coupons, and prize giveaways behind a five-day live-stream event from November 23 to November 27.
  • Wider retail trends: Across retail, Korean brands are leaning heavily into live video; fashion firm The Nature Holdings debuted kids' winter gear via live stream, while beauty brand KAHI broadcast directly from its traditional Hanok (Korean house) flagship store in Seoul's Bukchon district.
  • Market impact: Shoppers must track specific broadcast times to secure advertised prices, while smaller vendors face rising ad costs as major conglomerates dominate holiday airtime.

Live Streaming as the Primary Discount Gateway

Coupang's dual-track schedule highlights how South Korean e-commerce platforms leverage live stream commerce to drive traffic and convert bargain-seeking shoppers. While general discounts run throughout the fortnight, the most substantial savings are reserved exclusively for the live broadcast window between November 23 and 27. For consumers managing elevated living costs, tracking these real-time coupon drops has become essential, turning live streams into the main gatekeeper for top-tier discounts.

Omnichannel Strategy and the Small Seller Squeeze

Beyond electronics, South Korea's retail landscape is combining physical spaces with real-time digital engagement. By streaming from its Hanok flagship store in Bukchon, KAHI connects offline ambiance with online accessibility, answering texture and application questions for remote shoppers. Similarly, apparel brands use live video interaction to solve online sizing uncertainties for parents buying winter outerwear.

However, this holiday live-commerce rush creates friction for smaller merchants. As conglomerates like Samsung and LG capture premium placement and bid up advertising rates during the November shopping boom, independent sellers cannot win a direct spending war. To avoid being overshadowed, smaller merchants are forced to pivot toward niche products—such as specialized appliances for single-person households—and deliver clear, instant promotional value within the first few minutes of a broadcast.
