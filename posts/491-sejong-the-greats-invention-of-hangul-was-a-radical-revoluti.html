Sejong the Great’s Invention of Hangul Was a Radical Revolution, Not Just Kindness

September 09, 2026

While often framed as an act of benevolence toward his subjects, King Sejong the Great’s creation of Hangul in 1443 was actually a calculated, high-stakes political coup against the Joseon Dynasty’s entrenched elite.

  • What happened: King Sejong, the fourth monarch of the Joseon Dynasty, defied his Confucian scholar-officials to create a phonetic alphabet for the common people.
  • The status quo challenged: At the time, 96% of the population was illiterate, as the elite (yangban) monopolized power by using complex Chinese characters to maintain a rigid social hierarchy.
  • The motivation: Beyond humanitarianism, it was an intellectual act of liberation intended to break the elite’s "cartel" and weaken their dependence on Ming Dynasty China.
  • Modern impact: Today, the linguistic architecture of Hangul—which allows for trillions of possible combinations—has become a structural advantage for AI, voice recognition, and digital processing.

Breaking the Intellectual Monopoly

The Joseon Dynasty was founded on a foundation of subservience to the Ming Dynasty of China. The ruling class, accounting for only 4% of the population, occupied themselves with Chinese classics, effectively creating an "ideological colony." By keeping the vast majority of the population illiterate, they maintained an exploitative power structure where the masses had no access to the tools of governance or legal rights.

Historical records, including those dramatized in recent media, show that Sejong faced fierce resistance from his own officials. Scholars at the Jiphyeonjeon (the "Hall of Worthies," a royal research institute) accused him of betraying Confucian values and provoking the Chinese emperor by creating a "barbarian" script. Sejong had to develop the alphabet in secret for over a decade, knowing that to give the people a voice was to threaten the very foundation of the aristocracy’s power.

AI and the Future of Literacy

Sejong’s genius was in the alphabet’s scientific design. Based on the shapes of speech organs and elemental philosophies, Hangul can capture virtually any sound with mathematical precision. This unique structure has proven to be a "cheat code" for the modern AI era. While other ancient scripts were designed for restricted use by a small elite, Hangul’s logic perfectly integrates with modern binary algorithms and voice synthesis, placing a 600-year-old invention at the forefront of 21st-century technology.

However, modern South Korea faces a paradox: despite possessing this hyper-efficient script, the nation is seeing a rapid decline in literacy and deep-reading skills. In an era of digital stimulation, the habit of reading complex texts is vanishing. As we navigate an AI-driven future, the challenge is not just technological—it is maintaining the intellectual independence that Sejong fought to grant his people. True progress, then and now, relies on the "human" capacity for critical thought, which cannot be delegated to machines.
