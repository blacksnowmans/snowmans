Samsung Drops Microsoft OneDrive for Google Storage, Leaving Heavy Users with a Migration Nightmare

September 29, 2026

  • The Shift: Samsung is ending its long-running integration between Galaxy the Samsung Gallery app and Microsoft's OneDrive on September 30, 2025, replacing it with Google Storage.
  • The Catch: While folder-level syncing works well and speed is snappy, the native "Free up phone storage" button has vanished, forcing users to manually clear device storage via the My Files app.
  • The Heavy Burden: There is no direct migration tool for users with up to 1TB of photos currently stored in OneDrive, meaning heavy users face a tedious manual export-and-upload process.
  • Who is Affected: Galaxy users, particularly heavy storage users and those deeply embedded in the Windows ecosystem who rely on seamless PC-to-phone photo management.

The Google Transition: A Mixed Bag of Smart Features and Missing Tools

The breakup with Microsoft brings both relief and frustration. On the positive side, the new Google Storage integration fixes a major fear: random, unorganized photo timelines. Users can toggle synchronization on and off for individual folders—from downloads and screenshots to custom albums—making it much easier to keep things tidy. Furthermore, synchronization speed is impressive, taking just 2 to 10 seconds to reflect changes across devices like the Galaxy S26 Ultra and Z Fold.

However, the loss of the "Free up phone storage" button in the Gallery app is a massive headache for owners of 256GB devices. Because deleting a photo in the Gallery now wipes it from the cloud as well, users must resort to a clunky workaround: opening the My Files app manually to delete local device files while keeping the cloud backups intact. It is an unnecessary chore forced upon users in the name of a new ecosystem.

Ecosystem Friction and the 1TB Migration Wall

The real pain point emerges when stepping away from the smartphone. Unlike the tight Windows Explorer integration OneDrive once offered, the new Google setup struggles on PC. The Windows version of the Samsung Gallery app does not yet support the new Google sync, leaving users with a messy web interface that breaks down custom album folders into a chaotic chronological stream.

For power users hoarding hundreds of gigabytes or terabytes of data, the lack of an automated migration path from OneDrive to Google is unforgiving. Moving massive libraries requires a grueling cycle of downloading files to a PC or external hard drive, feeding them back onto the phone in batches, and re-uploading them to the cloud. As tech giants shift their partnerships, it is everyday users who are left holding the shovel.
