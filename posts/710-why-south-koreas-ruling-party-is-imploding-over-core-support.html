Why South Korea's Ruling Party is Imploding Over Core Supporter Backlash

October 05, 2026

  • South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating has plummeted from over 60% under former Democratic Party leader Jung Chung-rae to the 30% range.
  • Critics attribute the collapse to botched candidate nominations and factional infighting during the June 3 local elections, which alienated the Democratic Party's core, long-term base.
  • Newer party figures are reportedly pushing to sideline prominent progressive voices like commentator Rhyu Si-min, triggering fierce pushback from party members.
  • Despite local election victories, critics warn that the party is fracturing from within by abandoning the rank-and-file members who built its foundation.

South Korea's dominant, liberal Democratic Party is currently navigating a severe internal crisis. While the party secured a sweeping victory in the June 3 local elections—winning 12 out of 16 major metropolitan leadership posts—President Lee Jae-myung's public approval has cratered from more than 60% down to roughly 30%. Commentators and party insiders argue this drop is not a random fluctuation, but a direct consequence of self-inflicted factional warfare and botched candidate selections that alienated the party's rock-solid base.

The core of the dispute centers on a clash between long-standing party loyalists and a rising faction of lawmakers often dubbed "neo-Lee Jae-myung" loyalists. Following the removal of former leader Jung Chung-rae—who spearheaded a landmark prosecutorial reform that dismantled the traditional prosecutor's office in favor of independent investigative and indictment agencies—the party has seen mounting internal purges. Efforts by figures like lawmaker Kim Min-seok to marginalize prominent progressive commentator Rhyu Si-min have only deepened the rift, leading critics to warn that party leadership is mistaking its most dedicated supporters for political liabilities.
