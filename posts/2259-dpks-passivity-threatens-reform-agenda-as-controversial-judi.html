DPK’s Passivity Threatens Reform Agenda as Controversial Judicial Rulings Mount

November 12, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea is facing severe backlash from its own supporters for failing to counter a series of lenient court decisions benefiting figures from the prior administration, despite holding both the presidency and a massive parliamentary majority.

  • What happened: Courts have recently issued acquittals and sentence reductions for key figures tied to past administration scandals—including martial law troops—while the ruling party silently missed its Dec. 5 deadline to present a parliamentary investigation report.
  • What it changes: The prospective release of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo and former minister Lee Sang-min signals a broader collapse of judicial accountability, undermining the reform momentum of President Lee Jae-myung’s administration.
  • Who is affected: The ruling party's core voters are growing increasingly disillusioned by legislative inertia, creating serious political risks for the party ahead of upcoming general elections.

Judicial Pushback and Legislative Paralysis

A wave of controversial court rulings has triggered intense public frustration. Courts recently reduced the prison sentence of Park Sung-jae to 16 years while acquitting Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol and Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon in connection with the Myung Tae-kyun political influence scandal. In another controversial decision, judges acquitted eight military commanders and troops deployed during martial law on the grounds that they were simply following orders. With former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo nearing release and former Interior Minister Lee Sang-min potentially following suit, critics argue the judiciary is effectively neutralizing past state abuses.

Instead of deploying its legislative power to counter these judicial rulings, the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—South Korea’s ruling liberal party—has largely limited its response to mild statements of regret. The DPK failed to deliver its scheduled parliamentary investigation report on Dec. 5, letting the deadline pass without action. Despite holding nearly 180 seats in the National Assembly, party leadership has favored cautious rhetoric over concrete legislative action or impeachment proceedings, leading supporters to accuse the party of bureaucratic passivity.

A Squandered Mandate and Electoral Risks

The DPK currently operates under rare alignment: solid backing for President Lee Jae-myung, absolute control of parliament, and a highly mobilized base. However, by failing to confront judicial overreach or enforce parliamentary investigations, the party risks wasting its political momentum. Allowing key figures from previous scandals to walk free without a strong legislative response threatens to alienate the reform-minded electorate that brought the party to power.

Political inaction inevitably carries electoral consequences. If the DPK fails to demonstrate decisive leadership and restore public trust in equal justice, voter disillusionment will likely result in a severe backlash at the ballot box in the next general election.
