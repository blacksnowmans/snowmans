Rhyu Si-min Debunks Wealth Rumors and Rejects Political Smears

October 30, 2026

Prominent liberal author and former politician Rhyu Si-min has publicly dismantled long-standing rumors alleging he amassed tens of millions of dollars through illicit stock deals, revealing his actual net worth is under 5 billion won (about $3.6 million) earned entirely through book royalties.

  • What happened: Appearing on the political YouTube show Paksiyoung TV, Rhyu clarified that he retired from politics in 2013 at age 55 with zero savings, building his current wealth solely from writing annual bestsellers and saving his earnings.
  • The stock revelation: Rhyu revealed he recently bought stocks for the first time in his life, trusting President Lee Jae-myung’s economic pledge to push the national stock index to 5,000 following the resolution of Yoon Suk-yeol's failed insurrection crisis.
  • Pushback on conspiracy theories: Rhyu addressed persistent conservative claims linking him to the SillaJen biotech stock scandal, invoking "Russell's teapot" to argue that accusers must prove their allegations rather than forcing him to prove a negative.
  • Who is affected: South Korean political commentators and media figures whose smear campaigns lost traction as Rhyu's live appearance drew over 110,000 concurrent viewers, demonstrating his enduring public influence.

Deconstructing Political Smears with Financial Transparency

For years, conservative outlets and online channels have circulated wild claims that Rhyu accumulated upwards of 100 billion won (about $72 million) through corrupt ties, notably referencing a past stock manipulation scandal surrounding Korean biotech firm SillaJen. During his broadcast, Rhyu firmly set the record straight: his total assets do not even reach 5 billion won ($3.6 million). By contrast, high-ranking government figures often hold vast fortunes, such as former presidential secretary Kim Tae-won, who reported over 16.1 billion won ($11.5 million) in assets. Rhyu emphasized that his modest wealth was built transparently through post-retirement writing, regular tax payments, and bank deposits.

Rhyu's financial disclosures also highlighted his recent entry into the equity market. A lifelong conservative saver who previously held zero stocks, Rhyu converted part of his savings into equities following the political crisis surrounding Yoon Suk-yeol's martial law attempt and subsequent removal. Confident in President Lee Jae-myung's vision for national economic recovery, Rhyu made modest retail investments, putting to rest claims that he was an illicit insider trader.

Logical Fallacies and the Ecosystem of Political Attacks

Addressing the SillaJen conspiracy theories directly, Rhyu cited philosopher Bertrand Russell’s teapot analogy, pointing out the absurdity of demanding someone prove a non-existent fact. The allegations date back to attempts under Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure as prosecutor-general—including former senior prosecutor Han Dong-hoon—to target Rhyu through aggressive investigative pressure. Rhyu argued that forcing targets of political witch-hunts to "prove their innocence" violates fundamental principles of justice and the presumption of innocence.

Despite efforts by opposition commentators from the conservative People Power Party ecosystem to dismiss Rhyu's standing, the overwhelming live turnout on Paksiyoung TV signals that progressive figures retain immense digital sway. Analysts note that right-wing channels frequently use information-laundering tactics—taking edited clips from online forums, washing their origins through commentators, and spreading them across social media. Rhyu’s direct address offers a clear example of countering coordinated online attacks with facts and basic logic.
