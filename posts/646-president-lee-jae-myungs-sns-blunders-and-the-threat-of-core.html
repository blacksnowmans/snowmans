President Lee Jae-myung’s SNS Blunders and the Threat of Core Supporter Exodus

September 10, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is facing a sharp backlash from his own core base as an ill-timed social media post and controversial cabinet nominations expose deep internal fractures within the ruling Democratic Party.

    What happened: President Lee reposted a social media user's message targeting rival factions within the liberal camp, sparking fierce criticism from loyalists who feel alienated by the administration's internal power struggles. What it changes: Approval ratings have plummeted—with nearly 30% of core supporters withdrawing their backing—fueling widespread anxiety over upcoming elections and eroding the government's moral authority. Who is affected: President Lee, the Democratic Party's fragile majority in parliament, and progressive voters who fear that infighting will hand power back to conservatives.

The Cost of Online Divisiveness

For an administration wielding a commanding majority in the National Assembly, the sight of the president engaging in factional online spats has stunned political observers and core supporters alike. Rather than uniting the progressive camp, Lee's recent social media activity has transformed his digital channels into a battleground for internal party purges. Prominent party figures have openly warned that alienating the loyal base—traditionally referred to as "home rabbits"—while chasing centrist "mountain hares" is a recipe for electoral disaster.

This erosion of trust is starkly reflected in the numbers. While overall approval ratings hover near 60%, a staggering 28.4% of past supporters have withdrawn their backing, with strong approval ratings dropping sharply over recent months. Analysts argue that voters entrusted the ruling party with 180 seats to deliver sweeping reforms, not to bog down the executive branch in heavy-handed party meddling and petty turf wars.

Stumbling Appointments and Stalled Reforms

Compounding the administration's woes are controversial cabinet picks, including the Justice Minister nominee, which have triggered negative evaluations from nearly half the public. Critics point out that the government has lost its disciplined defense mechanism, reducing policy rollouts to unseemly public mud-slinging. Observers argue that without transparent explanations and a return to the core agenda of absolute prosecutorial reform, the administration risks alienating the very public that brought it to power.
