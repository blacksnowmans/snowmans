South Korea’s Parliament Paralyzed as Opposition Calls for Impeachment Over Special Counsel Bill

November 09, 2026

Tensions in the National Assembly have reached a breaking point as the conservative opposition calls for President Lee Jae-myung’s impeachment while the ruling Democratic Party pushes to pass a special counsel probe into past prosecutorial abuses.

  • The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, has launched street protests and threatened a filibuster to block the "Special Counsel Act on Fabricated Indictments."
  • The ruling Democratic Party (DP) is defending the bill as a necessary measure to investigate evidence manipulation and "prosecutorial overreach" that occurred during the era of the now-imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • The political deadlock is currently stalling a massive 820 trillion KRW (approximately $590 billion) national budget and critical housing welfare legislation.
  • DP leadership has slammed the opposition’s impeachment rhetoric as "insane" and a "shameless" attempt to obstruct the legitimate functioning of the state.

The Fight Over Legal Accountability

The "Special Counsel Act on Fabricated Indictments" has become the primary flashpoint in Korean politics. The ruling DP argues this is a mandatory step for judicial reform, aiming to investigate cases where the prosecution allegedly manipulated evidence to target political rivals during the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. To the DP, the bill is about restoring the rule of law and holding the prosecution service accountable for past political bias. However, the opposition PPP has framed the bill as a "prison break law" designed to shield President Lee from his own legal history, using this justification to move their protests from the assembly floor to the streets of Seoul.

By invoking the term "impeachment" and holding one-man protests in front of the presidential office, the PPP is escalating the conflict beyond mere policy disagreement. DP Representative Han Byung-do characterized these actions as a "parade of extremist rhetoric," arguing that the opposition is crossing a line by threatening the constitutional order of the Lee Jae-myung government. The DP maintains that the special counsel is not a "privilege" for the president, but a tool to vindicate victims of past prosecutorial madness.

Budgetary Hostages and Political Fallout

The timing of this clash is particularly damaging as it coincides with the year-end review of the 820 trillion KRW ($590 billion) national budget. This spending plan is the backbone of the government’s strategy for economic recovery and welfare expansion. However, the PPP is effectively using the budget as leverage, linking its passage to the withdrawal of the special counsel bill and other contentious legislation, such as the Democratic Activists Act. This "cliff-edge" tactic threatens to leave the country’s finances in limbo as the new fiscal year approaches.

The current gridlock reflects a worrying trend where judicial battles have completely swallowed the policy discourse in the National Assembly. While the DP is firm in its stance that judicial reform cannot be compromised, the PPP’s decision to weaponize impeachment rhetoric so early in the term suggests they are more interested in delegitimizing the current administration than offering policy alternatives. If the opposition continues to prioritize street rallies and filibusters over budget negotiations, they risk a significant public backlash from a population increasingly weary of political instability and economic stagnation. For President Lee, the successful passage of this budget and the special counsel act will be the ultimate test of his administration's governing momentum.
