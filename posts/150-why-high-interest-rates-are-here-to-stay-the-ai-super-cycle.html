Why High Interest Rates Are Here to Stay: The AI Super-Cycle

September 04, 2026

  • The shift: Global capital is moving away from the era of excess savings and cheap debt, getting sucked instead into the black hole of AI infrastructure.
  • The interest rate reality: High interest rates are not a sign of economic collapse, but rather the natural cost—and growing pains—of funding a massive industrial revolution.
  • Korea's position: South Korea sits at the center of the AI value chain, boasting critical supply lines spanning semiconductors, data centers, and the power infrastructure needed to run them.

For the past twenty years, investors have relied on a simple rulebook: cheap money, low interest rates, and endless liquidity driven by Asian trade surpluses buying up US debt. Fixating on every single Federal Reserve meeting and groaning over high mortgage rates means missing the bigger picture. The rules of the game have fundamentally changed.

Capital is no longer sitting around looking for real estate bubbles. Instead, astronomical amounts of money are being funneled directly into building out artificial intelligence. Constructing a single AI data center requires trillions of won (billions of dollars), and government deficits are only adding fuel to the fire. Pushing back against high rates by assuming equities are dead is like abandoning farming just because it's the rainy season. This is a structural transformation, not a temporary market glitch.

Beyond the Low-Interest Nostalgia

Why the Old Playbook No Longer Works

Clinging to the belief that stocks can only rally when interest rates are low is a dangerous trap. In this new cycle, companies capable of weathering temporary high rates while scaling AI infrastructure are the ones that will survive and dominate. Waiting around for central banks to slash rates is a losing strategy.

The current turbulence in the bond market—such as the 10-year US Treasury yield testing the patience of investors—is simply a bottleneck of capital formation. Once financial engineering catches up and introduces structured products like "AI MBS" (Mortgage-Backed Securities for infrastructure), institutional capital will flood in, naturally stabilizing rates. South Korea is uniquely positioned to benefit from this, holding a tight grip on the hardware, chips, and energy grids required to keep the AI engine running.

The Verdict

While local real estate markets groan under the weight of high borrowing costs, the real momentum belongs to the stock market, specifically the AI supply chain. Comparing South Korea's massive real estate market (valued at around 8,000 trillion won, or roughly $6 trillion) to its stock market (around 6,000 trillion won, or roughly $4.5 trillion) misses the point: the steepest asset growth over the next five years will overwhelmingly favor equities tied to technological supremacy.

Fixating on mortgage rates while the rest of the world races ahead with AI is a costly mistake. The anxiety surrounding high interest rates is nothing more than the growing pain of a new economic paradigm—and for forward-looking investors, it is the ultimate entry point.
