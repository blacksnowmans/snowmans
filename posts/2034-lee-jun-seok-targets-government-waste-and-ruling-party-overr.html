Lee Jun-seok Targets Government Waste and Ruling Party Overreach

November 06, 2026

Lee Jun-seok, a prominent lawmaker and former head of the conservative opposition, has launched a dual-track offensive against systemic government waste and the legislative tactics of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK).

  • Exposing Procurement Fraud: Lee demonstrated how items on Narajangteo (Korea ON-line E-Procurement System) are priced up to four times higher than retail, using a power strip as a prop.
  • Warning the Ruling Party: He cautioned the DPK against "legislative tyranny," citing the downfall of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s administration as a cautionary tale for those who ignore public sentiment.
  • Regional Expansion: The Reform Party (Lee’s minor opposition party) is aggressively moving into the Gyeonggi South region, a tech-heavy hub, to capture centrist voters.
  • Tax Justice: Lee called for stricter identity disclosure for high-value tax delinquents to restore public trust in the National Tax Service.

The "Power Strip Scandal" and Administrative Rot

During a legislative fair hosted by the Reform Party, Lee Jun-seok highlighted a glaring loophole in Korea’s public procurement system. He presented a standard electrical power strip that retails for about $36 (50,000 KRW) but is sold to government agencies through the state-run Narajangteo platform for roughly $145 (200,000 KRW). This nearly 300% markup represents a massive drain on taxpayer funds, suggesting that the "certified" procurement system has become a protected racket for overcharging vendors.

This "pocketbook" politics is a signature Lee Jun-seok move. By using a physical object to illustrate abstract corruption, he bypasses partisan bickering to speak directly to the taxpayer. While the government frequently discusses budget cuts, Lee argues that the real issue is not the size of the budget, but the "leaky pipe" of administrative inefficiency. His demand for a total overhaul of the procurement bidding process puts the Ministry of Economy and Finance on notice, shifting the political spotlight from ideological battles to practical reform.

A Warning to the "Imperial" Majority

Lee also directed sharp criticism toward the ruling DPK, led by President Lee Jae-myung. As the DPK pushes for a "Special Counsel Act on Fabricated Indictments" to rein in the prosecution, Lee warned that a party with an absolute majority often mistakes its power for a permanent mandate. He explicitly referenced the collapse of the previous administration, noting that those who blindly followed Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol during his presidency were eventually swept away by public anger following his impeachment.

The subtext of Lee’s warning is clear: the DPK is currently acting with the same "unbridled power" that led to the ruin of its predecessors. By invoking the memory of the martial law crisis during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure, Lee is positioning the Reform Party as the only rational check on a ruling party that he claims is becoming increasingly obsessed with protecting its own leadership from judicial scrutiny.

Strategic Shifts in the Gyeonggi Hub

Beyond rhetoric, Lee’s party is making tactical land grabs. Lee Ju-young, a Reform Party lawmaker and former pediatric doctor, is preparing to open a regional office in Suwon, the heart of Korea’s semiconductor and IT belt. This region is home to a younger, pragmatism-focused demographic that often feels alienated by the "all-or-nothing" warfare between the DPK and the main opposition People Power Party (PPP). By focusing on local issues and administrative transparency, the Reform Party aims to turn Gyeonggi South into a fortress for a third-way political movement.

"Unbridled power is always destroyed from within," Lee stated, reminding the ruling party that voter loyalty in swing regions like Gyeonggi is performance-based, not ideological.
