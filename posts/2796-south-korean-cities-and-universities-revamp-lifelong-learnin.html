South Korean Cities and Universities Revamp Lifelong Learning to Tackle Regional Decline

November 27, 2026

South Korean municipalities and regional universities are transforming adult lifelong education from simple leisure workshops into a strategic policy tool designed to retain local populations, boost vocational skills, and prepare residents for the AI era.

  • What happened: Local governments across South Korea—including Daegu, Busan, and Goseong County—and regional universities announced major annual milestones and strategic long-term blueprints for lifelong education.
  • Key achievements: Busan Institute of Science and Technology graduated 536 adult learners through decentralized classes held directly inside subway stations, while Kumoh National Institute of Technology in Gumi awarded honorary bachelor's degrees to 52 local residents.
  • Long-term strategy: Goseong County in Gangwon Province unveiled a 5-year development plan (2027–2031) following its designation as a official "Lifelong Learning City," while several municipal districts convened coordination councils to harmonize 2026 and 2027 educational budgets.
  • Who is affected: Working adults seeking career transitions, middle-aged and senior residents, and regional municipalities struggling with rapid demographic aging and population outflow.

Moving Beyond Hobbies to Vocational Survival

Lifelong education in South Korea was historically viewed as a secondary welfare benefit focused on leisure activities for seniors. However, recent joint initiatives between municipal governments and local academic institutions signal a deliberate shift toward practical vocational retraining. By bringing classrooms into daily public spaces—such as subway hubs in Busan or commercial plazas in Daegu—and partnering with institutions like Chodang University in Hampyeong County, local leaders are dismantling geographic and scheduling barriers for working adults. These programs allow residents to acquire university-level job skills without leaving their hometowns.

Addressing Regional Extinction with Strategic Roadmaps

For smaller rural jurisdictions like Goseong County as well as urban districts in Seoul and Daegu, structured adult education is emerging as a defense mechanism against regional decline. Localities are moving away from piecemeal, annual course planning in favor of binding five-year roadmaps. Activating local Lifelong Education Councils helps eliminate redundant courses across public libraries, welfare centers, and university extension centers, ensuring efficient spending. Furthermore, as institutions like the Korea National University of Transportation integrate AI transformation topics into public forums, lifelong learning is evolving into an essential bridge connecting regional workforces with rapidly changing industrial demands.
