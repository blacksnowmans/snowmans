The Fracturing of South Korea’s Ruling Party: The "New Lee" Purge That Failed

October 22, 2026

The "New Lee Jae-myung" faction within the ruling Democratic Party is struggling to consolidate power after a calculated attempt to push out the party’s traditional base backfired, leading to accusations of "smart dictatorship."

  • The Purge Strategy: Loyalists to President Lee Jae-myung sought to reshape the Democratic Party (DP), South Korea's liberal ruling party, by marginalizing the "pro-Roh" and "pro-Moon" factions—supporters of former presidents Roh Moo-hyun and Moon Jae-in.
  • The Failed Exit: Leadership reportedly expected the old guard to leave the party in a mass exodus, but influential liberal pundit Rhyu Si-min and others urged supporters to stay and "fight from within," foiling the plan to clear the field.
  • "Smart Dictatorship": Internal critics, including DP lawmaker Kim Young-hwan, have compared the current party atmosphere to a "totalitarian prison," where digital tools and aggressive online supporters are used to silence dissent.
  • Weaponizing Scandals: In a sign of desperation, pro-Lee influencers are attempting to revive the Sillajen case—a biotech stock scandal that was thoroughly investigated during the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol (the former prosecutor now serving a prison sentence)—to smear critics like Rhyu Si-min.

The Strategy to "Cleanse" the Party

The current internal strife is not a mere spat over seats; it is a battle for the soul of the DP. The "New Lee" faction views the party’s traditional roots as an obstacle to President Lee’s pragmatic, populist agenda. By signaling that they feel no "debt" to the political legacies of Roh or Moon, these newcomers have actively sought to isolate the party’s veteran wing. The goal was to provoke a mass resignation of traditionalists, allowing the DP to pivot and potentially absorb centrists from the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, or the smaller Reform Party.

However, this "cleansing" operation hit a wall. Instead of walking away, the traditionalist rank-and-file doubled down. The intervention of Rhyu Si-min—a heavyweight whose voice carries immense weight with liberal voters—shifted the narrative from "leaving a broken party" to "reclaiming the party’s history." This has left the presidential office and party leadership in a state of visible anxiety, as their carefully timed plan to rebuild the party in Lee’s image has stalled indefinitely.

Desperation and the Rise of Totalitarian Tactics

The leadership’s frustration is manifesting as a "smart dictatorship." Unlike the physical repression of the past, this involves a sophisticated system of social media labeling, the use of aggressive YouTube channels to "demonize" internal rivals, and the refusal to engage in meaningful dialogue. When the "exit strategy" failed, the administration's tone shifted toward direct hostility. President Lee’s own aggressive activity on X (formerly Twitter) is seen by observers not as a failure of emotional control, but as a deliberate signal to his base to increase pressure on internal "enemies."

Perhaps most telling is the attempt to revive the Sillajen biotech scandal. This case was a cornerstone of the legal attacks led by Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol during his time as a prosecutor to target the liberal elite. That the "New Lee" faction is now reaching for the same discredited narrative to target Rhyu Si-min suggests they are willing to adopt the tactics of their former persecutors to achieve internal hegemony. For the DP’s veteran members, the irony is bitter: a party that once defined itself by its fight for democracy is now being accused by its own members of stifling the very diversity it once championed.
