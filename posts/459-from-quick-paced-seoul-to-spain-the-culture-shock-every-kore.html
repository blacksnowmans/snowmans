From Quick-Paced Seoul to Spain: The Culture Shock Every Korean Traveler Faces

September 08, 2026

  • The core clash: South Korea's hyper-efficient "bali-bali" (hurry-hurry) culture hits a total wall when it crashes into Spain’s radically unhurried, slow-paced lifestyle.
  • Dining customs: Forget instant service; Spanish restaurants operate on a glacial rhythm centered around long conversations, and free tap water and public restrooms are virtually non-existent.
  • Practical adjustments: Travelers must navigate counter-intuitive building floor numbers (ground floor is PB), spotty local train schedules, and a social etiquette that demands direct eye contact and casual chatting with strangers.
  • The silver lining: Embracing local traditions like *sobremesa*—lingering at the table long after the meal ends—offers a powerful antidote to modern burnout.

Stepping off a plane in Spain after living in South Korea brings a very specific kind of disorientation. While Korea runs on the engine of "bali-bali"—where water hits your table the second you sit down and high-speed trains are a religion—Spain asks you to delete that vocabulary entirely. It is not just a difference in speed; it is an entirely different philosophy of existence.

For visitors wired for immediate gratification, the daily friction can be jarring. In Spain, public restrooms are rare and usually cost money, compelling savvy travelers to buy a quick espresso at a local bar just to use the facilities. Tap water isn't a given, restaurant service moves at a crawl, and local trains on the national rail network are famously prone to delays. Even mundane details like floor numbering require mental recalibration: what Koreans call the first floor is Spain's "Planta Baja" (ground floor), meaning the local first floor is actually Korea's second.

Yet the real lesson hidden beneath these inconveniences is cultural. In Korea, eye contact with strangers can feel aggressive or intrusive, and meals are fuel consumed efficiently between scheduled tasks. In Spain, making direct eye contact is basic manners, chatting up the person at the next table is standard, and *sobremesa*—the sacred tradition of lingering at the dinner table for hours of conversation over wine or coffee—turns eating from a biological necessity into a human connection.

Ultimately, traveling from one of the world's most hyper-connected, fast-moving societies to the sun-drenched, unhurried rhythm of Southern Europe is a masterclass in letting go of control. If you can surrender your obsession with schedules, Spain's inconveniences stop looking like flaws and start looking like an invitation to slow down.
