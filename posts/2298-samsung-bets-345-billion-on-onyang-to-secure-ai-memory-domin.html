Samsung Bets $34.5 Billion on Onyang to Secure AI Memory Dominance

November 13, 2026

Samsung Electronics has broken ground on a 46 trillion won (about $34.5 billion) project to transform its Onyang campus into a core packaging hub for High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) chips used in artificial intelligence.

  • What happened: Samsung Electronics Chairman Lee Jae-yong and South Korean President Lee Jae-myung attended the November 12 groundbreaking ceremony at the Onyang facility, which is pivoting from legacy assembly to cutting-edge HBM packaging.
  • Fast-tracked timeline: Production startup has been accelerated by a full year to June 2028, aided by rapid municipal permit processing in South Chungcheong Province.
  • Heavy equipment focus: Out of the 46 trillion won total budget, roughly 40 trillion won (about $30 billion) is allocated strictly to purchasing advanced semiconductor equipment.
  • Regional impact: The facility forms the anchor of a 144 trillion won (about $108 billion) regional tech cluster linking Asan, Sejong, and Cheongju, creating a tight production loop with Samsung's wafer fab in Pyeongtaek.

A Strategic Pivot to Advanced Packaging

This massive outlay reflects Samsung’s determination to regain the lead in the HBM market, where advanced back-end packaging has become the primary bottleneck for AI hardware supply chains. By delegating commodity memory production to overseas factories and concentrating high-end HBM packaging at domestic hubs like Onyang, Samsung is aggressively restructuring its memory division around high-margin AI chips.

Allocating nearly 87% of the project's capital directly to equipment purchases sends a strong signal to the market. Samsung is betting that heavy retooling will solve its packaging yield challenges. However, spending alone will not guarantee success; the key metric for investors will be how quickly Samsung can stabilize yields once these lines are installed.

Regional Integration and Execution Speed

The project significantly tightens vertical integration across South Korea's central region. By linking wafer fabrication in Pyeongtaek directly to advanced packaging in Onyang, Samsung aims to cut logistics overhead and speed up product delivery. This supply-chain consolidation is expected to provide a major boost to domestic Korean material, component, and equipment suppliers operating in the region.

Moving the operational target forward to June 2028 demonstrates how critical execution speed has become in the memory sector. While local government support cleared early administrative hurdles, Samsung's ultimate challenge will be deploying technical staff quickly enough to deliver stable mass production ahead of schedule.
