How to Turn a 37-Year-Old Run-Down Building into a Modern Asset for $450,000

September 27, 2026

A complete overhaul of a 37-year-old multi-family building in South Korea proves that deep renovation can beat tearing down and rebuilding from scratch.

  • What happened: A 1990-built, rundown multi-family house with basement flood issues and messy layouts was fully remodeled into a 7-unit building (5 rental units, 2 owner units) in just four months.
  • What it changes: It highlights a sustainable, cost-effective alternative to demolition, costing about 600 million won (roughly $450,000) while resolving structural flaws and adding modern smart-home tech.
  • Who is affected: Urban property owners and real estate investors looking for ways to boost asset value and rental yields without facing the massive costs of new construction.

Solving Basement Floods and Structural Limits

Old low-rise buildings in Korean cities often share a common nightmare: basement flooding during the monsoon season. This 37-year-old property was no exception, with water regularly backing up into lower-level units. Instead of relying on superficial cosmetic fixes, the construction team installed an automated drainage pump system hidden beneath the kitchen sink. Water is channeled into a concealed sump pit and automatically pumped out before any damage occurs. On the roof, a grid-pattern waterproof panel system was implemented to permanently eliminate leaks, prioritizing structural integrity over fast fixes.

The project also tackled the notorious layout issues of older multi-family homes, where shared, chaotic stairwells destroy tenant privacy. By adding a structured exterior wall and boundary setup, the team completely separated the entrances for the owners and the five tenant households. Smart spatial planning even turned an unmovable structural column in the owner's kitchen into a visual asset by wrapping it in "Super Mirror" stainless steel, creating an illusion of expanded space.

Smart Tech and High-Density Living

Inside the owner's multi-level unit, the building belies its age. The home is fully integrated with smart IoT controls, allowing residents to manage lighting, ventilation, and climate control via a smartphone app. Space is aggressively maximized throughout: a sliding slatted door in the kitchen reveals a compact utility room housing a built-in refrigerator, storage, an IoT hub, and an energy-recovery ventilator. Even the bathroom features universal design elements, including a concrete shower bench built to support up to 400 pounds.

Ultimately, this project shows that smart renovation is about more than aesthetics — it is a masterclass in risk management, legal compliance, and spatial efficiency. By preserving the skeleton of a 37-year-old building and upgrading its core mechanics, the owners successfully modernized an aging urban eyesore into a profitable, future-proof asset.
