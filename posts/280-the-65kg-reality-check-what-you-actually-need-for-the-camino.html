The 6.5kg Reality Check: What You Actually Need for the Camino de Santiago

September 07, 2026

As the Camino de Santiago draws record crowds topping 500,000 pilgrims a year, veteran travelers are stripping away romanticized notions of the 800-kilometer (about 500 miles) trek to offer a hard-nosed briefing on what it actually takes to survive it.

  • The Golden Weight: Keep your pack to 6.5kg (about 14 lbs) in a 30-liter bag—including your laptop and charger—to prevent debilitating foot and joint injuries.
  • Footwear Is Survival: Never rely on a single pair of shoes or flimsy slides; alternate between proper hiking shoes and sandals (such as Teva) to stave off blisters.
  • Ditch the Diet Myth: This isn't a weight-loss camp; expect to spend around €1,975 (about $2,150) for a 45-day trip, and fuel up with local food and wine to maintain your stamina.
  • Keep Your Small Luxuries: Pack small comforts like a traditional Korean exfoliating mitt (taetawol) and hair mist to maintain morale when physical exhaustion sets in.

The biggest misconception about the Camino is that it requires monumental spiritual preparation or extreme self-denial. In reality, modern pilgrims often stumble by over-packing for hypothetical disasters while ignoring basic creature comforts. If you forget something, you can simply buy it locally in Spain—treating local shops as your backup plan frees you from hauling dead weight across mountain passes.

At the same time, dismissing personal routines as frivolous is a mistake. Bringing items like familiar skincare products or a scent that grounds you isn't about vanity; it is a psychological defense mechanism against the grit and grime of a grueling 45-day journey. Ultimately, surviving the Camino isn't about grand gestures, but managing the small physical details that keep you moving forward one step at a time.
