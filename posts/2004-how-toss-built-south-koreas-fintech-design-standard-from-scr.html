How Toss Built South Korea's Fintech Design Standard from Scratch

November 06, 2026

Toss, South Korea’s dominant fintech super-app, built its industry-defining user interface not through polished initial graphics, but through a decade-long crusade of ruthless simplification, trial-and-error engineering, and custom toolmaking.

  • From clunky to iconic: Early versions of Toss were mocked by industry designers for crude visuals, but won millions of users by eliminating South Korea's notoriously cumbersome legacy digital security certificates (gongin injeungseo) and financial clutter.
  • Designer-led engineering: Lacking front-end developers in its early years, Toss's design team manually coded markup directly on GitHub and later taught themselves 3D software like Cinema 4D to eliminate reliance on external agencies.
  • Data-driven UX writing: Rewriting dry financial terms into plain, intuitive language on bank account sign-up buttons boosted user conversion rates by 300%, turning text editing into a core business strategy.
  • Proprietary systems: Toss institutionalized its design assets by creating the Toss Design System (TDS), crafting a custom typeface ("Toss Product Sans"), and launching automated internal workflow tools.

The Victory of Function Over Form

A decade ago, tech circles routinely criticized Toss for its crude interface and sharp-edged buttons. Yet while incumbent banks hid behind complex paperwork and mandated outdated security software, Toss focused entirely on removing friction. The design team defined their mission not as drawing pretty screens, but as designing invisible experiences—slashing inputs, removing unnecessary forms, and making money transfers effortless for everyone, including less tech-savvy users.

This pragmatic ethos was forged in extreme constraints. Without dedicated front-end engineers in the app's early days, designers held code-heavy meetings on GitHub to discuss Sass optimization and component reusability rather than color palettes. When rapid business expansion rendered their blue-heavy brand identity inefficient, they stripped away heavy background colors in favor of a clean, text-centric white background. That bold decision established the minimalist layout that serves as the blueprint for Korean mobile banking today.

Building Design as a Scalable System

The turning point arrived during the major overhaul known as "Toss 3.0." Faced with mismatched 3D assets from external video production agencies that couldn't adapt to mobile micro-interactions, the team took graphic production entirely in-house. Designers spent months mastering 3D rendering software after hours, eventually cutting asset production times from a month to a single day.

As Toss expanded into a comprehensive financial platform handling accounts, loans, credit scores, and insurance, it systematized its creative process. The company introduced dedicated UX writers, ran continuous user-testing iterations, spent eight agonizing months conducting quality assurance to roll out a flawless dark mode across thousands of screens, and forged its own typeface to ensure perfect numerical alignment. By prioritizing user clarity over surface elegance, Toss transformed from an ugly duckling into South Korea's ultimate UX benchmark.
