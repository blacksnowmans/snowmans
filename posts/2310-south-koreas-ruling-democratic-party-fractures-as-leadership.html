South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party Fractures as Leadership Showdown Triggers Mutual Ethics Inquiries

November 14, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party has descended into open factional warfare after three Supreme Council members boycotted official leadership proceedings, prompting party leader Kim Min-seok and dissident lawmakers to demand internal ethics probes into one another.

  • The leadership boycott: Three Supreme Council members—Choi Min-hee, Lee Seong-yoon, and Han Min-soo—staged a collective walkout on November 13 against party leader Kim Min-seok, leading remaining party leadership to preemptively strip their nameplates from the meeting room.
  • The disciplinary trigger: Leader Kim ordered an immediate ethics investigation into Representative Choi over controversial remarks, including leaked audio where she claimed President Lee Jae-myung had opposed the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, as well as separate comments comparing journalists to "a pack of hyenas."
  • Factional retaliation: The dissident faction held a press briefing calling for an inquiry into Kim himself, alleging that his divisive leadership, property tax policy confusion, and unaddressed ties to the Shincheonji religious group have brought disrepute to the ruling party.
  • Plunging approval: The infighting comes alongside sharp public backlash; recent Gallup Korea tracking shows Democratic Party favorability dropping from over 50% down to 38%, while the People Power Party, South Korea's main conservative opposition, rose to 26%.

A Destructive Chicken Game Inside Party Headquarters

The escalation from policy squabbles to punitive disciplinary measures signals that the ruling party’s leadership has functionally split. Kim’s decision to wield an ethics probe against a sitting Supreme Council member was intended as a swift disciplinary crackdown on internal dissent. Instead, it has backfired by turning isolated controversies—including Choi’s clashes with the press corps—into an all-out referendum on Kim’s own leadership style ahead of his 100th day in office.

By publicly removing the nameplates of absent councilors, party headquarters demonstrated an uncompromising posture that rejects compromise in favor of factional purges. In response, dissident lawmakers have framed the probe as political retaliation designed to distract from Kim's own policy stumbles and unresolved controversies, ensuring that every grievance will now be aired through formal party ethics machinery.

Erosion of the Ruling Mandate

For President Lee Jae-myung’s government, this civil war directly damages the administration’s legislative momentum. Disputing whether the president privately opposed the historic impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol creates confusion among core progressive voters and undermines the party's official narrative surrounding the transition of power.

More critically, the double-digit slide in favorability indicates that moderate voters are rapidly tiring of party infighting amid persistent inflation and economic pressure. Rather than providing steady legislative backing for President Lee’s domestic agenda, the Democratic Party leadership has trapped itself in a zero-sum struggle for factional survival where neither side appears willing to offer a political exit ramp.
