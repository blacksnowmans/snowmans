South Korea's High-Profile Judge Indicted Over Luxury Drinking Party

September 04, 2026

A senior South Korean judge who made headlines by sentencing former President Yoon Suk Yeol to life imprisonment is now facing criminal charges himself over a luxury drinking session hosted by lawyers.

  • The Charges: Senior Judge Ji Gwi-yeon has been indicted for violating South Korea's Anti-Graft Act (the Improper Solicitation and Graft Act) after allegedly having lawyers cover a 4.09 million won (about $3,000) tab at an exclusive bar in Seoul's Gangnam district.
  • The Backstory: Ji previously presided over high-stakes legal fallout from the Dec. 3 martial law crisis, shifting from granting Yoon's release requests to ultimately handing down a life sentence—a trajectory that drew intense partisan scrutiny.
  • The Legal Fallout: While the Corruption Investigation Office for High-Ranking Officials (CIO) indicted Ji under anti-graft rules, it dropped bribery charges due to a lack of direct active cases between the judge and the lawyers at the time, sparking criticism of a partial investigation.
  • Who Is Affected: The scandal strikes another blow to the credibility of South Korea's judiciary, fueling public cynicism about cozy legal cartels and the integrity of high-profile political trials.

When the Judge Becomes the Defendant

The indictment of Judge Ji turns the optics of South Korea's legal system upside down. Just months after wielding the ultimate authority in a historic treason trial, Ji finds his own conduct under the microscope. Investigators calculated that the drinking session amounted to roughly 1.36 million won (about $1,000) per person—comfortably clearing statutory thresholds for illegal entertainment, though a separate 4.16 million won gathering was dropped because individual shares fell just under the legal threshold.

To outside observers, the defense that no active trials linked the judge and the lawyers during the dinners stretches credulity. In South Korea's tightly networked legal circles, such high-end gatherings function less as casual outings and more as insurance policies—building informal access and unspoken favors before future dockets fill up. When a judge tasked with delivering monumental verdicts is simultaneously accepting thousands of dollars in hospitality from defense attorneys, the boundary between justice and networking collapses.

A Judiciary Trapped in Partisan Tides

Ji's handling of the Yoon Suk Yeol case had already made him a lightning rod, with critics accusing him of tacking between conservative and liberal political winds to ensure self-preservation. This latest revelation about his off-the-bench socializing threatens to erase whatever public legitimacy remained for those rulings. When a judge's personal spending and dinner companions overshadow the legal reasoning of a life-imprisonment verdict, the entire institution suffers.

Ultimately, this case exposes the deep-seated vulnerabilities of South Korea's legal establishment, where elite networking and ingrained perks continue to undermine public trust. If the arbiters of the law cannot maintain basic ethical boundaries, public faith in judicial verdicts risks becoming a casualty of a system protecting its own.
