President Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Rating Plunges to 32% as Youth Turn Away

October 22, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating has sunk to 32.1% amid widespread public fatigue over real estate troubles, economic pressures, and contentious cabinet appointments.
  • The Numbers: A survey by Janggil Research shows President Lee's positive performance rating at 32.1%, while negative evaluations reached 66.1%.
  • Youth Backlash: Over 77% of voters in their 20s and nearly 65% of those in their 30s express negative views of the administration's performance.
  • Policy Drivers: Skyrocketing housing costs, loan interest burdens, and controversial confirmation hearings for nominees like Kim Seung-won (Justice Minister) and Yong Hye-in (Gender Equality Minister) are alienating younger demographics.
  • Shifting Dynamics: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (33.4%) leads the conservative main opposition People Power Party (31.2%) within the margin of error, while the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party rose to 9.0%.

Youth Disillusionment and Policy Failures

The sharpest drop in support comes from voters in their 20s and 30s—a demographic crucial to the administration's political foundation. After taking power following the impeachment and imprisonment of Yoon Suk-yeol, President Lee faced elevated expectations regarding economic fairness and structural reform. However, persisting housing market insecurity and rising living costs have eroded confidence, leaving younger citizens feeling priced out of financial stability.

Self-inflicted political friction has compounded these economic pressures. Controversies during parliamentary confirmation hearings for cabinet nominees—specifically Justice Minister nominee Kim Seung-won and Gender Equality Minister nominee Yong Hye-in—hit a sensitive nerve regarding fairness among younger voters. As a result, the opposition People Power Party has now overtaken the Democratic Party among voters in their 30s (30.4% to 25.6%), underscoring a swift realignment across Seoul and its surrounding metro area.

Progressive Pressure and Waning Momentum

Instead of defecting directly to conservatives, many disgruntled progressive voters are engaging in a tactical shift by supporting the minor Rebuilding Korea Party, led by Cho Kuk. Rather than abandoning their political roots, liberal supporters are using secondary parties to signal their dissatisfaction and push President Lee's administration toward stronger reform initiatives and prosecutorial overhauls without officially relinquishing their alignment with the left.

At the same time, media outlets have begun running polls measuring public sentiment toward political figures positioned opposite President Lee, including Han Dong-hoon and main opposition leader Jang Dong-hyeok. The sudden focus on potential political counterweights early in the presidential term signals growing anxiety within political circles over an early loss of executive governing momentum.
