South Korea’s Ruling Democrats Face a Fractured Base as President Lee Jae-myung’s Numbers Slide

October 29, 2026

Plunging poll numbers for South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party reveal that traditional progressive voters are turning their backs on President Lee Jae-myung and party chairman Kim Min-seok amid growing frustration over insular governance.

  • A dead-cross among core supporters: Disapproval of Democratic Party (DP) leader Kim Min-seok rose to 63.4% in mid-October polling, with negative ratings among self-identified progressives surging by 7.4 percentage points to 52.5%—marking an outright majority of dissatisfied loyalists.
  • Economic shocks drag down the presidency: The party’s decline mirrors public disaffection with President Lee’s administration, which recently saw support drop 4.7 percentage points in a single day following an economic shock.
  • Convention scars and weaponized pressure: Internal wounds from the ruling party’s leadership race have festered, with critics accusing the leadership of sidelining rivals under extreme labels—such as "splittists" or associates of the Shincheonji fringe sect—and using prosecutorial pressure against party dissenters.
  • A warning of historical decline: Within progressive circles, alarms are sounding that the party risks disintegrating into a fractured, isolated administration, much like the factional splintering seen during the presidency of the late Roh Moo-hyun.

The Peril of Ruling Like an Opposition Underdog

The fundamental challenge facing President Lee is a failure to transition from a besieged reformer into South Korea’s ultimate authority. Lee spent years enduring severe political pressure and physical threats prior to the turmoil surrounding Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was later impeached and imprisoned. That history endeared Lee to grass-roots supporters as an embattled fighter. In supreme power, however, the public demands responsibility and consensus rather than personal grievance. When an administration in the presidential office continues to treat internal dissent as treason, it reduces its governing coalition to a hardened faction.

The atmosphere inside the ruling camp is alarming observers. Critics warn that the presidential office has become an echo chamber dominated by sycophants who dismiss adverse data as media bias and aggressively isolate critics. Worse, instances where internal dissenters face investigative pressure and search-and-seizure operations carry uncomfortable echoes of the prosecutorial overreach seen during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. Progressive voters who rallied to defend democratic norms are increasingly dismayed to see similar mechanisms deployed by their own leadership.

Progressive Buyer’s Remorse Ahead of Elections

This leadership vacuum explains why the ruling party is hemorrhaging support across the political spectrum. Moderates and center-left loyalists do not abandon a party simply over a leader’s charisma; they leave when they conclude that the government's competence and tolerance have collapsed. Progressive voters are now confronting an uncomfortable crisis of faith, questioning whether the leader they fought to elect is isolating the very base that brought him to power. If the Democratic Party hopes to protect its position in the upcoming legislative elections, it must abandon factional purges and act as a broad flagship for its entire coalition before its core energy evaporates completely.
