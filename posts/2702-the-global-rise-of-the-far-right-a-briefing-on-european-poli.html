The Global Rise of the Far-Right: A Briefing on European Political Shifts

November 24, 2026

Across Europe and North America, far-right political parties are rapidly transitioning from fringe movements to institutional power players, prompting an urgent and organized counter-mobilization from civil society.

  • Mainstreaming: Parties once considered political pariahs are now acting as essential coalition partners for traditional conservative parties struggling to secure parliamentary majorities.
  • Structural Cracks: The "firewall"—the long-standing political consensus that kept extremists out of government—is showing signs of collapse in countries like Spain.
  • Digital Resistance: In Germany, progressive civil society groups are bypassing traditional political channels, using sophisticated digital organizing and crowdfunding techniques to combat far-right misinformation.
  • Global Stakes: This is not a localized trend; it represents a coordinated attempt to reshape the European Union’s power structure by 2029, prioritizing nationalist agendas over climate commitments and human rights.

The Erosion of the Political 'Firewall'

The situation in Spain serves as a primary case study. The far-right Vox party—formerly dismissed as a fringe nuisance—has become a pivotal power broker. As mainstream conservative parties, such as the People's Party (PP), fail to win outright majorities, they are increasingly viewing alliances with Vox as a pragmatic necessity to seize power. This normalization of the extreme right fundamentally threatens established policy benchmarks regarding minority rights and immigration.

Similarly, in Germany, the Alternative for Germany (AfD) has adopted increasingly radical rhetoric, including calls for the mass deportation of over 2.6 million migrants. By framing environmental protections as an attack on national economic interests and actively courting anti-immigrant sentiment, they have successfully expanded their base. In response, German progressives are adopting tactics reminiscent of U.S. political campaigning, including "Super PAC"-style funding and grassroots digital defense networks, to blunt the AfD’s online influence.

A Strategic Assault on the EU

The far-right’s ambitions extend far beyond individual national borders. Their sights are set on a total reorganization of the European Union by 2029. By seeking to undermine current leadership—including efforts to force the departure of European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen—they aim to dismantle the EU's focus on multilateralism and climate action in favor of a bloc defined by nationalist sovereignty.

The danger here is that these movements are not merely seeking political office; they are attempting to rewrite the democratic social contract. As we have seen with the legal dissolution of extremist groups in France and the internal scandals plaguing Hungary’s Fidesz party, the far-right model is frequently prone to the very corruption and instability it claims to solve. For observers, the lesson is clear: democracy is not self-sustaining. Without constant vigilance and active participation from an organized civil society, the guardrails protecting individual rights and the rule of law can be dismantled from within.
