South Korea’s Conservative Opposition Fractures as High-Stakes Judicial Deadline Looms

November 14, 2026

South Korea’s main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), has descended into bitter factional warfare over internal purges and readmission disputes, just as an imminent Supreme Court deadline for jailed former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo pushes inter-party tensions to the brink.

  • Opposition civil war escalates: The PPP leadership under party chief Jang Dong-hyeok faces intense backlash over allegations of "fabricated" disciplinary actions against internal rivals, while firmly barring former interim leader Han Dong-hoon from returning to the party.
  • Alternative conservative alignments emerge: Outside the party apparatus, Reform Party lawmaker Lee Jun-seok met Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon to coordinate metropolitan policy, fueling speculation of a broader conservative realignment that bypasses the PPP's current leadership.
  • Judicial deadline sparks constitutional clash: Former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo’s detention on insurrection-related charges expires on November 20, prompting lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) to threaten Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae with impeachment if the Supreme Court allows Han's release before issuing a final verdict.
  • Presidential luncheon on the horizon: President Lee Jae-myung has invited ruling and opposition leaders to a cross-party luncheon this Sunday to negotiate an overdue supplementary budget and stalled prime ministerial confirmation hearings.

Factional Feuds Stall the Conservative Opposition

The People Power Party’s rapid descent into procedural infighting highlights an opposition struggling to redefine itself. Rather than mounting a coherent check on the ruling DPK, the PPP is consumed by zero-sum factional warfare. Party chief Jang Dong-hyeok has denounced Han Dong-hoon’s bid to rejoin as disruptive, while dissident lawmakers demand independent investigations into internal disciplinary abuses. This paralysis has opened the door for external figures like Lee Jun-seok, whose high-profile policy meetings with pragmatic municipal leaders like Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon offer conservative voters an alternative to the party’s dysfunctional central committee.

Judicial Pressure and the Governance Deficit

At the same time, the institutional standoff over Han Duck-soo exposes how deeply partisan conflict is eroding democratic norms. The ruling DPK's readiness to float the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho over standard procedural detention limits represents an aggressive encroachment by the legislature on the judiciary. In response, the PPP has taken to the streets in protest, further entrenching the polarization that divides the National Assembly.

While President Lee Jae-myung’s upcoming luncheon offers a nominal window for dialogue on the supplementary budget, substantive compromise appears remote. With the ruling coalition preoccupied with judicial retribution and the opposition locked in self-destructive internal battles, national governance and pressing economic matters continue to take a back seat to political survival.
