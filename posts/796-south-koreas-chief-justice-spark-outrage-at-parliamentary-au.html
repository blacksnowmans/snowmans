South Korea's Chief Justice Spark Outrage at Parliamentary Audit

October 07, 2026

  • Cho Hee-dae, the Chief Justice of South Korea's Supreme Court, refused to take the oath or testify during a parliamentary audit by the Legislation and Judiciary Committee, claiming his authority is equal to that of the president.
  • Opposition lawmakers accused Cho of budget misappropriation, alleging that millions of won in public funds were illegally diverted to hand out regular cash stipends to Supreme Court justices and fund luxury meals and wine.
  • The confrontation reflects plunging public trust in South Korea's judiciary, which critics argue has overstepped its constitutional bounds and shielded political allies.

South Korea's judicial leadership faced a fierce backlash during a parliamentary audit as Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae refused to take the statutory oath to testify. Lawmakers from across the political spectrum grilled Cho over a series of controversies, including a months-long vacancy on the Supreme Court bench, controversial rulings, and the alleged misuse of official expense accounts for luxury dining and cash stipends.

The audit quickly devolved into a bitter clash over the separation of powers. Cho defended his position by asserting that his authority as head of the judiciary is on par with that of the president, a claim that opposition members slammed as a dangerous distortion of constitutional order. Lawmakers also zeroed in on financial records showing tens of millions of won in operational funds—including money linked to the purchase of expensive wine and high-end omakase meals—being funneled to Supreme Court justices. While opposition figures labeled the spending an illegal diversion of taxpayers' money equivalent to embezzlement, Cho repeatedly denied personal knowledge of the transactions, insisting that administrative staff managed the budgets.

Critics argue that the judiciary's insistence on absolute independence has been weaponized into a shield for personal privilege and political maneuvering. With public confidence in the courts reportedly sliding sharply, the standoff at the National Assembly highlights deepening friction between the legislature and a beleaguered judicial establishment.
