President Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Rating Sinks to 35% Amid Media Raids and SNS Blunders

October 26, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating has plunged to a precarious 35.3%, signaling a potential crisis of legitimacy as his core support base begins to abandon the administration.

  • Disapproval hits record high: Negative sentiment surged to 62.3%, creating a massive 27-point gap that threatens to paralyze the government’s legislative agenda.
  • Opposition takes the lead: The conservative People Power Party (PPP), the main opposition, has overtaken the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) by 6.6 percentage points, the first lead outside the margin of error in four months.
  • Demographic collapse: The sharpest declines were seen among retirees (down 9.0 points) and seniors over 70 (down 7.0 points), while support from the President’s own progressive base dropped by 5.6 points.
  • Media suppression controversy: Public trust has been shaken by aggressive investigative raids on media outlets and the President’s erratic use of social media, which recently resulted in the accidental leak of confidential government documents.

A Crisis of Trust Among Core Supporters

The latest polling data from Realmeter suggests the administration has entered a "danger zone." The loss of support among progressives and Seoul residents is particularly damaging, as these groups were instrumental in Lee’s rise to power. Disillusionment appears to be rooted in a perceived shift toward authoritarianism. Recent police raids on media figures, such as Lee Jong-won of the outlet Sisa Tapa, have drawn uncomfortable comparisons to the "gagging" tactics used during the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president currently serving a prison sentence following his impeachment.

For many progressive voters, the sight of a Democratic Party administration using the same heavy-handed investigative powers they once condemned is a bridge too far. This "moral fatigue" is compounded by a lack of tangible results in stabilizing the economy and housing market. As the gap between positive and negative evaluations widens to nearly 30 points, the administration risks falling into a "lame duck" state where it can no longer effectively command the civil service or push major reforms through a skeptical public.

The Double-Edged Sword of SNS Politics

President Lee’s insistence on "direct communication" via social media is increasingly viewed as a liability rather than an asset. While the President defends his SNS activity as a vital tool to bypass traditional media "filters" and reach the people directly, the lack of a formal vetting process has led to significant friction with professional bureaucrats. By issuing directives or legal opinions online before they are reviewed by relevant ministries, the President is effectively undermining the expertise and stability of the professional civil service.

The risks of this unmediated approach were laid bare recently when a presidential post intended to tout successes in fighting voice-phishing accidentally included a photo of a document marked "confidential." Furthermore, the President’s tendency to engage in public spats with critics and private companies has diminished the dignity of the office. Critics argue that instead of fostering true communication, the President’s SNS has become an "echo chamber" that only resonates with his most hardcore followers while alienating the moderate voters needed to sustain a governing majority. Without a return to systematic, disciplined governance, the administration’s downward trend appears unlikely to reverse.
