Confirmation Hearing for Prosecution Chief Nominee Sparks Backlash

October 16, 2026

The nomination of Kim Ji-yong to lead the newly formed Major Crimes Investigation Agency (MCIA)—a specialized body designed to curb the prosecution’s historical overreach—has triggered widespread outrage following a disastrous parliamentary confirmation hearing.

  • The Nominee: Kim Ji-yong, a career prosecutor whose past actions in high-profile cases have drawn intense scrutiny.
  • The Controversy: Kim is accused of participating in a "politically motivated" prosecution of a fellow prosecutor, Jeong Jin-ung, to protect former Justice Minister Han Dong-hoon.
  • The Breach: Despite the Supreme Court later acquitting Jeong, Kim stood by the original indictment during his hearing, refusing to show remorse or acknowledge errors.
  • The Risk: Critics argue that appointing Kim would effectively turn the MCIA into a tool for the same "prosecutorial cartel" it was meant to replace, undermining President Lee Jae-myung’s judicial reform agenda.

A Test of Judicial Reform

The hearing centered on the controversial indictment of prosecutor Jeong Jin-ung, who was charged with "assault in the course of duty" during a physical confrontation while executing a search warrant related to the "Channel A" scandal. Opposition lawmakers and critics characterized the case as a retaliatory strike orchestrated to shield Han Dong-hoon, a former Justice Minister. When questioned if he still believed the indictment was justified, Kim flatly replied, "Yes, that is correct," despite the final acquittal by the Supreme Court.

For many observers, Kim’s demeanor—marked by evasive answers like "I do not remember"—was a painful reminder of the "political prosecution" culture that defined the tenure of the now-imprisoned former leader, Yoon Seok-yeol. The fact that Kim admitted he sought and received guidance from superiors to push forward with an indictment he knew was legally shaky suggests that he prioritized organizational loyalty over judicial integrity.

The Call for Withdrawal

The appointment process has exposed a significant blind spot in the Lee Jae-myung administration’s vetting system. The MCIA was established to create checks and balances by separating investigation from prosecution, a core promise of the current administration. Placing a figure like Kim at its helm, who has shown no inclination to reform the very systems he represents, threatens to render the entire project moot.

Public sentiment is turning rapidly against the nominee. If the administration proceeds with the appointment, it risks self-sabotage, alienating the base that voted for institutional change. To restore public trust, the administration must acknowledge the gravity of these findings and withdraw Kim's nomination immediately. True reform requires the courage to admit when a candidate is fundamentally unsuited for the mission at hand, rather than forcing a political appointment that contradicts the government’s own reformist principles.
