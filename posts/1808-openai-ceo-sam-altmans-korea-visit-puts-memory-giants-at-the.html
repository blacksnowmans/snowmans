OpenAI CEO Sam Altman’s Korea Visit Puts Memory Giants at the Center of Global AI Infrastructure

November 01, 2026

OpenAI Chief Executive Sam Altman met with top executives from Samsung Electronics and SK Group in Seoul this week, cementing a high-stakes supply chain partnership to secure the massive semiconductor volumes required for next-generation artificial intelligence infrastructure.

    What happened: Altman held back-to-back meetings with roughly 50 Samsung executives and shared a private luncheon with SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won, alongside key leaders from SK Hynix and SK Telecom. What it changes: The discussions signal a decisive shift toward cloud-based AI data centers, cementing demand for advanced high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and server-grade DRAM. Who is affected: South Korea's premier memory manufacturers, who are transitioning from component suppliers to indispensable pillars of the global AI ecosystem.

Altman’s return to Seoul underscores a stark reality for the artificial intelligence boom: even as public excitement fluctuates, the physical bottleneck of building massive data centers remains acute. By courting South Korea's memory heavyweights simultaneously, OpenAI is moving to secure predictable, high-performance hardware supply lines amid fierce global competition for advanced chips.

Crucially, Altman's recent critique of consumer-grade PC memory utilization—dismissing personal device RAM as wildly inefficient—signals that future semiconductor demand will concentrate almost entirely in massive cloud servers. For Samsung Electronics, South Korea's top tech titan, and SK Hynix, a dominant global supplier of HBM chips, this structural pivot validates their heavy investments in high-value server infrastructure over general-use consumer memory.
