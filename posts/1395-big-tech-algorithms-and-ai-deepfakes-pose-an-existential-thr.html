Big Tech Algorithms and AI Deepfakes Pose an Existential Threat to South Korea's Democracy

October 22, 2026

Rapidly evolving AI technologies and biased Big Tech algorithms are undermining democratic processes worldwide, leaving South Korea dangerously exposed as foreign disinformation and platform manipulation spill across its borders.

  • Global platform bias: Big Tech firms like Meta and X (formerly Twitter) have actively distorted political discourse, from shadow-banning candidates in Brazil—resulting in a $700,000 fine for Meta—to algorithmically boosting political echo chambers in the U.S. and Europe.
  • Emergence of AI swarms: Sophisticated AI bots and "data poisoning" networks, such as Moscow's Pravda network, generate millions of fake articles that corrupt AI training data and make digital astroturfing nearly impossible for ordinary users to spot.
  • Grassroots defense under pressure: Korean civic fact-checking projects like "Parti" are striving to counter election fake news, but civic efforts remain fragile following heavy political crackdowns under the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol that dismantled key media-monitoring networks.
  • Critical funding deficit: South Korea allocates 90 billion won (about $67 million) toward national AI development, but only 26 billion won (about $19 million) goes to basic AI safety, leaving almost no state budget for R&D aimed at protecting democratic elections.

How Tech Capital Weaponizes the Public Sphere

The weaponization of social media algorithms is no longer a theoretical risk—it is a documented reality. In Brazil, Meta restricted paid promotions for presidential candidate Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva while permitting automated recommendations for his opponent. In the U.S., X owner Elon Musk forced engineers to amplify his personal account exposure by a factor of 1,000. From Myanmar's Rohingya genocide to manipulated riot footage in France, platform algorithms consistently prioritize outrage and retention over truth because rage generates revenue.

This dynamic is now supercharged by generative AI. With automated "AI swarms" capable of crafting persuasive commentary around the clock, bad actors can deploy coordinated disinformation across international boundaries. During Brazil's election, distorted clips of Korean political rallies were recycled to trick South American voters—demonstrating that digital election interference operates completely without borders.

South Korea’s Preparedness Void

As South Koreans shift from domestic news portals to foreign social media apps like YouTube and Instagram, national regulatory leverage is evaporating. Overseas tech conglomerates operate largely outside Korean democratic oversight, refusing to grant local civic groups or regulators insight into their black-box algorithms.

Meanwhile, South Korea's domestic defenses remain severely underfunded and politically battered. Independent fact-checking networks like FactCheckNet were devastated during the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, facing prosecution referrals and penalties of 800 million won (about $600,000). While volunteer projects like Parti attempt to fill the gap before upcoming elections, South Korea cannot rely on volunteers alone. Without dedicated government investment into technological sovereignty and algorithm transparency, the country's public discourse risks being surrendered to foreign tech billionaires and foreign AI bots.
