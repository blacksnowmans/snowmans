The Democratic Party’s Civil War: Ethics, Religion, and the 34% Approval Crisis

November 17, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party is facing a severe internal rupture as Party Leader Kim Min-seok launched a sudden ethics investigation into his own Supreme Council member, Choi Min-hee, sparking allegations of a "palace coup" to deflect from deeper scandals.

  • Ethics as a weapon: Leader Kim referred Choi to the party’s ethics tribunal—effectively a political indictment—over remarks where she called reporters "hyenas" and made confusing statements regarding the prior impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • The Shincheonji factor: Critics argue the probe is a "frame shift" to bury a report alleging that 14,388 members of the controversial Shincheonji religious sect have infiltrated the party’s ranks to influence internal elections.
  • Legislative rebellion: Fifty-five first-term lawmakers, known as "The Min-cho," have publicly demanded Choi’s resignation, while 14 others refused to sign the statement, citing a lack of procedural fairness and impartiality.
  • Plunging support: The feud erupts as President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating hit a low of 34.6%, leaving the party vulnerable despite controlling a massive 821 trillion KRW (roughly $590 billion) "super budget."

Procedural Democracy vs. Political Expediency

The speed and method of the probe are the most telling details. Choi reportedly learned of her own investigation from news reports while sitting at dinner—a blatant bypass of the usual consultation between top party leaders who meet daily. To her supporters, this is not about maintaining high ethical standards but is instead an exercise in "fear politics." By targeting Choi, Leader Kim may be attempting to neutralize a high-ranking rival and rearrange the party hierarchy in what some insiders describe as a "pro-leadership coup."

The "smoking gun" in the ethics case involves Choi’s claim that President Lee Jae-myung once opposed the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. In the context of a private meeting, this appears to be a verbal slip where she confused the names of various officials or past political maneuvers. However, the party leadership is treating this as "spreading false information," a charge that could lead to her removal. Critics point out the hypocrisy in this move, noting that Leader Kim himself has faced no scrutiny for past unverified claims regarding "secret alliances" within the party.

The Shincheonji Shadow and the Approval Crisis

Beneath the surface of this ethics spat lies the "Shincheonji" issue—a religious group often accused of clandestine political meddling in Korea. A party task force reportedly found 14,388 names on the party rolls that cross-reference with the sect's membership. The leadership’s refusal to release the full data, citing "legal risks," has only fueled suspicions. If the party is seen as being "chosen by Shincheonji," it risks catastrophic losses in upcoming local and national contests. By focusing the news cycle on Choi’s "hyena" comments, the leadership effectively buries the more damaging religious infiltration narrative.

This internal friction comes at a perilous time for the Lee Jae-myung administration. With the 2027 university entrance exams approaching and diplomatic tensions rising—highlighted by Japan's recent refusal to allow Cho Kuk, leader of the Rebuilding Korea Party, to enter the country—the ruling party’s inability to maintain a united front is reflecting in the polls. If the Democratic Party cannot resolve its internal "fairness" crisis, the 34.6% approval rating may be a ceiling rather than a floor.
