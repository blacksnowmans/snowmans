The Double-Edged Sword of AI and the Global Interest Rate Crisis

October 11, 2026

The global economy is currently caught in a precarious trap: as market interest rates hit 24-year highs, the race to fund the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution is paradoxically tightening the financial noose on everyday households and businesses.

  • Interest Rate Shock: US Treasury yields have surged, with the 10-year yield hitting 5.36% and the 30-year reaching 5.73%, levels not seen since 2002.
  • The AI Capital Sinkhole: Massive, debt-fueled spending by global tech giants to build AI infrastructure is draining liquidity from bond markets, forcing interest rates higher.
  • Economic Instability: While AI may eventually lower production costs and tame inflation, the immediate burden of high borrowing costs threatens to push businesses and households to the breaking point.
  • Policy Challenges: The administration of President Lee Jae-myung faces the daunting task of navigating these global headwinds while addressing the structural inequalities left behind by the previous administration.

The Anatomy of the Yield Spike

The surge in US Treasury yields is not merely a central bank policy decision; it is a reflection of intense market anxiety. Driven by persistent inflation, ballooning US fiscal deficits, and the repatriation of Japanese capital—as Japan pivots away from its long-standing zero-interest-rate policy—the bond market is flooded with supply while demand wavers. This volatility is a warning light for the global economy, signaling that the cost of capital is becoming unsustainable.

In South Korea, these global shifts are felt directly through rising mortgage rates and consumer inflation. The financial instability inherited from the era of former leader 윤석열 씨 (now incarcerated following his impeachment) has left the economy vulnerable. The current administration is now tasked with managing these risks, as the lack of political stability during the previous regime left the country’s external economic defenses weak.

The AI Paradox: Innovation vs. Survival

AI is being hailed as the next major productivity driver, with the potential to act as a "game changer" for inflation much like China’s manufacturing boom did decades ago. However, the path to this future is fraught with peril. Tech companies are currently engaging in a high-stakes "chicken game," taking on massive debt to finance data centers. This competition for capital is sucking oxygen out of the market, causing borrowing costs to soar for everyone else.

While optimists at firms like Goldman Sachs see rates eventually cooling, others like Bank of America warn that if the US economy remains too robust—preventing inflation from falling—rates could stay higher for longer. For the average person, this is a cruel irony: a "strong" economy that keeps interest rates high is a direct threat to their livelihood.

The Need for Structural Resilience

Ultimately, the economy is a matter of structure, not prayer. As the Lee Jae-myung government moves to rectify the economic disparities left by the previous administration, the focus must remain on social safety nets. If the government relies solely on market autonomy during this transition, the resulting inequality could fracture society long before the promised benefits of the AI revolution arrive.

Technical progress is inevitable, but it must be tempered with realistic policy. The government must ensure that the costs of this technological shift do not fall disproportionately on the shoulders of the working class. Navigating the tunnel of high interest rates requires more than just waiting for the light; it requires active, precise management of fiscal and monetary policy to ensure the economy survives the journey.
