Seoul Mayor Hit by Ferry Scandal as President Lee Defends Overseas Diplomacy

November 02, 2026

Allegations of a 320 million won ($230,000) kickback in Seoul’s flagship river ferry project have triggered a fierce political storm, compounding broader battles over President Lee Jae-myung’s foreign policy and deepening public cynicism toward South Korea’s judiciary.

  • Scandal in Seoul: The Seoul Metropolitan Government awarded a major water-bus contract to Gadok Heavy Industries—a firm with zero shipbuilding experience—leading to internal audit findings of a suspected 320 million won ($230,000) kickback and whistleblower allegations of ordered document destruction.
  • Diplomatic defense: President Lee Jae-myung pushed back against attacks from the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) over his 14 overseas trips, insisting high-level summit diplomacy is essential for South Korea's export-reliant economy.
  • Factional splits: Polling shows a neck-and-neck battle between the ruling liberal Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) and the conservative PPP, with the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party drawing 9.1% support and splitting left-leaning voters.
  • Surging judicial distrust: Public frustration over high-profile court decisions—including Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s acquittal on political fund charges—has driven strong public backing for a parliamentary probe into Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.

Ferry Kickbacks and Municipal Cover-Up Allegations

The "Hangang Bus" river transit project was designed as a high-profile achievement for Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon. Instead, it has escalated into a serious corruption controversy for the conservative mayor. Awarding a municipal contract to an entity with no prior shipbuilding track record raised immediate questions, but the audit finding of a suspected 320 million won ($230,000) kickback moves the issue from administrative incompetence to potential criminality. Allegations from a whistleblower that city officials were instructed to destroy investigation request forms—shortly before the Board of Audit and Inspection dismissed the case—have deepened suspicions of an organized cover-up. Without a transparent, independent investigation, Mayor Oh faces a damaging erosion of public trust.

Diplomatic Debates and the Erosion of Judicial Trust

On the national stage, President Lee Jae-myung is confronting sharp criticism from the PPP over his international itinerary. Lee has taken a direct approach to counter claims that overseas travel neglects domestic issues, arguing that securing foreign economic partnerships is vital for maintaining domestic financial stability. While positive macroeconomic figures such as a current account surplus support the administration's stance, market fluctuations keep broader consumer confidence fragile. Compounding this policy debate is a systemic decline in public faith toward the legal system. With a majority of polled citizens favoring artificial intelligence over human judges and backing a parliamentary inquiry into the Chief Justice, South Korea's political institutions face growing pressure to deliver genuine judicial accountability.
