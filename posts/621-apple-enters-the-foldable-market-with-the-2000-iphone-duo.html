Apple Enters the Foldable Market with the $2,000 'iPhone Duo'

September 10, 2026

Apple has officially shaken up the foldable smartphone market with its long-rumored first foldable device, the iPhone Duo, launching at a starting price of 3.29 million won (about $2,450) in South Korea and $1,999 in the U.S.

  • What happened: Apple debuted its first foldable smartphone, entering a market pioneered by Samsung Electronics' Galaxy Z Fold series back in 2019. Pre-orders begin October 16, with an official release on October 23.
  • The specs & price: The base 256GB model starts at $1,999, while the top-tier 2TB version surges to over 5 million won (about $3,800). It features a D-shaped asymmetric design resembling a passport, an IP68 water-and-dust resistance rating, and runs on the A20 Pro chip. Samsung Display supplies the OLED panels.
  • Market impact: Apple's entry is expected to push foldable phones further into the mainstream. However, its ultra-premium pricing positions it firmly as a luxury status symbol rather than a mass-market device.
  • Who is affected: Samsung, which has dominated the foldable space largely alone, now faces a formidable rival, though the expanded market could lift demand for both brands. Early adopters and tech enthusiasts face a steep financial barrier.

A Masterclass in Engineering—With a Luxury Price Tag

Apple is notoriously late to the foldable game, but as usual, the company has spent years refining the hardware to solve persistent issues like screen creases and durability. By introducing top-tier features like an IP68 dust-and-water rating, advanced cooling systems, and seamless software continuity between the cover and inner displays, Apple has set a new hardware benchmark. Samsung's Galaxy series, while seasoned in this category, now faces a serious challenge in perceived build quality and brand cachet.

Yet the elephant in the room is the price. At nearly $2,000 for the base model and over $3,800 for maximum storage, the iPhone Duo crosses the line from consumer electronics into ultra-luxury goods. While the engineering is undeniably impressive, this pricing strategy feels less like a push for technological democratization and more like aggressive market stratification aimed strictly at the top 1%.

The Verdict on Apple's Foldable Strategy

Apple's entry validates the foldable form factor once and for all, turning it from a niche gadget category into a battleground for mainstream tech supremacy. But it also leaves a bitter aftertaste regarding consumer costs. When a smartphone costs more than a high-end laptop, it forces buyers to seriously question where utility ends and pure status symbol markup begins. Apple has proven it can build one of the best foldables on the market — but whether anyone outside of die-hard fans and the wealthy will follow remains to be seen.
