Ruling Party Leader Choi Min-hee Faces Two-Year Suspension, Sparking Internal Turmoil

November 28, 2026

The Ethics Committee of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) has issued a two-year suspension of party membership for Choi Min-hee, a senior member of the party’s leadership, effectively barring her from the 2028 general election.

  • The committee cited "damaging the dignity of the party" as the primary reason for the severe disciplinary action against Choi, who currently serves as a Senior Supreme Council member.
  • If the decision is finalized, Choi will immediately lose her leadership position and be disqualified from seeking the party's nomination for the next parliamentary elections in 2028.
  • Choi has officially rejected the ruling and filed for a retrial, calling the punishment an unacceptable and disproportionate response to her recent remarks and conduct.
  • The decision has ignited a fierce internal power struggle, with high-profile allies like Representative Jung Chung-rae accusing the committee of bias and demanding similar probes into other officials to ensure fairness.

The High Cost of "Dignity"

The ruling is exceptionally harsh for a sitting member of the party's top decision-making body. By stripping Choi of her membership for two years, the Ethics Committee is essentially handing her a "political death sentence." In the hyper-competitive landscape of South Korean politics, being unable to run in a general election cycle often marks the end of a career. The official charge of "damaging party dignity" is broad, suggesting that the party’s internal disciplinary body is moving to enforce a stricter code of conduct as the DPK seeks to maintain its image as the party in power under President Lee Jae-myung.

However, the severity of the punishment has led to immediate accusations of "political purging." Choi’s supporters argue that the punishment does not fit the "crime" and point to the lack of clear precedents for such a long suspension for non-criminal behavior. This has put the party leadership in a difficult position: upholding the decision risks alienating a vocal and influential faction of the party, while reversing it could undermine the authority of the Ethics Committee and signal that leadership members are above the rules.

Fractures in the Ruling Party

This move has exposed deep rifts within the Democratic Party. While some factions argue that strict discipline is necessary to maintain public trust, Choi’s allies have launched a counter-offensive. They have specifically called for the discipline of other prominent figures, such as Representative Kim Min-seok, questioning why Choi is being singled out while others with controversial records remain untouched. This "fire with fire" strategy suggests that the dispute is no longer about a single individual’s conduct, but is a proxy war between internal factions vying for influence within the ruling party.

The timing of this internal crisis is particularly inconvenient for the government. As the party enters the critical December legislative session—a period usually reserved for passing the national budget and key policy initiatives—this infighting threatens to derail the administration's momentum. If President Lee Jae-myung cannot stabilize his party’s internal discipline, the DPK may find it increasingly difficult to project the unity required to lead the country effectively through the end of the year.
