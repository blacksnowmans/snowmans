Why Progressive Pundits Are Sabotaging South Korea's Left

October 05, 2026

  • Prominent South Korean progressive writer You Si-min has criticized pro-government commentators for attacking figures within their own camp to curry favor with the current administration.
  • Pundits and minor party officials have targeted former President Moon Jae-in, Cho Kuk (leader of the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party), and influential media figures like Kim Eo-jun.
  • Critics argue this factional infighting is alienating core supporters, eroding solidarity, and dragging down presidential approval ratings.

South Korea’s progressive camp is eating its own. According to a recent critique by well-known writer and commentator You Si-min, certain pro-government pundits and minor party officials have made a habit of attacking fellow liberals—including former President Moon Jae-in and Rebuilding Korea Party leader Cho Kuk—to prove their loyalty to the current leadership under President Lee Jae-myung. The result is a toxic loop of sycophancy that damages the broader progressive coalition.

The core issue, You argues, is a complete lack of accountability among commentators who shape public opinion on YouTube and television. Rather than admitting past analytical errors or offering constructive debate, these figures launch personal attacks against anyone who doesn't fit their narrow definition of loyalty. By tearing down respected figures within their own movement to win favor with higher-ups, they are effectively doing the conservative opposition's work for them.

Self-Sabotage Disguised as Loyalty

In South Korean politics, attacking allies rarely wins over the opposing side. Conservative or swing voters aren't going to switch camps just because a liberal pundit bashes Moon Jae-in; instead, they simply sit back and watch the infighting. Meanwhile, core supporters who helped build the progressive movement feel betrayed and alienated.

This dynamic explains why approval ratings can take a nosedive even among dedicated progressive bases, such as supporters of the Rebuilding Korea Party who previously backed President Lee in massive numbers. When pundits use their platforms to attack movement veterans like You Si-min or media personality Kim Eo-jun just to grab attention, they fracture the very coalitions that brought their leaders to power. It is an exercise in short-term careerism at the expense of long-term political survival.
