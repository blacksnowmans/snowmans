The Democratic Party’s Legislative Blitz: Seizing Momentum Amid Political Standoffs

November 14, 2026

The Democratic Party (DP), the ruling party of South Korea, is currently pursuing an aggressive legislative and policy agenda designed to consolidate its grip on governance and hold both the opposition and the judiciary accountable.

  • Legislative Acceleration: Utilizing its 170-seat majority, the DP is pushing forward with budget approvals and "people's livelihood" bills while the main opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), engages in protests outside the National Assembly.
  • Judicial Confrontation: Party leadership has issued warnings against Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, explicitly mentioning the possibility of impeachment should the judiciary intervene to release former leader Han Duck-soo.
  • Strategic Infrastructure: The party’s "Mega Growth Special Committee" has unveiled a massive 10-year, $60 billion (approx. 84 trillion won) infrastructure plan, with $36 billion (approx. 50 trillion won) requested in government funding to secure power and water for the semiconductor industry.
  • Internal Realignment: The DP is navigating internal debates over comprehensive real estate tax reforms, balancing the need to appeal to suburban voters with its foundational principles of tax equity.

Legislative Power Moves in the Absence of the Opposition

With the PPP boycotting the National Assembly to hold street protests, the DP is characterizing the move as "betraying the people." DP floor leader Han Byung-do has publicly urged the opposition to return to the Assembly, labeling their absence as a dereliction of duty. The party’s stance is clear: while the opposition protests, the DP will use its legislative muscle to prove its competence by passing bills that address economic and social grievances.

This strategy serves a dual purpose. By occupying the vacuum left by the PPP, the DP is signaling that it is the only party effectively governing the country. Its internal management—such as reassigning leadership in problematic district committees—is part of a broader effort to tighten discipline and maintain the political momentum of the Lee Jae-myung administration.

Judicial Pressure and the "Red Line"

The relationship between the DP and the judiciary has turned increasingly adversarial. Tensions center on the potential release of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo, who served under the previous administration of the now-imprisoned Yoon Suk-yeol. The DP views the judiciary's potential leniency as a "red line" that would signify an attempt to protect the legacy of the former regime.

By openly discussing the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, the DP is warning the courts that judicial independence does not equate to immunity from democratic accountability. This is not merely about one man; it is a litmus test for the Lee Jae-myung administration’s goal of "eradicating deep-rooted evils" from the previous era. The message is blunt: the DP is prepared to use every constitutional tool at its disposal to ensure that the judiciary remains aligned with the current administration's interpretation of justice.
