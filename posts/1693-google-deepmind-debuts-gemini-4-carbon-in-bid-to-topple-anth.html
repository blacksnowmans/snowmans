Google DeepMind Debuts Gemini 4 Carbon in Bid to Topple Anthropic

October 29, 2026

Google DeepMind has unveiled Gemini 4 Carbon, a flagship AI model engineered for long-horizon autonomous tasks that edges past Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 on headline benchmarks while cutting nominal token pricing in half.

  • What happened: Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on its official blog on October 29, scoring 59 to Claude Opus 5.5’s 58 on the Artificial Analytics v5 index—just two weeks after rolling out its Gemini 4 Argon model.
  • What it changes: Carbon shifts Google’s focus from quick chat prompts to sustained agentic workflows, excelling at multi-day coding debugging, terminal tasks, and massive context comprehension across up to 1 million tokens.
  • Who is affected: Enterprise developers and security teams. Carbon is currently restricted to select cybersecurity defense agencies and partners under Google's Fairwind initiative, pending pre-deployment reviews with US bodies like the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI).
  • The cost reality: While Carbon’s base API prices undercut Opus 5.5 by 50% ($2 per million input tokens, $10 output), its heavy internal reasoning means real-world task execution averages $10.51 per task—cheaper than Opus ($28.12) but far higher than budget models like GPT-6.1 Sol ($1.27).

Specialized Muscle Over Universal Dominance

Carbon’s narrow benchmark lead should not be mistaken for total market supremacy. The model posted commanding wins in terminal operations (65.2% on TerminalBench 4.0) and massive-context pattern matching (96.2% on GraphWalk), yet it still stumbles in graphical user interface automation, trailing Opus 5.5 on OSWorld 2.1 (81.5% to 87.2%), and places third on Frontier SWE 2 behind Astra and Opus. Google has built a powerhouse for deep backend code maintenance and analytical endurance, not an omnipotent generalist.

The model's stark duality is clearest in cybersecurity. In Google's SecBench Pro testing, Carbon proved exceptionally sharp, discovering vulnerabilities at an 88.2% clip and crafting security patches ten times more effectively than rival large models. Yet because these capabilities were benchmarked without safety filters, Google faces an acute dual-use dilemma: the exact agentic intelligence that fixes hidden exploits over multi-day autonomous runs could paralyze infrastructure in hostile hands, explaining the tight operational lockdown.

The Hidden Math of Enterprise AI Adoption

For corporate buyers, Carbon illustrates how headline pricing tables have become actively deceptive. Advertised token rates overlook the massive computational overhead generated when advanced models deliberate before answering. While Carbon halves token rates compared to Opus 5.5, running complex enterprise workflows still costs real money. Teams must evaluate models based on total task-completion cost rather than published per-token fees.

Google’s blistering release cycle—launching four models in two months—signals that the frontier AI race has moved beyond raw conversational fluency into practical enterprise resilience. With hallucination rates halved and injection attack defenses holding firm, Carbon makes a compelling business case for legal and financial data pipelines. However, in an ecosystem churning out upgrades every fortnight, locking into a single model family remains an expensive operational gamble.
