South Korea’s Ruling Party Pushes to Impeach Chief Justice Amid Mounting Domestic Controversies

October 18, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has formally raised the prospect of impeaching Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de, escalating an institutional showdown with the judiciary even as party leadership scrambles to contain a string of self-inflicted messaging crises.

  • Judicial confrontation formalized: Ruling party leaders and senior lawmakers openly discussed drafting an impeachment motion against Chief Justice Jo, alleging judicial bias and unconstitutional court rulings dating back to Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s term in office.
  • Conservative opposition fires back: The People Power Party, South Korea’s main conservative opposition, condemned the move as an attack on constitutional order meant to shield President Lee Jae-myung, warning that an impeachment motion would trigger severe political fallout.
  • Emergency damage control: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok issued a public apology after senior lawmaker Choo Mi-ae sparked public outrage over a resurfaced remark calling municipal fire service budgets a "fiscal straw," just as the nation mourned a firefighter killed in the line of duty.
  • Outreach marred by friction: Party leaders conducted an economic tour across Busan and South Gyeongsang Province, but the trip was overshadowed by a protocol controversy in Sunchang—where a festival concert was cut short for political speeches—and gridlock in parliamentary audits.

A High-Stakes Gamble on the Judiciary

Floating the impeachment of a sitting Supreme Court Chief Justice represents an aggressive escalation by the ruling Democratic Party of Korea. Frustrated by major court rulings and lingering distrust toward a bench shaped during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s administration, party hardliners appear determined to enforce judicial accountability through legislative brute force. While this satisfies core partisans who demand rapid judicial reform, weaponizing the impeachment mechanism against the head of the judicial branch invites dangerous accusations of undermining the constitutional separation of powers.

The People Power Party has seized on this vulnerability, framing the offensive as a desperate bid to dismantle legal checks on President Lee Jae-myung's administration. By threatening retaliatory political measures, the conservative opposition is seeking to regain momentum during the ongoing parliamentary audit season, setting the stage for an intractable, zero-sum standoff in the National Assembly.

Internal Discipline Strains Under Scrutiny

The party's offensive on the courts stands in sharp contrast to its clumsy handling of day-to-day controversies. Forcing a senior figure like Choo Mi-ae to reckon with past insensitive language during a solemn national mourning period shows that leadership recognizes the electoral risks of appearing callous. Likewise, interruptions at a public concert in Sunchang to introduce politicians undercut the party’s promises to eradicate authoritarian protocol and connect with everyday citizens.

With unresolved parliamentary fights over media governance and looming investigations into suspected mass memberships by the fringe religious group Shincheonji, the ruling party faces a test of discipline. Holding the presidency and a commanding legislative majority gives the Democratic Party immense power, but unforced errors in empathy and decorum risk alienating moderate voters far faster than high-level constitutional battles.
