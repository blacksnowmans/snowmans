Daegu Relaxes Sunlight Regulations to Boost Housing Supply Amid Approaching Shortage

November 24, 2026

  • Daegu has unified and relaxed its apartment sunlight review threshold for redevelopment and reconstruction projects from 100% to 80%.
  • The short-term emergency measure aims to prevent a sharp drop in incoming move-ins next year and eliminate red tape that has stalled urban renewal projects.
  • While the move improves project viability and building design flexibility, it has triggered concerns over reduced sunlight for residents and ongoing financial burdens like reconstruction excess-profit charges.
  • Meanwhile, Daegu's apartment sales prices edged down by 0.01% while lease (jeonse) prices continued to rise, reflecting a frozen buying sentiment.

Faced with a cooling regional property market choked by high interest rates, rising construction costs, and unsold units, Daegu has rolled back strict administrative hurdles. The city's decision to lower the mandatory sunlight compliance rate from 100% to 80% is designed to give developers more breathing room in building layouts, allowing for better floor-area ratio utilization and increased unit counts. For years, rigid sunlight rules distorted designs and dragged out timelines, throttling new housing supply.

However, this regulatory relief collides directly with quality-of-life concerns and heavy financial realities. Critics and prospective residents worry that denser apartment placements will rob homes of natural light, directly impacting living standards and heating efficiency. At the same time, individual complexes like the Beomeo Ubang 1-cha reconstruction project in Suseong-gu face heavy headwinds, including hefty state-imposed reconstruction levies of about $43,000 (60 million won) per household. These parallel pressures show that administrative fixes alone cannot easily overcome broader market uncertainty and heavy tax burdens.
