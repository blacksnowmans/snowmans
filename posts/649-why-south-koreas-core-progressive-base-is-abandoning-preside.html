Why South Korea's Core Progressive Base is Abandoning President Lee Jae-myung

October 03, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is facing a severe political crisis as his core support base—the ideological backbone that carried him to victory—rapidly turns its back on his administration.

  • Plunging Approval:
    •  Support from key liberal demographics, including voters tied to minor allied parties, has plummeted from the high 90s to near 70% or lower, threatening the ruling Democratic Party's foundation ahead of upcoming elections.
  • Betrayal on Reform:
    •  Voters feel betrayed by watered-down judicial reforms, particularly compromises on the complete separation of investigative and prosecutorial powers, which critics argue preserves old establishment privileges.
  • Splitting the Coalition:
    •  Tensions with the Rebuilding Korea Party, a vital progressive ally, risk splitting the liberal vote and handing major advantages to the conservative opposition.
  • Communication Breakdown:
    •  The president's defensive social media posts and perceived pushback against progressive critics have alienated supporters rather than soothing their grievances.

The Anatomy of a Disillusioned Base

For months, the administration has pointed to a long list of bureaucratic and policy achievements as proof of its competence. But this technocratic defense misses the point entirely. When voters backed the current ruling bloc, they were not looking for a checklist of administrative outputs; they demanded a fundamental uprooting of entrenched, conservative-leaning power structures. By prioritizing safe compromises, perceived tokenism in judicial appointments, and a strategy of broad coalition-building that sidelines long-time progressive loyalists, the administration has engaged in a destructive form of "subtraction politics."

Figures within the broader progressive movement, including veteran commentator Yu Si-min and media personalities, have openly criticized the administration for softening its stance on prosecution reform and alienating core supporters. Instead of fostering a natural, organic expansion into the political center through bold and decisive governance, party leadership has attempted artificial engineering of the electorate—a tactic that risks repeating historical political blunders.

A Looming Electoral Trainwreck

The friction between the ruling Democratic Party and the Rebuilding Korea Party highlights a dangerous hubris within the government. Rather than pursuing a genuine, respectful merger or alliance ahead of the next legislative elections, the ruling camp has offered vague rhetoric paired with heavy-handed internal discipline. If alienated minor progressive parties decide to field independent candidates in critical constituencies like the Seoul metropolitan area, it will fracture the progressive vote and hand an unearned victory to the conservative People Power Party.

Ultimately, President Lee's administration is discovering that administrative competence alone cannot substitute for ideological clarity and political courage. Unless the leadership drops its defensive posture, honors the demands for genuine reform, and repairs relations with its foundational allies, it risks squandering its mandate and facing a devastating defeat at the ballot box.
