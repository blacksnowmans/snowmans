Nvidia Board Discusses Ousting Jensen Huang Over Alleged Violations of U.S. Export Restrictions

November 13, 2026

The global semiconductor industry was plunged into turmoil following reports on November 12 that Nvidia’s board officially discussed removing CEO Jensen Huang over allegations the company intentionally bypassed U.S. export limits to supply chips to China.

  • What happened: Nvidia’s board expanded an internal probe into whether the company circumvented U.S. trade restrictions to supply chips to China, sparking formal discussions about replacing Huang after 33 years at the helm and initiating a search for a successor—reportedly targeting key talent behind Apple Silicon.
  • What it changes: The scandal has fueled bipartisan momentum in the U.S. Congress for aggressive new enforcement legislation to close export loopholes, including indirect trade channels through Southeast Asia, effectively ending any tacit U.S.-China tech truce.
  • Who is affected: South Korean memory giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix face immediate uncertainty over their High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) supply strategies, alongside severe operational risks for their chip manufacturing plants located inside China.

Leadership Vacuum Threatens the AI Hardware Ecosystem

Huang has been the defining figure and chief architect of Nvidia since co-founding the firm in 1993. A sudden departure risks creating an unprecedented leadership void that could disrupt production schedules for next-generation AI hardware, such as the Blackwell chip architecture, and strain key customer contracts. While floating candidates from Apple's chip design team signals an intent to pivot toward a more tightly controlled hardware ecosystem, any internal chaos at Nvidia opens a strategic window for rivals like AMD, Intel, and major cloud providers developing proprietary AI silicon.

High Stakes for South Korea's Memory Supply Chain

For South Korea's semiconductor sector, the crisis highlights the risk of relying heavily on a single executive-driven alliance. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix—the world's top memory chipmakers—have anchored their lucrative HBM roadmaps around Nvidia's AI dominance. Beyond potential changes to memory supply allocations under a new Nvidia management team, Korean firms face heightened geopolitical exposure. Stricter U.S. enforcement threatens the regulatory waivers that currently allow Samsung and SK Hynix to maintain and upgrade their extensive manufacturing facilities in China. To weather the fallout, Korean chipmakers must move past personal executive ties and aggressively diversify their client base toward other global tech firms building custom AI infrastructure.
