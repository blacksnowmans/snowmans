OpenAI’s Leaked "Astra" Benchmarks Suggest 2027 Superintelligence Goal is Within Reach

November 18, 2026

Leaked performance data for OpenAI’s rumored next-generation model, GPT-6.2 "Astra," indicates a massive 22-percentage-point leap in scientific reasoning and the ability to code entire 3D games from a single prompt, signaling that the threshold for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) may be closer than anticipated.

  • Performance Breakthrough: Data pulled from hidden charts on OpenAI’s website shows a model—presumably GPT-6.2 Astra—scoring 89% on the "Bio Basics Bench," compared to 67% for previous flagship iterations.
  • Autonomous 3D Creation: Internal demonstrations reveal the model generating complex 3D dungeon crawlers and hyper-realistic industrial renderings, including textures and physics, using pure code without external assets.
  • Safety Bottlenecks: OpenAI is reportedly delaying the official launch to finalize "safety alignment" with four independent agencies, as the model’s capabilities are said to border on being "too powerful" for immediate release.
  • Market Competition: While the U.S. focuses on peak performance, China’s Tencent (one of the world’s largest technology conglomerates) has released the HY4 Flash, offering high-tier performance at 1/18th the cost of Western competitors.

The Shift from Text Generation to World Building

The Korean AI community is currently fixated on leaked data suggesting that OpenAI’s "Astra" and "Soul" models represent a generational shift. Unlike current models that act as sophisticated text predictors, Astra is being described as a 3D architect. In one demonstration, the model replicated the mechanics of a dungeon crawler game, including mob behavior and environmental textures, through a single prompt. This suggests we are moving past the era of AI-assisted coding into a phase where AI autonomously builds, tests, and deploys entire software ecosystems.

Reports from users accessing these models via Chinese API relay services—which often leak early versions of flagship models—highlight a "Soul" variant that is significantly less "defensive." This means it follows fewer restrictive guidelines than the current GPT-4o, offering more creative freedom but raising serious alarms regarding safety and misuse. The technical leap is so pronounced that researchers at Anthropic (an AI safety and research company founded by former OpenAI executives) now estimate we are only 1.3 years away from "superhuman" AI.

Geopolitics and the Price of Intelligence

While OpenAI maintains a cautious silence, the broader market is moving at a breakneck pace. Google is flooding the market with its "Gemini 4" series, using a naming convention based on the periodic table (Neon, Oxygen, Krypton) to categorize models of varying speeds and context windows. Google’s latest models can now process up to 512,000 tokens—roughly the size of several thick novels—with far less data loss than previous versions.

However, the real disruption may be economic rather than purely technical. Tencent’s HY4 Flash has entered the fray at a price point of roughly $0.07 (100 KRW) per million tokens. This makes high-level AI logic a commodity, potentially forcing Western firms to reconsider their premium pricing models. As Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI, recently hinted that the company has essentially reached the technical milestones for AGI, the industry is no longer asking if these models will change the world, but whether our social and safety frameworks can survive the speed of their arrival.
