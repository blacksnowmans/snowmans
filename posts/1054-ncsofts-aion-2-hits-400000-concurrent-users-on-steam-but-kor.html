NCSoft's Aion 2 Hits 400,000 Concurrent Users on Steam, But Korean Players Feel Betrayed

October 13, 2026

  • Global Hit vs. Local Backlash: NCSoft's Aion 2 global server hit a peak of nearly 400,000 concurrent users on Steam, but domestic players are fuming over perceived preferential treatment for overseas markets.
  • The "Test Server" Grievance: Korean players point out that the global version enjoys a smoother PC-only build, streamlined leveling mechanics (such as reduced pet upgrade caps), and fairer reward structures compared to the heavy grind demanded on home servers.
  • Economic Instability: In-game currency values have plummeted by 65% over three months, and botched gear patches—such as abruptly introducing range bonuses to craftable weapons—have wrecked player investments and spiked material prices.
  • Dual Fronts for the Publisher: While trying to appease domestic hardcores through new content like the "Sorrow's Vow" raid and scaled-back forced PvP, NCSoft faces a race to rebuild shattered local trust without letting its sudden global momentum slip.

South Korean gaming giant NCSoft is currently learning that a massive global launch cannot erase the deep-seated resentment of its home audience. When Aion 2 debuted its international server on Steam, it pulled an impressive 397,905 peak concurrent users, signaling a massive overseas appetite for the massively multiplayer online role-playing game (MMORPG). Yet, back in South Korea, the reaction was largely a bitter sigh: "The Korean server was just a test bed after all."

The core of the backlash lies in stark design disparities between the established domestic version and the fresh global build. Overseas players received a cleaner PC-optimized client and relaxed progression systems—such as lowering maximum pet levels from five to three to cut down on tedious grinding—while Korean veterans remained shackled to heavier systems built on years of sunk time and money. When developers later retroactively increased rewards and offered retroactive compensation for certain items, it did little to soothe high-end players whose asset values and hard-earned gear progression were upended by sudden patches.

This crisis points to a recurring trap for legacy Korean studios trying to balance global expansion with domestic retention. For years, local players have complained of being treated like cash cows while overseas markets receive smoother onboarding and fairer terms. If NCSoft wants Aion 2 to survive its own success, management will need to offer more than token apologies and temporary item drops. Listening to the frustrated voices at home is no longer optional if they want their new global crown to avoid resting on a foundation of sand.
