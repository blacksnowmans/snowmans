Surviving September: Why South Korea's Retail Investors Are Fleeing to US Stocks

September 07, 2026

  • Failing momentum: Daily trading volume on South Korea's main stock exchange has plunged by half to about 25 trillion won ($18.5 billion), reflecting extreme investor fatigue and sidelined foreign capital.
  • The US exodus: Frustrated by the persistent "Korea Discount"—undervaluation of local equities—retail investors are aggressively dumping domestic exchange-traded funds (ETFs) to load up on US indices like the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100.
  • Dividend shift: High-dividend ETFs have pulled in about 600 billion won ($445 million) as investors pivot toward defensive income rather than chasing volatile capital gains.
  • The September curse: Historical market data shows September is statistically one of the weakest months for global equities, serving as a high-volatility bridge before late-year rallies.

Playing Defense: High-Dividend Strategies and Tax Breaks

When directionless markets drain your portfolio's energy, playing defense is the only rational move. South Korean investors are increasingly parking their cash in active money market ETFs and high-dividend products. Crucially, a new tax rule introduced for 2026 allows investors to choose separate taxation on dividend income at a capped rate of around 30%, sidestepping heavy progressive taxes on high earners. In a grinding sideways market, locking in a predictable tax advantage beats gambling on speculative momentum every single time.

The temptation to blindly trust central bank rate cuts as a guaranteed market rocket must also be resisted. History shows that rate cuts can sometimes act as a terrifying confirmation of looming economic slowdowns rather than a magical cure-all. As tech giants and major chaebols like Samsung and SK step up shareholder returns with buybacks and higher payouts, investors need to look past the political noise and focus entirely on fundamental earnings quality and solid balance sheets.

The Bottom Line on the September Slump

September is a month for patience, cash preservation, and cold calculation. The real danger isn't a temporary market correction—it is burning through all your liquidity before the real opportunities arrive in the fourth quarter. Keep dry powder, lean into dividend protection, and let the fair-weather speculators exhaust themselves while you wait out the storm.
