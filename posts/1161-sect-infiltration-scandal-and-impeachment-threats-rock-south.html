Sect Infiltration Scandal and Impeachment Threats Rock South Korea’s Ruling Party

October 15, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has been thrown into turmoil following internal findings that over 30,000 followers of the controversial religious sect Shincheonji may have systematically joined the party to sway primary elections.

  • Mass infiltration uncovered: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok confirmed the party obtained testimonies and evidence showing organized factional meddling by Shincheonji, prompting leadership to prepare formal requests for prosecutorial investigation.
  • Opposition goes on the attack: The conservative People Power Party, South Korea's main opposition, demanded an independent special counsel probe and search-and-seizure warrants for the ruling party’s complete membership rolls.
  • Judiciary in the crosshairs: Alongside the sect scandal, the Democratic Party leadership threatened to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae if he refuses to step down over alleged constitutional violations.
  • Factional warfare erupts: Accusations of leaked closed-door meetings and disputes over the veracity of the internal findings sparked bitter public clashes among Democratic Party supreme council members.

Sectarian Infiltration and Internal Fractures

Allegations that a fringe, secretive religious group manipulated primary contests strike at the very heart of the ruling party’s electoral legitimacy. Shincheonji, long viewed with deep suspicion across South Korean society, has repeatedly faced accusations of political meddling. But confirming more than 30,000 suspected sect members on the party rolls elevates the issue from rumor to an existential risk. Instead of presenting a unified front, the Democratic Party’s leadership fractured immediately. While hardliners like Supreme Council member Jung Chung-rae warned of a clandestine anti-leadership alliance tied to the sect, rival factions openly confronted leader Kim Min-seok over unauthorized leaks and unverified claims aired to the press.

This infighting arrives at a dangerous moment for the governing camp. With party approval ratings stalling, bitter recriminations over who leaked confidential strategy sessions threaten to paralyze party discipline. The opposition People Power Party has seized the opening, framing the revelations as proof of compromised primaries and pressing for legal seizures of party data.

High-Stakes Escalation Against the Courts

Rather than retreating, the Democratic Party leadership is doubling down on a high-stakes institutional fight by targeting Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. Warning that patience has run out, Kim Min-seok declared that impeachment is actively under review unless Cho resigns. The conservative opposition has decried the push as an assault on the constitutional separation of powers, but the ruling bloc is channeling growing frustration from progressive civic groups to justify a sweeping overhaul of the judicial branch.

To prevent the dual crises from alienating ordinary voters, the party attempted to counterprogram with pragmatic governance—announcing expanded free cervical cancer vaccinations for women aged 18 to 26 and touting regional infrastructure projects. Yet pocketbook policies will do little to break through the headlines so long as the ruling party remains consumed by sectarian infiltration claims and constitutional showdowns with the bench.
