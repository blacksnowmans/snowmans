South Korea’s Assembly Prepares for Battle Over President Lee’s $610 Billion Budget

November 02, 2026

Following the conclusion of annual parliamentary audits, South Korea’s National Assembly has entered a high-stakes standoff as the ruling Democratic Party fights to pass President Lee Jae-myung’s record 821 trillion won (about $610 billion) budget proposal against aggressive opposition demands for deep spending cuts.

  • What happened: The Democratic Party of Korea (DP), the country's ruling party, is pushing to defend a 821 trillion won fiscal plan for next year, while the main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), demands spending cuts totaling 134 trillion won (about $100 billion).
  • Key budget priorities: The Lee Jae-myung administration's spending plan includes a 162 trillion won ($120 billion) Future Response Fund for AI, biotech, and green energy, the restoration of scientific R&D funding stripped during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure, expanded state funding for local fire services, and increased public housing.
  • Parallel legislative push: Alongside budget reviews, the DP is prioritizing economic and social welfare bills, including mandatory tender offer requirements during corporate M&As to protect retail stock investors, and realigning local tax allocations.
  • What comes next: President Lee will deliver a policy address to parliament ahead of legislative committee reviews, facing a statutory deadline of December 2 to pass the budget.

Fiscal Expansion vs. Austerity Politics

The core of the battle in Yeouido is a fundamental clash over economic strategy. The Lee administration views fiscal expansion as essential momentum to kickstart sluggish domestic growth and build long-term industrial competitiveness. By restoring scientific research and development funds that were severely cut during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure, the DP argues that state investment is non-negotiable if South Korea wants to maintain a competitive edge in strategic tech sectors.

Conversely, the opposition PPP has framed the spending plan as an irresponsible "blank check" that relies heavily on national debt. However, demanding a massive 134 trillion won reduction—a figure that would require gutting major infrastructure and social safety programs—appears more aimed at hampering the Lee administration’s core political agenda than offering a realistic fiscal alternative. With private spending muted, fiscal austerity risks deepening the economic slowdown.

Beyond Budget Numbers: Market Reforms and Policy Momentum

The ruling party is using the fiscal battle to advance broader policy and market reforms. High on the agenda is introducing mandatory public purchase rules for corporate takeovers, designed to protect minority shareholders from losing value during major M&As. By targeting systemic issues responsible for the persistent "Korea Discount" in financial markets, the DP intends to demonstrate that fiscal investment and structural market modernizations go hand in hand.

As President Lee takes the podium for his policy speech, the political stakes could not be higher. How the ruling party navigates the PPP's wall of opposition before December 2 will serve as a crucial test of President Lee's governing strength and set the trajectory for his economic reform drive.
