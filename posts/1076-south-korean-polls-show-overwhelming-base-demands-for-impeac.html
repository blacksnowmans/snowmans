South Korean Polls Show Overwhelming Base Demands for Impeachment, Exposing Lawmakers' Hesitation

October 13, 2026

New polling data publicized by progressive lawmakers reveals that a solid majority of South Koreans—and more than 85% of progressive voters—support aggressive impeachment proceedings, highlighting a growing disconnect between public sentiment and a hesitant legislature.

  • Clear majority support: Recent survey data broadcast by political platform Park Si-young TV indicates that 55.4% of respondents back impeachment in live phone interviews (CATI), with 40.9% opposed; automated response (ARS) polling showed 50.8% in favor against 44.9% opposed.
  • Overwhelming party base backing: Representative Shin Jang-sik of the Rebuilding Korea Party, a progressive reformist party, noted that impeachment backing among supporters of both the ruling Democratic Party of Korea and the Rebuilding Korea Party reaches between 85.5% and 86.5%.
  • Judicial accountability: The general figures match broad public approval (55.4%) for the potential impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, pointing toward demands for deeper institutional reform.
  • Legislative inertia: Despite decisive polling, politicians in the National Assembly continue to hesitate, cautious about blowback and swayed by conservative local power brokers rather than the broader electorate.

The Disconnect Between Yeouido and the Grassroots

The gap between the street and Yeouido—South Korea's legislative district—stems largely from the insular circles lawmakers inhabit. While general sentiment clearly tilts in favor of decisive institutional cleanups, legislators frequently take their cues from local community leaders and traditional neighborhood notables who skew conservative and risk-averse. By mistaking the caution of these vocal local influencers for the broader national consensus, politicians are paralyzing their own reform agendas.

This dynamic creates a dangerous blind spot. While lawmakers fret over whether moving forward will trigger a political backlash, public data demonstrates that the political center has already shifted. With public support clearing absolute majorities across both interview and automated polling methods, legislative foot-dragging reflects an outdated political calculation rather than genuine public caution.

The Clock Ticking on Institutional Reform

For the ruling Democratic Party and its reformist allies, stalling risks alienating the very voters who brought about change. Although Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol was removed from office following his impeachment and is currently serving a prison sentence, his tenure left deep institutional fallout that the public expects the government of President Lee Jae-myung to dismantle decisively.

Reform windows in South Korean politics are notoriously brief. Core party loyalists view the 85% approval threshold not as a point for compromise, but as a direct mandate to act against holdovers of the previous administration. If progressive lawmakers continue to waver out of institutional timidity, they risk squandering their political capital and deflating the momentum required to carry out long-term democratic reforms.
