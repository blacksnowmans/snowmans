Ruling Party and President Lee Face Deepening Polls Slump in South Korea

November 20, 2026

According to a new poll by South Korean news outlet News Tomato, a majority of voters want ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) leader Kim Min-seok to resign over a religious-group controversy, as President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating slides to 36.9%.

  • Backlash in the ruling party's heartland: In the News Tomato survey, 51.6% of respondents said Kim should step down over controversies linked to Shincheonji, a controversial religious sect—including 44.7% in Honam, the southwestern region that serves as the DPK's traditional stronghold.
  • Opposition overtakes the ruling party: Support for the main conservative opposition People Power Party rose 1.7 percentage points to 39.4%, pulling five points ahead of the DPK, which fell 3.2 points to 34.4%.
  • Hardening presidential disapproval: President Lee’s positive rating dropped 3.7 points over two weeks to 36.9%, while negative sentiment reached 59.2%, with 47.6% saying he is doing "very poorly" compared to just 13% saying "very well."
  • Fractures over discipline and property taxes: Voters split on how to punish DPK lawmaker Choi Min-hee (33.5% favored expulsion, while a combined 35.1% favored a warning or no penalty), and a proposal to raise the comprehensive real estate holding tax exemption for non-resident single-homeowners to about $1 million (1.4 billion won) drew 43.8% opposition nationwide—yet found majority backing among DPK supporters.

Why the Numbers Point to a Structural Squeeze

The polling drop reflects more than a passing scandal, exposing structural vulnerabilities in South Korea’s single five-year presidential system. Commentators on the political channel Park Si-young TV note that while Lee maintained strong momentum as governor of Gyeonggi Province—where re-election prospects keep local bureaucrats aligned—a single-term president faces immediate lame-duck pressures once approval slips into the 30s. With nearly half the electorate firmly opposed, rebuilding a governing majority in public opinion becomes steep.

Communication style and coalition politics are compounding the slide. Analysts cited on Park Si-young TV argue that Lee’s sharp posts on X and public rebukes of cabinet ministers have projected an image of anger and rigidity to swing voters. Meanwhile, the real estate tax divide highlights an identity clash inside the ruling camp: while traditional progressives oppose tax cuts for non-resident homeowners, a newer pro-Lee faction—described by commentators as driven more by opposition to former President Moon Jae-in and personal economic interests—is pushing against the government's own policy line.

South Korea’s past single-term presidents, including Kim Young-sam, Kim Dae-jung, and Roh Moo-hyun, all struggled when waning support and party infighting collided with rigid leadership. Without addressing core-supporter fatigue in Honam and recalibrating both governing tone and internal party discipline, the Lee administration and DPK leadership risk further policy paralysis as the country heads into upcoming political tests.
