Why the Recent Market Slump is Giving Way to a New Liquidity Rally

September 01, 2026

Recent market pullbacks driven by forced selling and exaggerated pessimism are clearing the way for a major liquidity-fuelled rebound.

    What happened: Markets dipped in June and July due to a mix of heavy selling by the National Pension Service (South Korea's state pension fund), speculative single-stock leverage ETFs, and overblown doubts about the semiconductor sector. What it changes: With those temporary supply-and-demand bottlenecks now resolving, attention is shifting to incoming macroeconomic liquidity. Who is affected: Global retail and institutional investors positioning for risk-on assets ahead of upcoming political and monetary catalysts.

The Real Engines Driving the Next Market Phase

Much of the recent anxiety centred on geopolitics, oil prices, and rising bond yields. However, as political milestones—including US midterm elections and upcoming diplomatic summits—approach, uncertainty is giving way to clarity. Historically, markets treat the resolution of uncertainty as a positive catalyst, clearing the path for aggressive capital to re-enter risk assets.

Crucially, the next phase of the pivot era extends beyond central bank interest rate cuts. The more powerful driver will be government fiscal policy and the deployment of substantial liquidity into the economy. Rather than signalling broken fundamentals, the recent semiconductor sell-off and market jitters reflect short-term fatigue. Investors who mistake temporary noise for structural decline risk missing out as the broader liquidity trend reasserts itself.
