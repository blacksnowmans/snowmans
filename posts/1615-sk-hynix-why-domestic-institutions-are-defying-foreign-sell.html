SK Hynix: Why Domestic Institutions Are Defying Foreign Sell-offs

October 28, 2026

Despite heavy, multi-day sell-offs by foreign investors, SK Hynix—South Korea’s second-largest chipmaker and a global leader in high-bandwidth memory (HBM)—has shown remarkable price resilience, signaling a shift in market control from foreign day traders to domestic institutional investors.

  • What happened: Foreign investors have been aggressively offloading shares, yet SK Hynix’s stock has found a firm floor and rebounded 15% from its October lows.
  • The shift: Domestic institutions, including pension funds, have stepped in to absorb the foreign sell-off, creating a “cement floor” that prevents the stock from collapsing.
  • The catalyst: Beyond technical support levels, the company recently posted record-breaking quarterly revenue of roughly $75 billion (104 trillion KRW) and a massive 79.7% operating margin, bolstered by insatiable AI demand.
  • What’s next: Analysts suggest that as the stock tests key moving averages, individual investors risk getting caught in "chasing" behavior; a disciplined exit strategy is being advised as institutional "smart money" looks to sell into future retail-driven rallies.

The Mechanics of the Rebound

The recent market volatility saw SK Hynix drop 26% in just one week in mid-October, a period of intense panic that cleared out many retail positions. However, the bounce back was not accidental. By analyzing Fibonacci retracement levels, it becomes clear that the stock hit a critical technical "buy zone." The current rally is not merely a technical correction; it is a fundamental revaluation backed by the company's dominance in the AI hardware supply chain. With AI data centers expanding globally, SK Hynix’s HBM—the essential memory component for AI chips—remains in high demand, securing its long-term growth trajectory.

What differentiates this movement from previous market cycles is the role of the "lead buyer." In the past, foreign outflows triggered panic selling among retail investors (often referred to as "ants" in Korea). This time, domestic institutions have methodically accumulated shares, effectively neutralizing foreign selling pressure. By keeping the stock price stable during the sell-off, these institutions have built a foundation for a more sustainable upward move. They are not buying for short-term gains; they are positioning for a significant valuation re-rating.

Avoiding the Trap of Retail Hype

As the stock approaches key resistance levels, market observers warn of an impending shift in sentiment. When the stock breaks through its 20-day and 60-day moving averages, media coverage will likely turn bullish, and individual investors will likely rush back into the market. This is the danger zone. Institutional players who accumulated shares at the bottom are not looking to hold forever; they are waiting for that surge of retail liquidity to exit their positions.

For investors, the lesson is clear: do not mistake the coming enthusiasm for a new secular bull market. Smart money is currently operating in the shadows, taking advantage of the market's quiet accumulation phase. The key to winning in this environment is not to join the crowd when the news cycle turns positive, but to set a clear target price and execute an exit before the inevitable "euphoria" phase peaks. In this game of psychological warfare, the most profitable exit is often the one made while others are still cheering.
