South Korea’s Culture Wars Hit the Screen: Far-Right Trolls Target Top Stars and Broadcasters

October 07, 2026

South Korea’s vibrant pop culture scene is increasingly being hijacked by fringe far-right groups waging online witch hunts against the country’s biggest celebrities and filmmakers over imagined ideological offences.

  • What happened: Far-right YouTubers and online communities have launched coordinated smear campaigns and review-bombing efforts against top actors like Lee Min-ho, veteran TV host Yu Jae-suk, and pop star IU, branding them as leftists or foreign agents.
  • The flashpoint: The trigger was Lee Min-ho joining the cast of the new historical thriller Assassins, which deals with the 1974 assassination of first lady Yuk Young-soo, drawing fierce backlash from critics who claimed the film distorts history.
  • The backlash: Despite attempts to tank the film's ratings with macro-driven review bombing on portals like Naver—prompting police investigations—the movie has crossed 2 million viewers, proving that internet mobs hold little sway over mainstream audiences.
  • Who is affected: A-list celebrities and creators find their normal artistic output subjected to absurd conspiracy theories and ideological purity tests by vocal minority groups hiding behind keyboards.

Ideological Purity Tests Target Korea’s Biggest Stars

The latest wave of outrage centers on global star Lee Min-ho’s involvement in the film Assassins, directed by Hur Jin-ho and starring veteran actors like Yoo Hae-jin and Park Hae-il. When Lee mentioned in promotional interviews that the central theme drawing him to the project was "freedom and truth"—concepts that far-right internet commentators have long tried to claim as their exclusive political branding—it triggered a meltdown among fringe groups. Accustomed to targeting liberal-leaning actors like Jung Woo-sung, these online trolls quickly added Lee and his co-stars to their hit lists, throwing around baseless accusations of leftist bias and threatening boycotts.

Similar outrage was directed at Yu Jae-suk, affectionately known as South Korea's "National MC." When Lee Min-ho appeared on Yu’s popular variety show You Quiz on the Block, commentators immediately flooded message boards with malicious comments, ludicrously labeling Yu a "hardcore leftist" simply for doing his job. These attacks mirror past campaigns where public figures were harassed over minor details like wearing blue clothing on election day or wearing face masks. Critics point out the staggering hypocrisy: the same fringe commentators who loudly champion hyper-capitalist meritocracy are quick to rage when entertainers successfully accumulate wealth through genuine talent and hard work.

The Echo Chamber of Online Mobs

The phenomenon reveals a desperate strategy by extremist groups to manufacture outrage where none exists. By deploying automated review-bombing—dropping scores on platforms like Naver while the film enjoys high satisfaction ratings elsewhere—these fringe actors attempt to punch above their weight. Yet, their manufactured controversies consistently backfire, serving mostly to drum up public curiosity and expose the insular nature of their echo chambers. As celebrities like IU take firm legal action against malicious cyberbullying and defamation, the public has made it abundantly clear that they have zero patience for toxic culture-war policing masking as political critique.
