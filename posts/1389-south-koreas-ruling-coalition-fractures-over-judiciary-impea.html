South Korea’s Ruling Coalition Fractures Over Judiciary Impeachment and Economic Policy

October 22, 2026

Friction within South Korea’s governing alliance erupted into open conflict as the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party pushed to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae while slamming the ruling Democratic Party’s policy retreats on labor and taxation.

  • Impeachment push: The Rebuilding Korea Party delivered a formal impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae to the ruling Democratic Party, alleging judicial election interference, though Democratic Party leadership favors a preliminary parliamentary investigation to avoid backlash.
  • Audit witness standoff: The ruling Democratic Party unilaterally approved the witness list for the presidential office audit, excluding key senior aides to President Lee Jae-myung despite furious protests from the main conservative opposition People Power Party.
  • Policy clashes: Cho Kuk, leader of the Rebuilding Korea Party, publicly attacked the Democratic Party for attempting to delay cryptocurrency taxation and grant exceptions to the national 52-hour workweek cap in regional development zones.
  • Presidential maneuvers: President Lee Jae-myung inspected a major public housing construction site and a traditional market in Incheon, seeking to keep public focus on housing supply and inflation amid escalating political battles in the National Assembly.

Judicial Escalation and Audit Warfare

The drive to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae marks a sharp escalation in the battle over judicial power. While the Rebuilding Korea Party is demanding swift action before the end of October, the ruling Democratic Party is treading carefully. Fearing potential political backlash if procedural justification falls short, Democratic Party floor leader Han Byung-do indicated the party will prioritize a formal parliamentary inquiry after the current audit season before contemplating impeachment. Meanwhile, the main conservative opposition People Power Party condemned the maneuver as a blatant attempt to intimidate the judiciary and tame the courts.

Tensions boiled over further in the National Assembly’s Steering Committee, where the Democratic Party used its majority to pass a presidential audit witness list that omitted top presidential staffers, including Senior Secretary for Political Affairs Kang Hoon-sik and Policy Chief Kim Yong-beom. Opposition lawmakers accused the ruling party of intentionally shielding key administration officials from public accountability, reducing policy oversight to shouting matches.

Cracks in the Progressive Alliance

Beyond partisan warfare with the conservative opposition, the ruling party is facing severe criticism from its left flank. Rebuilding Korea Party leader Cho Kuk explicitly denounced the Democratic Party’s recent legislative proposals, particularly its push to postpone cryptocurrency taxation and relax the 52-hour weekly work cap for mega-regional economic zones. Cho argued that sacrificing tax principles and labor protections for short-term political expediency betrays core reform values, exposing an ideological divide within the broader governing coalition.

President Lee Jae-myung sought to project a focus on governance amid the noise, visiting a public housing development in Incheon to pledge faster construction timelines and chatting with small merchants at a local market. Yet, governance risks persist. Concerns raised by civic groups over executive influence on the new Serious Corruption Investigation Office show that structural debates over law enforcement and the judiciary will continue to shadow the administration's economic messaging.
