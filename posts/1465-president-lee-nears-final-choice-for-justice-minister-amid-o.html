President Lee Nears Final Choice for Justice Minister Amid Opposition Outcry

October 26, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung is set to appoint a new Minister of Justice from a shortlist of two loyalist lawmakers, signaling a major push to overhaul the state prosecution and reverse legal actions taken during the previous administration.

  • Finalists Selected: Park Kyun-taek and Yang Bu-nam, both first-term lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), have emerged as the final candidates to lead the Ministry of Justice after a two-month vacancy.
  • Loyalist Profiles: Both candidates are former high-ranking prosecutors who served as key legal advisors to President Lee, defending him against investigations during the "prosecutorial republic" era of the now-imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • Reform Agenda: The appointments are designed to dismantle the influence of elite prosecutorial circles and potentially withdraw charges in cases the current administration deems "politically fabricated."
  • Fierce Opposition: The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition party, has slammed the move as a "bulletproof" appointment intended to erase the President's past legal liabilities.

A Strategic Strike Against the "Prosecution Cartel"

The selection of Park and Yang is a calculated move to place seasoned insiders at the helm of judicial reform. Both candidates reached the rank of High Prosecutors' Office Chief—one of the highest positions in the service—but notably hail from non-Seoul National University backgrounds. In the hierarchy-heavy world of Korean law, this is a pointed challenge to the "elite" cartels that the DPK argues have historically weaponized the law for political ends.

By choosing "Pro-Lee" fighters who personally witnessed and defended against the previous administration's investigations, the President is signaling that the Ministry of Justice will no longer act as a passive observer. Instead, it will likely take an active role in the "Special Act on Fabricated Prosecutions," a legislative push by the DPK to punish prosecutors who allegedly abused their power during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure. For the DPK, this isn't just an appointment; it is a counter-offensive meant to restore what they call "judicial justice."

The Opposition’s "Bulletproof" Allegations

The People Power Party’s reaction has been predictably explosive. Lawmakers like Na Kyung-won have accused the President of appointing his own "defense attorneys" to lead the ministry that oversees the nation’s legal system. The PPP argues that the ultimate goal of either Park or Yang would be to exercise the Minister's authority to drop ongoing cases or invalidate prior indictments against the President and his allies. They have framed the upcoming confirmation hearings as a "battle for the rule of law."

However, the administration views this "bulletproof" framing as a desperate attempt by the opposition to protect the remnants of the old prosecutorial system. From the government's perspective, dropping charges in "fabricated" cases is not about granting favors, but about "correcting the record." As the DPK leadership recently stated, the new Minister must be someone capable of performing duties that meet "the public's eye level"—a euphemism for clearing the names of those they believe were wrongly targeted by Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s "political prosecutors."

The upcoming confirmation hearings will be a flashpoint for the Lee administration. If appointed, either Park or Yang will likely move quickly to separate the prosecution's investigative and indictment powers, a long-held goal of the Korean liberal wing that would fundamentally change how justice is administered in the country.
