SK Hynix Crushes Peak-Cycle Fears with Historic Q3 Surge and AI Memory Sold Out Through 2029

October 29, 2026

South Korean memory giant SK Hynix has posted staggering third-quarter results, topping 100 trillion won (roughly $72 billion) in quarterly revenue with an unprecedented 80% operating profit margin, forcefully dismantling market speculation that the AI hardware boom has peaked.

  • Record-breaking financials: SK Hynix logged a quarterly operating profit of 83 trillion won (about $60 billion)—extending its streak of record-breaking earnings to six consecutive quarters as global AI memory demand outpaces supply by more than two to one.
  • Locked-in order books: The company confirmed during its earnings call that its DRAM and High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) capacity is effectively sold out through 2029 under binding long-term supply agreements (LTAs).
  • Twin-engine profitability: Strong pricing power extends beyond advanced HBM4 chips to legacy commodity DRAM, where unit prices have crossed $2, prompting the company to announce tens of trillions of won in shareholder returns over the next three years.
  • Market ripple effect: SK Hynix shares jumped over 11%, sparking a broader rally across Asian and US semiconductor peers, including Micron and SanDisk.

The Toll Booth for the Global AI Boom

SK Hynix's results signal a fundamental transformation in how memory makers operate. By securing multi-year supply contracts that lock in shipments through 2029, the company has insulated itself from the brutal boom-and-bust cycles that traditionally defined commodity DRAM manufacturing. Wall Street analysts now increasingly view the company less like a cyclical component vendor and more like an essential toll booth for global artificial intelligence infrastructure, commanding massive premiums from clients like Nvidia who face severe chip bottlenecks.

Furthermore, the rally is no longer purely speculative. SK Hynix's massive cash generation has translated immediately into substantial shareholder return pledges, reinforcing investor confidence. Global investment banks, including Goldman Sachs, forecast that average selling prices for HBM could surge another 122% next year, arguing that this upward supercycle is well-positioned to continue comfortably through at least 2028.

Structural Deficit vs. Macro Vulnerabilities

Major research houses, including Wolfe Research and RBC Capital, point out that incoming demand from autonomous driving data centers and humanoid robotics will dwarf current consumption levels, potentially extending structural shortages into 2030. SK Hynix’s aggressive pivot toward long-term agreements represents a calculated strategy to avert past price-war missteps and lock in profit floors before competitor supply catches up.

Nonetheless, an 80% operating margin also highlights an overheated, undersupplied market. External shocks—such as prolonged high interest rates from the US Federal Reserve or broader supply chain bottlenecks—could still weigh on sentiment. But with three full years of inventory legally spoken for, SK Hynix holds an unprecedented cushion against near-term macro volatility.
