Inside the Democratic Party's Strategy: Leader Kim Min-seok Tops Polls Amid Grilling Season

October 09, 2026

    What happened: The ruling Democratic Party balanced internal discipline, grassroots agricultural visits, and fierce parliamentary clashes during the October 8th parliamentary audit sessions, while party leader Kim Min-seok took the top spot in a presidential preference poll at 18.1%. What it changes: The party is attempting to shift away from chaotic infighting and project competence as a governing force, clamping down on hardliners while defending historical memory against conservative opposition bills. Who is affected: Farmers awaiting farmland inspection revisions, lawmakers caught in parliamentary committee shouting matches, and the ruling party leadership trying to hold onto political momentum ahead of the post-audit phase.

South Korea’s political landscape is currently dominated by the annual parliamentary audits, where the ruling Democratic Party—the progressive party supporting President Lee Jae-myung—finds itself managing a dual front: maintaining legislative discipline at home while pushing back against the conservative People Power Party.

Party leader Kim Min-seok made headlines by reining in party hardliners, urging moderation to prevent the party from losing its grip on the political narrative. Alongside internal management, Kim hit the agricultural field to inspect rice price stabilization policies and reassure farmers caught up in farmland ownership investigations. This push to address practical livelihood issues coincides with a fresh poll by Korea Information Research showing Kim leading presidential preference figures at 18.1%, setting up a three-way race alongside conservative figures.

Defending History and Battling in Committees

Meanwhile, the legislative battlegrounds saw high tension. Floor Leader Han Byung-do drew a hard line against a People Power Party proposal to disclose the names of those honored for the 1980 Gwangju Democratization Movement (5·18), denouncing it as secondary victimization and an attempt to erase history. In parliamentary committees like the Legislation and Judiciary Committee, exchanges descended into shouting matches over prosecutorial policies, judicial appointments, and attacks on the judiciary.

Beyond the capital's shouting matches, local councils and regional organizations—from Busan to Gyeonggi Province—are quietly pushing livelihood ordinances and local budgets. While media headlines focus on high-stakes political bickering, the Democratic Party’s long-term success will hinge on whether these grassroots efforts and upcoming post-audit blueprints can prove the party's practical competence to the public.
