The End of the PPT Era: Why Professionals are Switching to Web-Based Slides

September 07, 2026

South Korean corporate professionals are increasingly ditching traditional PowerPoint presentations in favor of HTML-based slides to eliminate formatting errors and improve visual consistency across devices.

  • The Shift: Seasoned employees are moving away from heavy, static PPT files to lightweight HTML code that renders perfectly in any web browser.
  • Key Advantages: HTML slides solve the age-old problem of font and layout corruption when opening files on different computers.
  • AI Integration: Modern AI tools like Claude (an AI chatbot developed by Anthropic) are being used to generate code, animations, and responsive designs that adapt to any screen size.
  • The Catch: While efficient, web-based presentations face significant hurdles regarding offline accessibility and internal data security.

From Static Slides to Interactive Experiences

For years, the "PPT tax"—the time spent wrestling with broken fonts, bloated file sizes, and compatibility issues—has been a staple of the Korean office experience. By shifting to HTML, workers are finding that their presentations are no longer just "slides," but interactive web experiences. These files are significantly smaller and can be distributed via simple links, ensuring the audience sees the design exactly as the presenter intended.

Beyond convenience, web technology offers superior data visualization. Unlike static images in a PPT that require manual re-editing, HTML slides can be programmed to update dynamically. Features like hover-effects and responsive layouts allow the audience to interact with the data, turning a passive lecture into an engaging session.

Strategic Implementation and Security Risks

The transition is not without its challenges. Because these slides rely on browsers, they require a stable internet connection unless local, offline frameworks are established. More importantly, South Korean firms—known for their rigid internal security protocols—must be wary. Any content based on a web browser can theoretically be vulnerable to external threats, making them a risky choice for meetings involving sensitive, confidential data.

Ultimately, the tool is only as good as the strategy behind it. While HTML slides provide a professional edge, they should be used with a degree of flexibility. For urgent, offline, or highly sensitive corporate environments, traditional software may still hold the advantage. The goal for the modern professional shouldn't be to obsess over the newest tool, but to master the art of conveying a message clearly, whether through a slide deck or a line of code.
