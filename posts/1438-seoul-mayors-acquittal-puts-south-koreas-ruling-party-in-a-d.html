Seoul Mayor's Acquittal Puts South Korea's Ruling Party in a Delicate Judicial Bind

October 25, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is walking a political tightrope after an appellate court acquitted conservative Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, prompting party leaders to pursue a parliamentary inquiry into the judiciary while actively resisting hardline calls for immediate judicial impeachments.

  • Court clears Seoul mayor: An appeals court overturned lower court scrutiny to acquit Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, drawing sharp backlash from the ruling DPK, which accused the bench of being a "vending machine of acquittals" for elite politicians and urged prosecutors to appeal.
  • Calibrated reform strategy: DPK leader Kim Min-seok pushed back against calls from within the party to immediately impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, opting instead to launch a formal National Assembly investigation into judicial bias.
  • Internal sect probe friction: The ruling party struggled with internal disagreements over releasing the findings of an internal inquiry into suspected mass enrollment by Shincheonji, a controversial religious movement, while rejecting the opposition's demands for an independent counsel.
  • Bipartisan budget deal: Despite bitter clashes during annual committee audits, lawmakers reached an agreement on a 26.2 trillion won (about $19.5 billion) supplementary budget aimed at easing high inflation and supporting low-income households.

Strategic Restraint on the Bench

The acquittal of Mayor Oh handed the DPK a volatile dilemma: how to satisfy an enraged party base without triggering accusations of institutional overreach. While hardliners demanded an immediate impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho, party leader Kim Min-seok chose a more deliberate path. Pushing an aggressive judicial impeachment without established procedural consensus could easily backfire, fueling conservative claims of judicial intimidation. By placing a parliamentary investigation first, the leadership is attempting to build public evidence of systemic bias on the record before determining whether to pull the impeachment trigger.

Navigating Party Integrity and Economic Relief

At the same time, the DPK is managing internal fissures over an investigation into whether Shincheonji members infiltrated the party rolls. Disagreements over how and when to make the findings public spilled into leadership meetings, as lawmakers weighed the risk of a political witch hunt against accusations of a cover-up. The party rejected calls from the main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), for an independent special prosecutor, arguing that the PPP is merely deflecting from its own past relationships with controversial religious figures during the tenure of Yoon Suk-yeol.

Crucially, the ruling party demonstrated that legislative gridlock will not entirely stall pocketbook priorities. By locking down the $19.5 billion extra budget alongside regional development plans, the DPK demonstrated an ability to compartmentalize bitter legal battles from everyday economic stewardship.
