Korea’s Housing Market Flashes Foreclosure Warnings as Shamanic Rumors Obscure Systemic Failures

October 30, 2026

South Korea’s real estate auction market is sounding urgent alarms as rising interest rates trigger a wave of foreclosures among overleveraged households, even as public attention remains vulnerable to bizarre conspiracy theories lingering from past political scandals.

  • Vulnerable housing falls first: Court-mandated property auctions are surging, led by non-apartment residential units such as villas (low-rise multi-family residences) and officetels (compact live-work studio units), where buyers relied on heavy borrowing.
  • Debunking real estate orthodoxy: Rising jeonse rates (Korea’s unique lump-sum lease deposit system) are failing to lift property sales because squeezed renters lack the liquidity to buy, demonstrated by high-demand districts like Seongnam’s Bundang, which saw property values rise despite lease-to-price ratios sitting below 40%.
  • Itaewon cult rumors debunked: An on-site investigation by producer Choi Seo-young revealed that viral allegations concerning a "Tenrikyo priest" conducting occult rituals at the 2022 Itaewon disaster site were entirely baseless, involving merely an ordinary citizen offering personal prayers.
  • Pressure on the administration: Calls are mounting for President Lee Jae-myung’s administration to introduce debt restructuring and expand public housing safeguards for defaulting homeowners rather than offering short-term deregulation.

The Canary in the Real Estate Coal Mine

The spike in court auctions is the predictable fallout of rapid monetary tightening colliding with years of reckless borrowing. During the low-rate boom, retail buyers stretched their finances to the limit—a phenomenon known locally as yeong-kkeul, or "dragging together every last bit of soul" to buy property. Because non-apartment housing units often carry significantly higher loan-to-value ratios than standard apartments, their owners are the first to default when monthly interest payments jump. If court auctions begin inundating the standard apartment sector—the core pillar of South Korean household wealth—the broader economy will face a severe contraction.

Market commentators who claim that higher rental costs will inevitably force tenants to purchase homes are ignoring current purchasing power. With the price-to-income ratio at historic highs, renters cannot bridge the capital gap simply because their leases have become more expensive. Relying on outdated market narratives risks luring more vulnerable households into debt traps at precisely the wrong moment.

How Distrust Feeds the Occult Framing

The bizarre speed with which the public embraced rumors of Japanese religious rituals at the Itaewon disaster scene highlights a lingering societal trauma. During Yoon Suk-yeol’s time in office, state affairs were frequently clouded by allegations involving shamans like Geonjin Beopsa and unverified spiritual advisors. That institutional opacity conditioned the public to suspect esoteric conspiracies behind national tragedies, culminating in the climate that preceded Yoon's eventual impeachment and imprisonment.

Fact-checking the rumor revealed that the individual at the site had no institutional ties, harbored deep antipathy toward the former leadership, and was merely mourning the victims. Sensation-driven gossip ultimately diverts attention from structural breakdowns: state negligence, crowd-control failures, and the absence of clear emergency chains of command. Moving past superstition toward verifiable accountability is essential for both housing stability and civic recovery.
