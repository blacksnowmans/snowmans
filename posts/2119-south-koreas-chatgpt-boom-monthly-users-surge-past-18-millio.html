South Korea’s ChatGPT Boom: Monthly Users Surge Past 18 Million as AI Deepens Its Grip

November 09, 2026

Monthly active users of OpenAI's ChatGPT in South Korea have crossed 18 million for the first time, cementing artificial intelligence as an essential daily utility across the nation's workforce.

  • Explosive user growth: Monthly mobile users jumped 44% year-over-year to reach as high as 18.76 million, meaning more than one in three South Koreans now actively use ChatGPT.
  • Deeper daily engagement: Average time spent on the app doubled over the past year, reflecting a shift from simple search queries to complex workflow integration like coding and document analysis.
  • Rising competitor: Anthropic’s Claude recorded a 15-fold increase in Korean users over the past year to secure second place, though ChatGPT retains a commanding 91% market share.
  • Security and sovereignty risks: Mass adoption has brought widespread data exposure—ranging from corporate trade secrets to cryptocurrency seed phrases—while foreign platforms dominate local tech infrastructure.

From Everyday Utility to Security Blind Spots

Data from mobile analytics platforms marks a fundamental paradigm shift in South Korean daily life. AI is no longer a novelty tool; it has become basic workplace infrastructure. Office workers and professionals routinely use ChatGPT to draft reports, debug software, and parse complex legal contracts. However, this surge in usage is closely tied to intense productivity pressures in South Korea's tight labor market, where failing to adopt AI tools is increasingly viewed as a professional handicap.

This rapid integration has exposed serious security blind spots. Security experts warn that users regularly paste sensitive corporate source code, internal client lists, and even cryptocurrency wallet recovery keys directly into prompts. Because default platform settings use input data to train underlying models—and public chat links can easily expose conversations—vital corporate assets and personal financial data are flowing unchecked to overseas servers.

Monopoly Dynamics and Tech Sovereignty

While Anthropic’s Claude has gained significant ground among developers for its advanced coding capabilities and natural writing style, breaking OpenAI’s dominance remains an uphill battle. OpenAI’s ecosystem lock-in keeps its market share near 91%, leaving South Korean tech firms struggling to keep pace against capital-heavy U.S. tech giants.

This market imbalance has elevated broader concerns over digital sovereignty. In response, the Lee Jae-myung administration has begun pushing policy initiatives aimed at recovering data sovereignty and building national AI capabilities. However, unless local tech ecosystems can offer viable alternatives, South Korea risks long-term dependency on foreign platforms for its core knowledge infrastructure.
