Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon Faces High-Stakes Appeal Over Illegal Polling Fees

October 04, 2026

  • The core issue: Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon is awaiting a crucial appellate court ruling in late November regarding allegations that illegal polling fees were paid on his behalf during a previous election.
  • The political fallout: In the first trial, Oh was fined 10 million won (about $7,000) and ordered to forfeit 21 million won (about $15,000)—a sentence that would strip him of his mayoral office if upheld.
  • The defense crumbles: Oh's legal team has introduced selectively edited call transcripts involving a political broker to argue he knew nothing of the payments, but prosecutors have dismissed the evidence as unreliable and key conservative figures have refused to back his narrative.
  • Broader instability: Alongside the criminal trial, local political circles are roiled by challenges to past election procedures, raising the specter of a disruptive mayoral by-election in the South Korean capital.

A Desperate Legal Defense and the Myth of Ignorance

South Korean politics has long played out like a high-stakes courtroom drama, but few cases carry as much immediate peril for a major officeholder as the current legal battle surrounding Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon. A prominent conservative politician belonging to the People Power Party—South Korea's main conservative party—Oh now finds himself fighting for his political life as his appellate trial reaches its final stages. A first-instance court already handed down a fine heavy enough to trigger a mayoral vacuum, ruling that Oh was directly aware of illegal polling operations conducted by political brokers during his campaign.

To overturn that verdict, Oh's camp has pinned its hopes on a newly submitted 2021 telephone transcript, attempting to paint the mayor as an oblivious bystander who never authorized the polling fees. Yet this defense looks increasingly threadbare. Prosecutors have pointed out that the defense cherry-picked only a handful of favorable clips from a vast archive of recordings, while veterans of the conservative bloc have declined to corroborate Oh's claims of total ignorance. For an experienced politician to plead complete unawareness while utilizing the fruits of disputed polling data strikes many observers as a hollow evasion of accountability.

The Cost of Judicial Brinkmanship for Seoul

At its heart, this scandal exposes the murky underbelly of South Korean electoral politics, where ambitious leaders lean on unregulated fixers to shape public opinion while dodging the paper trail. The first-instance judges drew a firm red line by ruling that willfully bypassing campaign finance laws carries severe consequences, and the independent special counsel has steadfastly demanded that the original penalties remain intact. If the appellate court and ultimately the Supreme Court uphold the conviction, Seoul will be plunged into the chaos of an unwanted by-election, forcing citizens to pay the administrative and financial price for political malpractice.

Elected officials in democratic capitals are meant to govern public infrastructure and civic life, not treat city hall as a bunker to outlast judicial scrutiny. As November's verdict approaches, the trial serves as a vital test of whether South Korea's legal system can hold powerful executives accountable, regardless of their political stature or how aggressively their defense tries to shuffle the deck.
