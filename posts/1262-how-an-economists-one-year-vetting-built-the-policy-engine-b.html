How an Economist’s One-Year Vetting Built the Policy Engine Behind President Lee Jae-myung

October 18, 2026

The economic blueprint of President Lee Jae-myung’s administration was forged through a rigorous, year-long audition conducted in 2020 by prominent progressive economist Choi Bae-keun, who sought to test whether the former provincial governor had the grit to break South Korea's bureaucratic orthodoxy.

  • What happened: After distancing himself from electoral politics following the 2020 legislative elections, economist Choi Bae-keun spent a full year auditing the policy record of then-Gyeonggi Province Governor Lee Jae-myung before committing to an intellectual alliance.
  • The catalyst: The 2020 COVID-19 pandemic relief debate became the decisive ideological split, pitting Lee’s universal cash payouts distributed via local currency against the means-tested relief pushed by former Prime Minister Lee Nak-yon and the Ministry of Economy and Finance.
  • The core agenda: Their collaboration developed Lee's signature "Basic" framework—guaranteeing basic income, public credit access, and basic housing—while advocating for a structural overhaul of state finance and the Bank of Korea's monetary mandate.
  • The key intermediaries: The partnership succeeded due to high-trust envoys rather than typical political courtiers, notably Lee’s former provincial secretary Kim Nam-jun (now a lawmaker in the ruling Democratic Party of Korea) and policy coordinator Dr. Kim Jae-yong.

The 2020 Pandemic Test: Universal Stimulus vs. Fiscal Conservatism

South Korean politics has long suffered from "polifessors"—academics chasing cabinet posts or parliamentary nominations by lending their credentials to political campaigns. Choi Bae-keun deliberately avoided this trap until Gyeonggi Province officials initiated contact in 2020. Unwilling to offer blind partisan backing, Choi scrutinized Lee’s track record, from debt restructuring and youth allowances in Seongnam to administrative crackdowns on illegal facilities across Gyeonggi. The turning point was Lee’s willingness to risk his political career against conservative fiscal bureaucrats by deploying universal disaster relief funds across Gyeonggi Province, demonstrating that progressive economic theory could translate into executable administrative power.

This clashed directly with the establishment approach of then-party rival Lee Nak-yon, who advocated for selective welfare limited to the most vulnerable to protect state coffers. Lee Jae-myung’s model treated public spending not as targeted charity, but as an economic catalyst: universal local currency distributions forced money into neighborhood businesses, generating measurable consumption multiplier effects. That policy duel defined the modern Democratic Party's internal shift away from technocratic compromise toward unapologetic universalism.

Overhauling the Bank of Korea and the "Basic" Doctrine

Beyond emergency relief, the Choi-Lee alliance sought to dismantle what both viewed as an expired growth model built on property speculation and passive central banking. By challenging the Bank of Korea to align more directly with public welfare and fiscal policy, they constructed the "Basic Series"—universal basic income, state-backed low-interest credit for vulnerable borrowers, and expanded public housing. Rather than treating finance as a speculative tool for entrenched conglomerates, they recast capital access as basic public infrastructure.

This ideological foundation stands in stark contrast to the severe cuts in research and development funding, aggressive tax relief for the wealthy, and economic paralysis that defined the period during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure before his impeachment and imprisonment. The careful, year-long vetting that brought Choi and Lee together illustrates why the current administration approaches national governance not through improvisational politics, but through a deeply entrenched economic philosophy aimed at rebuilding the country's social contract.
