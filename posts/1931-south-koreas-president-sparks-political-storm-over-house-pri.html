South Korea's President Sparks Political Storm Over House Prices

November 04, 2026

  • President Lee Jae-myung claims real transaction prices in key South Korean areas are plunging, vowing that the government will buy up properties in mass if a full-scale crash hits and turn them into public housing.
  • Critics, including Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon and the conservative opposition People Power Party, blast the remarks as completely detached from reality, pointing to private data showing Seoul apartment prices rising for 90 consecutive weeks.
  • The clash exposes a deep divide between official government diagnosis and everyday citizens struggling with high housing and key money (jeonse) deposit costs.

South Korean politics is gripped by a fierce debate over the housing market after President Lee Jae-myung claimed that property values in major areas are dropping significantly. Lee framed the current slump not as a temporary correction, but as the beginning of a structural shift, declaring that his administration would step in as a massive buyer of last resort if the market collapses, converting surplus units into public rental housing. The stated goal is to finally break South Korea's entrenched "unbeatable real estate" myth.

The reaction from the political opposition and local government has been scathing. Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon accused the president of living in an alternate reality, while the conservative People Power Party mocked that the only thing actually plunging is the president's approval rating. Critics argue that government claims of a market drop ignore the lived reality of families facing soaring rental costs and persistent price growth in mid-tier neighborhoods, warning that policymakers are relying on cherry-picked data.
