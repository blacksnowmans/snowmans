President Lee Jae-myung’s Social Media Strategy: A Shift in Political Communication

October 04, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung, leader of the Democratic Party of Korea (the country's main liberal opposition-turned-ruling party), is sparking controversy with a highly unconventional, real-time social media strategy that critics warn is eroding the dignity of his office.

  • The Approach: Unlike his predecessors who used spokespeople for official messaging, President Lee uses X (formerly Twitter) to engage in real-time, often aggressive, discourse.
  • The Controversy: His use of slang terms—such as “Jwa-ak-ha-ge” (a term perceived by critics as derogatory, similar to rhetoric used by extreme right-wing online communities)—has drawn widespread backlash.
  • The Impact: While these tactics consolidate his core “fandom” support, observers argue they alienate moderate voters and trap the President in a “filter bubble” that distorts his perception of national sentiment.
  • The Stakes: Critics fear this “digital populism” is replacing traditional political persuasion with a culture of hostility and mockery, potentially damaging the long-term health of South Korean democracy.

The Shift from Statesmanship to Digital Combat

President Lee’s social media usage acts as a direct, unfiltered window into his governing style. While some view this transparency as a modern evolution of communication, others argue it lacks the gravity and caution required of a constitutional leader. By frequently retweeting supporters and using specialized "fandom" terminology—such as terms for his core supporters and detractors akin to K-pop idol fan culture—he has built a unique, albeit polarizing, political brand.

The core issue lies in the tone. By adopting language often associated with extremist online forums (such as the infamous Ilbe, a site known for its far-right, inflammatory content), the President is accused of lowering the discourse. What his supporters may see as "relatable" or "authentic," the broader public often interprets as divisive and beneath the dignity of a head of state. This strategy prioritizes the "virtual majority" of the online echo chamber over the nuanced realities of national governance.

Algorithmic Isolation and the Future of Political Discourse

A significant concern among political analysts is the "filter bubble" effect. President Lee’s social media feed is heavily curated by algorithms, which reinforces a binary worldview: "us" versus "them." In this environment, every political interaction is framed as a struggle against enemies rather than a process of consensus-building. This constant state of digital combat appears to have left the President increasingly isolated from the concerns of the general public who do not participate in his specific online ecosystem.

Furthermore, the paradox of his platform regulation agenda has not gone unnoticed. While the President has called for stricter penalties against hate speech and anonymous online abuse, critics point out that his own rhetoric often mirrors the same polarizing tactics he seeks to legislate against. Ultimately, the question remains whether this aggressive style is a temporary political weapon or a fundamental degradation of the standards of public office. If the President continues to prioritize the language of his most fervent base over the language of national unity, he risks turning his greatest political asset—his digital reach—into a significant liability.
