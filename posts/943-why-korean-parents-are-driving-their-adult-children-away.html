Why Korean Parents Are Driving Their Adult Children Away

October 10, 2026

South Korean parents who treat their adult children like schoolchildren are facing an explosion of intentional estrangement, according to family counseling experts.

    The core illusion: Many parents believe that because they raised their children, they retain lifelong control and the right to discipline them. The reality check: Adult children in their 20s, 30s, and 40s are functioning professionals facing severe economic pressure, making constant parental criticism feel like intolerable abuse. The recommended fix: Experts advise treating adult children as social peers, cutting back contact to a simple weekly check-in, and dropping emotional manipulation entirely.

The End of Parental Authority

In South Korea’s intensely high-pressure society, family bonds are frequently strained by unrealistic expectations. According to Lee Ho-sun, a professor of Christian Counseling and Welfare at Soongsil Cyber University, the fundamental breakdown in parent-child relationships stems from a simple, outdated premise: parents acting as if their fully grown children are still in elementary school. When parents hurl casual insults—comparing siblings or criticizing physical appearance—they often destroy trust under the guise of "tough love."

This dynamic often mirrors authoritarian corporate or societal structures, crushing the self-esteem of adults who already face exhausting work environments. To reverse this, experts urge parents to view grown children not as subordinates, but as independent peers who deserve the same basic manners shown to a colleague. That means abandoning daily nagging, letting go of unsolicited advice, and offering simple, unconditional encouragement like "you'll do great" instead of old grievances.

Redefining Family Independence

True family harmony in a rapidly aging society requires mutual psychological and economic separation. Korean parents are warned against treating financial or emotional support as a transaction meant to buy lifelong compliance or obedience. By building independent post-retirement plans and scaling back communication to a respectful weekly text or brief call, parents can stop suffocating their children and start acting as safe havens.

Ultimately, the source notes, the transition from an oppressive hierarchy to a horizontal relationship is the only way to prevent adult children from cutting off contact entirely. Respect cannot be demanded; it is earned when parents step back, let go of control, and treat their children as equals.
