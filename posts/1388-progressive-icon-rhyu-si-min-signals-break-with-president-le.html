Progressive Icon Rhyu Si-min Signals Break with President Lee Jae-myung

October 22, 2026

Prominent progressive commentator Rhyu Si-min has declared that his political alignment with President Lee Jae-myung "cannot last forever," insisting that honest criticism takes precedence over personal loyalty.

  • What happened: Rhyu publicly asserted on YouTube that political comradeship must not censor independent commentary, warning that the Lee administration risks failure if it insulates itself from critique.
  • Why it matters: The rare public fracture between a leading progressive intellectual and the sitting president exposes growing internal cracks within South Korea's ruling bloc.
  • Who is affected: President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, whose core support base is increasingly divided amid declining public approval ratings.

A Critic's Duty Over Personal Ties

Rhyu argued that political ties formed during past struggles cannot bind a commentator indefinitely. In his view, prioritizing personal relationships over honest analysis leads to self-censorship, which ultimately fosters arrogance in power. The friction intensified after President Lee posted a social media shout-out on X to an individual who had repeatedly launched malicious attacks against Rhyu, leaving many progressive supporters baffled by the president's messaging.

Mounting Pressure on the Administration

The clash comes at a precarious moment for the government. A recent poll by the Korea Society Opinion Institute (KSOI) showed President Lee’s approval rating falling to a low of 34.9%, with disapproval standing at 58.8%. Negative sentiment exceeded 50% in almost every region outside the traditional progressive stronghold of Honam, as well as among key voters in their 40s and 50s.

Reactions within the ruling camp have been sharply split. While hardline pro-Lee factions condemned Rhyu's commentary as internal sabotage and bitter ill-will, others contend that an independent critic's job is precisely to highlight missteps before they become fatal. Although police investigations into Rhyu for defamation were dropped after President Lee indicated he did not wish to pursue punishment, the broader political fallout remains. Treating sharp critiques as betrayal risks isolating the presidency at a time when public trust is visibly fraying.
