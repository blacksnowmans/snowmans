South Korea Market Briefing: Surviving the Fed Rate Hike and Low-Volume Range

September 22, 2026

  • The Fed's Latest Move: The US Federal Reserve raised interest rates by 25 basis points, but tech-heavy indexes like the Nasdaq shrugged off the news, proving that the market had already priced in the shock.
  • What Changes: South Korea's daily trading volume has shrunk dramatically to around $15–20 trillion won (roughly $11–15 billion), creating a narrow, highly selective rotation market instead of a broad-based rally.
  • Who is Affected: Retail investors chasing random thematic stocks are taking heavy hits, while smart money is concentrating squarely on semiconductor supply chain firms and AI power infrastructure.

Resilient Tech Stocks and the Real Driver: Oil Prices

Despite another US interest rate hike that typically triggers panic, global and domestic markets have shown surprising defensive strength. Growth stocks, particularly in semiconductors, aerospace, and robotics, refused to break. This resilience signals that investors are no longer running for the exits at every piece of bad news; instead, they are looking past short-term macro noise to focus on the unstoppable AI transition. However, investors shouldn't ignore the hidden victims of high borrowing costs: heavily indebted companies facing a delayed wave of credit crunches, as tracked by rising bankruptcy filings.

The true unsung hero keeping the market afloat is the sharp drop in global crude oil prices, fueled by assurances of stable Saudi Arabian exports. Energy costs act as the ultimate remote control for inflation and Federal Reserve policy. As long as oil prices trend downward rather than threatening the $100 mark, the broader economy can endure higher interest rates. For everyday investors navigating South Korea's sluggish KOSPI and KOSDAQ indexes, daily charts matter less than watching where oil prices and semiconductor supply-chain orders are heading.

Navigating the Low-Volume Trap: Semiconductors and Power Infrastructure

With market liquidity drying up and trading turnover down to a fraction of past highs, the days of easy, across-the-board rallies are gone. Active exchange-traded funds (ETFs) managed by major Korean asset managers show that smart money is heavily concentrated in semiconductor parts, components, and equipment (known locally as "sobujang") rather than lagging large-cap stocks. Companies with strong year-over-year earnings growth—such as HBM equipment maker PSK Holding—are maintaining relative strength near their 52-week highs, acting as the true leaders of this foggy market.

Beyond semiconductors, the massive expansion of AI data centers is creating an unavoidable bottleneck: a severe shortage of electricity. This has turned power infrastructure into a prime hunting ground for long-term capital. From nuclear power and gas turbine builders like Doosan Enerbility to heavy construction firms handling data center builds, investors are betting on the physical grid rather than speculative themes. In a low-volume environment where rushing into every trendy stock is a recipe for losing money, patience and concentration on cash-generating sector leaders remain the only viable survival strategy.
