As South Korea’s Running Craze Booms, Cardiologists Warn of Hidden Heart Risks

October 14, 2026

South Korean cardiologists are cautioning that the nation’s explosive running boom is driving an increase in vascular damage and sudden cardiac emergencies, particularly among overworked young adults who confuse extreme grit with cardiovascular health.

  • The Warning: Dr. Lee Jong-young, a cardiologist at Hallym University Sacred Heart Hospital, warns that unregulated, high-intensity running can cause arterial damage and pathological cardiac hypertrophy rather than promoting wellness.
  • Tragedy Among the Young: High-profile incidents—such as the recent fatal cardiac arrest of a 20-something elite runner and graduate student from top science university KAIST—have punctured the belief that youth guarantees immunity.
  • Dangerous Misconceptions: Many Korean runners in their 20s and 30s run through chronic sleep deprivation and occupational stress, occasionally skipping prescribed medications under the false impression that intense mileage replaces clinical care.
  • Accessible Screening: Medical experts urge runners to utilize South Korea’s heavily subsidized healthcare system, where preventive exercise stress tests and echocardiograms typically cost only tens of thousands of won (roughly $20 to $50 out of pocket).

The Perils of 'Pushed-to-the-Limit' Cardio

Along Seoul’s Han River and across public tracks nationwide, marathon culture has shifted from a niche fitness pursuit to a ubiquitous social trend. However, cardiologist Lee Jong-young points out that continuous, maximum-exertion running strains blood vessels much like an overstretched elastic band. When pushed beyond normal metabolic capacity, the heart wall thickens abnormally. While this enlarged pump functions during youth, heart muscle efficiency naturally declines by about 1% annually with age, eventually transforming an artificially enlarged heart into a risk factor for heart failure in later decades.

The risk is compounded by the typical urban lifestyle in Korea. Young professionals dealing with late nights, processed diets, and chronic stress often arrive at the starting line with elevated systemic inflammation and high cholesterol. When individuals attempt full-distance marathons without structured conditioning, hidden coronary artery disease can trigger catastrophic outcomes. A cited study from the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) reveals that cardiac arrests occur at more than double the rate in full marathons compared to half-marathons, with coronary artery conditions serving as the primary cause.

Calibrating Pace Over Ego

Medical experts emphasize that cardiovascular exercise should aim for moderate intensity—leaving runners with light perspiration and mild breathlessness rather than complete physical depletion. Chasing personal records or obsessing over digital timing trackers often blinds runners to structural distress signals, such as sudden chest pressure, irregular breathing, or localized physical fatigue.

For those hitting the pavement, doctors advise objective testing before ramping up distance. Beyond South Korea’s inexpensive hospital screenings, basic self-assessments can reveal underlying limits: if climbing several flights of stairs triggers unbearable chest pain or severe breathlessness, track running should be halted immediately. True fitness lies in knowing when to slow down, not in treating cardiovascular strain as an endurance test.
