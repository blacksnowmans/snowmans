The Hidden Trap in South Korea's High-Yield Dividend ETF Boom

September 07, 2026

  • The Craze: Monthly dividend exchange-traded funds (ETFs) promising double-digit yields have become a massive retail investment trend in South Korea, driven by retirees and savers seeking steady cash flow.
  • The Catch: Many ultra-high-yield products—particularly covered-call ETFs offering returns around 18% to 20%—risk eroding the underlying principal (NAV), meaning high payouts can simply be investors getting their own money back.
  • The Tax Angle: Failing to utilize tax-advantaged accounts like Individual Savings Accounts (ISAs) and state-backed pension savings plans means losing up to 15.4% of returns straight to dividend income tax.
  • Who Is Affected: South Korea's older generations (the 5060 demographic) relying on fixed income, alongside younger retail investors chasing quick monthly payouts over long-term wealth growth.

South Korea’s obsession with monthly dividend ETFs has turned casual lunch-table chatter into a relentless pursuit of passive income. But behind the glossy marketing of 18% annual yields lies a fundamental misunderstanding of total return versus simple cash distribution. When a fund pays out massive dividends while its share price steadily drops, investors are effectively eating away at their own principal. Covered-call strategies—which cap potential gains during bull markets while exposing portfolios to the full brunt of market downturns—make this danger even sharper.

To survive retirement investing without watching capital evaporate, retail investors need to shift their focus from headline dividend rates to net asset value (NAV) stability. That means moving away from outdated, high-stress physical real estate like commercial shops or officetels, and instead leveraging tax-sheltered wrappers like South Korea's ISA and national pension structures. Combined with a boring, disciplined strategy of regular, automated contributions rather than trying to time the market, long-term survival depends on patience over flashy yields.
