Why AI Earnings Are Beating Macro Fear—and Where Korea Fits In

October 20, 2026

The recent rebound in U.S. technology stocks proves that the underlying cash-generation power of artificial intelligence is overpowering broader macroeconomic fears of sustained high interest rates.

  • What happened: After market sentiment plunged into extreme panic with 73% bearish outlooks, the Nasdaq jumped 1.11% as heavy earnings growth pulled Big Tech valuation multiples down to attractive entry points.
  • Macro threats remain: European sovereign debt is under severe pressure—with French 10-year yields nearing 5.0% amid fiscal protests—while energy inflation keeps global central banks constrained.
  • Semiconductor realignments: Chinese chipmaker ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) posted an 81.7 billion yuan (about $11.5 billion) quarterly operating profit by filling legacy DRAM shortages created as South Korean leaders pivot to High Bandwidth Memory (HBM).
  • Korea's lag: South Korea's stock markets continue to decouple from global gains, dragged down by persistent governance flaws and controversial subsidiary spin-offs like SK Hynix’s Solidigm.

Why AI Fundamentals Defy Macro Drag

Investors often overreact to minute movements in bond yields and central bank rhetoric, falling victim to behavioral biases like loss aversion. Yet, the fundamentals of top tech firms have stayed remarkably resilient. Amazon Web Services maintains an operating margin near 39%, and massive earnings across Big Tech have pushed price-to-earnings multiples for AI leaders down toward historical value entry points. While late entrants face punishing borrowing costs of 10% to 15% to build out data centers, established giants with immediate power supply and hardware access are capturing high-margin revenues today.

European Fiscal Cracks and Korea’s Structural Discount

Outside the tech sector, global macroeconomic risks are multiplying. In Europe, fiscal austerity measures in France triggered violent civil unrest, blowing out the France-Germany 10-year bond spread to 1.5 percentage points and pushing British yields past 5.45%. At the same time, stubborn refining margins and elevated crude prices (Brent trading above $109) mean inflation will not disappear quietly, preventing aggressive central bank easing.

These global currents leave South Korea in an uncomfortable position. While global benchmarks rally, South Korean equities remain among the worst performers worldwide. Local memory chip giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have ceded legacy DRAM market share to Chinese rivals like CXMT to focus on high-margin HBM. Furthermore, Korea's persistent corporate governance issues—exemplified by shareholder-diluting subsidiary listings—continue to alienate international capital, leaving domestic equities detached from global tech momentum.
