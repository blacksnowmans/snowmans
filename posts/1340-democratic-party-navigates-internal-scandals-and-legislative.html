Democratic Party Navigates Internal Scandals and Legislative Battles

October 20, 2026

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country's governing party under President Lee Jae-myung, is balancing a delicate act: attempting to contain internal political fallout while maintaining an aggressive offensive against the conservative opposition and the Seoul city government.

  • Addressing "Firefighting" Rhetoric: Leadership is demanding an apology from senior DPK lawmaker Choo Mi-ae following her controversial remarks regarding firefighting finances, exacerbated by a tragic firefighter death in Bucheon.
  • "Hangang Bus Gate": The party is zeroing in on Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, demanding investigations into alleged kickbacks related to the "Hangang Bus" public transport project.
  • Judicial Reform: DPK leadership is now actively gauging support for the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, a push originally initiated by the smaller Rebuilding Korea Party.
  • Legislative Oversight: The party is using the annual parliamentary audit to criticize the opposition People Power Party (PPP) for "showmanship" instead of policy-driven oversight.

Internal Cleanup and Policy Shifts

The political climate grew tense after senior DPK lawmaker Choo Mi-ae made disparaging comments regarding firefighting budgets just as a firefighter died in a factory blaze in Bucheon. The DPK leadership, led by Chairman Kim Min-seok, moved quickly to distance the party from her remarks, publicly demanding an apology to prevent the scandal from damaging the party’s standing as the ruling power. This isn't just about PR; it’s about DPK’s desire to project competence as the party of government, specifically by promising to increase central government funding for local fire services to address systemic safety gaps.

However, the party is not without its own friction. While the DPK is pushing for higher standards for its members, the fact that they have yet to move beyond verbal warnings for Choo suggests a lingering hesitation to fully discipline its senior figures. Critics argue that if the DPK intends to be a truly responsible governing party, it must move beyond mere apologies and impose tangible consequences for rhetoric that insults frontline public servants.

Aggressive Oversight: The Seoul City Front

Outside of internal cleanups, the DPK is aggressively targeting Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon over his signature "Hangang Bus" project. DPK Floor Leader Han Byung-do has accused the Seoul Metropolitan Government of deliberately delaying an investigation into alleged kickbacks worth millions of dollars (hundreds of millions of KRW). During the parliamentary audit, the party successfully highlighted potential procedural irregularities, including claims that the Mayor’s office was kept in the dark about suspicious activity—a claim the DPK is openly skeptical of.

Simultaneously, the DPK is intensifying its pressure on the judiciary. With the Rebuilding Korea Party leading the charge, the DPK is warming up to the idea of impeaching Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. Having previously been cautious, the DPK now views this as a necessary step in its broader agenda to reform a judiciary that it claims has lost the public's trust. Whether this is a genuine institutional check or a calculated political move to consolidate power remains a subject of intense debate as the parliamentary audit season reaches its midpoint.
