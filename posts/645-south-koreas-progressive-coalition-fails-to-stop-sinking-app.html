South Korea's Progressive Coalition Fails to Stop Sinking Approval Ratings

September 10, 2026

  • President Lee Jae-myung's approval rating has dropped to 38.2%, with negative evaluations climbing to 58.8% in recent polls.
  • The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (South Korea's main liberal party) is shedding core supporters nearly three times faster than it is winning over new ones.
  • Discontent is spilling over to minor progressive allies like the Rebuilding Korea Party, signaling a wider crisis for the liberal bloc ahead of local elections.
  • Controversial policies, clumsy communication on judicial and real estate reforms, and a hesitant stance on overseas military deployments have alienated the base.

South Korea's progressive camp is facing a severe reality check. Rather than expanding its tent, the administration's aggressive push to court the political center has backfired, alienating loyal voters without attracting enough new ones. The numbers tell a grim story: for every new convert the government wins, it loses roughly three former supporters. This erosion spans both the moderate and progressive bases, leaving many who voted for reform feeling sidelined and disillusioned.

The core of the crisis lies in a widening gap between grand reform rhetoric and tangible, consistent governance. On high-stakes issues—ranging from real estate tax adjustments to judicial reform and potential troop deployments to the Strait of Hormuz—the administration has appeared reactive, opaque, and overly sensitive to short-term complaints. When policies constantly shift to appease vocal minorities or panic-driven public feedback, they lose their reformist edge, leaving ordinary citizens and working-class families with a deep sense of relative deprivation.

The Cost of Communication Failures and Faltering Alliances

Beyond policy missteps, the administration is paying a heavy price for a perceived lack of transparency and arrogant communication. Unexplained cabinet appointments and conflicting messages over key security and economic decisions have robbed the public of any clear narrative to defend. Meanwhile, political friction with allies like the Rebuilding Korea Party highlights a deeper institutional breakdown within the broader progressive coalition. Unless the ruling party drops its top-down approach, restores mutual trust, and focuses on delivering visible relief to everyday households, the current slide risks turning into a permanent loss of political momentum.
