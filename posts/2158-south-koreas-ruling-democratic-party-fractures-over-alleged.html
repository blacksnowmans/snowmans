South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party Fractures Over Alleged Shincheonji Database Leak

November 10, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has plunged into a fierce internal crisis over allegations that its leadership illicitly cross-referenced internal voter registries against confidential personal data from Shincheonji, a controversial fringe religious sect.

  • The Overblown Infiltration: While factional rivals alleged that tens of thousands of Shincheonji members covertly swayed the Democratic Party's internal election, verifiable records show only about 3,300 sect-linked members held voting rights—accounting for a negligible 0.4% of the roughly 780,000 total ballots cast.
  • The Data Privacy Scandal: Party leadership faces intense scrutiny over suspicions that it illegally recycled a 2020 public health database—originally compiled with 212,000 Shincheonji members by disaster management authorities during the COVID-19 pandemic—to screen its own members, which would represent a severe breach of South Korea's Personal Information Protection Act.
  • Escalating Civil War: Lawmaker Song Young-gil, who serves as a special advisor to President Lee Jae-myung, reignited factional conflict by publicly declaring an "organized infiltration," prompting furious calls from fellow members for his political retirement over bad-faith factionalism.
  • Members Sue Leadership: A survey by pollster Flower revealed that over 61% of Democratic Party supporters believe Party Leader Kim Min-seok must apologize and take responsibility, prompting rank-and-file members to crowdfund roughly $25 (33,000 won) each to file a court injunction to suspend Kim’s duties.

Weaponizing a Fringe Sect for Factional Gain

In South Korean politics, weaponizing ties to Shincheonji—a secretive religious group deeply stigmatized following early pandemic outbreaks—remains an effective smear tactic. In this case, figures inside the ruling party drastically inflated the sect’s actual footprint, portraying a tiny fraction of votes (0.4%) as an orchestrated plot of more than 30,000 infiltrators. Instead of addressing substantive party issues, certain factions seized on unverified hearsay from former sect members to overturn election dynamics and suppress rivals.

Severe Legal Exposure for the Ruling Camp

The scandal shifts from routine infighting into criminal liability over the origin of the registry. If party leaders used an official pandemic quarantine database compiled by the state in 2020 for internal political purges, it constitutes a flagrant violation of data protection laws. Prominent party lawmakers notably refused to view the data, explicitly warning that handling unverified and likely illegal databases leaves the party vulnerable to direct prosecutorial investigations.

This infighting poses a self-inflicted blow to President Lee Jae-myung’s administration. Rather than supporting the executive agenda and passing bread-and-butter legislation, the ruling party is paralyzing itself with conspiracy-driven purges. The spectacle of party members paying out of their own pockets to haul their own party chairman into court signals a dangerous breakdown of procedural legitimacy within the ruling camp.
