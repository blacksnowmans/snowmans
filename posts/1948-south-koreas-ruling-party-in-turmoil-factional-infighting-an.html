South Korea’s Ruling Party in Turmoil: Factional Infighting and the Crisis of Internal Democracy

November 04, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party is being shaken by internal factional warfare, aggressive purges, and a high-stakes leadership controversy that threatens to undermine President Lee Jae-myung’s administration.

  • What happened: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok recently claimed a "secret three-way alliance" was plotting against the party leadership, later issuing a vague apology without providing evidence. Meanwhile, senior party member Choi Min-hee accused Kim of shouting her down and stripping her of her right to speak during a closed-door leadership meeting to block her from raising sensitive issues.
  • What it changes: The clashes expose a deepening rift between the party's traditional mainstream factions (associated with former presidents Roh Moo-hyun and Moon Jae-in) and a rising "New Lee Jae-myung" faction seeking to consolidate total control.
  • Who is affected: President Lee Jae-myung’s government faces collateral damage as ruling party infighting distracts from key legislative goals, while party members and voters grow exhausted by authoritarian backroom politics.

The Breakdown of Internal Democracy

When a party leader uses closed-door meetings to silence dissenting voices, it signals a drift toward authoritarianism. The controversy surrounding Choi Min-hee—where she was reportedly cut off and berated for attempting to bring up a sensitive religious sect controversy—strikes at the core of what a democratic party should stand for. Critics argue that using manufactured conspiracies and raw power to muzzle elected members mirrors the heavy-handed tactics the Democratic Party has long criticized in its political opponents.

By prioritizing factional dominance over open debate, the current leadership is alienating the party's base. The Democratic Party was built on democratic participation and fierce internal debate. Smothering opposing views under the guise of "party unity" does not heal divisions; it creates a culture of obedience that ultimately weakens the administration it is supposed to support.

A Dangerous Diversion from Governance

President Lee Jae-myung is working to steer the country and correct the wreckage left by impeached former president Yoon Seok-youl, who is currently imprisoned. At a time when the public demands stability and competence, the ruling party leadership is bogged down in petty turf wars, conspiracy theories, and purges aimed at sidelining the party's historical mainstream.

Politics must be judged by results, not manufactured loyalty tests. If the Democratic Party's leadership continues to treat internal critics as enemies to be crushed rather than partners to be persuaded, it will squander the political capital hard-earned by the current administration. True party unity comes from shared values, not enforced silence.
