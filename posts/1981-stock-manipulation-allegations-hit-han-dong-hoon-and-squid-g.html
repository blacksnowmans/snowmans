Stock Manipulation Allegations Hit Han Dong-hoon and Squid Game Star Lee Jung-jae

November 05, 2026

South Korean financial regulators are investigating allegations of high-level stock manipulation and insider trading involving Han Dong-hoon, a prominent conservative politician and former prosecutor, and Squid Game actor Lee Jung-jae.

  • The "Galbi" Effect: A single viral photo of Han and Lee dining together in late 2023 triggered a $165 million (230 billion KRW) surge in the market cap of Daesang Holdings, a company tied to Lee’s domestic partner.
  • Suspicious Capital Flows: Lee’s companies, including Artist United and Artist Studio, allegedly engaged in "sweetheart" deals, such as acquiring $3.6 million (5 billion KRW) in bonds at a 0% interest rate.
  • Strategic Exits: Insiders reportedly sold off their holdings at the peak of the hype, realizing profits of up to eight times their initial investment within weeks of major public announcements.
  • Regulatory Heat: The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has referred the case to the Serious Crime Investigation Office (SCIO), with lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea demanding a full probe into "power-capital collusion."

The Anatomy of a Political Theme Stock

The controversy centers on the intersection of political influence and entertainment capital. In November 2023, Han Dong-hoon—then a high-profile figure in the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition—met Lee Jung-jae for dinner. When photos of the meeting went viral, investors flocked to "theme stocks" associated with the pair. Specifically, Daesang Holdings saw its valuation explode because its Vice Chair, Lim Se-ryung, is Lee’s long-time partner. Critics argue this was not a casual dinner but a calculated move to signal political ties and inflate stock prices without any change in corporate fundamentals.

The financial mechanics behind Lee's companies, Artist United and Artist Studio, suggest a more sophisticated scheme. In 2021, these entities acquired convertible bonds at 0% interest—a highly unusual move that effectively provided risk-free capital. Following the "Galbi photo" hype and a subsequent $13.7 million (19 billion KRW) rights offering, the stock price hit a record high. The following day, major stakeholders exited their positions, pocketing astronomical gains. This "pump and dump" pattern has left retail investors holding the bag, leading to accusations that Han’s political "halo" was weaponized for private profit.

Philanthropy or Image Laundering?

Adding a layer of irony to the scandal is Han Dong-hoon’s recent foray into "barrier-free" cinema. In December 2024, Han participated in voice recording for the movie 1 Victory, distributed by Lee Jung-jae’s Artist Company. While framed as a charitable contribution for the visually impaired, the timing has raised eyebrows. Critics view it as a sophisticated PR stunt designed to "wash" the images of both the politician and the entertainment firm while they were under intense scrutiny for financial irregularities.

Under the current administration of President Lee Jae-myung, the government has signaled a zero-tolerance policy toward financial crimes involving the former prosecutorial elite. During the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment, such cozy relationships between prosecutors and capital were often overlooked. However, the SCIO is now under pressure to prove that no one is above the law. If investigators find that non-public information regarding corporate filings or political appointments was used to coordinate these stock movements, Han and Lee could face severe legal consequences for disrupting the integrity of the capital markets.
