SK Hynix Posts Monster Q3 Earnings, Commits $75 Billion to Shareholder Returns

October 28, 2026

South Korean memory giant SK Hynix announced historic third-quarter revenue of 104.62 trillion won (about $78 billion) alongside an operating profit of 83.3 trillion won (about $62 billion), silencing market skeptics with an unprecedented 80% operating margin.

  • Unprecedented profitability: Q3 revenue rose 328% year-on-year, propelled by explosive demand for artificial intelligence server memory including HBM4 and DDR5.
  • Historic capital return: The company confirmed a 100 trillion won (about $75 billion) shareholder return package for the fiscal year—equivalent to roughly 11% of its 900 trillion won market capitalization.
  • Capacity sold out through 2029: Five-year long-term agreements (LTAs) signed with 14 global tech customers have pre-allocated over 50% of the company's 2027–2028 output, with DRAM and HBM effectively booked out through 2029.
  • Subsidiary IPO scrapped: Management abandoned plans for a separate public listing of its NAND subsidiary Solidigm, choosing to retain cash flows and avoid diluting shareholder value.

Locking in the AI Hardware Monopoly

The earnings release demonstrates that SK Hynix has transitioned from a cyclical commodity supplier into an indispensable tollbooth for the global AI ecosystem. With volume production of next-generation HBM4 ramping up and DDR5 shipments expanding over 40% quarter-on-quarter, the manufacturer is operating in an environment where demand hopelessly outstrips fab lead times. By securing multi-year contracts with deposit requirements from 14 major cloud service providers, the company has insulated itself against traditional semiconductor downturns, guaranteeing high margins regardless of broader macroeconomic noise.

The NAND flash division is undergoing a parallel restructuring. Rather than chasing raw unit volume, SK Hynix is shifting domestic capacity toward 321-layer enterprise SSDs designed to handle heavy AI workloads. Between long lead times on advanced packaging and hyperscaler infrastructure spending showing no signs of slowing down, the company holds pricing leverage that traditional memory makers have never previously enjoyed.

A Decisive Break from the Korea Discount

Beyond operating metrics, the company's capital allocation represents a deliberate challenge to the "Korea Discount"—the chronic undervaluation of South Korean equities caused by poor governance and weak minority shareholder returns. Delivering a 100 trillion won payout via a 20,000-won per share quarterly dividend and large-scale share cancellations shows an aggressive adherence to returning over 50% of free cash flow.

Crucially, CEO Kwak Noh-jung's decision to cancel the Solidigm initial public offering eliminates the threat of a "split-off listing," a practice where Korean conglomerates list attractive subsidiaries separately, draining value from parent-company investors. By integrating Solidigm’s enterprise SSD capacity directly into the parent balance sheet and distributing windfall profits back to investors, SK Hynix is establishing a rare corporate governance benchmark for the Korean market.
