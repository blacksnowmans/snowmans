South Korea's Ruling Party Pushes Regional Special Zones Amid Labour and Judicial Clashes

October 22, 2026

  • Mega-Region Push: The ruling Democratic Party is fast-tracking a sweeping regional development bill to build mega-economic hubs outside Seoul, creating a direct clash over proposed exemptions to South Korea's 52-hour workweek cap.
  • Impeachment Friction: Junior opposition ally the Rebuilding Korea Party formally handed over an impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, but the Democratic Party is holding back to weigh a prior parliamentary investigation.
  • Party Discipline: Senior figures faced strict internal warnings over careless remarks about firefighting budgets, prompting the party to table a bill shifting a heavier financial burden onto the central government.
  • Staking Out Foreign Policy: Leadership pushed back against controversial remarks by a U.S. military commander nominee regarding the Demilitarized Zone, framing it as a defense of South Korean sovereignty.

Balancing Regional Growth with Labour Protections

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party, the liberal majority party supporting President Lee Jae-myung, is facing high-stakes political math as it tries to balance aggressive economic reform with party discipline. At the center of the legislative agenda is the new super-regional special zone bill. Designed to counter deep demographic decline in rural areas by bundling regional economies into massive growth hubs, the project has run straight into a wall over the 52-hour workweek. Business leaders want flexible working hours to kickstart new industries, but labour groups and progressive allies argue this guts workers' rights. Party leadership is walking a tightrope, signaling they will look for alternative paths that protect the core value of labour.

At the same time, the ruling party is playing a tactical game over the judiciary. The Rebuilding Korea Party—a smaller progressive ally—is aggressively pushing to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae within November, aiming to punish a judiciary it views as overly lenient toward past abuses under the jailed former leader Yoon Suk-yeol. While the public appetite for breaking up political-judicial cartels is high, the Democratic Party's floor leadership is urging caution. Rather than rushing blindly into an impeachment battle that could backfire if rejected by the Constitutional Court, the ruling party is leaning toward holding a formal parliamentary investigation first to build ironclad legal grounds.

Internal Stumbles and Diplomatic Lines

The party's heavy-handed policy drive has also required internal cleanup. After veteran lawmakers drew fierce public backlash for careless remarks regarding firefighter finances, leadership issued formal warnings and quickly pivoted to a substantive fix: drafting legislation to have the central government cover 80% of firefighter personnel costs, moving away from an unfair system that dumped the burden onto local budgets.

On the foreign policy front, the ruling party demonstrated a firm hand regarding national sovereignty. Floor leadership publicly expressed strong regret over inappropriate remarks concerning the Demilitarized Zone made by a nominee for U.S. Forces Korea commander, making it clear that South Korea's constitutional sovereignty is non-negotiable. By appointing veteran diplomats and former unification figures as senior advisors, the party is trying to steady its foreign policy compass amid a volatile international landscape.
