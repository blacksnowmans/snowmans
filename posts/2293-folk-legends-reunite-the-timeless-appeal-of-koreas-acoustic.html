Folk Legends Reunite: The Timeless Appeal of Korea’s Acoustic 80s

November 13, 2026

Legendary folk groups Yeohaeng Sketch and Dongmulwon are reviving the analog romance of the 1980s through their ongoing "Folk Forever" concert series, bridging the gap between aging fans and a new generation seeking "emotional healing."

  • Yeohaeng Sketch (Travel Sketch) and Dongmulwon (The Zoo), two pillars of the late-1980s folk scene, recently held their fourth annual "alumni reunion" performance to celebrate their shared history.
  • The artists shared nostalgic memories of Daehangno—Seoul’s historic theater district—and the late Kim Kwang-seok, South Korea’s most iconic folk singer-songwriter who was an original member of Dongmulwon.
  • A major joint concert featuring both groups alongside Scenery on a Bicycle (Jajeongeo Tan Punggyeong) is scheduled for December 11, 2026, at the Yonsei University Auditorium, a premier private institution in Seoul.
  • Beyond music, the groups are promoting "lifestyle wellness," sharing practical physical therapy tips for aging fans during their broadcasts to maintain the "stamina for nostalgia."

The Cradle of Korean Folk: From Small Theaters to Stardom

The "Folk Forever" movement is more than a tribute act; it is a living history of the late 80s and early 90s, a period when acoustic music dominated the Korean youth culture before the rise of K-pop idols. Members Luca C and Nam Joon-bong of Yeohaeng Sketch, alongside Park Ki-young and Yoo Jun-yeol of Dongmulwon, first crossed paths in the small theaters of Daehangno. During their peak, the demand was so high that venues had to place cushions in the aisles—known as "extra seating"—to accommodate fans who couldn't get a chair.

This era was defined by a horizontal relationship between the artist and the audience. Unlike the polished, high-production values of modern K-pop, these folk performances thrived on "human imperfection." Park Ki-young recently demonstrated this resilience during a live performance of the classic "On the Street" (Geori-eseo); despite his monitors falling out mid-song, he continued the performance, later calling the mishap the "beauty of live music." This authenticity is what continues to draw audiences who are fatigued by the hyper-digital nature of the modern music industry.

Music as a Vessel for Personal and National Memory

For many Koreans, these songs are deeply intertwined with personal milestones. Nam Joon-bong of Yeohaeng Sketch recently shared that he played Dongmulwon’s music at his father’s funeral three years ago because it was his father's favorite. This illustrates the unique role folk music plays in Korea: it is a "diary of the times" that provides comfort during both national transitions and private grief. The lyrics often focus on the mundane beauty of life—as seen in the hit "Isn't That Just How Life Is?"—offering a "don’t worry, it’s okay" message to a society often criticized for its hyper-competitive atmosphere.

The upcoming 2026 concert at Yonsei University aims to be a "giant plaza" where different generations can meet. By including Scenery on a Bicycle—famous for their soundtrack work in iconic Korean films like The Classic—the lineup ensures a broad appeal. The performers’ goal is to quench a "cultural thirst" that digital streaming cannot satisfy. By combining music with practical health advice—such as calf-stretching routines to combat the sedentary lifestyle of their aging fanbase—these artists are positioning themselves as lifelong companions to their listeners rather than just fleeting entertainers.
