The Erosion of Accountability: Lee Jae-myung and the "Bad Power" Within South Korea’s Progressive Bloc

October 02, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s administration faces a growing crisis of legitimacy as critics—including those within his own progressive camp—accuse his government of adopting the same "imperial" behaviors it once condemned in its conservative predecessors.

  • Leaked Confidentiality: President Lee recently posted sensitive state documents on social media, sparking outrage over a breach of protocol that would likely end the career of any rank-and-file civil servant.
  • Culture of Impunity: Critics argue that the administration’s response to internal scandals reveals a "rules for thee, but not for me" mentality, undermining the moral authority the Democratic Party of Korea (South Korea's main liberal party) once held.
  • The "Lee-First" Priority: Observers point to a shift from "citizen-first" governance to an "Lee Jae-myung first" culture, where proximity to the President and displays of loyalty have eclipsed systemic fairness.
  • Internal Dissent Silenced: Prominent progressive voices are reportedly being sidelined or attacked as "internal enemies" for questioning the administration's direction, leading to fears of an unchecked, one-man rule.

The Crisis of Presidential Conduct

The President’s casual handling of classified materials is more than a social media blunder; it is a symptom of a deeper disregard for state standards. By treating sensitive documentation with such flippancy, the administration risks normalizing behavior that it previously decried as corrupt or incompetent. When top leadership is exempt from the laws governing ordinary officials, the principle of equality before the law becomes a hollow promise. History has shown that when an administration loses its sense of decorum and treats its own staff as tools for image management rather than public servants, it eventually alienates the very public that brought it to power.

Furthermore, the atmosphere surrounding the President is increasingly defined by sycophants and political opportunists. Recent incidents, such as high-profile figures walking out of live media appearances to attend to the President, suggest a sycophantic hierarchy where the administration’s internal whims take precedence over public duties. This "imperial" style of management—where personnel decisions are based on personal preference and loyalty rather than professional vetting—risks repeating the failures of previous governments that crumbled under the weight of their own isolation and arrogance.

The Dilemma of the Progressive Bloc

For many progressive supporters, the current situation presents a painful dilemma: how to hold a government they helped elect accountable without inadvertently aiding the conservative opposition. However, the author argues that blind, unconditional support is a "poison" that will only lead to the movement's self-destruction. If the progressive bloc continues to ignore the "bad power" emerging within its own ranks, it will lose its moral footing entirely.

The path forward, according to the critique, requires the establishment of a robust, independent coalition within the liberal camp—a group of leaders who are willing to prioritize the health of the nation over the protection of a single individual’s ego. True political power in a democracy is not inherited or assumed; it is maintained through the courage to challenge injustice, even when that injustice comes from one's own side. Without such internal checks, the administration’s drift toward an unchecked, single-leader system may inevitably lead to its downfall.
