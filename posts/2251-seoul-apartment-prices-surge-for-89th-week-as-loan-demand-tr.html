Seoul Apartment Prices Surge for 89th Week as Loan Demand Triggers Transit Backlash

November 12, 2026

Seoul apartment prices extended their gains for an 89th consecutive week, driven by a 6.4 trillion won (about $4.6 billion) monthly surge in household loans despite government efforts to tighten mortgage lending.

  • Sustained buying panic: Household debt swelled as fear of missing out outweighed financial curbs, pushing Seoul's average jeonse (lump-sum lease deposit) price to a record 720 million won (about $515,000).
  • Regional divergence: Ulsan's industrial hub saw apartment prices jump 4.2% and unsold inventory drop 19.7% on local job investments, while the satellite city of Bundang jumped 35% ahead of flagship redevelopment selections.
  • Outrage over transit-less supply: Suburban residents in Gimpo and Hanam are fiercely protesting government plans for tens of thousands of new homes, demanding subway line extensions before adding housing density.
  • Shift toward smaller, newer homes: Soaring construction costs have cooled speculative bets on historic Gangnam redevelopment projects, pushing buyers toward smaller units (accounting for 47.2% of Seoul sales) and newer existing apartments.

The Infrastructure Trap in New Housing Plans

The Lee Jae-myung administration faces escalating resistance over its suburban supply push. While urban planning authorities aim to curb prices by announcing mega-developments—such as 37,000 housing units planned for Gimpo—residents view building homes without transport networks as administrative negligence. Key infrastructure commitments, such as extending Seoul Subway Line 3 into Hanam, have yet to break ground. Without guaranteed mass transit, piling thousands of new households into suburban pockets threatens to paralyze regional commutes.

Regulatory Restrictions Fail to Dampen Buyer Anxiety

Strict credit limits are proving ineffective against deep-seated market anxiety. With lease prices rising for 39 consecutive months, young buyers in their 30s and 40s are taking on heavy debt to secure smaller homes or fleeing to neighboring Gyeonggi Province. This survival-driven buying spree has created new price highs in compact units and outer suburban markets. Until government policy pairs housing delivery with realistic transit timelines, financial curbs alone will fail to restore market stability.
