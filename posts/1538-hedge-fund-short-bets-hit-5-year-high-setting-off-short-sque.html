Hedge Fund Short Bets Hit 5-Year High, Setting Off Short Squeeze Alarms in South Korea

October 27, 2026

Global hedge funds have pushed short-selling positions to a five-year high, sparking warnings from Goldman Sachs that any sudden market bounce could trigger a violent short squeeze across South Korean equities.

  • Record Short Exposure: Data from Goldman Sachs Prime Brokerage shows short-selling bets reaching five-year peaks, signaling extreme market pessimism.
  • Explosive Upside Reversals: High concentrations of short positions create structural fragility, where minor price bounces force funds into emergency short-covering, rapidly driving up stock prices.
  • Macro Headwinds: WTI crude oil prices hovering near $100 per barrel and escalating Middle East tensions are driving inflation fears and bond market volatility.
  • Institutional Capacity Limits: South Korea's National Pension Service (NPS), the country's primary public fund, has nearly exhausted its domestic equity allocation limits, constraining its ability to cushion market drops.

Anatomy of the Squeeze and Geopolitical Pressures

A short squeeze occurs when heavily shorted stocks rebound, forcing short sellers to buy back shares to limit theoretically infinite losses. The mechanics were recently demonstrated by Samsung E&A, a major South Korean plant engineering firm. Heading into its earnings report, short interest in the company stood at 13%. When the firm posted stronger-than-expected results and announced share buybacks, short sellers were forced to cover rapidly, causing short interest to plummet to 2% while market buying pushed the stock significantly higher.

External macro factors are complicating this picture. Crude oil touching $100 per barrel threatens to reignite global inflation, adding pressure to central bank rate policies. At the same time, international investors are grappling with shifting bond yields and geopolitical friction in the Middle East, leaving market sentiment tense as domestic stock indices test key support levels between 5,000 and 5,500 points.

Policy Challenges for the Lee Jae-myung Administration

Domestic institutional liquidity remains tight. The NPS holds little remaining buffer under its domestic stock allocation targets, meaning market stabilization cannot rely solely on public pension intervention. Market analysts are advocating for structural policy reforms, such as routing private retirement funds and specialized growth funds into the market to build sustainable buying capacity against foreign sell-offs.

Regulatory enforcement also remains a priority. Following the impeachment and removal of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol from office, the government under President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea has focused on restoring trust in capital markets. While regulators and the Korea Exchange (KRX) have established central monitoring networks to prevent illegal naked short selling, fully restoring investor confidence will require long-term transparency alongside prudent risk management by retail investors.
