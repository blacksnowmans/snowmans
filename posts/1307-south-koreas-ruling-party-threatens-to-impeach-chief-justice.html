South Korea’s Ruling Party Threatens to Impeach Chief Justice Over Kim Keon-hee’s Imminent Release

October 19, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party is ramping up pressure to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de, accusing him of deliberately stalling court proceedings to allow Kim Keon-hee—the imprisoned wife of ousted former leader Yoon Suk-yeol—to go free when her detention mandate expires on November 1.

  • The November 1 deadline: Kim Keon-hee, sentenced to five years in prison during her second-instance trial, will be released automatically unless the Supreme Court issues a final verdict by October 31.
  • Allegations of judicial sabotage: Ruling party lawmakers claim Chief Justice Jo is intentionally delaying en banc decisions and stalling panel assignments to let Kim’s detention period run out.
  • Plea for ex-officio warrants: Special prosecutors and liberal politicians are urging the Seoul Central District Court, led by Chief Judge Oh Min-seok, to issue an ex-officio arrest warrant on separate pending charges to prevent her release.
  • Partisan clash over impeachment: The main conservative opposition People Power Party warns that impeaching Chief Justice Jo would paralyze the high court, a claim Democrats dismiss as political scare tactics.

A High-Stakes Judicial Delay

The controversy centers on what liberal lawmakers describe as a calculated effort by the judiciary to aid conservative political forces. Kim Keon-hee’s legal detention period ends at midnight on October 31. If the Supreme Court’s full bench does not hand down a ruling before then, she will walk out of prison on November 1. Special prosecutors have requested additional arrest warrants from Supreme Court panels, but court leadership has dragged its feet on assigning the matter to a bench, fueling suspicions of an intentional bottleneck.

For President Lee Jae-myung’s administration and the ruling Democratic Party, Kim’s potential release represents a direct challenge to post-impeachment judicial accountability. Over the summer, around 1,000 petitions urging her release were submitted to the court alongside aggressive filings from her defense team. Critics argue that Chief Justice Jo’s broader inaction—which includes delaying rulings on recusal motions against judges and refusing to nominate new Supreme Court justices—amounts to a severe dereliction of official duty designed to give conservative activist groups a rallying point.

Impeachment and the Battle for Judicial Reform

In response to the growing stalemate, senior Democratic Party figures, including Chief Supreme Council Member Kim Min-seok and party spokesperson Lee Yong-woo, have signaled that parliamentary impeachment is now on the table. While the opposition People Power Party claims that removing Chief Justice Jo would leave the Supreme Court dysfunctional and potentially disrupt trials involving Yoon Suk-yeol, legal experts note that an acting chief justice system would step in automatically without paralyzing court operations.

With only days remaining before the detention deadline, pressure is mounting on lower court judges, particularly Seoul Central District Court Chief Judge Oh Min-seok, to exercise their legal authority and issue a new warrant in parallel cases involving Kim. Should the judiciary fail to act, the resulting political firestorm will likely push the National Assembly to take the drastic step of removing the chief justice from office.
