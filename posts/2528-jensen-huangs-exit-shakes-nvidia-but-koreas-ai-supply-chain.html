Jensen Huang’s Exit Shakes Nvidia, but Korea’s AI Supply Chain Holds Firm

November 20, 2026

The sudden resignation of Jensen Huang has wiped out hundreds of billions in market value, yet Nvidia’s technical roadmap remains anchored by deepening partnerships with critical South Korean component manufacturers.

  • Nvidia’s market cap plummeted by approximately $525 billion (731 trillion KRW) as investors reacted to the leadership vacuum, despite the company reporting quarterly revenue that doubled year-over-year.
  • Samsung Electro-Mechanics, the electronic component arm of the Samsung Group, saw its shares surge over 9% after expanding its "AI alliance" with Nvidia to supply high-end capacitors and server substrates.
  • The Nvidia board has officially rejected calls for Huang’s return, appointing Colette Kress as interim CEO while considering Ian Buck, the "father of CUDA," as a potential permanent successor.
  • South Korean giants, including Hyundai Motor Group and memory leaders SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics, maintain that their AI chip supply chains and technical collaborations remain unaffected by the management turmoil.

The Korean Supply Chain Paradox

While Nvidia’s stock experienced extreme volatility in New York, the reaction in Seoul was surprisingly bullish for its partners. The market is distinguishing between "leadership risk" and "ecosystem momentum." Samsung Electro-Mechanics is the primary beneficiary, moving beyond supplying FC-BGA (high-end semiconductor substrates) to providing MLCCs (Multi-Layer Ceramic Capacitors)—tiny but essential components that regulate electricity in high-performance AI servers. This diversification suggests that even without Huang at the helm, Nvidia’s hunger for Korean hardware is growing.

Other major players are also holding steady. Hyundai Motor Group, which uses Nvidia chips for its "Software-Defined Vehicle" (SDV) initiatives, stated publicly that technical cooperation is proceeding as planned. Similarly, the "HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) alliance" involving SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics appears insulated from the boardroom drama. For these companies, the massive industrial gears of the AI transition are already locked in place; a change in CEO at Nvidia cannot easily derail multi-year development cycles and supply contracts.

Geopolitics and the Post-Huang Era

The leadership crisis coincides with tightening pressure from the U.S. Congress. Hardliners in Washington are using the instability to push for even stricter controls on AI chip exports to China. Reports suggest the U.S. House of Representatives is concerned that Chinese military entities are still attempting to bypass sanctions to acquire Nvidia’s Blackwell-series chips. This political pressure adds a layer of complexity for any successor, who must balance Nvidia’s massive Chinese revenue against escalating national security mandates.

Domestically, Nvidia’s influence is expanding into the Korean public sector. HIRA (the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service), a government agency that oversees South Korea’s medical billing, recently adopted Nvidia GPUs to build an AI-based audit system. This move reflects a broader trend: while the stock market frets over the loss of a visionary founder, the actual integration of Nvidia’s technology into state infrastructure and private manufacturing continues unabated. The "cult of personality" around Huang is being tested by the sheer inertia of the global AI ecosystem he helped build.
