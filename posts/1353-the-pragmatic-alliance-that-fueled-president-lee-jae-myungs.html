The Pragmatic Alliance That Fueled President Lee Jae-myung’s Rise

October 20, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung’s journey to the South Korean presidency was forged through a ruthless, transactional alliance with the Gyeonggi Eastern Alliance, a hardline underground leftist activist network that helped him seize municipal power in Seongnam and reshape the ruling Democratic Party of Korea.

  • The origin: Lee entered a decisive electoral pact in 2010 with the far-left Democratic Labor Party in Seongnam, sharing municipal administrative posts and procurement contracts with key Gyeonggi Eastern Alliance operatives.
  • The party takeover: After building a powerhouse base in Gyeonggi Province, Lee’s inner circle mobilized hundreds of thousands of registered party members to overwhelm the traditional party establishment once loyal to former Presidents Roh Moo-hyun and Moon Jae-in.
  • The fallout: Despite receiving critical campaign backing from figures linked to the dissolved Unified Progressive Party and its jailed radical organizer Lee Seok-ki, Lee systematically discarded these hardline allies once their ideological liabilities threatened his national ambitions.

From Seongnam Backrooms to Central Party Dominance

Lee’s ideological flexibility has always served his political realism. In 1996, while working as a civic activist lawyer, he briefly explored running under South Korea’s conservative New Korea Party banner before pivoting radically to the left a decade later. In 2006, he publicly slammed the liberal administration of President Roh Moo-hyun as cowardly and pledged his vote to the far-left. That rhetorical drift positioned Lee to court the Gyeonggi Eastern Alliance—an organized ideological faction rooted in South Korea's 1980s nationalist student movement—ahead of his victorious 2010 run for mayor of Seongnam.

Once inside Seongnam City Hall, Lee granted prominent alliance leaders leadership roles on his transition team and awarded public cleaning contracts to newly established social enterprises run by faction veterans. CNP Strategic Group, a consultancy founded by convicted hard-left figure Lee Seok-ki, designed Lee's mayoral campaign pamphlets. Lee repeatedly denied impropriety, and prosecutors eventually closed criminal inquiries without charges, but the alliance secured a vital foothold inside legitimate municipal governance.

Shedding the Hardliners on the Road to the Blue House

That local network soon set its sights on national politics. Following primary clashes against Moon Jae-in in 2017, Lee loyalists initiated aggressive grass-roots recruitment campaigns under slogans urging backers to join the Democratic Party and wrest control from moderate loyalists. Polling outfits like STI—formerly founded by Lee Seok-ki before changing its name—helped construct the "New Lee Jae-myung" brand, deliberately marginalizing the legacy of prior liberal leaders to center the party entirely around Lee.

Yet this partnership was strictly mercenary. As Lee transitioned from provincial governor to party boss and finally to the presidency—following the chaotic misrule, impeachment, and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—he froze out the radical activists whose fringe reputations posed severe electoral risks. Left behind, several alliance veterans bitterly complained of being abandoned as disposable tools. The trajectory of this fractured alliance illustrates the cold calculation driving President Lee’s rise: a readiness to leverage extreme factions to capture institutional power, paired with the discipline to cut them loose the moment they outlive their usefulness.
