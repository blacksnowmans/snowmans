Packing for the Camino de Santiago: A 7.8kg Lesson in Letting Go

September 07, 2026

  • What happened: A traveler meticulously packs a 7.8kg backpack ahead of an 800km trek along the Camino de Santiago, the famous pilgrimage route spanning from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port in France to Santiago de Compostela in Spain.
  • What it changes: The process shifts from simple logistics to an exercise in managing anxiety, trading bulky comfort items (like Crocs) for lightweight insurance (sandals and toe socks) to prevent debilitating blisters.
  • Who is affected: Anyone preparing for long-distance endurance travel who struggles to balance the fear of being unprepared against the physical cost of excess weight.

The Arithmetic of Anxiety: Why Every Gram Matters

Packing for an 800km journey is less about physical survival and more about confronting personal anxiety. Seasoned pilgrims on the Camino de Santiago frequently warn that overpacking is the primary cause of early muscle strain and severe blisters. Yet, fighting the urge to bring "just in case" items is a psychological battle. Dropping a 200g item here and a 200g item there is the only way to keep a pack under the 8-kilogram threshold.

Every gear decision reflects a negotiation with fear. Swapping bulky Crocs for lightweight sports sandals, selecting toe socks to prevent friction, and breaking down toiletries into minimalist portions all serve one purpose: survival on the trail. While essentials like a lightweight sleeping bag and layered clothing are non-negotiable, the real realization hits when the scale settles at 7.8kg—a tangible compression of personal habits and worries into a single load.

Traveling Light as Self-Reflection

The beauty and the trap of modern travel lie in the availability of supplies along the route. Villages and pilgrim hostels (albergues) offer most basic necessities, meaning much of what we pack to soothe our anxieties is ultimately redundant. The gear list—from blister-management kits to split-up security cards and anti-theft zipper lines—is essentially an engineering feat designed to buy peace of mind.

Ultimately, the true objective of packing for the Camino isn't just about crossing the finish line with an intact body; it is about learning what can be left behind. As the journey progresses along the yellow arrows of Spain, the real victory may not be carrying 7.8kg across 800 kilometers, but finding out how much of that weight can be shed along the way.
