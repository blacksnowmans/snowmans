Why AI Infrastructure is Defying High Interest Rates and Boosting Chipmakers

September 05, 2026

  • What happened: Despite volatile interest rates and surging energy costs, depository receipts for memory chip giants like SK Hynix (South Korea's major memory chipmaker) and Micron Technology surged roughly 8% in a sudden market rebound.
  • What it changes: Big tech companies are funnelling nearly all their operating cash flow into AI data centers, proving that AI infrastructure spending has become a matter of survival rather than choice.
  • Who is affected: Global memory suppliers, South Korean chip exporters navigating currency shifts, and power grids struggling to keep up with surging energy demands.

Global markets are caught in a roller coaster of shifting interest rates and unpredictable oil prices, yet semiconductor stocks are breaking away from the broader economic anxiety. Tech giants are aggressively borrowing and spending to build out artificial intelligence infrastructure, shrugging off the textbook assumption that high borrowing costs would crush capital expenditure. With operating profit margins hovering in the high 30s, these companies view the race for data center dominance as too crucial to slow down.

Behind the soaring valuations and tight supply of High Bandwidth Memory (HBM)—where SK Hynix holds about 50%, Samsung Electronics sits at 32%, and Micron trails at 18%—lies a massive structural vulnerability. AI servers consume staggering amounts of electricity, pushing local power grids to their limits just as environmental regulations tighten. While major players fight for market share and race toward advanced 12-layer HBM4 chips, the fundamental question remains whether this debt-fueled infrastructure boom can achieve sustainable growth without triggering a severe environmental or financial backlash.
