South Korea’s Real Estate Market: Auction Surge and the "Jeonse" Myth

October 29, 2026

South Korea’s property market is flashing warning signs as apartment auction volumes hit an 11-year high, signaling a potential correction that mirrors the post-2008 financial crisis landscape.

  • Auction Surge: Apartment auction filings are nearing 140,000 cases, with properties frequently selling for just 70-80% of their appraised value.
  • Cooling Demand: Transaction volumes in Seoul and the six major metropolitan cities have plummeted, while the number of unsold properties in Seoul has spiked to over 78,000.
  • The "Jeonse" Trap: Rising jeonse (the unique Korean system of long-term, lump-sum housing deposits) is no longer a reliable indicator of impending home price hikes, but rather a reflection of supply shortages and tenant desperation.
  • Policy Realities: Market instability is exacerbated by inconsistent lending regulations and the lingering impact of supply-side failures during the administration of the now-imprisoned former leader, Yoon Seok-yeol.

For years, the conventional wisdom in Korea held that rising jeonse prices would inevitably pull home purchase prices upward. However, this logic is failing in the current economic climate. In a market dominated by fears of a downturn, tenants are choosing the stability of jeonse over the risk of home ownership, regardless of rising deposit costs. The narrative that “rising rent triggers a buy-in” is increasingly being pushed by market players seeking to offload assets to desperate buyers, rather than reflecting genuine demand.

The current market dysfunction is further complicated by political hesitation regarding tax reform. While some argue that increasing property taxes will simply push costs onto tenants, this is a flawed premise. Rental prices are driven by supply and demand, not tax rates. The reluctance of policymakers—including those within the ruling Democratic Party—to push for necessary tax normalization due to fears of political backlash only serves to protect vested interests rather than resolve the fundamental lack of housing supply left behind by the previous administration.

Market Volatility and the "Young-Geul" Fallacy

The era of "Young-Geul" (an acronym for "scrapping every cent together" to buy a home) has left a trail of financial wreckage now appearing as a surge in auctions. Properties that once fetched 110-120% of their appraised value during the 2021 market mania are now struggling to find buyers at discounted rates. This shift confirms that the market is firmly in the hands of sellers who cannot find takers, as mortgage lending standards tighten and the pool of liquidity dries up.

Investors and home-seekers alike must remain skeptical of data points that suggest a broad-based recovery. Localized price jumps are often isolated incidents tied to specific development projects or relocation demand, not a nationwide trend. As the Lee Jae-myung government navigates these structural imbalances, the most prudent approach is to prioritize data-driven analysis over the speculative hype pushed by vested industry insiders. With auctions on the rise and transaction volume stalling, the market is signaling that the days of guaranteed capital gains are firmly in the rearview mirror.
