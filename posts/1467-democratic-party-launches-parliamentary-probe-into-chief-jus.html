Democratic Party Launches Parliamentary Probe into Chief Justice Amid Judicial Tensions

October 26, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party has formally initiated a parliamentary investigation into Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, escalating a high-stakes battle over judicial accountability following a series of controversial court rulings.

  • The probe targets alleged power abuse, systemic trial delays, and perceived bias within the Supreme Court under the leadership of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.
  • The move was triggered by the recent acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, a ruling that reformists within the ruling party claim ignores public common sense and protects the establishment.
  • The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition party, has slammed the investigation as "political stalking" and a "trick" to intimidate the judiciary.
  • The political standoff has contributed to a seven-month vacancy in Supreme Court justice seats, paralyzing the nation’s highest legal body and delaying justice for thousands of citizens.

The "Ladder Strategy" vs. Immediate Impeachment

The Democratic Party (DP) is currently pursuing what observers call a "ladder strategy." Rather than rushing to an immediate impeachment motion—which carries the risk of being rejected by the Constitutional Court—the party leadership, including Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok, is using the parliamentary investigation to build a public case first. By exposing trial delays and the concentration of administrative power in the Chief Justice’s office, the DP aims to create the necessary political momentum for structural reform. However, this cautious approach has faced internal criticism. Cho Kuk, leader of the Rebuilding Korea Party (a smaller progressive party), argued that the DP has already missed the "golden time" for judicial reform by hesitating while public anger was at its peak.

In my view, the DP is walking a fine line between satisfying its core supporters and maintaining the stability of the state. By opting for an investigation over immediate impeachment, the party is trying to avoid a "boomerang effect" where a failed impeachment could provide the judiciary with a political shield. However, with the public increasingly frustrated by trial bottlenecks, the DP must produce tangible evidence of misconduct quickly, or risk appearing as though they are simply using the legislature to bully a co-equal branch of government.

Judicial Independence or Political Retribution?

The opposition PPP, led by Floor Leader Jeong Jeom-sig, views this move as a direct assault on the independence of the courts. They argue that the DP is "testing the waters" for impeachment to pressure judges ahead of trials involving the current administration. The PPP has challenged the DP to file a formal impeachment motion if they have evidence of wrongdoing, rather than using a parliamentary probe to "tame" the Chief Justice. This conflict is further complicated by the DP's push for a "Special Counsel Act on Manipulated Indictments," which seeks to reinvestigate cases from the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the former leader who was impeached and is currently imprisoned.

The current deadlock over judicial appointments is perhaps the most damaging aspect of this confrontation. With Supreme Court seats remaining vacant for over half a year, the "bottleneck" in the legal system is no longer just a political talking point—it is a functional crisis. While the DP blames the Chief Justice for failing to lead reforms, the PPP blames the ruling party for holding up the confirmation process. Ultimately, this battle is about more than just one Chief Justice; it is a fundamental struggle over who holds the final authority to define "justice" in the post-Yoon era under President Lee Jae-myung’s government.
