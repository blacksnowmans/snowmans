Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon Acquitted in Controversial Appellate Ruling Over Illegal Polling Allegations

October 26, 2026

A South Korean appellate court has overturned a lower court's finding to acquit Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon of illegal political funding charges, sparking intense public backlash over judicial bias and political favoritism.

  • What happened: Senior Judge Gu Hoe-geun of the Seoul High Court cleared Mayor Oh, classifying money routed through intermediaries to political broker Myung Tae-kyun for unpublished election surveys as a "private transaction."
  • What it changes: The acquittal removes the immediate threat of a Seoul mayoral by-election, allowing Oh to remain in office while insulating key conservative figures tied to the polling scandal.
  • Who is affected: Seoul citizens face continued leadership under Oh despite widespread skepticism, with 74.2% of surveyed residents demanding a separate criminal investigation into bribery allegations surrounding his flagship river ferry project.
  • The political backdrop: The decision intensifies public criticism of Chief Justice Jo Hee-de’s judiciary, which opponents accuse of partisan overreach and protecting conservative politicians.

A Flawed Legal Logic Sparks Judicial Distrust

The appellate ruling completely flips a lower court judgment that had previously recognized illicit campaign finance transactions involving broker Myung Tae-kyun, raising sharp questions about judicial consistency. The bench acknowledged that funds flowed from Oh’s campaign circle through an intermediary to Myung for private polling during the 2021 Seoul mayoral race. However, the court bizarrely ruled that because the intermediaries were not personally close when the money changed hands, the arrangement was merely a private business deal rather than a coordinated scheme to bypass South Korea's Political Funds Act.

By over-applying the principle of giving the defendant the benefit of the doubt, the bench ignored paper trails, text messages, and call logs, effectively creating a loophole for politicians to hide behind third-party brokers. Critics argue this decision reflects a troubling trend under Chief Justice Jo Hee-de, where conservative-leaning judges have delayed crucial trials and issued lenient rulings for figures connected to imprisoned former President Yoon Suk-yeol, while clashing with the constitutional authority of President Lee Jae-myung’s administration.

Mounting Scandal Surrounds Oh’s Municipal Projects

While the acquittal grants Oh temporary political survival, his municipal governance remains deeply troubled. Public attention has rapidly shifted toward his controversial "Hangang Bus" river ferry initiative. The project faces growing accusations of supplier rebates, inflated viability assessments, and opaque contracting, prompting more than seven in ten Seoul residents to call for an immediate prosecution probe. Though Oh has evaded a criminal conviction in court for now, his administrative credibility continues to crumble under the weight of unresolved municipal scandals.
