Apple’s $2,400 ‘iPhone Duo’ Foldable Faces Backlash Over Snapping Hinges and Cracked Screens

October 26, 2026

Apple’s first foldable smartphone, the "iPhone Duo," is facing a wave of consumer fury following reports that screens are cracking and internal hinges are breaking within hours of unboxing.

  • What happened: Early buyers and tech reviewers reported horizontal display cracks on the 3.29 million won (about $2,400) flagship device, with some screens failing in under six hours and after fewer than 20 folds.
  • The root cause: Hardware teardowns reveal that thin metal components inside the custom anti-reverse hinge mechanism snap under pressure, physically striking and cracking the flexible display panel from beneath.
  • Customer service contrast: Apple is charging $49 for screen protector replacements, drawing sharp criticism in South Korea, where market leader Samsung Electronics offers one free replacement during the first year.
  • Who is affected: Early adopters worldwide face significant delays for replacements due to ongoing supply shortages and reported manufacturing yield issues.

A Structural Misstep in Apple's First Foldable Hardware

The controversy escalated rapidly after early buyers on Reddit and social media posted photos showing identical horizontal cracks across their screens. Extreme durability tests by major tech creators, including JerryRigEverything and Korean tech channels, reproduced the failures instantly. Applying slight reverse pressure or introducing minor dust into the hinge caused display blackouts and audible cracking noises. Hardware teardowns by outlets such as MacRumors confirm that the failure is not a defective display layer, but an engineered hinge flaw: undersized metal stoppers designed to prevent reverse bending break off under normal operating stress and puncture the OLED panel from behind.

Premium Pricing Meets Aggressive Service Policies

Compounding user frustration is Apple’s rigid post-purchase support. While Samsung Electronics built customer goodwill over multiple foldable generations by offering a free screen film replacement in the first 12 months, Apple immediately charges $49 for the same service. Furthermore, media reports indicate that Apple internally rejected up to 40% of initial production runs due to quality control failures. With unit yields struggling and replacement stock scarce, buyers who paid top dollar are left waiting weeks for repairs or exchanges.

The launch highlights the formidable technical hurdles of foldable engineering, where complex multi-layer stress management cannot be solved by brand appeal alone. Without an official acknowledgment of the design flaw and a more accommodating repair policy, Apple risks alienating its most loyal customer base in high-end smartphone markets.
