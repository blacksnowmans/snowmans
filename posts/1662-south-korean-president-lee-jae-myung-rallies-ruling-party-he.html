South Korean President Lee Jae-myung Rallies Ruling Party Heavyweights Amid Economic Headwinds

October 29, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung convened a high-stakes dinner with the Democratic Party of Korea’s most senior lawmakers on October 28, issuing a firm directive for the ruling party to step up, align with the presidential office, and deliver practical relief for struggling households.

  • Who attended: President Lee hosted veteran lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party who have served five or more terms in the National Assembly at the presidential office.
  • The core demand: Lee urged the party leadership to proactively push economic legislation rather than waiting for executive cues, emphasizing that the ruling majority bears ultimate responsibility for resolving livelihood hardships.
  • Conduct and unity: Lawmakers were warned to keep their public conduct grounded in public expectations and to take the lead in healing social divisions, rather than getting bogged down in factional clashes.
  • The veterans' pledge: Senior parliamentarians agreed to pool their legislative experience to back the administration's policy priorities and smooth friction within the party ranks.

Disciplining the Majority Party

The meeting at the presidential office was far from a ceremonial social call. Facing rising consumer prices and severe cost-of-living pressures, the Lee administration is feeling the heat. While the conservative opposition continues to clash with the government in parliament, Lee made it clear that the public will judge the ruling bloc by its results, not by its excuses. His warning that lawmakers must match "public eye-level expectations" in both speech and conduct serves as a sharp reminder: an arrogant or disorganized ruling party quickly forfeits public trust.

By summoning lawmakers who have won five or more elections, Lee is tapping into institutional memory to steady the ship. These veteran politicians are expected to bridge the gap between ambitious newer lawmakers and the executive branch, ensuring that government policy translates into concrete legislation and budgetary support without legislative misfires.

From Political Dinners to Measurable Relief

The Democratic Party now faces the difficult task of governing through economic headwinds while continuing to rebuild state institutions damaged during Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure. That double burden requires absolute cohesion between the executive branch and the legislature. If veteran lawmakers act merely as an echo chamber for executive orders, public skepticism will only grow.

Voters do not cast ballots to watch politicians exchange pleasantries over dinner. Whether this meeting marks a real turning point depends entirely on what happens on the floor of the National Assembly. Unless this alignment yields immediate, visible relief for ordinary citizens, even the most seasoned political wisdom will look like empty 여의도 (Yeouido) theater.
