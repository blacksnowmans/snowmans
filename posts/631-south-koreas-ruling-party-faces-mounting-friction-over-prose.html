South Korea’s Ruling Party Faces Mounting Friction Over Prosecutorial Reform and Declining Approval

September 10, 2026

  • What happened: President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating has stalled in the low 40s (around 43.3%) for eight consecutive weeks, while a fast-tracked prosecutorial reorganization plan pushes ahead through the Ministry of Justice without proper parliamentary oversight.
  • What it changes: The rushed timeline—aiming for an October launch with thousands of staff transferred and 140,000 backlog cases dumped on the police—risks a botched reform that could paralyze the justice system and backfire politically on the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, South Korea's main liberal party.
  • Who is affected: President Lee’s core base, who feel alienated by internal party rules disputes, alongside law enforcement agencies bracing for an unmanageable caseload and institutional infighting.

South Korean politics is currently defined by a widening chasm between political elites and the grassroots supporters who put them in power. President Lee's stagnant approval ratings are not merely a reflection of routine policy disagreements; they signal a deep, growing frustration among a base that views politics not as blind fandom, but as a strategic tool for systemic reform. When party leadership bypasses democratic norms—such as controversies during recent convention rules debates—it alienates the very people whose mobilization forms the foundation of the administration.

Compounding these political vulnerabilities is a high-stakes standoff over prosecutorial reform. The Ministry of Justice has advanced a reorganization plan for a new prosecution agency, retaining a 2,292-person staff while bypassing the National Assembly’s Legislation and Judiciary Committee. Critics argue this creates a bureaucratic loophole rather than true reform. By rushing an October launch and offloading 140,000 backlog cases onto an already strained police force, the administration risks handing its conservative critics a ready-made narrative of incompetence, while alienating progressive voters who demand thorough, uncompromised structural change.
