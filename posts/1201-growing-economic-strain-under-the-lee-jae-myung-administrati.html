Growing Economic Strain Under the Lee Jae-myung Administration Sparks Public Backlash

October 16, 2026

Public frustration is mounting across South Korea as the administration of President Lee Jae-myung faces sharp criticism over market turbulence, record small-business failures, and a worsening cost-of-living squeeze.

  • Equities sink: The benchmark KOSPI index breached the key 5,500 threshold amid heavy declines in national champions like tech giant Samsung Electronics, wiping out retail investors' savings and retirement funds.
  • Small businesses fold: Bankruptcy filings and business closures among small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and self-employed store owners have hit historic highs as commercial loan delinquency rates surge.
  • Housing ladder fractures: South Korea's traditional jeonse system (a unique lease model based on large lump-sum deposits rather than monthly rent) is vanishing, pushing tenants into expensive monthly leases.
  • Inflation erodes incomes: Rapid food and commodity inflation has outpaced statutory minimum wage increases, reducing real household wages and dampening domestic consumption.

The Growing Divide Between Official PR and Street Reality

Hopes were initially high that the center-left Democratic Party government under President Lee Jae-myung would stabilize the economy following the chaotic turmoil seen during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's time in office. However, those expectations are rapidly giving way to disillusionment. Critics note a widening chasm between the presidential office's upbeat rhetoric—where ruling-party figures such as lawmaker Kim Min-seok tout macroeconomic indicators and national prestige—and the bleak financial reality confronting ordinary citizens.

The distress is particularly visible in local commercial districts. Burdened by elevated interest rates and sluggish domestic demand, mom-and-pop entrepreneurs who survived previous downturns through emergency borrowing are reaching their credit limits. With insolvency rising, shuttered storefronts and commercial lease vacancies increasingly line neighborhood streets, signaling deep cracks in the country's employment foundation.

Structural Distortions and the Call for Practical Policy Pivots

Key progressive economic policies have also produced harsh side effects on everyday livelihoods. Regulatory interventions in the property market have accelerated the disappearance of jeonse contracts, leaving first-time job seekers and young families burdened by steep monthly rents. At the same time, rapid minimum wage increases designed to bolster lower-income households have driven struggling shopkeepers to cut staff and work solo, accelerating job losses without restoring real purchasing power.

Addressing these converging headwinds will require the Lee administration to move past defensive public relations and address core market distortions. Pressure is growing from small merchants, retail investors, and the main conservative opposition, the People Power Party, for pragmatic debt relief, cross-party consensus, and practical policy adjustments before the broader economy sinks into an extended slump.
