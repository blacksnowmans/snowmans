South Korea's Left Warns President Lee Jae-myung Is Dismantling His Own Coalition

October 29, 2026

Prominent liberal commentator Rhyu Si-min has issued a sharp warning that President Lee Jae-myung is alienating his core progressive base, drawing uncomfortable parallels to the political unraveling of his predecessor, Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

  • The critique: Speaking on the political commentary channel Park Si-hyung TV, Rhyu argued that the Lee administration is actively dismantling the progressive coalition rather than broadening it, even as presidential approval ratings hover precariously in the 30% range.
  • Online friction: President Lee sparked widespread backlash after sharing a post from FM Korea, an online forum often associated with anti-immigrant sentiment, which questioned priority access to public postpartum care centers for multicultural families.
  • Staff departures: Signs of strain are mounting inside the ruling camp, highlighted by the resignation of former Justice Minister Jung Sung-ho—a long-time confidant who openly urged the president to stop reacting impulsively on social media.
  • Electoral risk: Commentators warn that an insular, "pro-Lee" faction risks driving out mainstream liberals, potentially dividing the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) and gifting an advantage to the main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP).

Echoes of Conservative Self-Destruction

The alarm sounding across South Korea’s progressive establishment stems from a haunting precedent. During his presidency before being impeached and imprisoned, Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol dismantled his own conservative coalition through insularity and unilateral governance. Now, liberal intellectuals like Rhyu warn that President Lee is repeating that exact trajectory by reducing a broad political movement to a narrow, loyalist vehicle. In South Korean progressive history, voters backed past presidents like Kim Dae-jung and Roh Moo-hyun not out of personal fealty, but as instruments to carry out systematic reform. Sidelining established wings of the Democratic Party threatens to break that contract.

Governance by Smartphone vs. Institutional Reform

The president’s recent digital conduct has only deepened anxiety among his supporters. Instead of tackling South Korea’s severe regional disparities—where commercial postpartum facilities are shuttering and public infrastructure is desperately needed—President Lee leaned into cultural grievance politics by validating an internet post targeting multicultural households. In aging rural counties, immigrant mothers are keeping communities afloat, making public maternal support an essential demographic policy rather than a privilege to be contested.

Rather than providing structural solutions, chasing fleeting engagement on polarizing online forums signals a troubling lack of discipline at the top. For a presidency that campaigned on bold administrative execution, alienating core voters while taking cues from antagonistic internet communities is a fast track to political isolation.
