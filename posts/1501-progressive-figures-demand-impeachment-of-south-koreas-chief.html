Progressive Figures Demand Impeachment of South Korea’s Chief Justice Over Alleged Bias

October 26, 2026

South Korean progressive commentators and political figures are mounting a campaign to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae, accusing the judiciary of weaponizing court rulings to protect conservative elites while aggressively targeting progressive leaders.

  • Impeachment Push: Prominent voices, including former lawmaker Choi Kang-wook, are urging the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), South Korea's ruling progressive party, to immediately move to impeach Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae for failing to uphold judicial neutrality.
  • Allegations of Selective Justice: Critics argue the courts are stalling trials for key figures involved in former leader Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's failed December 3 martial law attempt—such as former Deputy Prime Minister Choi Sang-mok—while historically fast-tracking cases against progressives, including President Lee Jae-myung during his time in opposition.
  • Ruling Party Hesitation: The DPK is facing sharp internal criticism for relying on ineffective parliamentary investigations rather than using its legislative majority to hold top judges accountable.
  • Reshaping the Political Landscape: Recent court decisions favoring conservative leaders like Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon have altered election dynamics, raising concerns among progressives about a potential conservative revival.

A Judiciary Accused of Political Calculations

The campaign against Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae reflects mounting public frustration over perceived judicial double standards following the downfall of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. While Mr. Yoon remains incarcerated after his historic impeachment and removal from office for leading an insurrection, commentators argue that conservative networks within the legal system are systematically insulating his key accomplices. By dragging out procedural motions and delaying trials until routine judicial transfers take place, the courts stand accused of using administrative delay as a political shield for conservative allies.

This approach stands in stark contrast to the judiciary's past swift actions against progressive figures, such as the rapid handling of election law cases involving President Lee Jae-myung when he served as opposition leader. Critics contend that under Chief Justice Jo, the Supreme Court has abandoned deliberate legal debate in favor of politically driven outcomes, undermining its constitutional mandate as an independent safeguard of democracy.

Legislative Passivity Risks Long-Term Political Fallout

Despite holding a firm majority in the National Assembly, the ruling DPK has drawn sharp criticism from its base for taking a cautious stance. Rather than pursuing direct impeachment against Chief Justice Jo, party leadership has leaned on non-binding parliamentary investigations—measures that lack enforcement power over judges who refuse to testify. Critics charge that DPK lawmakers are overly focused on upcoming party nominations and avoiding political backlash, even welcoming judicial rulings that preemptively canceled difficult local elections.

This hesitation may prove costly. By failing to decisively challenge judicial overreach, the ruling party risks demoralizing its reform-minded base and ceding political momentum to conservative forces like the main opposition People Power Party. Confronting judicial bias is not merely a tactical maneuver, but an essential step to ensure political accountability and preserve constitutional order.
