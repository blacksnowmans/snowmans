Support for Kim Min-seok Plummets Amidst "Red Guard" Controversy

November 06, 2026

Support for Kim Min-seok, a prominent figure within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, has cratered to 25.6%, signaling deep voter dissatisfaction with the party’s recent internal power struggles and policy instability.

  • Falling Approval: Kim’s approval rating has hit a record low, fueled by backlash over alleged "Red Guard" tactics—where supporters used aggressive, organized smears against party rivals.
  • The "Cult" Allegation: The party is reeling from evidence that supporters linked opponents to the Shincheonji Church, a controversial religious group, to manipulate primary results.
  • Public Rebuke: Recent polling shows that 55% of respondents support public criticisms made by author Rhyu Si-min, who slammed internal party factions for undermining President Lee Jae-myung.
  • Legislative Fallout: In a symbolic break from Kim’s influence, the party’s parliamentary caucus recently voted nearly unanimously (166 to 1) to expel a legislator linked to these controversies.

The Failure of "Politics by Smear"

The Democratic Party is currently grappling with a self-inflicted crisis. By deploying "Red Guard" tactics—coordinated online attacks designed to silence dissent—Kim’s camp successfully alienated the party’s core base. The strategy of labeling opponents as being in league with the Shincheonji Church backfired spectacularly once the public realized these were baseless, bad-faith smears. This has led to the derogatory label "Yucheonji-dang" (The Shincheonji-Democratic Party) circulating online, a damaging brand for a party that should be focusing on the legislative agenda of the Lee Jae-myung administration.

The internal frustration is compounded by a lack of policy consistency. From mortgage regulations to financial oversight, the government’s shifts have left markets and households confused. When a party spends its political capital on factional purges rather than coherent governance, the public responds at the ballot box—or in this case, in the approval ratings.

Internal Reform vs. Partisan Loyalty

The recent intervention by author Rhyu Si-min, a highly influential public intellectual, represents a turning point. By openly criticizing those who prioritize factional interests over the President’s success, Rhyu has given voice to a silent majority of party members who are tired of the infighting. The fact that his critique resonated with 55% of the public suggests that the party’s traditional base is no longer willing to tolerate "politics as usual."

The near-unanimous vote in the parliamentary caucus to expel a controversial lawmaker shows that the winds are shifting within the halls of the National Assembly. Legislators appear to be sensing that the tide of public opinion has turned against the old-guard "Red Guard" style of politics. For the Democratic Party, the path forward is clear: abandon the divisive, personality-driven framing that has characterized the last few months and return to the substantive, people-focused policy work that got President Lee Jae-myung elected in the first place. If they fail to do so, the 25% approval floor may not hold.
