Motorcycle Myths: What Korean Riders Get Wrong About Clutches, Braking, and Engine Heat

September 11, 2026

  • Clutch control: Using the clutch at 2,000+ RPM is essential for smooth engine rotation rather than something to avoid entirely.
  • Emergency braking: Relying purely on brakes without using engine braking in the initial seconds leaves riders vulnerable in traffic.
  • Cooling engines: Coasting in neutral to save an overheating engine actually hurts liquid-cooled bikes by slowing down the water pump.
  • Core takeaway: Fancy mechanical tricks and debates pale in comparison to basic defensive driving.

Every online motorcycle community has its eternal debates: how long to ride the clutch, whether to pull it during an emergency stop, and how to keep engines from cooking in summer traffic. When Korean motorcycle creators Motojob and Ilsik sat down to thrash out these points, it exposed a broader truth: most riders operate on pure gut feeling rather than mechanical reality.

The core issue is that internet forums are full of well-meaning old wives' tales that do more harm than good. From treating clutch discs like fragile heirlooms that must never be slipped to coasting toward traffic lights in neutral to "save" the engine, riders often abuse their machines out of a misplaced desire to protect them. Modern liquid-cooled engines and high-compression setups require actual mechanical engagement—like keeping the RPMs in a healthy sweet spot—to function properly.

Ultimately, all the technical nitpicking about gear-downs and thermal efficiency misses the forest for the trees. As the creators noted, no amount of advanced braking technique will save a rider who ignores road hazards and rides aggressively. Mastering your bike's mechanics is important, but it is only a tool to serve the ultimate goal: defensive riding that gets you home safely.
