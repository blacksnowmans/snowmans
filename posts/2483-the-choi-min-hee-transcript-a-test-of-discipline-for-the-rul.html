The Choi Min-hee Transcript: A Test of Discipline for the Ruling Party

November 18, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is grappling with internal strife after a leaked private transcript of Representative Choi Min-hee exposed a strategic effort by detractors to weaponize private conversations against party members.

  • The Incident: A private transcript of Representative Choi Min-hee was leaked and published by Sisa Journal, a major investigative weekly, sparking a debate over "legalistic" attacks within the ruling party.
  • The Context: Despite Choi’s remarks reportedly defending President Lee Jae-myung, selective edits by extremist-aligned leakers have turned the incident into a tool for factional isolation.
  • The Internal Friction: The controversy has triggered harsh criticism of senior DPK leaders for their "cowardly neutrality" and of first-term lawmakers for prioritizing personal re-election over political principles.
  • The Precedent: Party insiders are calling for a return to the "self-purification" tactics used during the previous Moon Jae-in administration to sideline divisive fringe groups that threaten party unity.

The Failure of Leadership and the "Rubber Stamp" Freshmen

The silence of senior DPK members is particularly telling in this crisis. While heavyweights like Choo Mi-ae, the former Justice Minister and party leader, have called for the party to protect its internal democracy and self-cleansing capabilities, many other veterans are hiding behind a "both sides are at fault" narrative. This risk-averse behavior leaves the party vulnerable to influence from hardcore YouTube influencers who profit from magnifying minor internal disagreements. By refusing to take a stand, these senior figures are essentially allowing the party's agenda to be dictated by the loudest, most extreme voices in the digital space.

Furthermore, the current crop of first-term lawmakers has failed to provide the "breath of fresh air" expected of them. Instead of driving reform, many have become "rubber stamps" for the party establishment, paralyzed by the fear of losing their next nomination. When personal survival becomes the primary goal, the party loses its ability to engage in healthy internal criticism. This creates a vacuum where selective leaks and character assassinations, like those targeting Choi, can flourish without being checked by a principled center.

Purging the Old Politics of Division

The current situation mirrors the "Ddong-pa-ri" (housefly) incident during the Moon Jae-in administration—a period where divisive, extremist trolls masquerading as supporters sowed deep discord within the party. Back then, the DPK leadership successfully isolated these elements to maintain a unified front. The current administration under President Lee Jae-myung faces a similar challenge. If the party cannot distinguish between genuine policy debate and malicious sabotage disguised as "information," it risks losing its legitimacy as a governing body.

Ultimately, the Choi Min-hee transcript is a symptom of a structural weakness. Using private conversations as legal or political ammunition against colleagues is a breach of the basic trust required for a functional political organization. For the DPK to support the Lee Jae-myung government effectively, it must regain its ability to self-regulate and move past the "old politics" of factional warfare. The party’s survival depends on whether it can prioritize its collective future over the petty interests of its most divisive supporters and its most timid representatives.
