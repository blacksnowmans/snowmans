Factional Warfare and Scandal Send South Korea’s Ruling Party Ratings Plummeting

November 17, 2026

Severe internal factional warfare and targeted disciplinary purges within South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) have triggered a devastating collapse in public support, plunging party approval into the 20s and threatening President Lee Jae-myung’s governing momentum.

  • What happened: Senior DPK leader Kim Min-seok launched an ethics probe into colleague Choi Min-hee using a month-old leaked recording, triggering allegations of a manufactured purge to divert attention from leadership missteps.
  • Escalating scandals: The conflict erupted alongside death threats against Choi in an internal party group chat and public outrage over her remarks accusing photojournalists at the National Assembly of acting like "hyenas."
  • Voter backlash: A mid-November poll by Every Research showed DPK support collapsing to 27.2%, trailing the main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) at 41.5%, while President Lee Jae-myung’s disapproval hit 61.9%.
  • Factional mobilization: A block of 55 freshman DPK lawmakers, dubbed the "Deomincho 55," mobilized to support leadership’s crackdowns, alienating party reformers and core voters.

Weaponized Leaks and Double Standards

The sudden escalation centers on DPK Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok, who sat on a private recording of Choi Min-hee for nearly a month before abruptly calling for an ethics investigation. The timing appears calculated to deflect from Kim’s own declining support and recent controversies involving allied lawmakers like Park Sung-joon and Park Hee-seung. While leadership moved at record speed to target Choi, serious blunders by pro-leadership figures were quietly overlooked, exposing a glaring double standard that has eroded trust among party loyalists.

At the same time, internal party discipline has broken down entirely. Choi faced horrific sexual abuse and death threats from an anonymous member inside an authenticated party group chat, forcing her to file charges at Yeongdeungpo Police Station. Rather than uniting to address these threats or manage Choi's friction with press associations, DPK leadership allowed the controversies to be weaponized for factional advantage, deepening the sense of moral failure within the ruling camp.

A Dangerous Fallout for President Lee

The political price of this infighting is disastrously steep. The DPK’s traditional base—voters in their 40s and 50s—has deserted the party in droves. In the 40s demographic, DPK support sank to 27.0% against the PPP’s 42.1%, while many 50s voters shifted to the minor liberal Rebuilding Korea Party. Meanwhile, a lawmaker's reported insult toward a citizen protesting the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol highlighted a growing disconnect between DPK politicians and the public.

By prioritizing internal power struggles over legislative reform, DPK leaders have handed the opposition a major victory. With President Lee Jae-myung appearing reluctant to intervene, the party risks squandering its mandate and paralyzing the administration's reform agenda.
