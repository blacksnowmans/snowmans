South Korea’s Ruling Party Pushes Unprecedented Impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice

November 23, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) has officially committed to passing an impeachment motion against Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de in the National Assembly by December, setting off a fierce constitutional clash over judicial independence.

  • What happened: The ruling DPK launched a dedicated task force to fast-track Chief Justice Jo Hee-de’s impeachment, aiming for a full parliamentary vote before the end of the year.
  • What it changes: To blunt accusations of subverting court decisions, the DPK removed the controversial substance of recent rulings—such as one involving former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo—from the impeachment grounds, pivoting exclusively to alleged procedural defects and administrative delays.
  • Who is affected: Chief Justice Jo faces immediate suspension from office if the motion passes the DPK-controlled legislature, while the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) has launched street protests, calling the effort a retaliatory strike designed to intimidate the bench and protect President Lee Jae-myung.

DPK Recalibrates Strategy to Counter Backlash

By narrowing the impeachment grounds to procedural defects—such as case assignment fairness and intentional trial delays—the DPK is attempting to build a bulletproof legal case for the Constitutional Court while avoiding charges of interfering with judicial verdicts. However, the move has exposed subtle rifts within the ruling party. Moderate figures like Representative Gwak Sang-eon have argued that the vote should be left to individual lawmakers' discretion rather than mandated as a binding party line, warning of a catastrophic political backlash if the Constitutional Court ultimately throws out the case.

Despite internal hesitation, DPK leadership is pressing forward at full speed. Party strategists have even discussed aligning the motion’s introduction with symbolic political dates to maximize public pressure on the judiciary. With the DPK holding a clear legislative majority, passing the impeachment motion in the National Assembly remains structurally straightforward, leaving the opposition with few legislative levers to block it.

Opposition Outrage and Budget Collision

The opposition PPP has responded with all-out resistance, staging major rallies in Daegu and accusing the ruling party of attempting a "political coup" against the judicial branch. Minor opposition leaders, including Reform Party head Lee Jun-seok, joined the chorus of criticism, condemning the DPK for abusing its numerical dominance. The opposition maintains that suspending the head of the judiciary undermines the constitutional separation of powers simply to force the courts into compliance with the administration.

This high-stakes gamble also threatens to derail routine parliamentary business. The DPK’s self-imposed December deadline directly overlaps with the statutory cutoff for passing next year’s national budget. As both sides dig in for an unyielding confrontation, the legislature faces complete paralysis over the state budget and major policy measures heading into the final weeks of the year.
