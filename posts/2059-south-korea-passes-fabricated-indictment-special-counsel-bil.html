South Korea Passes "Fabricated Indictment" Special Counsel Bill Targeting Former Prosecutors

November 07, 2026

South Korea’s National Assembly passed a massive special counsel bill on Wednesday to investigate allegations that prosecutors fabricated evidence and targeted political opponents during the administration of the now-imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

  • Unprecedented Scale: The bill authorizes a "mammoth" investigation team of up to 309 personnel for a period of up to 200 days, making it the largest special counsel probe in the nation's history.
  • Focus on the Past: The probe targets 12 specific cases, including the Daejang-dong land development and North Korean remittance allegations that were used against current President Lee Jae-myung before his election.
  • Political Gridlock: The People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, boycotted the vote and declared a "total struggle" outside of parliament, labeling the bill a "shield" for the President.
  • Internal Dissent: Even within the liberal bloc, some high-profile figures have raised concerns regarding the constitutionality of the bill and the speed of its passage.

A Massive Strike Against "Political Prosecutors"

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country’s ruling liberal party, used its legislative majority to override a filibuster by the conservative PPP. This marks the seventh special counsel appointed since President Lee Jae-myung took office. The probe’s primary mission is to examine whether prosecutors under the former administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who is currently incarcerated following his impeachment—coerced testimony, concealed evidence, or manipulated records to secure indictments against political rivals.

The scope of the investigation essentially creates a temporary prosecutor's office the size of a major regional branch. Investigators will have the power to dig through the "cabinets" of the elite investigative units that handled cases involving the President and other officials from the previous Moon Jae-in administration. The goal is to determine if the state's power to prosecute was weaponized into a tool for political suppression.

Constitutional Questions and Legislative Fallout

The timing of the bill has effectively frozen the National Assembly. As the PPP moves its protests to the streets, negotiations for the upcoming national budget have ground to a halt. The opposition argues that the bill is a "bulletproof" measure—a term used in Korea to describe legislation designed to protect a specific politician from legal jeopardy—and claims it undermines the foundation of the rule of law.

However, the most significant challenge may come from within the liberal camp. Cho Kuk, leader of the Rebuilding Korea Party, and some senior DPK members like Kwak Sang-eon have voiced concerns about procedural haste and potential constitutional conflicts. The core of the dilemma is the inherent "conflict of interest" in a sitting president appointing a special counsel to re-examine cases in which he was the primary target. For this probe to be viewed as a legitimate pursuit of justice rather than a cycle of political revenge, the special counsel will need to maintain a level of neutrality that silences even its most vocal critics.

The 12 Cases Under Scrutiny

The investigation will focus on several high-profile sagas that dominated the Korean news cycle for years. These include:

  • The Daejang-dong Development: Allegations of illicit profits and bribery involving city officials during Lee Jae-myung's tenure as Mayor of Seongnam.
  • Ssangbangwool North Korea Remittances: Claims that a private company illegally sent money to North Korea on behalf of the provincial government.
  • Targeted Probes: Six additional cases involving officials from the Moon Jae-in era who claim they were subjected to "dust-shaking" investigations—a Korean term for relentless, broad searches intended to find any possible dirt on a target.
"The conduct of the prosecution during the Yoon Suk-yeol years was like a locomotive without brakes. This special counsel is a necessary rite of passage to restore normalcy to a judicial system that was used as a weapon."
