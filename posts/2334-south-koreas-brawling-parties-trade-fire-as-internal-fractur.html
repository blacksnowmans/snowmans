South Korea’s Brawling Parties Trade Fire as Internal Fractures Deepen

November 15, 2026

South Korea’s political deadlock reached a boiling point on November 14 as bitter social media brawls and mounting internal feuds within both the ruling and main opposition parties completely paralyzed parliamentary work.

  • What happened: Han Dong-hoon, leader of the main opposition People Power Party (PPP), engaged in a sharp online dispute with Choi Min-hee, a lawmaker from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, over election law trials and audio recordings.
  • Ruling party unrest: Pro-government factions within the Democratic Party demanded Choi’s expulsion and launched an internal ethics inspection, claiming her recent remarks damaged party standing and dragged down approval ratings.
  • Opposition fractures: The PPP struggled with allegations of rigged internal disciplinary procedures, highlighted by a poorly attended rally in Busan led by senior official Jang Dong-hyeok and public jabs from Reform Party lawmaker Lee Jun-seok.
  • Who is affected: Everyday South Koreans, as urgent economic legislation and budget reviews remain abandoned while lawmakers focus on factional warfare.

Personal Feuds Masking Deeper Factional Cracks

The fierce exchange on social media between PPP leader Han Dong-hoon and Democratic Party lawmaker Choi Min-hee—who serves as chair of the parliamentary science and technology committee—quickly devolved into personal insults. Han accused Choi of making absurd excuses despite clear audio evidence, while Choi fired back, demanding Han stop lying and accusing him of political manipulation regarding ongoing court cases involving President Lee Jae-myung and jailed former president Yoon Suk-yeol. Beyond the online rhetoric, however, both leaders represent parties struggling to maintain control over their own ranks.

Inside the ruling Democratic Party, pro-Lee Jae-myung grassroots groups staged protests outside Choi’s constituency office, demanding her expulsion for alleged disloyalty to President Lee’s administration. Democratic Party leadership responded by launching an internal ethics probe into Choi, signaling growing intolerance for internal dissent. Meanwhile, the opposition PPP faced severe friction over claims that party leaders fabricated disciplinary actions to silence internal critics. When PPP Supreme Council member Jang Dong-hyeok traveled to Busan to lead an outdoor protest, a noticeable absence of party lawmakers turned the event into a visible boycott of the leadership.

A Complete Breakdown of Legislative Duty

Rather than addressing economic headwinds such as high inflation and weak domestic demand, both major parties are using their opponents' internal turmoil for political point-scoring. Democratic Party spokespersons mocked the PPP’s leadership breakdown, while PPP officials labeled the ruling party’s ethics probes as a factional purge. By weaponizing each other's vulnerabilities, South Korea’s political establishment has effectively abandoned policy debate, leaving critical national priorities stuck in legislative limbo.
