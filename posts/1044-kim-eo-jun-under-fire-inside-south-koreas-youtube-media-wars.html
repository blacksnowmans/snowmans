Kim Eo-jun Under Fire: Inside South Korea's YouTube Media Wars

September 23, 2026

South Korea’s dominant progressive media figures, led by veteran podcaster Kim Eo-jun, are facing coordinated attacks from both conservative critics and infighting within the liberal camp.

  • The Spark: Critics, including former lawmaker Choi Hyuk-jin, have accused Kim Eo-jun of running "political operations" rather than journalism, following controversial election forecasts.
  • The Data: Despite claims that Kim's influence is waning, a recent SisaIN media credibility survey ranked his YouTube show Kim Eo-jun's News Factory as South Korea's most trusted broadcasting program (6.3%), beating out major terrestrial broadcasters like MBC and KBS.
  • The Internal Feud: Pockets of the progressive camp have joined conservative voices in calling for tax audits and boycotts against top progressive commentators like Kim Eo-jun and Yoo Si-min.
  • The Defense: Kim has defended his strict self-management rules—avoiding alcohol, real estate speculation, and private one-on-one meetings—citing the intense trauma and state-backed surveillance he has faced since former President Roh Moo-hyun's death.

The Battle Over New Media and Legacy Ratings

The controversy highlights a deep anxiety among legacy media and political operators facing the shift to alternative digital platforms. While traditional outlets push narratives that YouTube commentators are losing ground to short-form video clips, empirical data suggests otherwise. Mainstream terrestrial evening news programs have increasingly been eclipsed by independent YouTube operations that tap into a massive public appetite for unfiltered political critique.

Yet, this immense influence has made these figures targets. Criticism has spilled over from conservative circles into the progressive bloc itself, where some former allies have turned on the very ecosystem that helped build their profiles. Accusations of "political scheming" and demands for state tax investigations into popular commentators reflect a self-destructive streak within parts of the liberal political establishment.

Surviving the Machine

To understand Kim Eo-jun's resilience, one must look at the survival playbook he adopted following the tragic suicide of former President Roh Moo-hyun in 2009. Having spent over a decade navigating hostile administrations—including those of conservative presidents Lee Myung-bak, Park Geun-hye, and Yoon Suk Yeol—Kim instituted draconian personal and financial protocols to prevent state agencies or political opponents from finding pretexts for legal entrapment.

By keeping his accounts meticulously audited, avoiding speculative investments, and strictly controlling his professional interactions to eliminate vulnerable gray areas, Kim has managed to weather years of potential legal threats. While critics dismiss these precautions as paranoia, supporters view them as necessary armor against a ruthless political establishment.
