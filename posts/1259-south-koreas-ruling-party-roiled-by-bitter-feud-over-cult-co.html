South Korea’s Ruling Party Roiled by Bitter Feud Over Cult Conspiracy Claims

October 18, 2026

An explosive public feud between prominent figures in South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has revealed how unverified cult infiltration allegations and backroom factional warfare are shaking the party in the early days of the Lee Jae-myung administration.

  • The disclosure: Former lawmaker Choe Kang-wook revealed on a political broadcast that Rep. Park Sun-won, a Democratic Party Supreme Council member and former deputy director of the National Intelligence Service, privately admitted he had no idea where allegations originated regarding the Shincheonji religious sect infiltrating the party's leadership convention.
  • The denial and legal threat: Park dismissed Choe's account entirely, claiming he does not even have Choe’s phone number, and ridiculed the former lawmaker in leaked Telegram chats with supporters while threatening criminal defamation charges.
  • The watchdog scandal: The dispute follows a separate controversy in which Kang Min-goo, a deputy chief of the party’s internal ethics watchdog, was caught on camera messaging Park about needing to "take control" of the unit to carry out an "operation" against a rival lawmaker's faction.
  • The political impact: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok quickly sacked Kang to contain the fallout, but senior figures such as Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee have openly warned that the party's disciplinary apparatus risks being weaponized for factional score-settling.

Anatomy of an Unsubstantiated Smear

The controversy traces back to the Democratic Party's national convention, where Rep. Park Sun-won raised alarms that Shincheonji—a secretive fringe religious organization frequently embroiled in political controversies—was systematically packing the party membership with "blue ribbon" directives. Park drew parallels to past allegations concerning the mobilization of 56,000 Shincheonji members in the conservative People Power Party during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was later impeached and imprisoned. However, when pressed by reporters and party officials for evidence of actual infiltration into the Democratic Party, Park failed to produce transaction logs or organizational directives, vaguely pointing to open-access internet wikis like Namuwiki before conceding that no coordinated meddling could be confirmed.

Choe’s disclosure that Park subsequently called him pleading for help—and allegedly uttered, "I don't know either, it's driving me crazy" when asked who ordered the Shincheonji narrative—cuts directly against Park’s credentials as a veteran national security bureaucrat. Rather than offering transparent documentation, Park’s decision to dismiss Choe as "that person" and casually promise lawsuits via messaging apps only magnifies suspicions that explosive claims were weaponized purely for internal advantage.

Factional Skirmishes Threaten the New Administration

The brawl is symptomatic of a deeper malaise inside the ruling party. The leaked bench message from Kang Min-goo, urging Park to help him secure party headquarters clearance so he could target fellow lawmakers, confirmed fears that internal audit machinery was being viewed as a factional weapon rather than an impartial arbiter. While party leader Kim Min-seok acted decisively by dismissing Kang, the underlying factional friction remains unresolved.

With South Korea facing severe economic headwinds, including employment shortages and inflation, public patience for palace intrigue among the ruling elite is thin. After enduring the constitutional crisis that ended in Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s removal, voters expect the Lee Jae-myung administration and its party to deliver stability. Instead, senior figures are trading lawsuits over dubious rumors, exposing an internal culture still trapped in zero-sum factional warfare.
