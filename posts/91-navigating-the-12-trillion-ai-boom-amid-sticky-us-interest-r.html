Navigating the $1.2 Trillion AI Boom Amid Sticky US Interest Rates

September 27, 2026

  • Massive Tech Spending: Hyperscale tech companies are pushing capital expenditures (CAPEX) toward $1.2 trillion, driven by relentless demand for AI infrastructure despite high borrowing costs.
  • High Yields Here to Stay: US 10-year Treasury yields hovering above 5% reflect supply dynamics, growing national debt, and term premiums rather than just economic fundamentals.
  • The Efficiency Race: Raw AI performance is now a baseline; future winners will be decided by token price efficiency and cost structures rather than heavy spending alone.
  • Korean Chip Giants: Foreign outflows during recent share buybacks by Samsung Electronics—South Korea's leading memory chip and consumer electronics giant—were routine technical adjustments, leaving the door open for passive inflows tied to SK Hynix, a major global chipmaker.

Why 5% US Bond Yields and $1.2 Trillion in Tech CAPEX Matter

Global financial markets are grappling with a paradox: US Treasury yields stubbornly holding above 5% even as mega-cap tech firms flood the AI ecosystem with unprecedented cash. While conventional wisdom suggests high rates should choke off speculative growth, the current market is governed by a different rulebook. Earnings growth—specifically surging earnings-per-share among major tech companies—is outpacing the drag of higher interest rates, proving that the AI supercycle is largely unfazed by costly debt.

At the same time, the composition of this high-rate environment points to structural pressures rather than temporary inflation spikes. With US national debt topping $40 trillion and monthly interest payments crossing $100 billion, investors are demanding a hefty "term premium" to compensate for fiscal uncertainty and a tight-lipped Federal Reserve. Yet, because capital is chasing guaranteed returns rather than hiding in safety, the AI infrastructure buildout continues to swell.

The Battle for Token Efficiency and Korean Semiconductor Flows

As capital expenditures scale toward $1.2 trillion, the AI battleground is shifting from raw model capability to token economics. Companies that cannot deliver cost-efficient AI services—such as Meta leveraging cheaper token pricing through newer models—risk being left behind, regardless of initial hype. For investors, simply buying headline AI stocks is no longer enough; the focus must shift to who is building the most efficient infrastructure, from advanced memory packaging to data center cooling.

Locally, South Korean markets are weathering this macro volatility well. Foreign investor sell-offs during recent corporate share buybacks were predictable tactical moves rather than a rejection of domestic fundamentals. Looking ahead, attention turns to upcoming institutional milestones, including passive fund flows linked to SK Hynix's American Depositary Receipt (ADR) listings and potential Nasdaq inclusion. In a market dominated by fear, the numbers—not the noise—continue to dictate where the smart money moves.
