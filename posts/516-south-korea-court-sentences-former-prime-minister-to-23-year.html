South Korea Court Sentences Former Prime Minister to 23 Years for Dec. 3 Martial Law Attempt

September 30, 2026

  • What happened: A South Korean court sentenced former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo to 23 years in prison for his role in the short-lived Dec. 3 emergency martial law declaration, officially classifying the move as an attempted "self-coup" by the previous administration.
  • What it changes: The ruling establishes a hardline judicial precedent that treats attempts to subvert the constitutional order with severe retributive penalties, completely rejecting defense arguments that framed the martial law as a "warning" or "enlightening" measure.
  • Who is affected: Former high-ranking officials facing prosecution for the martial law crisis, and anyone attempting to justify extra-legal political maneuvers under the guise of emergency powers.

The sentencing of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo marks a critical turning point in how South Korea's judiciary handles systemic attacks on democracy. Rather than treating the six-hour martial law crisis as a minor political miscalculation, the court squarely named it an internal insurrection aimed at paralyzing the constitutionally mandated legislature. The heavy sentence reflects not only the gravity of participating in a power grab alongside former President Yoon Suk-yeol, but also Han's subsequent efforts to conceal the crime through falsified documents and evasive testimony.

For an outside observer, the ruling underscores the fragility—and resilience—of South Korean democratic institutions. The court explicitly credited ordinary citizens, who physically blocked armed troops from shutting down the National Assembly, with halting the slide into authoritarianism. By handing down a 23-year prison term, the judiciary is sending an unambiguous signal to future leaders: using state power to dismantle constitutional checks and balances carries devastating legal consequences, no matter how brief the attempt or how illustrious the official's prior career.
