Democratic Party Leadership Faces Backlash Over Botched Handling of Religious Group Allegations

November 06, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is grappling with growing internal distrust after leadership mishandled rumors linking party members to Shincheonji, a controversial religious group.

  • What happened: DPK Representative Choi Min-hee confirmed that rumors of a secret "three-party alliance" between DPK figures and Shincheonji were unfounded after directly contacting the lawmakers involved.
  • Why distrust grew: DPK Leader Kim Min-seok abruptly halted an unannounced presentation on the issue, sparking online speculation that leadership was attempting a cover-up to protect parliamentary seats.
  • Information leak: Findings from an internal DPK task force on Shincheonji, led by Representative Park Sun-won and reported on October 2, were kept secret from party members before leaking to the Maeil Business Newspaper, a conservative daily.
  • Who is affected: Top DPK leadership—including Leader Kim Min-seok and former Leader Jung Chung-rae—face mounting pressure from party supporters demanding accountability, full transparency, and genuine apologies.

How Poor Communication Fueled Conspiracy Theories

The controversy stems less from the rumors themselves than from how DPK leadership responded to them. When Party Leader Kim Min-seok unilaterally stopped an unscheduled announcement regarding the Shincheonji allegations, supporters interpreted the abrupt intervention as an attempt to gag speakers. Speculation quickly spread online that the party was suppressing the issue to safeguard a 150-seat majority in the National Assembly. As Lawmaker Park Joo-min noted, apologies from leadership ring hollow when they fail to provide clear facts, leaving individual lawmakers exposed to persistent rumors.

A Call for Transparency Under the Lee Administration

The management of internal information exacerbated the crisis. A specialized task force created by Kim Min-seok and headed by Representative Park Sun-won briefed leadership on October 2, but kept the findings strictly confidential. Rather than being shared transparently with rank-and-file members, details emerged through media reports. Meanwhile, former Party Leader Jung Chung-rae has drawn criticism for refusing to issue a clear apology to members, further alienating supporters who expect high ethical standards from the ruling party during the presidency of Lee Jae-myung.

For DPK supporters who expect a clear contrast with the opaque governance seen during the presidency of Yoon Suk-yeol, the party leadership's defensive posture has damaged internal credibility. Rebuilding that trust will require DPK leadership to move beyond election-oriented damage control and embrace procedural transparency.
