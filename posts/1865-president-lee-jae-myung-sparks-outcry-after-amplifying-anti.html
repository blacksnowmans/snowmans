President Lee Jae-myung Sparks Outcry After Amplifying Anti-Multicultural Forum Post

November 02, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung has triggered intense controversy after endorsing a viral post from an internet message board on social media, signaling a potential rollback of welfare support for multicultural families.

  • What happened: President Lee shared a post on X (formerly Twitter) from FM Korea—a right-leaning online forum popular among young men—that claimed giving multicultural mothers priority access to public postpartum care centers amounts to "reverse discrimination" against native Koreans. Lee praised the post, writing, "Thank you for the good point."
  • What it changes: Presidential aides quickly scrambled to justify the remark by floating revisions to national maternal health decrees, effectively elevating fringe online grievance rhetoric into official state policy.
  • Who is affected: Immigrant and multicultural households risk losing vital public support, while vulnerable minorities are scapegoated amid broader economic anxiety.
  • Internal fallout: The misstep highlights a dysfunctional advisory circle inside the presidential office and growing passivity within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) ahead of Lee's 100th day in office.

Mainstreaming Fringe Resentment

The controversy centers on Seoul's public postpartum centers, where multicultural households—alongside persons with disabilities and single-parent families—receive secondary admission priority. The online complaint cited by President Lee argued that twin mothers should take precedence, portraying targeted welfare as an unfair privilege. The presidential office defended Lee's post as an attempt to correct policy overreach in the name of "fairness." In reality, institutional welfare programs exist precisely to assist families facing acute language, cultural, and economic barriers. Borrowing the language of an online forum to dismantle decades of welfare consensus mirrors populist scapegoating seen abroad, weaponizing public frustration against marginalized groups.

This episode also exposes a dangerous breakdown in executive discipline. Rather than vetting policy issues through institutional channels, presidential staff acted as passive courtiers, rushing to draft regulatory amendments to match the president's late-night social media habits. When senior aides refuse to provide candid pushback, unverified internet discourse easily bypasses bureaucratic oversight.

The Cost of Lone-Wolf Governance

President Lee built his political brand as an outspoken outsider who connected directly with the public on social media, bypassing mainstream media gatekeepers. Yet what served as a lifeline during his tenure as a provincial governor has turned into a strategic liability in the presidency. When the head of state engages in real-time debates over online commentary, the gravity of national policy gives way to impulsive messaging.

Compounding this isolation is the silence of the ruling DPK. Lawmakers who once aggressively defended Lee during his election campaign have largely gone quiet, leaving the president to fight rhetorical battles alone while they focus on internal party factionalism. As the administration nears its 100-day milestone with stagnant polling numbers, relying on unvetted online impulses risks alienating moderate voters and eroding the core protections owed to society's most vulnerable.
