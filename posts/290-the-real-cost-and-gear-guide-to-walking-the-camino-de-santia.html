The Real Cost and Gear Guide to Walking the Camino de Santiago

September 07, 2026

  • Realistic Budgeting: Expect to spend between $3,500 and $5,500 (approx. 5 to 7.6 million KRW) for a 30- to 40-day trek, covering flights, modest lodging in albergues (pilgrim hostels), and food.
  • Pack Smart: A 38L pack is often more practical than a smaller 30L bag to avoid constant repacking; invest in a waist pouch for quick access to essentials like passports and phones.
  • Footwear Strategy: Avoid simple bathroom slippers; opt for closed-toe, water-resistant footwear like Crocs for indoor use and rainy weather.
  • Safety First: Prioritize high-quality trekking poles, as gear failure on rough terrain can lead to serious injury.

Gear That Saves Your Trek

Many novice pilgrims mistakenly prioritize ultra-light, small backpacks, only to find they cannot fit their necessary gear, leading to daily frustration. Experienced trekkers suggest a 38L capacity over a 15L-30L bag. The extra space isn't for "more stuff," but for the physical and psychological room to organize gear without having to dump your entire bag every time you need a change of socks or shoes. Furthermore, skip the chest-mounted bags, which often cause shoulder strain. A reliable waist-mounted hip belt allows you to keep power banks, phones, and travel documents accessible at all times, saving hours of effort over the course of the 500-mile journey. When it comes to footwear, don't rely solely on running shoes. While they offer initial comfort, they wear down quickly on Spain’s rocky terrain. Look for durable, cushioned trekking shoes and carry closed-toe sandals for the hostel environment—thin, flimsy flip-flops will prove useless if you encounter rain or cold dorm floors.

Budgeting and Safety Realities

The "romantic" notion of the Camino often masks the physical labor of the trip. Between daily laundry, navigating hostels, and the relentless walking, this is an endurance test. Your budget should reflect this: while a shared albergue bed costs about $11–$13 (15,000–18,000 KRW), opting for private rooms will drive costs up to $26 (35,000 KRW) or more per night. Carrying cash in 200-euro increments is essential, as many rural vendors and hostels do not accept cards. Beyond the financials, safety is paramount. Do not treat gear as a place to cut corners—especially trekking poles. Low-quality, twist-lock poles can collapse under pressure, and in extreme cases, accidents on steep sections have resulted in severe injuries. If your physical condition falters, do not hesitate to rest or use local transport; the Camino is a marathon, not a race. Save the Spanish emergency number (112) and your local consular office contact in your phone before you depart. Ultimately, the most successful pilgrims are those who prioritize reliability over aesthetics, choosing gear that will keep them moving when the terrain gets tough.
