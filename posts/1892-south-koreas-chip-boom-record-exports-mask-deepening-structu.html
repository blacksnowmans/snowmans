South Korea's Chip Boom: Record Exports Mask Deepening Structural Risks

November 03, 2026

  • What happened: South Korea's Kospi stock index rebounded to reclaim the 5,800 level, driven by strong institutional buying, while October semiconductor exports surged by a massive 331% year-on-year.
  • What it changes: Global tech giants are moving away from spot market purchases, locking in multi-year supply deals for AI memory through 2029 to secure scarce hardware.
  • Who is affected: Local memory giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix face intense pressure as they balance massive high-end demand with rising competition in legacy chips from Chinese rivals.
  • Key political context: President Lee Jae-myung’s administration faces growing calls to upgrade national infrastructure and support domestic manufacturers as geopolitical tech tensions mount.

South Korea’s semiconductor sector is caught in a paradox of record-breaking financial success and mounting structural vulnerability. While high-bandwidth memory (HBM) used in artificial intelligence infrastructure is sold out for years, a heavy reliance on a single product category leaves the broader trade balance exposed. The recent market rebound demonstrates that investor appetite for major chipmakers remains robust, but underneath the soaring export figures lie fierce challenges from overseas competitors and internal talent retention.

Meanwhile, the global race for AI hardware is triggering friction across the entire tech supply chain. Executives like OpenAI CEO Sam Altman have faced backlash from users over memory scarcity spilling into consumer devices, while Chinese memory makers are aggressively expanding their footprint in legacy markets. For South Korean manufacturers, maintaining a technological lead is no longer just about outperforming rivals in advanced packaging—it requires defending against low-cost commoditization while managing the heavy power and environmental demands of domestic fabrication plants.
