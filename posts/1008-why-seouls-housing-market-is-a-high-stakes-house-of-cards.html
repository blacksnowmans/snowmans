Why Seoul's Housing Market is a High-Stakes House of Cards

October 12, 2026

Seoul’s soaring apartment prices—often eclipsing those in New York—are no longer just a quirky real estate headline, but an existential crisis for a generation priced out of homeownership.

  • Soaring Cost Burden: Seoul’s Price-to-Income Ratio (PIR) sits at an alarming 23, meaning an average household must save every penny for 23 years to buy an average apartment priced over 1.4 billion won (about $1 million).
  • The Redevelopment Trap: Aging complexes—from iconic old developments like Daechi-dong's Eunma Apartment to massive 1990s-era First-Phase New Towns built as suburban bedroom communities—face skyrocketing construction costs and hundreds of thousands of dollars in renovation fees that threaten to price out original owners.
  • Illusion of Affordability: Weakening Korean won exchange rates and foreign speculative buying have created an artificial price buffer, masking deep underlying economic vulnerabilities from local first-time buyers.
  • Policy Missteps: Critics argue that decades of demand-side lending curbs and punitive short-term property taxes have failed, missing the urgent need for structural tax overhauls and public supply models like land-lease homes.

The myth of bulletproof real estate is colliding with a harsh demographic reality: low birth rates, an aging population, and an economy cooling into near-zero growth. Building towering skyscrapers to replace older complexes only kicks the financial can down the road, saddling future generations with astronomical maintenance costs and the terrifying prospect of vertical slums.

Ultimately, treating real estate as an endless wealth generator rather than a basic utility has pushed Korean workers to their breaking point. Policymakers must stop relying on temporary Band-Aids and artificially propping up a bloated market. Only by facing the painful reality of a market correction and shifting toward sensible, holding-tax-heavy property policies can South Korea hope to build a sustainable housing future.
