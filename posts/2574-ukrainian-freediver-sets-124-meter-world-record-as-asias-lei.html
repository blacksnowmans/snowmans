Ukrainian Freediver Sets 124-Meter World Record as Asia's Leisure Market Expands

November 21, 2026

Ukrainian freediver Nataliia Zharkova set a new women’s Constant Weight (CWT) world record by descending 124 meters underwater on a single breath during an official AIDA competition.

  • What happened: Ukrainian athletes Nataliia Zharkova and Kateryna Sadurska achieved historic depth records, while the Philippine Department of Tourism actively courted South Korea’s fast-growing market at a local PADI festival.
  • What it changes: High-altitude training is expanding with a new freediving center planned at Kyrgyzstan’s Lake Issyk-Kul, while environmental concerns and illegal poaching incidents are forcing dive agencies to tighten safety and ethical guidelines.
  • Who is affected: Recreational divers in South Korea looking for tropical destinations, international training organizations, and competitive athletes pushing human physiology to extreme limits.

Record Depth and Asia's Booming Tourism Demand

Zharkova’s 124-meter dive using a monofin demonstrated extreme physiological efficiency and mental control, overcoming nitrogen narcosis and severe chest compression under immense water pressure. Her achievement, alongside record attempts by fellow Ukrainian athlete Kateryna Sadurska, highlights remarkable focus despite national instability back home. Meanwhile, indoor pool discipline showed equal momentum, with team Sardinia claiming nine medals and setting a new national distance record at the Torino Italian Indoor Championship.

Beyond elite athletic performance, market dynamics in Asia are shifting quickly. The Philippine Department of Tourism aggressively marketed to South Korean enthusiasts at the PADI Freediving & Mermaid Festival held in South Korea. Because domestic Korean waters are cold and seasonally restricted, South Korean divers represent a major customer base for tropical destinations like Anilao, Cebu, Bohol, and Boracay. The festival also showcased the surge of mermaid diving—a hybrid water sport combining artistic performance with monofin technique—which has evolved from a social media trend into a primary revenue driver for Korean dive centers.

Global Expansion, Environmental Ethics, and Safety

The global reach of underwater sports is extending to unconventional locations. Landlocked Kyrgyzstan announced plans to build a dedicated freediving center along the southern shore of Lake Issyk-Kul. Situated 1,600 meters above sea level, the high-altitude, non-freezing lake offers specialized atmospheric conditions that require advanced equalization skills, making it a potential hub for high-altitude dive training between Europe and Asia.

This rapid growth has amplified calls for environmental accountability and safety. At the COP31 climate conference, UNDP Goodwill Ambassador Sahika Ercumen warned of rising sea temperatures and microplastic pollution observed during deep dives. Meanwhile, news of a diver arrested in Indonesia for illegally poaching an endangered sea turtle sparked widespread outrage, prompting calls for major certification bodies like PADI, SSI, and AIDA to mandate stricter ecological ethics. Dive experts also emphasized that strict one-on-one buddy protocols must remain mandatory to prevent hypoxia-induced blackouts as the sport attracts broader mainstream interest.
