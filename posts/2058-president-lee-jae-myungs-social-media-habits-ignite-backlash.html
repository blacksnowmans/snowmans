President Lee Jae-myung’s Social Media Habits Ignite Backlash and Factional Rifts

November 06, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is facing sharp criticism as his frequent, combative social media posts and YouTube retweets deepen political divides, alienate moderate voters, and drive his approval ratings down to record lows.

  • What happened: President Lee has repeatedly used social media to target internal party critics, share controversial YouTube Shorts, and accuse opponents of "factional fanaticism," sparking debate over whether his direct communication is democratic outreach or petty score-settling.
  • What the numbers show: A poll by Yeoron Josa Gongjeong shows President Lee’s approval rating stalled at 32.6% (with disapproval at 66.1%), while 62.5% of respondents viewed his social media activity negatively due to its aggressive tone and frequency.
  • What it changes: The president's online feuds are eroding executive momentum, triggering bureaucratic inaction, and emboldening investigative agencies like the Corruption Investigation Office for High-ranking Officials (CIO) to assert pressure on the administration.
  • Who is affected: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea faces growing internal friction as hardline loyalists clash with internal dissidents, isolating President Lee from broader public support.

A Double Standard on Factionalism and Digital Politics

President Lee’s online messaging has increasingly shifted from policy promotion to defensive skirmishes. By sharing a YouTube video criticizing "slogan politics" and lecturing the public on avoiding partisan fanaticism, Lee claims to champion open debate. However, critics argue his refusal to accept scrutiny—often dismissing media coverage or arguing directly with netizens—reveals a glaring double standard. Instead of fostering national unity, his retweets act as implicit signals for his core online base to attack internal party opponents, raising concerns over factional purges ahead of upcoming parliamentary elections.

The Risk of Self-Inflicted Political Isolation

Appealing strictly to a passionate core base is proving dangerous for executive governance. When approval ratings remain trapped in the low 30s, civil servants grow risk-averse and policy execution stalls. Furthermore, a weakened administration opens the door for the judiciary and investigative bodies to turn their focus toward government insiders. Progressive supporters who remember the prosecutorial overreach during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's presidency now express concern that the current administration is exposing itself to similar vulnerabilities through emotional, undisciplined leadership.

By confining his focus to online battles, President Lee risks becoming like a dolphin stranded on a sandbar, isolated from the broader electorate. Rather than following the inclusive leadership of past Democratic presidents like Kim Dae-jung, Lee’s reliance on social media as a political weapon threatens to paralyze his own administration from within.
