South Korea’s Ruling Party Pushes Election Law Overhaul and Chief Justice Impeachment

November 05, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea is mounting a high-stakes campaign to repeal a controversial election law clause criminalizing campaign speech while moving to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae over alleged constitutional violations.

  • The legislative push: A bill pending before the National Assembly’s Legislation and Judiciary Committee would scrap the crime of "publishing false facts regarding actions" under the Public Official Election Act, which critics argue penalizes subjective memory and chills free expression.
  • The judicial clash: Lawmakers are demanding a parliamentary inquiry and impeachment proceedings against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, accusing the top court of judicial overreach and permitting trials conducted by parties lacking legitimate adjudicative authority.
  • Legal ramifications: If enacted before final rulings are handed down, the amendment would legally dismiss (myeon-so) ongoing prosecutions initiated under the challenged statute.
  • Partisan battle lines: The People Power Party, South Korea's main conservative opposition, has denounced the bill as a corrupt legislative shield for political figures, while the ruling camp argues it dismantles a statute long weaponized for selective prosecution.

Decriminalizing Campaign Speech

In South Korea's electoral arena, candidates have frequently faced criminal charges over subjective phrasing, imprecise recollections, and off-the-cuff remarks. Advocates of the reform argue that during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who was subsequently impeached and is currently imprisoned—state prosecutors routinely exploited the ambiguous "false facts" clause to target political rivals. By criminalizing campaign statements rather than allowing voters to judge their credibility at the ballot box, the statute effectively placed courts and prosecutors above democratic debate.

The opposition People Power Party contends that repealing the clause is designed to retroactively rescue ruling-bloc politicians currently facing trial. However, reform-minded lawmakers within the Democratic Party, backed by party leader Kim Min-seok, insist that passing the revision before final verdicts are delivered is the cleanest way to resolve the issue. In their view, if a law is inherently flawed, terminating ongoing proceedings through formal dismissal is the natural constitutional remedy rather than a backroom political deal.

Confronting the Top Court

The concurrent push to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae represents a severe escalation between the legislature and the judiciary. The most explosive charge leveled against Cho is that the Supreme Court oversaw or tolerated proceedings assigned to individuals lacking lawful adjudicative power. Critics view this not as a mere administrative error, but as a breach of constitutional due process that invalidates court rulings at their foundation.

Under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, the ruling coalition sees legislative reform and judicial accountability as two sides of the same coin. Proponents maintain that launching a National Assembly investigation and pursuing impeachment are necessary constitutional checks to end judicial exceptionalism, curb overreach, and reset the balance of power across the branches of government.
