South Korea's Live Commerce Shift: Short-Form Content, AI Dominance, and Global Fee Volatility

November 13, 2026

Traditional TV home shopping in South Korea is aggressively pivoting to 60-second creator clips and artificial intelligence, while cross-border merchants face rising friction from unstable policies across major global online marketplaces.

  • Home & Shopping, a major South Korean TV home shopping network, selected 10 creator teams to produce short-form promotional videos for small businesses, replacing traditional 60-minute broadcast formats.
  • A new industry report from payment technology firm PortOne revealed that five major global e-commerce marketplaces altered their financial rules 135 times over nine months, altering seller margins roughly every two days.
  • South Korean cosmetics companies are leveraging live streaming to expand abroad, fueling a 62.7% surge in K-beauty exports to the United States.
  • Automated AI commerce is rapidly scaling in East Asia, with AI chatbots already handling over 5% of total e-commerce transaction volume in neighboring China.

Adapting to Short-Form Media and Unpredictable Platforms

The traditional Korean TV home shopping model is facing a structural crisis as viewers abandon long broadcasts for mobile video apps like TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Networks like Home & Shopping are responding by acting as talent incubators, pairing small and medium-sized enterprises with short-form creators. Success in modern digital retail no longer depends on buying 60-minute television slots, but on capturing a consumer's attention within the first few seconds of a mobile scroll.

At the same time, South Korean sellers operating on international marketplaces are experiencing severe margin compression due to platform dependency. With major global e-commerce platforms shifting financial rules 135 times in nine months—70% of which directly impacted profitability—small vendors are struggling with unexpected fee increases and payout delays. To protect their businesses, merchants are increasingly forced to diversify their distribution channels and build direct-to-consumer platforms rather than relying on a single global marketplace.

AI Automation Versus Local Authenticity

The rise of artificial intelligence in live commerce is creating a dual-track market. In China, 24-hour automated AI shopping bots now generate over 5% of e-commerce transactions, drastically reducing streamer fatigue and labor overhead. South Korean retailers are taking note, rapidly integrating AI into product recommendations, inventory management, and customer support to protect their margins.

However, automation cannot fully replace human connection. Regional initiatives—such as live-streamed farm-to-table sales led by local agricultural producers in Goheung County—demonstrate that trust and authenticity remain critical selling points. As AI takes over routine transactions, human creators will need to double down on storytelling, personal interaction, and brand building to retain consumer loyalty.
