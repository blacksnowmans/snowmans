Prominent Commentator Rhyu Si-min Warns Lee Jae-myung Administration Against Opportunism and Corruption

October 28, 2026

Prominent liberal commentator Rhyu Si-min warned that President Lee Jae-myung’s administration must welcome public criticism to prevent political power from rotting, while cautioning against opportunistic allies within its ranks.

  • What happened: Rhyu Si-min appeared on the popular political YouTube broadcast Park Si-young TV, offering a sharp analysis of power dynamics and commentary norms inside South Korea's progressive ecosystem.
  • What it changes: Rhyu dismissed claims that sharp commentary harms presidential approval ratings, arguing instead that suppressing critique transforms minor institutional missteps into fatal corruption.
  • Who is affected: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea and the Lee Jae-myung government, which Rhyu urged to distinguish between value-driven allies and profit-seeking opportunists who desert when poll numbers drop.
  • Disinformation addressed: Rhyu publicly debunked persistent rumors alleging he amassed a 10 billion won (about $7.2 million) fortune, comparing malicious smear campaigns to cult propaganda tactics.

Navigating the Anatomy of Power and Criticism

Rhyu used a vivid "fish market" analogy to explain the nature of political power: every fish market naturally carries a slight fishy smell, representing the minor frictions and corruption inherent to governing. However, when an administration stifles critics who point out the smell, that minor odor quickly turns into a rotting stench. Drawing parallels to the dark period during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure—whose administration systematically suppressed critical media before his impeachment and imprisonment—Rhyu stressed that President Lee Jae-myung must treat constructive criticism as a vital cleansing agent.

He also introduced a framework dividing political actors into three categories: those driven by core values, those pursuing pure self-interest, and those in between. Rhyu warned that when an administration faces inevitable drops in public support, self-interested opportunists are the first to defect. For the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, maintaining a core staff anchored in shared values rather than short-term gains remains essential to long-term stability.

Debunking Smears and Preserving Democratic Integrity

Addressing personal attacks, Rhyu directly refuted false rumors claiming his personal assets exceed 10 billion won (about $7.2 million) or even 50 billion won. He clarified that he had never owned a single stock until recently purchasing a modest holding to support President Lee’s economic vision for South Korea's capital markets. Rhyu compared the relentless propagation of financial rumors to the deceitful proselytizing methods of the Shincheonji fringe religious group, noting that such smears aim to undermine a commentator's credibility through fabricated claims of hypocrisy.

Ultimately, Rhyu emphasized that political analysis must move away from preachy, normative judgments and focus instead on providing objective, high-density information to citizens. For international observers watching South Korea's political landscape, his message serves as a reminder that the ultimate test for the Lee Jae-myung administration lies not in avoiding criticism, but in preserving transparency and accountability while managing the internal dynamics of power.
