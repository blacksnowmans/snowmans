A Play Contest Revives the Legacy of Legendary Korean Activist Rev. Moon Ik-hwan

October 18, 2026

A major playwriting competition with a total prize pool of 100 million Korean won (about $75,000) has been launched to bring the dramatic life of Rev. Moon Ik-hwan, one of South Korea's most influential pro-democracy and reunification figures, to the modern stage.

  • What happened: Organizers announced an open, anonymous call for stage scripts across all theatrical genres—including plays, musicals, and poetic dramas—with rolling submissions accepted through December 31, 2027, judged by a panel led by acclaimed film director Lee Chang-dong.
  • What it changes: Top finalist scripts will be published as a collection by Munhakdongne, a major Korean publishing house, creating a permanent repertoire for local theater troupes, university drama groups, and youth ensembles.
  • Who is behind it: The initiative fulfills a 30-year dream of Moon's family, including his son, veteran actor Moon Seong-keun, who sought outside playwrights after finding it emotionally impossible to write an objective script himself.
  • Who is affected: Contemporary theater artists and younger generations gain a new entry point into South Korea's modern struggle for civil liberties and inter-Korean peace.

Bringing History to the Stage

Rev. Moon Ik-hwan was a prominent Old Testament scholar and poet who became a moral pillar of South Korea's pro-democracy movement. Over his lifetime, he served 10 years and 3 months in prison across six separate incarcerations for defying authoritarian regimes, including the brutal Yushin dictatorship of Park Chung-hee in the 1970s. In 1989, he made national headlines by making a risky trip to Pyongyang to discuss peaceful reunification, enduring severe prosecution under South Korea's National Security Law upon his return.

Preserving Democracy Through Art

The contest serves as a cultural safeguard for Korea's modern political history. In recent years, public education curricula under conservative leadership frequently downplayed or erased the contributions of pro-democracy activists. These attempts to reshape history reached their peak during the administration of Yoon Suk-yeol, who was later impeached and is currently in prison. By bypassing textbook controversies and taking Moon's story directly to live theater, the organizers aim to ensure that South Korea's history of civilian resistance remains a living conversation rather than a forgotten past.
