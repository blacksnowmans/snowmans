South Korea’s Ruling Party Fractures Over Leader’s Closed-Door Apology for Religious Sect Scandal

November 05, 2026

Internal feuding within South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has reached a boiling point after party leader Kim Min-seok offered a tearful, closed-door apology over allegations involving Shincheonji—a controversial fringe religious group—provoking fierce backlash from rival party factions and the conservative opposition.

  • What happened: Democratic Party Chair Kim Min-seok issued a private apology during a leadership meeting on November 4 regarding allegations of ties to Shincheonji, but Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee boycotted the session, publicly accusing Kim of actively suppressing internal discussion about the group.
  • What it changes: Rival party factions and opposition lawmakers are now demanding an official probe into potential violations of the Political Parties Act, elevating a political dispute into a potential legal crisis for party leadership.
  • Who is affected: President Lee Jae-myung’s ruling administration faces unwanted legislative friction and compromised party unity, while the main opposition conservative People Power Party gains significant political leverage.

Exploding Internal Rift Over Gag Allegations

The controversy surrounding Kim Min-seok stems from alleged ties to Shincheonji, a secretive religious organization frequently accused of attempting to exert clandestine political influence in South Korea. Rather than quelling concerns, Kim’s emotional apology behind closed doors ignited widespread criticism within his own party. Senior figures inside the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—particularly allies of Supreme Council member Jung Chung-rae, including lawmakers Han Min-soo and Lee Seong-yun—rejected the private contrition as an insincere theatrical attempt to evade public accountability.

The crisis worsened significantly when Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee skipped the leadership meeting entirely and revealed that Kim had repeatedly pressured her to stop raising questions about the religious group. Lawmakers demanding a transparent investigation argue that using leadership authority to silence internal inquiries regarding external political interference may constitute a violation of South Korea's Political Parties Act. What began as a dispute over political ties has rapidly transformed into a debate over party transparency and internal democratic governance.

A Dangerous Distraction for the Ruling Administration

The main opposition conservative People Power Party (PPP) moved quickly to capitalize on the DPK's internal turmoil. With Yoon Suk-yeol currently imprisoned following his impeachment and removal from office, the conservative opposition has struggled for momentum, making the ruling party's self-inflicted crisis a welcome opportunity. PPP lawmakers immediately called for Kim's resignation and urged law enforcement agencies to launch a full investigation into the allegations.

For President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, the timing could hardly be worse. As the government attempts to pass key legislation and secure approval for national budget priorities, party leaders are instead embroiled in factional infighting. Unless Kim Min-seok provides full transparency regarding his actions and accepts concrete accountability, the scandal risks crippling the ruling party's cohesion when legislative momentum is needed most.
