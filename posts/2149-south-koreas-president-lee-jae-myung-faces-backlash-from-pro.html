South Korea’s President Lee Jae-myung Faces Backlash from Progressive Base Over Conservative Shift

November 09, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is facing mounting backlash from traditional liberal supporters who accuse his administration of abandoning the core values of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea in favor of conservative rhetoric and factional politics.

  • What happened: President Lee Jae-myung has sparked outrage among party loyalists by adopting conservative talking points, including harsh rhetoric targeting foreign workers, multicultural families, and alleged misuse of unemployment benefits.
  • What it changes: The president’s shift signals a departure from the human rights and welfare-focused legacy of past liberal leaders, fracturing the Democratic Party's internal identity and alienating its traditional base.
  • Who is affected: Vulnerable workers, immigrant families, and long-time Democratic Party supporters who feel betrayed by the administration's rightward economic and social policies.

A Rightward Drift Driven by Political Debt

Critics within South Korea's progressive camp argue that President Lee’s recent agenda mirrors strategies historically used by the main conservative opposition, the People Power Party, as well as rhetoric seen during Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure. Rather than strengthening social safety nets, the current administration has increasingly framed welfare recipients as sources of moral hazard. Political observers suspect this rightward pivot stems from political debts Lee accumulated while securing party control and the presidency, forcing concessions to establishment interests and corporate conglomerates at the expense of traditional liberal policies.

A Stark Contrast with Past Liberal Legacies

This ideological drift stands in sharp contrast to the legacies of former liberal presidents Roh Moo-hyun and Moon Jae-in. While the Moon administration earned international acclaim—including top rankings on Bloomberg’s innovation and crisis management indices during the COVID-19 pandemic—Lee's governance is increasingly criticized as insular and factional. Critics point to Lee's reliance on controversial inner-circle figures and closed political networks, such as the Gyeonggi Eastern Alliance faction, rather than respected policy experts.

Tensions are further fueled by President Lee’s apparent rivalry with his predecessor. Retaining divisive figures like Choi Dong-seok as head of the Ministry of Personnel Management and targeting critical civic commentators have led loyalists to accuse Lee of trying to erase Moon Jae-in's influence. For many long-time party members, the administration's actions represent not a pragmatic evolution, but a damaging betrayal of the Democratic Party's democratic heritage.
