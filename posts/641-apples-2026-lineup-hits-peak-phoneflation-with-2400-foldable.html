Apple's 2026 Lineup Hits Peak 'Phoneflation' With $2,400 Foldables

September 10, 2026

  • Apple debuted its first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, alongside the iPhone 18 Pro series and updated wearables at its September 2026 event.
  • The iPhone Duo starts at 3.29 million won (about $2,400) for the 256GB model and scales past $3,700 for higher capacities, crossing firmly into laptop-tier pricing.
  • Surging hardware costs and aggressive premium positioning continue to inflate household communication expenses in South Korea, where high-end phone adoption is exceptionally high.

Apple's latest product rollout confirms that "phoneflation"—the relentless march of smartphone prices toward luxury goods territory—has officially crossed a dangerous threshold. While tech enthusiasts marvel at the engineering packed into the company's first foldable device and the standard iPhone 18 Pro series, the accompanying price tags force a blunt question: who is actually supposed to afford this?

The standout shocker is the iPhone Duo. It culls Apple's best available mobile tech into a 7.6-inch foldable display, but weighs in at a heavy 254 grams and carries a base price that makes high-end MacBooks look reasonably priced. Even the standard iPhone 18 Pro series, starting at roughly $1,500 (1.99 million won), pushes buyers toward higher storage tiers that effectively land real-world costs well into the mid-$2,000 range. When combined with new peripherals like the ANC-equipped AirPods 5 and health-focused Apple Watches, equipping yourself with the latest Apple ecosystem is an increasingly punishing financial commitment.

The Price of Progress

The underlying drivers of this price explosion—rising costs for mobile processors, camera modules, and advanced thermal management like oversized vapor chambers—are real. Yet the local impact in South Korea is uniquely acute. Korean consumers have a notoriously high appetite for flagship devices, meaning these generational price hikes act as an immediate tax on household budgets. When a standard upgrade cycle requires dropping the equivalent of a monthly salary on a pocket-sized computer, technical marvels like variable camera apertures and nano-texture displays start to feel less like consumer benefits and more like forced extravagance.
