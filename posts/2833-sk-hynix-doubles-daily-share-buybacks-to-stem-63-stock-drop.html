SK Hynix Doubles Daily Share Buybacks to Stem 63% Stock Drop

November 28, 2026

On November 27, SK Hynix, South Korea's leading memory chipmaker, announced it is doubling its daily share buyback orders from 350,000 to 700,000 shares in an aggressive bid to halt a severe drop in its stock price.

  • What happened: SK Hynix doubled its daily share repurchase order cap to 700,000 shares, deploying approximately 800 billion won (about $570 million) in daily funds to absorb market selling pressure.
  • What drove the decision: Despite record earnings and technological leadership in high-bandwidth memory (HBM), the company's stock has plunged 63% from its peak due to memory downturn fears and aggressive foreign dumping.
  • What changes: The accelerated buying pace creates an immediate bid on the order books, while management signaled that additional follow-up buyback programs will be launched once the current phase finishes.
  • Who is affected: Institutional short-sellers face higher risk taking downside bets, while retail shareholders receive a firm psychological floor during heightened market volatility.

An Aggressive Shield Against Market Panic

SK Hynix’s move goes far beyond routine capital management; it is a desperate survival strategy aimed at reshaping market supply and demand. A 63% peak-to-trough decline for a flagship technology firm points to panic selling rather than failing fundamentals, particularly as the company maintains a dominant position supplying HBM to global artificial intelligence leaders like Nvidia. By committing roughly 800 billion won ($570 million) daily, executive leadership is directly confronting the market, signaling that current valuation levels are unacceptably low.

Accelerating the buyback schedule creates a visible wall of bids on the daily order books. This tactical intervention serves a dual purpose: it forces speculative short-sellers to reconsider their positions due to rising liquidity risks, while providing retail and domestic investors with the reassurance needed to stem further panic. Rather than merely shrinking total shares over time, this concentrated deployment acts as an immediate shock absorber against foreign equity outflows.

Buying Time for the Semiconductor Cycle

While financial engineering can defend equity prices in the short term, corporate buybacks alone cannot fix broader macroeconomic concerns surrounding the semiconductor sector. SK Hynix’s aggressive intervention is best understood as buying critical "golden time"—establishing a buffer of stability until global market confidence in AI chip demand and memory cycle recovery fully returns. If the company can maintain its tech edge in next-generation HBM while using its balance sheet to anchor its market cap, this decisive move could mark the bottom for its depressed stock.
