South Korea’s Judiciary Faces Crisis Over Ethics and Political Bias

September 30, 2026

South Korea’s judiciary, long considered the final bastion of justice, is facing a severe crisis of legitimacy amid allegations of financial misconduct by Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae and accusations that the courts are stalling politically sensitive trials involving First Lady Kim Keon-hee.

  • Financial Scandals: Evidence suggests the Supreme Court under Chief Justice Cho has systematically falsified expense reports to cover up the use of public funds at luxury restaurants, potentially violating South Korea's anti-corruption laws (the "Kim Young-ran Act").
  • Trial Delays: The Supreme Court’s decision to refer cases involving the First Lady to a "full bench" (a larger panel of justices) is being criticized as a deliberate tactic to delay rulings and avoid political fallout.
  • Institutional Crisis: Critics argue these actions reveal that the judiciary is regressing to a state of subservience to executive power, undermining the constitutional mandate of judicial independence.

A Pattern of Financial Misconduct

Public outrage erupted after investigations by the National Assembly’s Legislation and Judiciary Committee revealed that Supreme Court leadership frequently bypassed spending caps on business expenses. To comply with the 50,000 KRW (about $35) per-person limit, officials allegedly inflated the number of attendees in their reports. In one instance, a dinner reported for 38 people was held at a venue with a maximum capacity of only 33, while another report claimed 15 diners for a meal that served only five.

The Court’s defense—blaming the discrepancies on "clerical errors" by lower-level staff—has been widely rejected by lawmakers. By manipulating public records to justify high-end dining, the institution meant to uphold the rule of law appears to be actively circumventing it. For many observers, this demonstrates a culture of entitlement that treats taxpayer funds as a personal slush fund.

Political Interference and the "Full Bench" Controversy

The suspicion that the judiciary has become a political tool is further fueled by the handling of legal cases involving First Lady Kim Keon-hee. Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae has moved these cases to the "full bench," a move that observers believe is designed to drag out proceedings indefinitely. In South Korea, moving a case to a full bench often signals a shift toward a drawn-out, high-level deliberation that can stall momentum for months or even years.

These delays occur against a backdrop of accusations that the judiciary is protecting the presidential office. When the highest court in the land is perceived as a "playground" for personal or political interests, the foundational trust required for a democracy begins to crumble. The judiciary’s failure to act with speed and transparency in these cases suggests to the public that the law is not applied equally to those in power.

The authority of the judiciary is rooted in its integrity. When that integrity is sacrificed for political convenience or luxury dining, the institution loses the very moral mandate that grants it power.

Whether Chief Justice Cho can restore public faith remains to be seen. However, the current strategy of deflecting responsibility onto subordinates and hiding behind procedural delays is only deepening the public’s conviction that the South Korean courts have lost their way. To recover, the judiciary must prioritize radical transparency and demonstrate that no individual—regardless of status—is above the swift administration of justice.
