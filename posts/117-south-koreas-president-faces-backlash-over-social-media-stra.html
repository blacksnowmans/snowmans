South Korea's President Faces Backlash Over Social Media Strategy and Political Strategy

September 29, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is facing sharp criticism from commentators and the public as his frequent social media posts—urging supporters to take online action—begin to resemble direct marching orders rather than standard political communication.

  • What happened:
    •  President Lee has used social media to rally supporters online, drawing criticism for comparing digital comments to citizens' physical resistance against martial law.
  • What it changes:
    •  The strategy risks alienating moderate voters and deepening political polarization, while forcing South Korea's liberal establishment into defensive legal maneuvers.
  • Who is affected:
    •  The ruling Democratic Party of Korea, progressive media figures, and the broader South Korean public are caught in the crossfire of shifting political loyalties.

The Perils of 'Direct Democracy' via Social Media

President Lee's continuous stream of posts on X (formerly Twitter) suggests a leader under intense psychological pressure, increasingly isolated from constructive criticism. Rather than treating dissent as a normal part of democratic governance, the administration's supporters have begun branding critics as hostile actors. Analysts note that when a leader divides the public into absolute moral camps, it severely undermines the foundational pluralism required for a healthy democracy.

This dynamic was highlighted recently when prominent political commentator Rhyu Si-min was targeted with legal complaints after criticizing the administration's stance on prosecutorial reform. Instead of dismissing the claims as a weak legal maneuver, the Democratic Party, South Korea's main liberal opposition-turned-ruling party, leaned on internal party protocols to defend the action. Critics argue that using bureaucratic and legal threats to silence commentators demonstrates an alarming intolerance for debate.

Shifting Loyalties and the Echo Chamber

Prominent progressive broadcaster Kim Ou-joon has sought to defend the administration by framing public criticism as mere emotional hostility, urging loyalty to prevent conservative parties from regaining power. However, this hardline stance has alienated moderate viewers who demand accountability and basic competence rather than uncritical partisan devotion. Commentators warn that surrounding a leader with loyalists who manage their mood instead of offering real counsel ultimately leads to self-inflicted political wounds.

As the administration doubles down on its messaging, critics argue that true political courage requires confronting uncomfortable truths rather than retreating into online echo chambers. For a public that famously resisted authoritarian rule during past national crises, the current climate is viewed as another test of democratic resilience—one where independent critical thinking remains the ultimate safeguard against political decay.
