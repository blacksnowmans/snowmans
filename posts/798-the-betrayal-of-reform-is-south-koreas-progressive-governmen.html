The Betrayal of Reform: Is South Korea's Progressive Government Turning on Its Own?

September 17, 2026

  • South Korea's progressive government, led by President Lee Jae-myung, is facing intense criticism from within its own base over a wave of investigations targeting fellow progressives and independent media.
  • Recent moves include National Security Act raids on progressive outlets like Chajusibo and pressure on reformist figures such as former Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae, sparking accusations of political purges.
  • Critics argue the administration is abandoning core democratic values in favor of consolidation of power, alienating the civic groups and voters who helped bring them to office.

South Korea's progressive movement is undergoing a painful reckoning. Voters who fought for years to install a democratic government are now watching in disbelief as the Lee Jae-myung administration turns its political and prosecutorial tools against its own allies. What was supposed to be an era of triumphant reform has instead descended into internal infighting, with state security laws and sweeping investigations being deployed against left-leaning media outlets and prominent reform figures.

The situation has fractured the progressive camp. Figures who once symbolized the fight against institutional corruption—most notably former Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae—now find themselves marginalized and under attack from party loyalists. Observers note a disturbing irony: rather than dismantling the overreaching powers of the prosecution and state apparatus, the administration appears to be repurposing them to silence internal dissent and enforce rigid party discipline.

Power, Control, and the Death of Critical Support

At the heart of this crisis is a shift in how the ruling party handles criticism. Rather than engaging with grassroots debate, the administration's inner circle—frequently active on social media late into the night—has been accused of treating dissenting citizens and moderate voices as political enemies. This top-down, win-at-all-costs mentality has sidelined veteran reformers and left many voters feeling betrayed.

For a public that has weathered decades of political turmoil, the lesson is painfully familiar. Blind loyalty—famously summarized by past slogans of letting leaders do whatever they want—only breeds authoritarian tendencies within democratic parties. Disillusioned progressives are now arguing for a return to "critical support": backing the government when it gets things right, but holding it fiercely accountable when it strays from democratic principles.
