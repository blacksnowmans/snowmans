Nvidia’s Earnings Lift Seoul Stocks, Even as Export and Leadership Risks Mount

November 19, 2026

Nvidia’s third-quarter earnings beat sparked a sharp rebound in South Korean equities on November 18, pushing the benchmark KOSPI index up 1.99% to reclaim the 5,000 mark as investors bet that booming AI chip demand outweighs mounting geopolitical and leadership risks.

  • Tech rally returns: Institutional investors, foreign funds, and South Korean pension funds returned as net buyers after a five-day slump, lifting Samsung Electronics by 2.3% and SK Hynix by 4.2%—South Korea’s two dominant memory chipmakers—alongside robotics and AI-related shares to push the KOSPI to close around 5,060.
  • Long-term memory lock-in: Ahead of full-scale shipments of its next-generation Vera Rubin AI chip, Nvidia is moving to secure long-term memory supply from Samsung and SK Hynix—backed by roughly $390 billion in supply guarantees—while sending positive signals on next-generation high-bandwidth memory (HBM4).
  • Parts makers join in: As Nvidia expands its manufacturing network across South Korea, Taiwan, and the United States, it revised a high-performance chip substrate (FC-BGA) contract with Samsung Electro-Mechanics, a major South Korean components maker, and deepened cooperation with packaging partner Amkor.
  • Scandals shadow the surge: The rally held firm despite media reports raising succession concerns over CEO Jensen Huang’s personal controversies, as well as reports by BBC and YTN linking Nvidia to the "Alice Huang" scandal—allegations that Nvidia helped Chinese tech firm Alibaba bypass U.S. export controls in 2022 as China’s People’s Liberation Army sought AI chips for weapons.

A widening split inside South Korea’s memory boom

Nvidia’s numbers—echoed by U.S. rival Micron surging 4.7% in after-hours trading to $712—confirm that AI memory demand is still far outstripping supply. For Seoul’s market, that provides a vital psychological floor, turning Nvidia’s order book into a lifeline for domestic tech valuations.

Yet this boom will not lift all chipmakers equally. While high-value HBM orders are surging, spot prices for conventional memory used in smartphones and PCs remain sluggish. That divergence favors SK Hynix, whose close partnership with Nvidia and heavy focus on HBM give it clearer earnings momentum, while forcing Samsung Electronics to defend weaker consumer-chip margins even as it races to catch up in HBM4.

Geopolitical crossfire remains the real threat

Korean investors largely shrugged off leadership rumors around Jensen Huang, betting that Nvidia’s entrenched global supply chain matters more than one executive. The U.S.-China export fight is far harder to ignore. Even with prior U.S. export approvals, Nvidia has opted to halt AI chip shipments to China amid mounting scrutiny, and a tougher Washington crackdown over alleged export-control circumvention could easily catch Korean suppliers in the crossfire.
