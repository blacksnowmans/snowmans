Stalled Reforms and Selective Probes Trigger Backlash from South Korea’s Progressive Base

October 22, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea faces mounting frustration from its core base as key reform bills stall in the National Assembly while law enforcement agencies face accusations of selective justice.
  • What happened: Legislative action on core priorities—including media reform legislation, regional growth support bills, and special prosecutor appointments—has been repeatedly delayed due to parliamentary scheduling and tactical floor strategies.
  • Law enforcement row: Recent raids and investigations targeting prominent progressive commentator Rhyu Si-min and the independent online outlet Sisa Tapa have drawn fierce criticism from supporters alleging biased state probes.
  • Media leadership deadlocks: Newly introduced citizen recommendation panels for selecting public broadcasting chiefs have failed to install reformist leadership, highlighting lingering political patronage in public media governance.
  • Who is affected: President Lee Jae-myung’s administration and the ruling party risk alienating key supporters who demand swift reform and equal accountability.

Legislative Gridlock and Strategic Miscalculations

The National Assembly’s failure to pass critical legislation highlights a growing disconnect between parliamentary tactics and public expectations. While floor leadership within the Democratic Party of Korea, the country's ruling liberal party, cites procedural caution and scheduling constraints to justify delays, supporters view the slow pace as a failure of political will. High-priority measures—such as media reform laws and special prosecutor appointments to investigate political manipulation allegations—remain bogged down in parliamentary maneuvers. Delaying these key agenda items risks squandering the political momentum needed to deliver on President Lee Jae-myung’s reform promises.

Selective Justice and the Battle for Media Integrity

Compounding the legislative stalemate is deep skepticism over the political neutrality of investigative authorities. Swift search-and-seizure operations targeting progressive figures like Rhyu Si-min stand in sharp contrast to the sluggish responses regarding smear campaigns and fabricated media attacks against the left. This perceived double standard reflects lingering institutional habits from Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure, where investigative power was frequently criticized for selective application. Meanwhile, public media reforms remain incomplete, as citizen recommendation panels intended to neutralize political influence have instead served as shields for traditional media elites. Without decisive action to enforce equal justice and institutional reform, state power will continue to face crisis of legitimacy among the public.
