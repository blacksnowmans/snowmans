The "Reservation War": Essential Tips for Your Camino de Santiago Journey

September 07, 2026

  • The Reality Check: If you wait until you arrive in France to book travel and lodging for the Camino de Santiago, you risk exorbitant price hikes and having nowhere to sleep.
  • Logistics: Navigating the "French Way" requires securing transport from Paris to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port—the primary starting point—and booking critical hostels (albergues) in advance.
  • Safety First: Travelers must be hyper-vigilant against petty crime on long-distance trains and buses, as these routes are common targets for pickpockets.
  • Cultural Context: South Koreans are now the largest group of Asian pilgrims on the route, reflecting a growing trend of locals seeking healing and introspection away from Korea's hyper-competitive daily life.

Planning for the Camino de Santiago—a centuries-old pilgrimage route—has evolved from a spiritual endeavor into a logistical gauntlet. For the novice pilgrim, the journey begins long before the first step on the trail; it begins with the "reservation war." As the route gains popularity, particularly among South Koreans, securing your spot is no longer just about convenience—it is about basic survival.

The most common path starts in Paris. From there, pilgrims must reach Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port. Options include the TGV (high-speed rail), overnight trains, buses, or regional flights. Among these, the overnight train is a favorite for budget-conscious travelers, often costing around $75–$100 (approx. 100,000 KRW). However, navigating the SNCF Connect app is critical: search specifically for "Paris Austerlitz" station, and ensure you book a "Berth" rather than a standard seat to avoid a grueling 11-hour nightmare.

Strategic Survival: Lodging and Safety

Don't Gamble with Roncesvalles

While experienced pilgrims may prefer the spontaneity of "walk-in" lodging, newcomers should not risk it. The town of Roncesvalles features only one albergue (pilgrim hostel). If it is full, you have no local alternatives. Similarly, if you plan to traverse the grueling Pyrenees mountains, booking a spot at the Orisson or Borda mountain refuges is essential. These spots often fill up 5–6 months in advance. At roughly $58 (approx. 88,000 KRW) including meals, these refuges offer a vital community experience and a necessary buffer for physical recovery.

Safety on the Move

While the romantic image of the Camino suggests a safe, meditative journey, the transit routes leading to it are far from immune to crime. Whether on a night train or bus, your gear is vulnerable. Always lock your backpack to your bed and keep valuables physically attached to your body while sleeping. Many pilgrims overlook this, but losing your gear before you even reach the trailhead is the fastest way to end your pilgrimage before it begins.

Ultimately, "perfect" preparation is a myth, but avoiding the worst-case scenarios is entirely within your control. By treating the booking process as a fundamental part of your pilgrimage rather than a chore, you secure more than just a bed—you buy the peace of mind necessary to actually focus on the walk itself when you finally arrive.
