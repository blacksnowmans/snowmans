South Korea’s Judiciary Faces Backlash Over Martial Law Rulings and Kim Keon-hee's Detention

October 28, 2026

South Korea's legal system is drawing intense scrutiny as courts issue controversial rulings on the martial law fallout involving impeached former leader Yoon Suk-yeol, even as a court approves the detention of Yoon's wife, Kim Keon-hee.

  • Courts under fire: Recent rulings by judges like Lee Jin-gwan are applying legal interpretations that critics warn could clear officials tied to Yoon's martial law attempt and related scandals.
  • Kim Keon-hee detained: Kim Keon-hee was taken into custody over influence-peddling and job-selling allegations, with her detention extendable up to eight months through appeals.
  • Political friction: President Lee Jae-myung’s administration is pushing to tighten policy alignment with investigative agencies, drawing pushback from critics worried about political neutrality.

The Judiciary's Softpedaling on Martial Law

The legal reckoning for Yoon Suk-yeol's aborted martial law decree is running into severe headwinds inside South Korea's courtrooms. Critics argue that recent lower-court logic—such as dismissing abuse-of-authority charges for officials who merely stood standby on the night of the decree—threatens to unravel the entire case against those who facilitated the takeover attempt. Sentences for key figures like former Minister Park Sung-jae have seen steep reductions, fueling public anger and widespread suspicion that parts of the judiciary are shielding political allies from accountability.

Meanwhile, Kim Keon-hee’s formal arrest brings a measure of legal gravity to a scandal-plagued administration, though her defense continues to dismiss the charges. Despite attempts within the legal establishment to stall or soften fallout, the public appetite for accountability remains at a boiling point. The overarching question is whether South Korea's courts can uphold the rule of law or if political self-preservation will ultimately dilute the historical record of a constitutional crisis.

Lee Jae-myung’s Governance Strategy

As the legal battles play out, the administration of President Lee Jae-myung is moving to assert tighter coherence across government agencies. Debates over inviting investigative agency heads to cabinet meetings highlight a central tension: balancing institutional independence with executive oversight. Lee has defended the practice as a necessary step to share national priorities, pushing back against opposition fears of political interference.

Ultimately, the ongoing turmoil is less about administrative procedures and more about a nation trying to sweep away the remnants of unchecked power. How the courts handle the remaining martial law trials will shape South Korea’s political landscape for decades to come.
