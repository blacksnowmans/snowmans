How South Korea's Police Shielded a Powerful Politician's Family

October 12, 2026

South Korea's National Police Agency is facing intense scrutiny following revelations that its 20-month investigation into academic resume fraud allegations against the daughter of Han Dong-hoon—a leading conservative politician and former Justice Minister—was riddled with superficial checks, missing evidence, and outright deception.

  • What happened: Police closed the case without charges after a nearly two-year inquiry that involved zero search warrants, zero witness interviews, and just a single written questionnaire sent to the suspect.
  • The cover-up: The head of the National Office of Investigation publicly claimed police had conducted numerous witness interviews, a statement flatly contradicted by official records showing zero witnesses were ever questioned.
  • The double standard: Critics contrast the passive handling of Han's family with aggressive investigations into political rivals, such as a separate probe involving Democratic Party figures that saw 131 search warrants executed in just six months.
  • Who is affected: Public trust in South Korea's law enforcement agencies and judicial fairness under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, who took office following the impeachment and imprisonment of former leader Yoon Suk Yeol.

A Masterclass in Administrative Foot-Dragging

The handling of the allegations against Han Dong-hoon's daughter highlights how institutional power can distort the rule of law. While ordinary citizens face swift and invasive police tactics, investigators gave elite figures wide berth. During 20 months of supposed inquiry into alleged ghost-written essays and copyright violations, police did not execute a single search warrant or request court-ordered seizures—standard procedures in any serious white-collar probe.

Compounding the procedural failures, prosecutors independently blocked international legal assistance requests aimed at gathering overseas evidence, bypassing the Supreme Prosecutors' Office and the Ministry of Justice to prevent the case from landing on Han's desk while he served as Justice Minister. The National Police Agency offered no resistance, quietly acquiescing to a move that effectively shielded the suspect from thorough examination.

A Crisis of Institutional Credibility

The scandal escalated from poor police work into an outright credibility crisis when National Office of Investigation chief Woo Jong-soo lied to journalists about the scope of the inquiry. Publicly boasting of extensive witness testimony when records showed none existed exposed a deep rot within the nation's premier investigative body. Critics are now demanding a full internal audit and a total reinvestigation from the ground up.

For ordinary South Koreans who watch politicians' children leverage connections for elite university admissions while everyday students play by strict rules, the episode is a bitter reminder that equality before the law remains an aspiration rather than a reality. If law enforcement agencies cannot hold powerful figures accountable without political calculation, public faith in South Korea's democratic institutions will continue to erode.
