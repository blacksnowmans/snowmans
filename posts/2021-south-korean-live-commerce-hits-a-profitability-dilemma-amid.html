South Korean Live Commerce Hits a Profitability Dilemma Amid AI Push and Global Expansion

November 06, 2026

South Korean e-commerce platforms are locking horns in an increasingly expensive live-commerce and AI battle, driving strong top-line sales while severely squeezing operating margins.

  • Kakao's solid earnings: Tech giant Kakao reported a 7% year-over-year increase in Q3 operating profit to 240.3 billion won (about $175 million), anchored by its Talk Biz messenger commerce ecosystem and plans to reach 10 million monthly active AI users by year-end.
  • CJ OnStyle's margin squeeze: CJ OnStyle, the home-shopping and live-commerce arm of media group CJ ENM, nearly doubled its mobile live transaction volume, but Q3 operating profit plummeted 46.8% to 6.7 billion won (about $4.9 million) under heavy marketing and infrastructure spending.
  • Global K-beauty surge: South Korean sellers are leveraging live streaming and short-form video to expand overseas, driving a 212% surge in orders for Korean beauty products on Southeast Asian e-commerce giant Shopee's Brazil platform.
  • Regulatory shadow: South Korean courts upheld a fine of over 400 billion won (about $290 million) against retail titan Coupang over personal data privacy violations, highlighting escalating legal liabilities for big tech platforms.

The High Cost of Content-Driven Shopping

The latest earnings figures highlight a widening divide in South Korea's live-commerce ecosystem. Platforms are successfully pulling in shoppers through entertainment-focused streams—such as cosmetics brand Arollcell topping Naver Shopping Live rankings by partnering with popular creator RalRal—yet turning viewer traffic into net profit is proving difficult. Kakao has managed to monetise its ubiquitous KakaoTalk messaging app via integrated shopping features, but traditional retail players like CJ OnStyle are finding that live-stream production and influencer marketing devour cash faster than sales growth can replace it.

Short-Form Videos and Overseas Lifelines

To break out of saturated domestic discount wars, South Korean merchants are integrating short-form video commerce and targeting overseas shoppers. As global platforms like TikTok parent ByteDance embed shoppable links directly into short-form content, Korean brands are adopting similar models to engage international audiences. With domestic consumption slowing and heavyweights like Coupang facing strict regulatory penalties over compliance failures, expanding cross-border live commerce into high-growth regions like Latin America has turned from an experiment into an essential survival strategy.
