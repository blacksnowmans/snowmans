How a 14-Year Delivery Truck Driver Reached Early Retirement on Dividends

August 30, 2026

  • The Shift: A South Korean delivery worker quit his grueling 14-year driving job after building a portfolio of high-yield dividend funds and side hustles.
  • The Strategy: He relies on US covered-call exchange-traded funds (ETFs) like NVDY and bicycle delivery gigs to generate a steady monthly cash flow of about $1,400 to $1,500 (2 million won).
  • The Goal: With a current net worth of roughly $400,000 (560 million won) and financial assets around $240,000 (337 million won), he plans to help his wife retire using a multi-tiered pension and dividend system.

Trading the Driver’s Seat for Financial Independence

For 14 years, waking up meant bracing for sharp pains in the ankles, knees, and lower back while operating a 1-ton delivery truck in South Korea. The work traded physical health for short-term wages, forcing drivers to pay steep out-of-pocket fees for substitute vehicles just to take a single day off. Realizing that staying on the road meant breaking his body beyond repair, he set a clear psychological threshold: accumulating about $140,000 (200 million won) in financial assets. That milestone gave him the confidence to walk away from toxic work conditions and take control of his time.

Rather than quitting blindly, he tested a hybrid model. By combining about $700,000 (100 million won) in dividend stocks yielding steady monthly payouts with side gig work like bicycle deliveries, he secured a reliable baseline income. He argues that true early retirement—often called the FIRE movement—isn't about total freedom from labor, but rather finding a sustainable mix of manageable work and asset income that protects against market volatility.

Navigating High-Yield Covered Calls and Daily Routines

To maximize cash flow, he embraced controversial high-dividend covered-call products, specifically the YieldMax NVDA Option Income ETF (NVDY). While critics warn that covered-call funds cap stock upside and expose investors to sharp downturns, he treats the monthly payouts as a priority. Having collected enough dividends over two years to recover his initial principal, he now views the asset as a zero-risk income generator. Even if payouts halve during a market correction, his physical resilience and bicycle delivery backup plan keep him secure.

Today, his daily routine rejects the trope of the lazy retiree. Waking up at 5:30 AM, he spends hours cycling along the Han River for health and blood sugar management, studies English language and literature through the Korea National Open University, and manages household finances alongside his wife. Their ultimate target is a net worth of about $500,000 to $500,000+ ($480,000–$500,000 USD / 600 to 700 million won) to fund a joint retirement anchored by personal pensions, housing pensions, national pensions, and dividend pipelines.
