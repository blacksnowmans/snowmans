South Korea's Push for 12-Hour Trading Sparks Retail Backlash

September 16, 2026

  • What happened: The Korea Exchange (KRX) is expanding daily trading hours to a grueling 12-hour window, running from a 7:00 AM pre-market straight through to an 8:00 PM after-hours market.
  • The driver: The move is a defensive play against NXT (Nextrade), South Korea's newly launched alternative trading system, and an attempt to keep pace with global exchanges chasing round-the-clock trading.
  • Who is affected: Local retail investors—fondly or ruefully known as "ants"—face intense psychological pressure, evening earnings-disclosure shocks, and an environment heavily skewed toward high-frequency trading firms.
  • The real demand: Investor advocacy groups argue that the market doesn't need longer hours; it needs infrastructure fixes, such as shortening South Korea's sluggish T+2 settlement cycle.

Longer Hours, Lower Mental Health for Retail Investors

South Korean retail investors are pushing back hard against the KRX's expanded schedule, calling it a form of forced virtual labor rather than a market upgrade. While institutional players and foreign funds rely on automated trading systems and night shifts, individual investors glued to their mobile trading apps are left vulnerable. With the after-hours market running from 4:00 PM to 8:00 PM, corporate earnings announcements—traditionally dropped right after the 3:30 PM regular close to prevent intraday panic—will now collide directly with thin evening liquidity. This sets the stage for erratic price swings and wild spikes on low volume.

Behind the official rhetoric of "global competitiveness" lies a turf war. When Nextrade opened its alternative venue and began capturing evening trading volume, the KRX scrambled to protect its turf and fee revenue. Critics point out that encouraging day-and-night trading does little for actual market health; it simply drives up transaction frequency, padding brokerage commissions for the KRX and securities firms at the expense of retail accounts. In a market already weighed down by the perennial "Korea Discount," forcing exhausted workers to monitor volatile tickers until 8:00 PM is a recipe for severe burnout.

The Antidote: Step Away From the Screen

Ultimately, successful stock investing has always been a game of psychology and stamina, not screen time. The proliferation of trading hours turns the stock market into a 24/7 casino, preying on human impulsivity and draining investors' mental energy through constant over-trading.

Local commentators are drawing a parallel to real estate: just as a homeowner with a primary apartment in Seoul doesn't obsessively check housing prices every hour, long-term equity investors should treat solid blue-chip stocks with the same patience. The best defense against the KRX's 12-hour trap is simply turning off the phone app, ignoring the hype of after-hours volatility, and letting strong fundamentals compound in peace.
