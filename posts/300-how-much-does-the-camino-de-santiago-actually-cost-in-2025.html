How Much Does the Camino de Santiago Actually Cost in 2025?

September 07, 2026

Walking the famous French Way of the Camino de Santiago is often romanticized as a dirt-cheap, backpacker’s dream, but a recent reality check from a 34-day trek reveals that total expenses easily cross the $5,500 mark (about 8.1 million won).

  • The Total Bill: A 34-day pilgrimage combined with short city stays in Paris and Barcelona totaled about $5,500 (8.1 million won), including Asiana Airlines flights.
  • On-the-Trail Costs: Excluding flights and city tourism, the 34 days strictly on the trail cost about $2,400 (3.5 million won), averaging roughly $70 (100,000 won) per day.
  • Hidden Budget Drainers: Rising costs for private accommodations, last-minute transport "jumps," and restaurant meals mean the trail is no longer a budget-travel bargain.
  • Gear Investment: Walkers cannot skimp on fundamentals—investing around $240 (350,000 won) in high-end trail shoes proved essential for finishing injury-free.

The Real Cost of the Trail: Beyond the Budget Myth

The popular image of sleeping in cheap church-run hostels and spending next to nothing daily no longer matches reality. While basic public pilgrim hostels (*albergues*) still cost a modest $11 to $20 (15,000 to 30,000 won), they operate on a strict first-come, first-served basis. When physical exhaustion sets in or public beds fill up, tired travelers are forced to pay $20 to $40+ for private rooms, Airbnbs, or hotels. Add in daily meals—where a simple breakfast coffee and sandwich runs $8 and a traditional pilgrim dinner costs around $17—and daily expenses quickly compound.

Transport adds another layer of unpredictable costs. Because it is impossible to predict exact arrival dates on an 800-kilometer walking journey, booking cheap advance tickets for trains or flights home is nearly impossible. Last-minute regional bus connections, luggage forwarding services (*donkey services*), and emergency flight bookings from Santiago to Barcelona regularly blow holes in tight travel budgets.

My Take: Pay for Your Comfort, Not the Average

The biggest takeaway from these disclosed expenses is that obsessing over a theoretical "budget average" is a waste of energy. Pilgrims can complete the journey for much less by exclusively using public dormitories, cooking every meal, and walking through every blister. However, doing so requires sacrificing physical comfort, time, and peace of mind.

The wisest approach is treating gear—especially footwear—as a non-negotiable investment rather than an expense to trim. Spending top dollar on cushioned, durable trail shoes and functional gear prevents injuries that would end the trip prematurely. Ultimately, the Camino is not a financial test to see who can spend the least, but a personal journey. Match your spending to your physical limits, keep your own pace, and accept that the money spent is simply the cost of moving safely across Spain.
