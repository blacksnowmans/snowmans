Calls for Impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae Intensify Amid Judicial Reform Deadlock

October 27, 2026

Supporters of President Lee Jae-myung are calling for the immediate impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, arguing that the judiciary remains a stronghold of conservative bias despite the imprisonment of the previous leader, Mr. Suk-yeol Yoon.

  • What happened: Influential liberal commentators and hardline members of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) are demanding the removal of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, the head of South Korea’s Supreme Court.
  • The trigger: The movement gained momentum following warnings from pundit Kim Ou-joon that the court might release Kim Keon-hee, the wife of the imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, on bail or through expired detention warrants.
  • The risk: While the DPK holds a significant majority in the National Assembly, the party leadership is hesitant, fearing that a rejection by the Constitutional Court (the body that finalizes impeachments) would grant the judiciary a "blank check" of legitimacy.
  • Who is affected: The move threatens to paralyze the Supreme Court and further polarize the relationship between the executive branch and the judiciary during the current Lee Jae-myung administration.

The Shadow of the Former Administration

The push to remove Chief Justice Cho is rooted in the belief that the "judicial clock" has stopped since the 2024 political transition. Proponents of impeachment argue that even though Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol was removed from power and remains behind bars, the judicial hierarchy he appointed continues to protect the interests of the People Power Party (PPP), the country’s main conservative opposition. They point to delayed trials for high-profile conservative figures and a perceived bias in how cases are assigned within the Supreme Court.

The debate has been framed largely around the fate of Kim Keon-hee. Activists argue that the judiciary is looking for legal loopholes to release her, which they view as an act of political defiance against the current government’s mandate. However, critics within the liberal camp suggest that making impeachment "conditional" on a single person’s detention is a strategic error. They argue that if the Chief Justice has violated the Constitution or engaged in "judicial power-tripping," he should be impeached regardless of whether Kim Keon-hee is released.

Strategic Hesitation in the Ruling Party

Despite the roar from the DPK’s grassroots base, the party leadership remains cautious. This hesitation stems from a "Constitutional Court phobia." For an impeachment to succeed, six out of nine justices on the Constitutional Court must agree. If the case is dismissed, it would be a catastrophic political defeat for President Lee’s party, potentially providing the conservative opposition with the momentum they need to claim "political persecution."

"The risk of a dismissal is the DPK's greatest fear, as it would effectively immunize the current judicial leadership from further reform efforts."

There is also a concern regarding the ongoing "insurrection" trials related to the previous administration's conduct. Some fear that impeaching the Chief Justice now could delay final convictions, or worse, allow conservative legal elites to frame any future guilty verdicts as the result of a compromised, intimidated court. For now, the DPK is balancing the need to satisfy a base that demands a "cleansing" of the judiciary with the cold reality of the legal hurdles required to remove the nation's top judge.
