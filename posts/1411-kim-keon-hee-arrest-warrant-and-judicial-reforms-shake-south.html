Kim Keon-hee Arrest Warrant and Judicial Reforms Shake South Korean Politics

October 22, 2026

South Korean prosecutors are seeking an additional arrest warrant for Kim Keon-hee, the wife of jailed former president Yoon Suk-yeol, as the ruling Democratic Party of Korea faces mounting public backlash over controversial judicial reforms and cratering leadership ratings.

  • What happened: Special prosecutors requested a second arrest warrant hearing for Kim Keon-hee on Oct. 27 regarding the "Mega Magic" corruption case, while the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) pushed three major judicial reform bills through the parliamentary committee.
  • What it changes: The proposed reform legislation would nearly double the number of Supreme Court justices from 14 to 26, drawing severe criticism from opposition lawmakers and senior judicial administrators who argue the move violates constitutional neutrality.
  • Who is affected: DPK Chairman Kim Min-seok’s job approval rating collapsed to 25.6%, driving dissatisfied progressive voters toward the minor opposition Rebuilding Korea Party, which surged to 9% in national public opinion polls.

Kim Keon-hee's Legal Jeopardy and the Battle Over Judicial Reform

The upcoming October 27 arrest warrant hearing for Kim Keon-hee marks a critical moment for the special prosecution team. Given that heavy prison sentences ranging from 5 to 20 years have already been handed down in the related Mega Magic scandal, legal experts view her additional detention as legally unavoidable to maintain equity with other pre-trial detainees. Releasing her would severely damage public trust in the judiciary under Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. At the same time, the judiciary is locked in a fierce dispute with the ruling DPK over three reform bills passed by the National Assembly's judiciary committee. The most controversial measure—increasing the Supreme Court bench from 14 to 26 justices—has triggered filibuster threats from the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main conservative opposition party, and warnings of unconstitutionality from the Court Administration Office.

Cratering DPK Leadership and the Rebuilding Korea Party's Surge

A recent Realmeter poll delivered a sharp rebuke to ruling party leadership, recording a dismal 25.6% approval rating for DPK Chairman Kim Min-seok against a 67.6% disapproval rating. The collapse stems from ongoing factional infighting, poor communication, and a failure to satisfy key reform demands among the party's base. Disillusionment with DPK leadership has fueled a noticeable surge for the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party, led by Cho Kuk, which reached 9% nationwide support and 16.3% among voters in their 40s. While DPK lawmakers like Jung Chung-rae attempt to regain momentum through popular policies—such as a bill mandating national budget coverage for firefighter payrolls—voters seeking clear-cut reform are increasingly shifting their alignment.

The broader political landscape remains tense as investigative fairness comes under scrutiny. A recent raid on independent news outlet Sisa Tapa Media by the newly established Serious Crimes Investigation Office—executed immediately after its chief's appointment—has drawn accusations of targeted suppression. For President Lee Jae-myung’s government, managing judicial impartiality while maintaining public faith in state institutions remains a delicate balancing act.
