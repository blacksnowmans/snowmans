South Korea's Ruling Party Pushes Election Reform and Probes Martial Law Actions as Audit Wraps Up

October 31, 2026

As South Korea's annual parliamentary audit drew to a close, the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) moved aggressively to counter election conspiracy theories, demand accountability over the Dec. 3 martial law attempt, and press ahead with internal party reforms.

  • Election Integrity: DPK leadership called on the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) to halt ungrounded election fraud claims and join bipartisan talks on reforming the National Election Commission (NEC), the state election watchdog.
  • Martial Law Accountability: The DPK confirmed its proposed parliamentary investigation into Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae will focus strictly on his actions during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's illegal Dec. 3 emergency martial law declaration.
  • Audit Behavior: Ruling party lawmakers sharply criticized opposition figures and agency heads for turning committee audits into disruptive political theater designed for viral social media clips.
  • Internal Transparency: The DPK announced it will brief its members next week on an internal probe into allegations that the Shincheonji religious group organized mass party enrollments.

Drawing a Line on Democratic Norms and Audit Conduct

The Democratic Party's offensive on the final day of the parliamentary audit reflects a deliberate effort to solidify its legislative momentum under the Lee Jae-myung administration. By demanding systemic reforms to the NEC, DPK Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok aimed to box in the opposition PPP, which continues to face criticism for echoing fringe conspiracy theories regarding voting procedures. DPK leaders argued that institutional reform, rather than partisan grandstanding, is the only way to preserve public trust in constitutional bodies.

Simultaneously, the DPK confronted what it characterized as severe decorum failures during committee sessions. Lawmakers singled out key agency chiefs and opposition figures for creating loud disruptions solely to generate short-form videos for online supporters. This firm stance extends to internal party discipline as well, as the DPK prepares to publish its findings on the Shincheonji religious group's alleged party infiltration, demonstrating a push to address ethical risks within its own ranks.

Probing the Role of the Judiciary During the Dec. 3 Crisis

The most consequential battle line remains the aftermath of the Dec. 3 martial law crisis. The DPK’s decision to narrow its planned parliamentary probe into Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae strictly to his whereabouts and decisions during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s unconstitutional decree is a calculated move. While the PPP has labeled the probe a retaliatory attack on judicial independence, the DPK maintains that examining how the head of the judiciary reacted during an attempt to subvert constitutional order is a fundamental duty of the National Assembly.

Investigating the actions of constitutional leaders during the martial law episode is essential for establishing state accountability. So long as the opposition dismisses these inquiries as political vendettas, the political rift over the legacy of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s ousted administration will continue to dominate South Korea's legislative agenda.
