President Lee Jae-myung’s Mexico Trip Sparks Outrage and Domestic Political Turmoil

October 03, 2026

  • During a state visit to Mexico, President Lee Jae-myung publicly attacked prominent commentator Rhyu Si-min, reigniting old conflicts over the National Security Act.
  • The President faced backlash for trivializing the French Revolution while discussing domestic reform, drawing criticism for poor statesmanship and historical ignorance.
  • South Korea's main opposition party, the People Power Party (PPP), led by Chairman Jang Dong-hyuk, is gaining ground as President Lee’s approval ratings and his own party's standing decline.
  • Public trust is plummeting, with the Democratic Party (DP)—led by Chairman Kim Min-suk—facing internal fracturing and a sharp drop in support, now polling at roughly 35%.

A Diplomatic Stage Used for Personal Vendettas

President Lee Jae-myung’s state visit to Mexico City has turned into a diplomatic embarrassment rather than a showcase of leadership. During a meeting with Korean expatriates, the President abandoned diplomatic decorum to lash out at Rhyu Si-min, a former politician and influential commentator. This public rebuke followed Rhyu’s recent criticism of the President’s handling of prosecution reform. By dragging domestic political scores onto the international stage, President Lee has signaled a shift toward a more aggressive, reactionary style of governance that critics warn is isolating his administration.

Compounding the controversy, the President attempted to justify his reform difficulties by referencing the French Revolution, bizarrely claiming it "met with a backlash because it went too far, too fast." This flippant characterization of a foundational moment in modern democratic history has been widely mocked by scholars and the public alike as a display of superficial, "half-baked" knowledge. It reflects a growing perception that the President is prioritizing the suppression of political dissent over the actual business of statecraft.

Internal Fractures and the Rise of the Opposition

Back home, the political landscape is equally volatile. Democratic Party Chairman Kim Min-suk is grappling with a devastating decline in support. Recent polling shows his party's approval rating at 35%, with a widening perception that the DP is becoming an echo chamber for radical supporters rather than a party of governance. Even within the party's core base, support for Chairman Kim has seen a significant double-digit percentage drop, suggesting that even traditional loyalists are becoming disillusioned with the "Lee Jae-myung-only" leadership structure.

The Paradox of the Opposition as a "Check"

In a bizarre twist, President Lee appears more reactive to the criticism of PPP Chairman Jang Dong-hyuk than to his own party's supporters. When Jang highlighted the President’s recent social media activity—which included retweeting content from fringe online communities—the President promptly deleted the posts. This has led to a common joke among political observers: the only effective "brake" on President Lee’s erratic behavior is the opposition leader, not his own aides.

As the administration prepares for the launch of the new Investigative Agency (a key part of his controversial prosecution reform), the public is increasingly viewing these moves not as genuine reform, but as a mechanism for political retaliation. With the President’s approval slipping and the opposition successfully painting the administration as reactive and petty, the government faces a precarious future. If the administration continues to ignore internal warnings and focus on personal vendettas, it risks losing the very mandate it claims to be exercising.
