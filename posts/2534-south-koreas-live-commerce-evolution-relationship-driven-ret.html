South Korea’s Live Commerce Evolution: Relationship-Driven Retail and the Rise of Senior Spenders

November 20, 2026

South Korea’s live commerce market has matured into a mainstream retail powerhouse, moving beyond simple product sales to become a primary channel for high-value services like international travel and professional matchmaking.

  • Hanatour, South Korea’s largest travel agency, celebrated the five-year anniversary of its "Hana LIVE" platform, recording 120 million cumulative views and 133,000 confirmed bookings for high-ticket travel packages.
  • Grip, a leading specialized live commerce platform, identified the "5060" generation (those in their 50s and 60s) as the market’s new "big spenders," who prioritize emotional connection with sellers over low prices.
  • Consumer data reveals a "deliberate" buying habit: while viewership peaks at 8:00 PM, actual transactions surge at 11:00 PM as shoppers take several hours to weigh the information provided during the broadcast.
  • The sector is expanding into complex "intangible" services, with Duo, the country’s prominent matchmaking agency, now using live broadcasts to explain membership procedures and build trust with potential clients.

The "Gray Wave" and the Shift to Relational Commerce

The latest data from Grip signals a significant demographic shift. Once considered a playground for tech-savvy Gen Z and Millennials, live commerce is now being dominated by older consumers. These "senior" shoppers are driving a trend known as "relational commerce." Unlike younger users who might chase the absolute lowest price via search engines, these older buyers value the curator’s expertise and the personal rapport built through real-time chat. For this group, the seller is not just a vendor but a trusted advisor.

This deep engagement explains the unique "time-lag" in purchasing. Because live commerce often features high-involvement products in Korea—ranging from premium skincare at Shinsegae Live Shopping (the broadcasting arm of the major department store group) to luxury tours—consumers watch the entertainment and information at dinner time but wait until just before bed to commit their funds. This suggests that live commerce has successfully moved from "impulse buying" to a "considered research" channel.

From Commodities to High-Trust Services

The success of Hanatour is a landmark for the industry. Selling a vacation package—an intangible service that can cost thousands of dollars—requires significantly more trust than selling a bottle of shampoo. By reaching 120 million views, Hanatour has proven that real-time video can replace the traditional travel agent. The ability for a guide to show live footage of a hotel or answer specific questions about a local itinerary in the chat removes the "uncertainty gap" that often plagues online travel bookings.

However, the industry faces challenges in sustainability. The recent decision by comedian Park Sung-kwang to pause his popular six-year-old live channel "Kwang-pari" highlights the intense physical and creative toll on individual creators. As the market moves toward 2027, the focus is shifting toward "C.A.M.E.R.A"—a trend emphasizing that who is selling is more important than what is being sold. For global brands, this means the Korean market now demands a sophisticated blend of entertainment, deep product knowledge, and long-term relationship management rather than one-off discount events.

"The future of the market lies in whether platforms can sustain the energy of their creators while maintaining the high level of transparency that senior consumers now demand."
