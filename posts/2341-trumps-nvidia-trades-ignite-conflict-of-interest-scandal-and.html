Trump’s Nvidia Trades Ignite Conflict-of-Interest Scandal and Chip Policy Turmoil

November 15, 2026

Donald Trump purchased up to $5 million (about 6.7 billion won) in Nvidia shares immediately before moves to roll back U.S. export controls on AI chips to China, sparking an explosive conflict-of-interest scandal that has thrown global technology supply chains into disarray.

  • The trade: Revelations that Trump bought heavily into Nvidia right before lifting curbs on AI chip exports to China—triggering an immediate share rally—have drawn fierce allegations of personal profiteering from core national security policies.
  • Cabinet in chaos: U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick faces mounting calls for dismissal and Senate hearing demands after deflecting blame by claiming he merely followed Trump's directives on regulatory easing.
  • Executive intervention: Amid intense pressure from White House hardliners over Nvidia's alleged facilitation of bypass exports to China, Trump reportedly placed a personal phone call to CEO Jensen Huang urging him to fight and keep his job.
  • Allies caught in the middle: Washington hardliners are responding by doubling down, demanding allies like South Korea block indirect chip pathways to China, while Beijing threatens immediate retaliatory restrictions on critical rare earth exports.

A Compromised Strategy and White House Infighting

The scandal strikes directly at the credibility of American technology policy. Washington has spent years framing its semiconductor blockade against Beijing as an existential matter of national security. That justification collapses the moment export deregulation looks coordinated with a leader's personal brokerage account. What should be strategic statecraft now carries the stench of transactional insider trading.

Inside the administration, the rift is widening into open warfare. Commerce Secretary Lutnick’s public attempt to shield himself by pointing to Trump's direct orders reveals a broken chain of command, while reports of Trump coaching Huang behind the scenes undermine the Commerce Department’s regulatory authority. With Senate scrutiny looming and hardliners maneuvering to reclaim control of the trade agenda, U.S. export policy has lost any pretense of predictability.

Severe Exposure for South Korea’s Chip Industry

For South Korea's memory and logic chipmakers, this dysfunction creates immediate peril. Administration hawks seeking to project toughness are already ratcheting up pressure on Seoul to police and sever third-party supply routes into China. Yet complying leaves Korean firms dangerously exposed to Beijing, which is openly preparing to weaponize its rare earth monopoly against global tech manufacturing if semiconductor restrictions tighten further.

Korean industry is effectively trapped between a volatile Washington and a retaliatory Beijing. When U.S. policy levers shift based on internal factional battles and suspect trading patterns, Korean executives cannot reliably plan long-term capital investments or export strategies. Seoul must rapidly establish an assertive, independent semiconductor diplomacy to shield domestic producers from being dragged down by American political turbulence.
