Apple’s First Foldable Stumbles in Korea: Why the $2,400 iPhone Duo Is Facing Heavy Backlash

October 26, 2026

Apple’s first foldable smartphone, the iPhone Duo, is drawing sharp criticism from early adopters in South Korea over software bugs, unrefined ergonomics, and overheating that fail to justify its steep 3.29 million won (roughly $2,400) price tag.

  • Hardware paradox: While reviewers praise its seamless Under-Display Camera (UDC) and rich haptic stereo speakers, the matte nano-texture glass degrades viewing angles, causes white sparkle noise, and fails to render true blacks under indoor lighting.
  • Severe software rough edges: The phone suffers from unaligned setup menus, stuttering orientation switches, app letterboxing, and a high-offset landscape keyboard that strains thumbs and ruins typing balance.
  • Practical friction in Korea: Eliminating the physical SIM tray forces local buyers onto eSIM-only setups—incurring reissuance fees during carrier switches—while a rigid 50:50 multitasking layout collapses into an unusable sliver whenever the virtual keyboard pops up.
  • Durability and thermal alarm: Reports of early screen fractures overseas, coupled with rumored 60% manufacturing yields at Foxconn, compound user frustration as the phone's vapor chamber radiates heat past 37°C (98.6°F) during basic web browsing.

First-Generation Flaws at Ultra-Luxury Pricing

The reception in Seoul underscores a familiar hazard of first-generation hardware: ambitious design colliding with rushed software. Nano-texture glass may excel on desktop monitors like the Pro Display XDR, but on a handheld device constantly viewed from off-axis angles, the etched surface scatters light and compromises OLED contrast. Combined with third-party apps and popular games failing to scale natively to the unfolded display, the device gives the distinct impression of a rushed launch meant to hit calendar deadlines rather than Apple’s usual standard of polish.

Productivity—the primary selling point for large-screen foldables—takes the hardest hit. Attempting to split the screen between research documents or AI chat windows reveals an inflexible interface that cannot dynamically adjust partition ratios. Once the on-screen keyboard appears, it consumes more than half the display, frequently glitched by laggy swipe gestures. For professionals expecting a pocket-sized workstation, the software layer currently functions more like an impediment than an asset.

An Uphill Battle Against Established Rivals

In South Korea, the device faces an unforgiving benchmark: Samsung Electronics, which has spent years trimming millimeters and grams from its Galaxy Z Fold series. Side by side, early testers note that a bare iPhone Duo feels noticeably heavier and more fatiguing on the wrist than even a fully cased Galaxy Z Fold 8. That physical fatigue, paired with palm-warming thermal spread from the chassis, makes daily handling a chore.

Apple's loyal customer base has long tolerated premium pricing in exchange for refined user experiences, but asking consumers to spend 3.29 million won to act as beta testers tests the limits of that goodwill. Until Apple patches its erratic orientation animations, rethinks its rigid multitasking UI, and proves its hinge durability over time, the iPhone Duo remains an expensive novelty best watched from the sidelines.
