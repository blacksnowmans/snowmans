South Korea's Ruling Party Faces Trust Crisis Amid Diplomatic Push

September 10, 2026

  • President Lee Jae-myung's state visit to France highlights high-stakes discussions on foreign troop deployments and aerospace defense collaboration.
  • Relations between the ruling Democratic Party and its progressive ally, the Rebuilding Korea Party, are strained over broken merger promises and past election disputes.
  • Observers warn that fractured trust within the progressive camp threatens their prospects in upcoming by-elections.

President Lee Jae-myung’s state visit to France this week brings South Korea's foreign policy and defense strategy to the forefront. Beyond traditional diplomatic engagements, the summit is expected to tackle sensitive discussions regarding potential troop deployment and joint aerospace defense initiatives. Drawing on France's model of independent nuclear and defense capabilities, the push aims to integrate cutting-edge technology and space industries rather than relying solely on diplomatic pageantry.

However, back in Seoul, the progressive political landscape is grappling with a severe crisis of trust. Tensions between the Democratic Party and the Rebuilding Korea Party—led by former Justice Minister Cho Kuk—have flared up over failed merger discussions from past local elections. Cho recently revealed that senior Democratic Party figures discouraged him from running in Busan to avoid a polarized electoral showdown, only to later face public criticism for the decision. Critics argue that internal power struggles and a lack of accountability within the Democratic Party are damaging the unity of the broader progressive bloc.

Ultimately, these internal fractures pose a greater threat to the progressive movement than any opposition offensive. As parties look ahead to crucial by-elections, superficial alliances and political posturing will no longer suffice. Without genuine self-reflection, honest apologies, and a restoration of mutual trust between South Korea's liberal factions, their grand political ambitions risk collapsing from within.
