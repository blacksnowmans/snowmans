South Korea Releases Mid-Year Land Values, Triggering Tax Concerns

October 29, 2026

  • South Korean local governments nationwide have released their official mid-year land value assessments (known as *gaebyeol gongsijiga*) for properties that underwent subdivision, merging, or boundary changes in the first half of the year.
  • These government valuations serve as the foundational basis for calculating property taxes, comprehensive real estate holding taxes, and various state development charges.
  • Property owners have until November 27 to review their valuations online via the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport’s official portal or at local municipal offices, and to file formal objections if they believe the assessments are unfair.

The simultaneous release of thousands of land valuations across regions from Wanju and Dangjin to Jeju serves as an annual stress test for South Korean property owners. While administrative updates of this scale often fly under the radar, they directly dictate household tax burdens. Because a higher valuation translates directly into heavier property and health insurance holding taxes, these figures function less as abstract statistics and more as a direct strike on disposable income.

For ordinary citizens, navigating the opaque appraisal process requires vigilance rather than passive acceptance. Automated administrative pricing often leaves little room for individual circumstances, making the November 27 deadline a critical window for taxpayers to push back against inflated assessments. In an economic climate already squeezed by high living costs, failing to verify one's property score can prove an expensive oversight.
