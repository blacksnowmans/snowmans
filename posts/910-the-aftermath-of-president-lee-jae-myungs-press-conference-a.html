The Aftermath of President Lee Jae-myung’s Press Conference: A Call for Systemic Reform

October 10, 2026

Following President Lee Jae-myung’s recent press conference, South Korea’s political landscape is in turmoil, with public opinion deeply divided over whether the president’s outreach represents genuine sincerity or a failure to address the core issues plaguing his administration.

  • The "Hidden Hand" Problem: The administration is under intense pressure to sever ties with unofficial, private advisors who claim to influence state affairs, a practice that has severely eroded public trust.
  • Alienated Supporters: President Lee’s base feels betrayed by his past silence in the face of political attacks, leading to a profound sense of emotional abandonment among those who helped bring him to power.
  • Institutional Breakdown: The controversy highlights a critical failure in the presidential staff system, which is currently unable to filter information or prevent the president from becoming isolated.
  • Path Forward: The focus is shifting from the mechanics of the press conference to the urgent need for a cabinet reshuffle and a return to transparent, institutionalized governance.

The Crisis of Governance and Communication

The most pressing issue remains the influence of "unofficial channels"—individuals who lack formal roles but claim to have the president's ear. President Lee’s plea for these figures to cease their activities is a necessary first step, but it reveals a structural weakness: the presidential office is currently failing to function as a transparent, formal entity. When policy is perceived to be influenced by shadows rather than a professional system, the result is deep public suspicion and the appearance of "privatized" power.

Furthermore, the president’s struggle to reconnect with his supporters illustrates a failure of political leadership. Supporters aren't just angry about policy—they feel discarded. By remaining silent during political firestorms, the president was perceived as complicit in the attacks against his own side. To recover, the administration must stop relying on sterile, scripted press events and instead engage in "raw," authentic communication. Moving toward more open, even combative, media platforms could help bridge the gap, but only if the president displays the courage to pivot toward a more humble, direct style of leadership.

Beyond the Rhetoric: What Actually Matters

Political change will not come from rhetorical appeals but from tangible actions, specifically personnel reform. The current distrust is a "market" that has already set in; it cannot be dismantled by mere words. Replacing key figures in the administration is the only way to signal that the internal systems are finally being overhauled.

Critics often point to economic indicators as the source of falling approval ratings, but this is a superficial reading. The true crisis is the collapse of leadership and the absence of a functioning internal check-and-balance system. The goal for the public and the political sphere should not be the destruction of the current administration, but its normalization. If the administration continues to ignore the need for structural repair, it risks irrelevance. Real political progress will only begin when the president moves past the current defensive posture and embraces a transparent, system-led approach to governance.
