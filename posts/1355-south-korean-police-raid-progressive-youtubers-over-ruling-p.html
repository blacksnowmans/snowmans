South Korean Police Raid Progressive YouTubers Over Ruling Party Primary Polls, Sparking Internal Feud

October 20, 2026

South Korean police have launched search-and-seizure raids against prominent progressive commentators over alleged opinion poll tampering during the ruling Democratic Party of Korea's primary race, triggering accusations of state overreach and bitter internal factional warfare.

  • The Raids: Police executed search warrants against progressive political channels, including Park Si-young TV, investigating whether on-air remarks during the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) national convention violated election laws by encouraging supporters to manipulate polling responses.
  • Friendly Fire: Unlike routine clashes between rival camps, the criminal complaints originated from within the ruling progressive bloc itself, with allies of the winning primary faction targeting commentators who backed their rivals.
  • The "Notebook" Controversy: Critics, including Park Si-young TV consultant Lee Hye-yeon, suspect the raids are linked to a memo circulated by DPK figure Kim Min-seok, sparking fears that the party leadership is assembling a blacklist to purge dissenting digital creators.
  • Chilling Effect: Legal and political figures, such as former lawmaker Choe Kang-wook, warn that deploying criminal investigators against standard campaign rhetoric sets a dangerous precedent that criminalizes regular political speech.

A Dangerous Shift Toward Criminalizing Politics

Mobilizing police power to target on-air commentary during an intra-party primary represents an alarming judicial encroachment into South Korea's public discourse. Rather than resolving convention disputes through debate or internal party reconciliation, victorious insiders have weaponized the penal code. This development is especially jarring under President Lee Jae-myung's administration, which came to power promising to dismantle the widespread abuse of prosecutorial and police power that characterized Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's time in office. Instead, the shadow of judicial overreach continues to hang over the political arena.

Subjecting YouTube panels to search-and-seizure operations over speculative remarks or partisan cheerleading threatens party autonomy. When routine primary posturing is treated as criminal obstruction of business, democratic debate is effectively paralyzed. As local DPK figures such as Mapo district chair Lee Ji-eun have pointed out, dragging intra-party rivals before magistrates is a self-inflicted wound that alienates the grassroots base.

Fracturing the Progressive Coalition from Within

The deeper damage lies in the growing paranoia surrounding progressive new media. Independent YouTube networks have long functioned as the lifeblood of the liberal base, countering the country's conservative-dominated traditional press. Yet, rumors of targeted lists and retaliatory investigations suggest the ruling party's leadership is turning on the very voices that sustained it. If the DPK relies on search warrants rather than dialogue to discipline its own coalition, it risks suffocating the vibrant digital ecosystem that drove its political success.
