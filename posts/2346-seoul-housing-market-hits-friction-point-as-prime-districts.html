Seoul Housing Market Hits Friction Point as Prime Districts Slump

November 15, 2026

Seoul’s premier apartment markets are experiencing sharp price cuts alongside a severe transaction freeze, signaling a structural downturn in South Korea's real estate engine as high mortgage rates and tight credit squeeze out buyers.

  • Price drops in prime areas: Asking prices in key Seoul riverside neighborhoods, such as Seongdong and Gangdong districts, fell by up to 400 million won (about $290,000) in a single month, while Songpa District in the affluent Gangnam area slipped 0.35% in a week.
  • Unregulated spillover: Strict credit caps in Seoul have created a "balloon effect," pushing capital into outer regions like Suwon’s Gwonseon-gu in Gyeonggi Province, which surged 5.53% over three months.
  • Rental market pressure: Blocked sales have forced prospective buyers into South Korea's jeonse (lump-sum deposit) rental system, driving up rental costs even as nationwide jeonse fraud cases surpassed 40,000.
  • Imminent policy choices: The South Korean government is set to decide in December whether to lift land transaction permit controls in Seoul, balancing market normalization against reigniting speculative buying.

Market Divergence and the Transaction Freeze

Despite an overall weekly apartment price rise of just 0.10% across Seoul, the market is splitting down the middle. Lower-priced outer districts like Seongbuk-gu rose 0.39% as priced-out buyers sought entry-level homes, but iconic high-end districts are faltering. The 0.35% drop in Songpa District demonstrates that even Seoul's most resilient "Gangnam 3" submarket is losing momentum. Government officials, including Prime Minister Han Seong-sook, argue that public supply measures are steering the market toward a stable downward trajectory. However, field experts note that the decline is driven by an absolute transaction cliff—buyers simply cannot take on heavy mortgages under current borrowing limits and elevated interest rates, leaving even discounted properties unsold.

Policy Dilemmas and the Looming 2026 Supply Cliff

This freeze in buying activity has spilled directly into the rental market. Unable to purchase homes, families are staying in jeonse leases, driving deposit prices higher across major metropolitan hubs. While approximately 40,000 new apartment completions slated across the Seoul capital region through early next year should offer temporary relief to renters, the mid-term outlook remains volatile. Analysts warn that if mortgage rates reach the 6% to 8% range, forced liquidations by over-leveraged owners could accelerate price drops. Furthermore, high construction costs and frozen credit have choked off new building permits, setting the stage for a severe housing supply drought by 2026 once current completions clear out.
