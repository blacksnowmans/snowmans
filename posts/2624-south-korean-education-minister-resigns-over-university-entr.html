South Korean Education Minister Resigns Over University Entrance Exam Chaos

November 23, 2026

South Korean Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin stepped down on November 22 following severe public backlash over mismanagement of the national college entrance exam, with President Lee Jae-myung approving his dismissal within hours in an effective firing.

  • What happened: Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin resigned to take responsibility for operational failures and poor preparation surrounding the College Scholastic Ability Test (Suneung), South Korea's high-stakes national university entrance exam.
  • Presidential action: President Lee Jae-myung accepted Choi's resignation immediately and formally signed the dismissal order just seven hours later, declaring exam fairness an uncompromisable principle.
  • Political reaction: The People Power Party, South Korea's main conservative opposition party, demanded a full investigation and personal accountability from President Lee, while the ruling Democratic Party of Korea called for appointing an education expert to navigate ongoing industry changes.
  • Student and sector fallout: Affected students forming the Suneung Countermeasures Coalition demanded concrete victim remedies rather than just political resignations, while education officials called for sweeping reform of the rote-memorization testing system.

A Swift Presidential Dismissal to Quell Public Anger

The fallout from this year's Suneung highlights how politically sensitive university entrance exams remain in South Korea. Mismanagement around exam administration—including controversies over artificial intelligence integration—and Minister Choi's past public missteps severely eroded trust among test-takers and parents. By completing Choi's removal in less than eight hours on the very day he tendered his resignation, President Lee Jae-myung signaled zero tolerance for administrative failures that jeopardize test fairness.

However, replacing top leadership has become a predictable script whenever the national exam faces disruption. Suneung missteps have repeatedly claimed the heads of both the education ministry and national evaluation agencies. By treating Choi's exit as an immediate firing, the presidential office sought to contain public anger quickly, but it now faces the dual burden of managing post-exam university admissions while searching for a qualified successor.

Political Battle Lines and Demands for Structural Reform

The opposition People Power Party quickly used the scandal to target the administration, insisting that a minister's departure does not relieve President Lee of his obligation to explain the systemic failure directly to the public. Conversely, the ruling Democratic Party focused on damage control, urging the presidential office to appoint a seasoned professional capable of guiding the ministry through rapid shifts in educational policy.

For students and educators, political resignations offer little practical relief. The Suneung Countermeasures Coalition pressed the government for immediate measures to remedy the disruption caused to test-takers. Meanwhile, education leaders such as Education Superintendent Ahn Min-seok argued that the crisis exposes the fundamental limits of South Korea’s traditional, rote-memorization testing framework. Unless the government addresses these underlying structural flaws, simply replacing ministers will do little to break the cycle of recurring exam crises.
