Israel Faces Deep Political Paralysis as Netanyahu Fails to Win Majority

October 29, 2026

  • Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's conservative bloc has fallen short of a parliamentary majority following a razor-thin general election.
  • Hardline far-right figures led by Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich secured 13 seats, positioning themselves to heavily influence any coalition talks.
  • The deadlock leaves Israel's next government entirely unpredictable, with potential outcomes ranging from a broad anti-Netanyahu coalition to another snap election.
  • Arab voter turnout surged in direct opposition to nationalist and anti-Palestinian rhetoric, becoming a decisive factor in the fractured results.

Israel is staring down a severe political crisis after the latest general election left long-standing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu unable to secure the parliamentary majority needed to govern. Despite falling short of the numbers, Netanyahu continues to insist he will form a right-wing nationalist government. However, with exit polls and final tallies signaling deep gridlock, his grip on power faces its most serious challenge yet.

The ballot box has laid bare a deeply fractured society where anti-Palestinian sentiment and far-right extremism have taken center stage. Far-right leaders Ben-Gvir and Smotrich leveraged open hostility and promises of controversial West Bank settlement expansion to capture 13 crucial seats. For international observers, this normalisation of grievance politics serves as a stark warning about the fragility of democratic norms when polarization is weaponized for electoral gain.

As the country weighs its options—ranging from a messy anti-Netanyahu alliance to yet another paralyzing return to the polls—the path forward remains entirely uncertain. What is clear is that a political system built on exclusion and division ultimately risks imploding from within, leaving citizens to bear the heavy cost of prolonged instability.
