South Korea’s Justice Ministry Sparks Outrage Over New Investigative Office Plan

September 10, 2026

South Korea’s ruling liberal camp is up in arms following the Ministry of Justice's unveiling of a new restructuring plan that critics say resurrects powerful prosecution powers under a different name.

    The Ministry of Justice released an organizational plan for the newly formed Corruption Investigation Office and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (SCIA), which critics argue creates a bloated, mutated version of the old prosecution service.
  • Over 600 applicants hastily withdrew their support after realizing the new bodies retain heavy prosecutorial control rather than enforcing a true separation of investigation and indictment.
  • Kim Ji-yong, a hardline career prosecutor who previously opposed the dismantling of prosecutorial powers under the Moon Jae-in administration, was nominated as the inaugural head of the SCIA.
  • The plan dumps a massive backlog of 170,000 unresolved cases onto police while raising police investigative budgets by a paltry 300 million won (about $215,000).

The sudden move—announced quietly while President Lee Jae-myung was on a state visit to France—has left grassroots party members and liberal activists feeling profoundly betrayed. Activists who spent years marching in candlelight protests to break up the feared prosecutional monopoly see the new rules as a backdoor betrayal. Instead of stripping prosecutors of their sweeping powers, the Justice Ministry's enforcement decrees add layers of bureaucratic oversight, allowing career prosecutors to maintain their grip on high-stakes criminal probes.

This is a high-stakes political gamble for the administration. By placing veteran prosecutors back into key leadership roles to appease institutional stability, the government risks alienating its core base. If the ruling Democratic Party, South Korea's main liberal party, fails to block these measures, it risks shattering the foundational promise of judicial reform that brought them to power.
