Packing for the Camino de Santiago: How to Keep Your Backpack Under 5 Kilograms

September 07, 2026

Preparing for the Camino de Santiago is an exercise in ruthless minimalism, where every extra gram directly punishes your ankles and knees.

    The 10% Rule: Pack no more than 10% of your body weight; statistics show that 90% of pilgrims carrying packs over 7kg (about 15 pounds) suffer back or ankle pain within the first three days. Ultra-Light Gear: Cutting out bulky winter sleeping bags in favor of a 275-gram Decathlon liner and trimming a shampoo bar in half keeps the final backpack weight down to a manageable 5.2kg (about 11.5 pounds). Wardrobe Minimums: Limit clothing to just three functional, quick-drying items—what you wear plus two changes—relying on daily sink washes rather than packing extra sets. Blister Prevention: Prioritize durable socks like Darn Tough, sports tape for joint support, and specialized foot-care items over luxury comforts.

The Physics of Packing: Why 5.2 Kilograms is the Magic Number

For anyone prepping for a long-distance trek, gear choice is make-or-break. Opting for a 34-liter Deuter backpack keeps the frame breathable under the summer sun, but hitting the target weight requires constant editing. The author initially aimed for a 4kg target, which proved entirely unrealistic after factoring in water, a cooling pad, and basic layers. Shaving off redundant cases, heavy toiletries, and "just in case" items is the only way to avoid chronic musculoskeletal strain. On the Camino, where public albergues (pilgrim hostels) cost around €10 a night and fill up fast, carrying a lighter pack means moving faster and securing a good bed at the end of an eight-hour walking day.

Sleep systems demand the same brutal logic. Heavy 600g down sleeping bags are a luxury liability in late summer; swapping them out for a lightweight sleeping bag liner cuts weight in half while avoiding sketchy hostel blankets. Staying warm isn't a matter of heavy gear, but of clever layering—pulling on a windbreaker, fleece, or even a poncho if the temperature plummets. Similarly, toiletries are notorious weight-thieves. Swapping heavy liquids for single-use enzyme washing powders, half a shampoo bar, and a single tube of sunscreen eliminates pounds of dead weight.

Survival Hacks: Tape, Socks, and Analog Life

Foot care is where a pilgrimage is won or lost. The packing list relies on three pairs of high-performance socks, athletic tape instead of bulky ankle braces, and a compact kit for treating blisters. Little tricks—like replacing heavy carabiners with safety pins to dry laundry on the outside of the pack, or swapping wireless earbuds for a wired set to dodge charging stress—show an understanding of trail life. While a 5.2kg bag will still feel heavy after hours on the dusty trails of northern Spain, it represents the exact boundary between protecting your body and crushing it under your own anxiety.
