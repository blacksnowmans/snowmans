Why South Korea's Fast-Moving Tech Users Are Heading Back to ChatGPT

September 03, 2026

  • The shift: Many South Korean tech users who previously abandoned OpenAI's ChatGPT for Anthropic's Claude are returning to ChatGPT.
  • The driver: While Claude offers superior writing and comprehension, users are prioritizing raw utility—such as coding, spreadsheet analysis, and plugin ecosystems—over literary polish.
  • The broader risk: As users increasingly rely on AI for critical decisions, the battle between AI giants risks turning automated advice into an opaque advertising channel.

When Anthropic’s Claude first launched, it won over many users with its natural tone, contextual depth, and ability to handle massive documents. For a while, early adopters declared ChatGPT obsolete. But a reverse migration is now underway among impatient users who need tools that do more than write pretty sentences.

The core issue comes down to execution. While Claude excels at text, OpenAI's ChatGPT operates as a fully fledged workhorse equipped with web browsing, data analysis, and coding environments. For professionals juggling tight deadlines and complex tasks, an AI that debugs code and analyzes spreadsheets beats an articulate conversation partner every time. It is a pragmatic choice driven by productivity rather than brand loyalty.

Beyond daily utility, the AI landscape faces larger tremors, including impending public offerings from major players like Anthropic that threaten to shake up tech sector investments. Yet the deeper concern may be user complacency. When people blindly trust AI recommendations without verifying sources, they risk becoming passive consumers being steered by corporate monetization models rather than objective advisors.
