Behind the Scenes of South Korea's Most-Watched Morning Broadcast

September 16, 2026

Behind South Korea’s leading alternative morning news show, Kim Ou-joon's Humble Opinion Doesn't Come Easy, lies an army of unseen producers, writers, and technicians whose daily grind keeps the digital-first broadcast alive.

  • The Daily Engine: Weekdays at 7:05 AM, the independent political broadcast goes live, tackling the country's most contentious political news.
  • The Unsung Workforce: End-credits reveal dozens of producers, writers, and sound engineers working through the night to vet facts and draft scripts.
  • Direct Engagement: The show regularly bypasses traditional media gatekeepers by hosting large-scale public broadcasts, fostering a direct line with its loyal audience.

For international observers watching South Korea's volatile political media landscape, commentary often focuses solely on charismatic hosts and polarizing figures. Yet the staying power of alternative media platforms like Kim Ou-joon's operation relies less on star power and more on rigorous production infrastructure. An eight-person writing staff, dedicated technical crews, and external composers like Yoon Il-sang and Second Moon combine to give the show its polished, cohesive cadence. In a fragmented media environment where trust in mainstream outlets is often fractured, this disciplined execution is what secures an audience.

Ultimately, these closing credits offer a rare glimpse into the mechanics of modern political commentary in Seoul. While critics and fans alike fixate on the political rows dissected on air, the real story is the methodical, labor-intensive assembly line required to produce three hours of live daily television outside the traditional corporate broadcasting establishment. It is a reminder that South Korea's robust political discourse is sustained not just by opinions, but by relentless behind-the-scenes labor.
