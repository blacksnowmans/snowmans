The Missing Agenda: Decoding the South Korea-France Summit

September 10, 2026

While President Yoon Suk-yeol touted a "Global Strategic Partnership" upgrade during his recent visit to Paris, the most telling aspect of the summit was the conspicuous silence regarding the Strait of Hormuz.

  • What happened: South Korea and France formally upgraded their diplomatic ties, highlighting a military intelligence-sharing agreement and plans for joint naval training.
  • The "Elephant in the room": Despite repeated questioning, the South Korean delegation pointedly avoided clarifying its stance on potential military involvement in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • The criticism: Analysts argue the administration is prioritizing cosmetic diplomatic "wins" over transparent engagement with the public on critical national security issues.
  • Financial scope: The summit also included a roughly $1.1 billion (10억 유로) "Lumiere Partnership" to bolster the two nations' video and media industries.

Diplomacy Through Omission

President Yoon’s administration framed the meeting as a historic milestone, claiming the relationship has been elevated to a "Global Strategic Partnership" for the first time in 22 years. However, much of the "new" security cooperation—such as the military intelligence protocols—consists of repackaged agreements from earlier this spring. Relying on recycled successes suggests an attempt to manufacture prestige rather than achieve substantial progress.

The most glaring issue remains the government’s "strategic ambiguity" regarding the Strait of Hormuz. In a democracy, foreign policy is not a private affair to be conducted behind closed doors. By refusing to provide a clear position on potential naval deployment, the administration leaves the public in the dark about matters that directly impact national security and the lives of citizens. The silence is not diplomatic prudence; it is an evasion of accountability.

Domestic Politics and the Specter of "Targeted" Investigations

The confusion in Paris is matched by a growing sense of disorder in South Korean domestic politics, particularly regarding the prosecution of Democratic Party lawmaker Kim Seung-won. Critics argue that the investigation, which failed to yield any credible convictions, was a politically motivated "hit job" orchestrated during Han Dong-hoon’s tenure as Minister of Justice.

The situation has taken a bizarre turn as Han, now a politician, has begun demanding explanations from the current Ministry of Justice regarding these same investigations—essentially questioning the very operations he once oversaw. This performative contradiction has left even members of his own party, the People Power Party (South Korea's main conservative party), visibly frustrated. When political actors treat the justice system as a tool for public relations or partisan warfare, it erodes the foundation of public trust.

Ultimately, the summit served as a reminder that diplomatic success is not measured in photo ops or vague partnerships. True strength in foreign policy requires a government that is honest at home and prepared to defend its strategic decisions before its own people. Without transparency, the "Global Strategic Partnership" is little more than a polished title for a policy of silence.
