Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Ratings Stagnate as Economic and Judicial Pressures Mount

October 26, 2026

Recent polling indicates a worrying stall in public support for South Korean President Lee Jae-myung, as the ruling Democratic Party falls behind the conservative opposition for the first time in months amid economic volatility and stalled reforms.

  • President Lee’s approval rating dipped to 41.2%, while negative sentiment remains high at 62.3%, driven by a slumping stock market and real estate instability.
  • The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the current ruling party, saw its support drop to 39.7%, losing its lead to the People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, which rose to 42.0%.
  • Public demand for judicial accountability is surging, with 60% of respondents supporting an arrest warrant for Kim Keon-hee, wife of the ousted and imprisoned former leader Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • Calls are intensifying to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae over allegations that he is "privatizing" judicial power and favoring opposition figures like Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon.
  • A majority of citizens (5 to 3 ratio) now support the independent exercise of wartime operational control (OPCON), reflecting growing distrust in U.S. security guarantees ahead of a potential second Trump administration.

A Stalled Mandate and the "Honeymoon" Hangover

The current stagnation in approval ratings suggests that the Lee administration’s "honeymoon phase" has curdled into public frustration. While the President's core base remains firm, moderate voters are drifting away, citing a lack of "decisive action" on both the economy and judicial reform. The sharp drop in the DPK’s popularity—falling below the critical 40% mark—indicates that a legislative majority is no longer a shield against criticism. Voters are increasingly impatient with what they perceive as a "lukewarm" response to the remnants of the previous administration's scandals, particularly those involving Mr. Yoon and his wife.

The focus on Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae highlights a deepening crisis of trust in the legal system. Accusations that Cho is positioning himself as a future political contender while allegedly protecting PPP figures have turned the judiciary into a primary political battlefield. For the Lee administration, the path forward is narrow: it must stabilize a rocky stock market and volatile housing costs while simultaneously satisfying the public's appetite for "justice" regarding the Yoon era. Failure to do so risks turning this current plateau into a permanent decline.

National Sovereignty and the Trump Factor

The poll results regarding military sovereignty signal a significant shift in the South Korean psyche. The 5-to-3 majority in favor of declaring the independent exercise of wartime operational control—the authority to lead troops during a conflict, currently held by a U.S.-led command—reflects a pragmatism born of geopolitical uncertainty. With the possibility of Donald Trump returning to the White House, many Koreans feel the nation can no longer afford to leave its security entirely in foreign hands. This "sovereignty-first" sentiment provides President Lee with a powerful mandate to pursue a more autonomous defense policy, even as he grapples with domestic scandals.

Ultimately, the administration faces a double-edged sword. The same public that demands "bold reform" and "judicial justice" is also highly sensitive to the economic "bread and butter" issues that are currently flagging. To break the deadlock, the DPK leadership must move beyond internal power struggles and present a clear, unified vision for both the economy and the rule of law. As the numbers show, the public’s patience is not infinite; they are no longer waiting for promises, but for tangible results.
