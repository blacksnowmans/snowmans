South Korea Bets on "Physical AI" as Industrial Giants and Regional Hubs Pivot to Robotics

November 05, 2026

South Korea is aggressively shifting its artificial intelligence ambitions from digital chatbots to embodied hardware, catalyzed by a 1.3 trillion won (about $950 million) regional hub project in North Jeolla Province and major platform bets by conglomerates like Hyundai and SK Telecom.

  • A provincial robotics capital: Jeonbuk Special Self-Governing Province has committed 1.3 trillion won to establish the Saemangeum reclaimed coastal area as a national hub for physical AI, supported by a 500 billion won (about $370 million) autonomous walking robot (AAR) corridor linking the industrial cities of Iksan and Gunsan.
  • Conglomerates build physical bodies: Automaker Hyundai Motor is expanding its specialized eccentric-wheel mobility base, MobED, while gaming giant NCSoft has entered the race with Vary Vision 3B, a lightweight computer-vision model tailored for compact delivery bots and drones.
  • Targeted state contracts: Domestic robotics firms are securing dedicated public capital, including a 7.3 billion won (about $5.4 million) government humanoid grant won by software specialist Clobot, alongside surgical AI deployments by medical robotics firm Curexo.
  • Strategic pivot under Lee Jae-myung: The administration of President Lee Jae-myung has designated physical AI as a critical sovereign sector, expanding regulatory sandboxes to accelerate testing while facing growing debate over industrial job displacement.

Hardware Grounding in the Post-LLM Era

South Korea's push into "physical AI"—intelligent systems capable of navigating physical laws, operating machinery, and manipulating real-world objects—reflects a calculated strategic pivot. Rather than attempting to outspend American hyperscalers on massive general-purpose language models, Korean industry is leveraging its traditional strength: advanced manufacturing and hardware integration. From Hyundai's balance-maintaining transport modules to SK Telecom's development of synthetic training data alongside the Ministry of Science and ICT, the focus is squarely on deploying AI where steel meets the floor.

Regional Stakes and the Labor Balance

For Jeonbuk, a rural province long struggling with youth depopulation and deindustrialization, the 1.3 trillion won project represents an economic survival strategy. By tying hydrogen energy infrastructure and local universities to high-spec robotics testing grounds at Saemangeum, local leadership hopes to retain technical talent outside of the Seoul metropolitan bubble.

The broader challenge for President Lee Jae-myung will be managing the social fallout. While the current administration aims to foster public-private collaboration that avoids the corporate favoritism seen during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, deploying autonomous physical labor directly threatens blue-collar manufacturing employment. If Seoul wants physical AI to anchor the country’s next export cycle, state-funded retraining and equitable access to automation technologies must advance as quickly as the robots themselves.
