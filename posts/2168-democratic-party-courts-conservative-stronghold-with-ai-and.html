Democratic Party Courts Conservative Stronghold with AI and Robotics Vow

November 10, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party is making a strategic push into South Korea’s conservative heartland, promising a high-tech industrial revival for the city of Gumi to dismantle decades of regional political loyalty.

  • High-Level Outreach: Kim Min-seok, a senior leader of the Democratic Party (DP), led a field meeting in Gumi to announce a "Future Vision" for the Daegu-Gyeongbuk (TK) region.
  • Industrial Pivot: The DP pledged to transform the aging Gumi National Industrial Complex into a global hub for Artificial Intelligence (AI) and robotics.
  • Fiscal Support: The party is reviewing a proposal to provide 2 trillion KRW (about $1.4 billion) in national firefighting funds to relieve the financial burden on local governments.
  • Political Expansion: The move aims to grow the DP’s regional membership to 100,000, signaling an aggressive attempt to win over voters ahead of the 2028 general elections.

Revitalizing the "Engine of Growth"

Gumi is more than just an industrial city; it is the symbolic birthplace of former President Park Chung-hee and has long been the fortress of South Korean conservatism. However, the Gumi National Industrial Complex, once the engine of the country’s economic rise, has struggled with aging infrastructure and a "brain drain" of young talent. By selecting this city for its "Future Vision" declaration, the Democratic Party (DP)—South Korea's current ruling party—is positioning itself as the only force capable of practical economic modernization.

The party's strategy focuses on "AI Transformation" (AX). Rather than traditional manufacturing, the DP is promising legislative and budgetary support to integrate AI and robotics into existing factory lines. This is a direct challenge to the People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, which has traditionally taken the region's support for granted. DP leadership argued that during the term of Mr. Yoon Suk Yeol (who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment), the concentration of resources in the Seoul metropolitan area left regional economies like Gumi on the brink of collapse.

Breaking the "TK Neglect" Narrative

For years, residents in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region have complained of "TK Passing"—a feeling of being neglected by the central government. The DP is attempting to heal this rift with substantial fiscal carrots. The proposed $1.4 billion injection into firefighting services is a prime example. By shifting the cost of public safety from cash-strapped local municipalities to the national treasury, the Lee Jae-myung administration aims to show immediate, tangible benefits to conservative voters who have historically viewed the DP with suspicion.

A Strategy of Pragmatism Over Ideology

In my view, this is a calculated "grand strategy" move by President Lee’s party. By entering Gumi with a blueprint for robots and AI, they are attempting to replace old ideological divisions with a narrative of "competence versus stagnation." If the DP can prove that a liberal administration is more effective at saving Gumi’s factories than the conservatives have been, the political map of South Korea will be fundamentally rewritten.

The target of 100,000 party members in a "hostile" province is ambitious, but it reflects a new confidence in the DP's "nationwide party" strategy. In a region where young people are desperate for high-tech jobs, the promise of becoming a "Robotics Mecca" is a potent alternative to the nostalgia-heavy politics of the past. Success here would not just be a win for Gumi, but a sign that the DP has successfully moved beyond its traditional strongholds to become a truly national governing force.
