Korea's "Last-Place" Public-Company Appointment Sparks Cronyism Controversy

October 06, 2026

  • What happened: Ha Dong-keun, newly appointed head of the Korea District Heating Corporation (a state-run energy provider), ranked dead last in both document screening and interviews among finalists for the $240,000-a-year (320 million won) post.
  • The connection: Ha is closely tied to the "Seongnam faction"—a network of aides from President Lee Jae-myung’s local government days—and reportedly officiated the wedding of Kim Hyun-ji, a powerful presidential secretariat official.
  • The fallout: Coming less than two years into the administration, the appointment has triggered widespread backlash, with even core supporters breaking ranks and critics branding the move a blatant political reward that undermines the government's rhetoric of fairness.

When an applicant finishes 8th out of 8 in document screening and 5th out of 5 in interviews, common sense says they are out of the running. But in South Korea's state-owned enterprises, competence is apparently negotiable. The appointment of Ha Dong-keun to lead the Korea District Heating Corporation has laid bare the uncomfortable reality of patronage hiring under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung.

While legally sound—passing through the standard ministerial recommendation and board review committees—the process was a masterclass in checking boxes while ignoring substance. Evaluators gave Ha near-failing marks on domain-specific knowledge for an energy infrastructure giant where technical expertise is non-negotiable. Yet he secured the post, alongside other Seongnam-linked figures like Nam Kwang-woo, who similarly bagged a top environmental technology post despite mediocre interview scores.

This is not an isolated personnel misstep; it is a symptom of political decay. For an administration that rode to power on promises of fairness, sliding into closed-door cronyism barely halfway through its term signals an early onset of lame-duck arrogance. When political loyalty consistently beats out competence for public sector leadership, the real cost is paid by taxpayers and demoralized rank-and-file workers stuck watching well-connected outsiders take the helm.
