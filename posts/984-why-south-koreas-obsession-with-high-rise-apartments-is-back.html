Why South Korea's Obsession with High-Rise Apartments is Backfiring

October 11, 2026

South Korea’s relentless pursuit of high-rise apartment living as a status symbol is colliding with demographic reality, leaving regional markets with unsold skyscrapers and a looming crisis in aging housing.

  • The flashpoint: A newly launched 49-story apartment complex in South Chungcheong Province received just 5 applications for 465 available units, exposing the severe risks of blindly building high-rises outside major capitals.
  • The maintenance trap: Towering concrete blocks carry exponential maintenance and utility costs, a punishing burden for retirees living on fixed incomes.
  • The reconstruction myth: Soaring construction costs and strict floor-area regulations mean the lucrative "reconstruction lotto" of the past—where older low-rise buildings generated massive windfalls—is mathematically dead.
  • The demographic shift: With over 1.7 million empty homes already registered nationwide, South Korea's rapid aging and population decline threaten to devastate property values that make up 90% of seniors' wealth.

For decades, South Koreans have treated housing not merely as shelter, but as a giant trophy proving one's social standing and wealth. The higher the floor and the shinier the view, the higher the perceived personal value. But experts like architect Kim Ji-ho argue that this obsession has blinded the country to the structural and financial absurdities of vertical living, especially as the population shrinks and ages rapidly.

The traditional Korean apartment—rigidly built with load-bearing concrete walls—offers zero flexibility for changing lifestyles, remote work, or aging in place. Meanwhile, upscale retirement communities have devolved into out-of-reach luxury resorts for the ultra-wealthy, while rural and provincial areas face a glut of unlivable, abandoned properties. True residential value in a super-aging society is no longer about height or brand prestige, but "compressed cities"—walkable neighborhoods where hospitals, daily amenities, and social connections sit within a 15-minute radius.
