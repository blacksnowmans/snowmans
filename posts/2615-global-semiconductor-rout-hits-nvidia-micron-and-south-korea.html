Global Semiconductor Rout Hits Nvidia, Micron, and South Korea’s Memory Giants

November 22, 2026

A sudden convergence of regulatory probes, executive upheaval, and mounting AI bubble fears triggered a brutal global semiconductor sell-off, sending severe shockwaves through South Korean tech giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix.

  • Nvidia plunged 14% in a single day following news of a U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) accounting probe and leadership turmoil, sparking a sell-off that dragged down Broadcom, TSMC, and Micron.
  • South Korea's memory leaders took direct hits, with SK Hynix tumbling after a local brokerage issued a rare target price downgrade below market value right ahead of its U.S. ADR listing.
  • Retail investors face staggering losses, as South Korean individual traders who bought roughly 800 billion won ($570 million) of Nvidia shares on the dip accumulated an estimated 6 trillion won ($4.3 billion) in paper losses.
  • Legacy memory concerns compound the panic, with falling NAND flash prices and price-fixing lawsuits troubling the broader memory sector despite heavy HBM demand.

From Silicon Valley Probes to Seoul's Market Panic

The rout began in the U.S. when Nvidia dropped 14% in a single session after reports surfaced that the SEC had opened an inquiry into its accounting practices and circular financing arrangements—where capital invested in AI startups allegedly cycles back as chip purchases. Chaos deepened following news of long-time CEO Jensen Huang's resignation amid scandal, leaving interim CEO Colette Kress to manage executive uncertainty. The contagion rapidly spread: Broadcom sank 28% in a week, TSMC dropped nearly 20% on fresh U.S.-China export restriction fears, and Micron extended a week-long slide that has wiped out 46% of its value since June, despite announcing a massive 49 trillion won ($35 billion) stock buyback plan.

South Korean Tech Giants Face a Crucial Test

The global fallout delivered an immediate blow to South Korea's financial markets, heavily weighted toward tech exporters. SK Hynix, South Korea's top high-bandwidth memory (HBM) producer, broke below 1.12 million won after BNK Investment & Securities slashed its target price to 1.05 million won—a level lower than the prevailing stock price. Meanwhile, retail traders in South Korea—often called "Seohak ants" for their aggressive buying of overseas tech stock—sank hundreds of millions of dollars into Nvidia dips and leveraged ETFs like SOXL, only to see paper losses balloon into trillions of won.

Although credit rating agency Moody's emphasized that the core financial health and technology positions of Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix remain sound, market sentiment has shifted abruptly. Investors are moving away from speculative AI hype toward verifiable revenue and accounting transparency. With consumer memory demand lagging and AI bubble risks gaining traction in quantitative models, Korean chipmakers face a demanding environment where narrative alone will no longer defend valuations.
