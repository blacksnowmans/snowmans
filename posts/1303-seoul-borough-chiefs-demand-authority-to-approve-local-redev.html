Seoul Borough Chiefs Demand Authority to Approve Local Redevelopment Projects

October 19, 2026

An association representing Seoul’s 25 borough heads has voted to demand authority to designate redevelopment zones directly, seeking to bypass the central Seoul Metropolitan Government to accelerate local housing supply.

  • What happened: Seoul’s 25 district mayors agreed to push for national law amendments allowing local borough offices to approve small-to-medium redevelopment sites under 30,000 square meters (about 323,000 square feet, or under 800 housing units).
  • What it changes: Approval timelines for small urban renewal projects could drop from several years to 6–12 months by bypassing municipal reviews by the city government.
  • The pushback: Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon and city planners strongly oppose the initiative, warning it will result in chaotic, fragmented developments lacking crucial infrastructure like schools and roads.
  • Who is affected: Property owners in aging low-rise neighborhoods, prospective homebuyers, and redevelopment unions who fear local political interference and administrative delays.

Central Control vs. Local Ambition

Under current regulations, the Mayor of Seoul holds sole authority to designate urban renewal zones, acting as a safeguard to ensure cohesive master planning across the capital. Facing acute housing shortages, a coalition of 16 Seoul borough offices led by Dongjak District crafted a proposal to transfer designation power for smaller sites to local district heads. They are also demanding the power to approve plan modifications of up to 20%—including floor-area ratios and density limits—without municipal intervention.

The Seoul Metropolitan Government has forcefully rejected the proposal. City officials pointed out that district offices already control seven out of nine steps in the redevelopment approval process, where the vast majority of project bottlenecks actually occur. Furthermore, city surveys revealed that 17 out of Seoul’s 25 district offices do not employ a single professional experienced in urban planning committee reviews. A coalition representing 65 Seoul redevelopment union leaders also voiced opposition, fearing local political meddling and arbitrary decisions by borough chiefs seeking quick election wins.

High Risk for Small-Scale Urban Renewal

While bypassing city reviews offers a shortcut, small-scale redevelopments carry structural financial flaws, including higher per-unit construction costs and slimmer profit margins. In top-tier locations—such as Yongsan’s Cheongpa area or Gwangjin’s Guui-dong—maximizing density allowances can offset these high costs. However, in average neighborhoods, artificially splitting larger sites into smaller 30,000-square-meter pockets just to rush local approvals risks burdening residents with steep construction costs while producing poorly planned, overcrowded housing.
