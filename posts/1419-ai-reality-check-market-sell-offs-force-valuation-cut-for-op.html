AI Reality Check: Market Sell-Offs Force Valuation Cut for OpenAI as Focus Shifts to Practical Utility

October 24, 2026

Global tech markets face a sharp reckoning over massive infrastructure spending, forcing AI leaders like OpenAI to lower valuation targets while accelerating the shift toward practical, autonomous enterprise tools.

  • Market Shock: Tech shares plummeted—with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index falling 6.72% and South Korea’s benchmark Kospi index dropping over 2%—as investors grew wary of big tech’s huge capital expenditures relative to immediate profits.
  • Valuation Adjustments: OpenAI trimmed its target valuation from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion for a $30 billion funding round, while Anthropic pushes for an upcoming IPO despite disclosures showing $80 billion in cumulative losses.
  • Enterprise Adoption: Uber deployed ChatGPT to 30,000 employees globally, while South Korea's BNK Financial Group selected Samsung SDS, the IT services arm of Samsung Group, to build a dedicated group-wide generative AI platform.
  • Hardware Alliances: Korean component makers like Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are expanding supply ties with Nvidia for high-performance substrates and sensors, cementing hardware supply chain integration despite market turbulence.

From Hype to Enterprise Efficiency

The honeymoon phase of blank-check spending on AI data centers is giving way to strict balance-sheet scrutiny. Despite Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang reassuring markets that memory supply shortages will persist through 2029—a nod to South Korean chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix—investors pulled back on fears that software revenues are not keeping pace with hardware costs. OpenAI’s decision to lower its target valuation during fundraising reflects a subtle shift in leverage toward investors who now demand clear paths to profitability.

At the same time, the technology itself is rapidly evolving from simple chat interfaces to background-operating autonomous agents. Anthropic’s preview of "Claude Duet," capable of completing tasks even when a user's laptop is closed, illustrates this pivot. In South Korea, enterprise adoption is moving from experimental use to core infrastructure. Beyond BNK Financial Group's platform project, LG CNS, the digital transformation unit of LG Group, surpassed 30,000 corporate users for its custom enterprise AI services in just eight months.

Physical Integration and Industry Friction

Hardware and physical applications are becoming the ultimate test for AI's real-world value. Korean suppliers are capturing key roles in Nvidia’s expanding ecosystem, particularly in high-performance flip-chip ball grid array (FC-BGA) substrates and robotic sensing technology. Meanwhile, industrial firms like S-Oil are deploying physical AI safety solutions across hazardous project sites in Korea.

However, this rapid integration brings severe growing pains. Recent security exploits highlight the risks of granting autonomous AI direct system access, while major music publishers have filed copyright lawsuits against Anthropic and AI music startup Suno. These clashes mirror a widening philosophical rift between AI developers calling for stricter regulation and hardware giants pushing for unhindered expansion.
