Political Warfare Erupts as Seoul Mayor Acquitted and Chief Justice Faces Impeachment Threats

October 27, 2026

The acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon on political fund charges has triggered a direct confrontation between the ruling Democratic Party and the judiciary, paralyzing the final days of the annual National Audit.

  • The Seoul High Court cleared Mayor Oh Se-hoon of charges related to illegal political expenses, prompting the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) to threaten impeachment against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.
  • Opposition lawmakers from the People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, leveled "luxury golf" allegations against First Lady Kim Hye-kyung, leading to a temporary shutdown of parliamentary proceedings.
  • New reports revealed that imprisoned former leader Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol and his wife have accumulated approximately $1.6 million (2.2 billion won) in prison commissary accounts, sparking a debate over "fandom politics" and inmate privileges.
  • The Justice Ministry is facing backlash over potential candidates for the top post who previously served as personal lawyers for President Lee Jae-myung, with the opposition claiming the move is intended to drop remaining charges against the President.

The Judiciary Under Siege: Acquittals and Impeachment

The appellate court’s decision to acquit Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon has fundamentally altered the political landscape. The ruling DPK, which had pushed for a conviction in the "Myung Tae-kyun-gate" scandal—a complex case involving allegations of third-party political fund payments—slammed the ruling as "arbitrary" and "divorced from public sentiment." Some ruling party lawmakers went as far as suggesting the introduction of "AI Judges" to eliminate perceived human bias, while others formally proposed a bill to force the Chief Justice to provide multiple candidates for Supreme Court vacancies to dilute his power.

This aggressive stance toward the judiciary highlights a deepening crisis of institutional trust. In South Korea's current polarized environment, court rulings are no longer viewed as final legal arbiters but as tactical setbacks or victories in a larger partisan war. The DPK’s move to weaponize impeachment against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae serves as a warning shot: the ruling party is prepared to reshape the judicial branch if its decisions interfere with the administration's political narrative. The PPP has responded by threatening street protests, accusing the government of "judicial intimidation."

National Audit Devolves into Personal Vendettas

The National Audit, an annual parliamentary review meant to scrutinize government policy, has instead become a theater for personal attacks. The PPP’s focus on First Lady Kim Hye-kyung’s alleged luxury golf lessons is a clear attempt to mirror the ruling party’s long-standing criticisms of the imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. Meanwhile, the revelation that Mr. Yoon and his wife hold $1.6 million in prison funds—money donated by die-hard supporters—illustrates the bizarre extremes of Korean political fandom. Even behind bars, the former leader remains a potent financial and symbolic figure for the opposition.

Beyond the personal mudslinging, significant policy shifts are being contested with little room for compromise. The government’s plan to have the head of the Major Crime Investigation Office (MCIO) attend Cabinet meetings has drawn fire from the PPP, who argue it compromises the agency's independence. Simultaneously, the DPK is pushing for the 독자선언 (independent declaration) of Wartime Operational Control (OPCON), seeking to reclaim command of South Korean troops from the United States. These structural changes to the nation’s security and legal apparatus are being hurried through a legislature that is currently more interested in scoring points than building consensus.
