Tokyo’s Hard-Right Turn: Sanae Takaichi Pushes Military Ambitions Under the Guise of International Law

October 22, 2026

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s alignment with U.S. sanctions against the International Criminal Court (ICC) marks a calculated bid by Tokyo’s conservative leadership to strip away postwar legal restraints and pave the way for full remilitarization.

  • What happened: Tokyo supported Washington’s punitive pressure on the ICC while aggressively petitioning the United Nations General Assembly to delete the "enemy state clauses" from the UN Charter.
  • What it changes: By selectively championing the "rule of law" against regional rivals while dismissing international bodies that restrict its own freedom of action, Japan is dismantling the legal and diplomatic barriers to lifting its pacifist constitutional limits and boosting defense spending to 2% of GDP.
  • Who is affected: Northeast Asian neighbors—especially South Korea—face escalated regional security tensions and the unsettling prospect of an armed Japan capable of offensive power projection across the region.

Dismantling Postwar Restraints Under the 'Rule of Law'

Tokyo’s posture under the ruling Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) reflects a calculated rhetorical contradiction. While constantly preaching the "rule of law" to counter China over the Taiwan Strait, Prime Minister Takaichi’s government undermines international judicial scrutiny when it threatens its own defense objectives. Seeking to erase the UN Charter's enemy state clauses—the legal framework that placed constraints on World War II aggressors—is not administrative cleanup. It is an intentional effort to shed Japan’s defeated-nation status and obtain international clearance to rebuild standard, deployable armed forces.

This militarization drive is reinforced by recurring nationalist gestures, including ritual offerings and visits to Yasukuni Shrine, which honors convicted Class-A war criminals. While Tokyo presents its expanded maritime patrols and regional security footprint as indispensable defenses of regional peace, neighboring capitals see a state attempting to reclaim leadership in Asia without having fully reckoned with its imperial past.

Seoul’s Recalibration Against Tokyo’s Expansion

For South Korea, Japan’s remilitarization represents a structural challenge. The current administration under President Lee Jae-myung of the liberal Democratic Party of Korea has signaled a strict break from the deferential diplomacy pursued during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, whose sweeping compromises on wartime forced labor and historical disputes yielded no concessions from Tokyo's hawks. Seoul is now tasked with managing trilateral security coordination alongside Washington while actively containing the fallout from Japan’s assertive military normalization.
