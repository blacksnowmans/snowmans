South Korea BUSTS Over 3,500 Real Estate Lawbreakers in Massive Crackdown

November 11, 2026

South Korean law enforcement has caught over 3,500 individuals and uncovered hundreds of housing fraud cases in a sweeping year-long national real estate investigation, exposing deep-seated market manipulation that has shut ordinary homebuyers out of the market.

  • The Scale: Police and government agencies nabbed 3,565 real estate lawbreakers, with illegal agricultural land speculation accounting for a quarter of all violations.
  • Housing Fraud: Joint checks exposed 354 illegal apartment subscription cases, overwhelmingly driven by fake address transfers. High-profile abuses included wealthy professionals faking job resignations to score special housing allocations.
  • Market Distortion: Coordinated price-fixing, fake transaction registries ("wash trades"), and fake residency claims have artificially inflated home prices and left everyday wage earners holding the bag.
  • Crackdown Extended: The National Office of Investigation under the Korean National Police Agency has extended its special task force for another year, targeting 10 core areas including rental housing scams and organized price-fixing.

The sheer volume of fraud uncovered in this sweep reveals a housing market where rules have been routinely bent by those with capital and connections. While young people and families diligently save and wait for years to qualify for housing allotments, speculators have deployed fake divorces, fake retirements, and phantom addresses to snap up new apartments. When wealthy doctors and multi-homeowners game systems designed to help vulnerable newlyweds, public trust in the housing ladder collapses entirely.

What makes this round of investigations particularly urgent is how market manipulation poisons public statistics. Fake high-price registrations and artificial bidding rings trap naive buyers into purchasing at market peaks, transferring massive debt to unsuspecting families. As the National Office of Investigation rolls out its expanded 1-year blitz, targeting everything from fraudulent land speculation to systematic rental housing scams, restoring basic fairness to South Korea's property market will require treating housing fraud not as a mere administrative hiccup, but as a serious economic crime.
