Lee Jae-myung Administration Faces Crisis as Opposition Surges in Polls

October 26, 2026

The administration of President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea have entered a period of severe political instability as public disapproval ratings have surged past the critical 50% threshold.

  • Disapproval hits a record high: Negative sentiment regarding President Lee’s performance reached 57.7% in recent phone interview polls, signaling a breakdown in public trust across demographic lines.
  • Opposition takes the lead: The conservative People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main opposition party, has overtaken the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) in support ratings, leading 42.6% to 36.0%.
  • Economic and strategic failures: Voters are abandoning the government due to a combination of a stagnant real estate market, a stock market slump, and controversial rhetoric from presidential aides regarding "martial law."
  • Fragmenting liberal base: The Rebuilding Korea Party, a minor liberal party led by former Justice Minister Cho Kuk, is successfully absorbing "disappointed" DPK voters, currently holding around 9% support.

A Collapse in the Liberal Stronghold

The most damaging aspect of these figures is the erosion of support in the Honam region, the traditional southwest heartland of the liberal DPK. When a ruling party loses its "concrete" base, it signals that the public has moved beyond mere political disagreement to a fundamental loss of confidence. This shift is largely driven by bread-and-butter issues; the cooling of the housing market and the stock market's recent slump have hit the middle and working classes hard. For international market observers, this indicates that the Lee administration is losing its mandate to drive aggressive economic policy, as it must now pivot entirely toward political survival and internal party discipline.

Analysis: Self-Inflicted Wounds and Leadership Voids

Compounding the economic woes is a series of strategic blunders within the presidential office. The mention of "martial law" by aides—regardless of the actual intent—is a massive political miscalculation in a country with a living memory of military authoritarianism. This rhetoric, combined with the DPK’s internal power struggles, paints a picture of a government that is struggling to maintain control over its own narrative. Senior lawmakers like Park Hong-geun are attempting to pivot toward legislative investigations to regain momentum, but without a unified front, the Lee government risks an early "lame duck" status.

Unlike the administration of Yoon Suk-yeol-ssi (who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment), which maintained a specific brand of prosecutorial discipline, the current Lee Jae-myung administration appears increasingly disorganized. The "two-top" leadership structure of the DPK is showing its limits, with internal factions more focused on party hegemony than on addressing the public's economic anxiety. If the administration cannot consolidate its message and offer a clear solution to the asset market downturn, the current polling "warning light" will likely evolve into a permanent state of political paralysis.
