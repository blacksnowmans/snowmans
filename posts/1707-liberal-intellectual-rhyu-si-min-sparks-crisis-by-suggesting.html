Liberal Intellectual Rhyu Si-min Sparks Crisis by Suggesting President Lee Jae-myung May Not Finish Term

October 30, 2026

Rhyu Si-min, a prominent liberal commentator and influential voice for the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), has sent shockwaves through the political establishment by suggesting that President Lee Jae-myung might not complete his five-year term.

  • Rhyu Si-min, an author and former cabinet minister, warned that the Lee Jae-myung administration faces a "collapse of the camp" unless it fundamentally changes its governance style and communication.
  • Core loyalists within the DPK, South Korea's liberal ruling party, reacted with fury, describing the criticism as "internal sabotage" and "friendly fire" during a period of high political tension.
  • The DPK leadership is attempting to minimize the damage, with key figures like Representative Kim Min-seok stating there is "no need to even evaluate" such provocative claims.
  • The controversy highlights growing internal anxiety over President Lee’s informal communication style—particularly on social media—which some fear is undermining the administration’s legitimacy.

The Weight of Internal Criticism

Rhyu Si-min is not just any critic; he is a heavyweight intellectual whose words carry immense weight among the liberal base. By suggesting the President might not finish his term, Rhyu has touched on the ultimate taboo for a party that rose to power following the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. Rhyu’s critique went beyond policy, comparing the current administration’s "unilateralism" to the previous era and warning that the government is like a "ship facing a backflow" without a clear vision or a leadership capable of unifying the nation.

The reaction from the "Pro-Lee" faction was swift and visceral. Lawmakers described the remarks as "throwing a grenade into their own barracks," a Korean military metaphor for a catastrophic betrayal by a comrade. For a ruling party currently navigating a difficult legislative environment against the People Power Party, the main conservative opposition, such a high-profile indictment from within their own ideological camp is seen as a strategic disaster that provides ammunition to their rivals.

Damage Control and Governance Risks

The DPK leadership is now in a delicate position. They must decide whether to dismiss Rhyu as a "cynical observer" or take his warnings as a necessary wake-up call. While some lawmakers, such as Representative Han Min-soo, tried to frame the comments as a plea for unity, the prevailing sentiment is one of damage control. The party is desperate to prevent these remarks from eroding the President’s "national momentum," especially as critics like Park Yong-jin, vice-chair of the Regulatory Reform Committee, warn that the public's "anxiety" is growing faster than the government's ability to promote its successes.

Ultimately, this rift exposes the "double anxiety" facing the Lee Jae-myung administration: the external pressure from the opposition and the internal fear that the government’s communication style is too polarizing. Whether this controversy leads to a pivot in how President Lee handles the "weight of his words" or simply hardens the lines of internal party loyalty will determine the stability of his remaining years in office. For now, the "grenade" has been thrown, and the ruling party is left scrambled to repair the damage to its front line.
