Apple's 2026 Lineup: iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro, and the $2,700 Price Tag

September 10, 2026

Apple's latest hardware showcase delivered its most ambitious lineup yet, headlined by the company's first-ever foldable phone and a massive leap in on-device AI processing.

  • iPhone Duo: Apple's debut foldable phone features a unique 1:1.4 aspect ratio, a nano-texture display, and an entry price of $1,999 (about $2.7 million KRW).
  • iPhone 18 Pro: Powered by a 2-nanometer A20 Pro chip, a 3x larger vapor chamber, and variable aperture cameras, with prices starting at $1,199.
  • Apple Intelligence Expansion: Siri gains deep contextual awareness, multilingual support (including Korean) arriving in October, and extreme on-device privacy safeguards.
  • Peripherals: AirPods 5 gains swipe volume controls and IP57 durability, while Apple Watch 12 introduces a ceramic finish and "Live Rewind" audio transcription.

The Foldable Era Arrives — at a Steep Cost

The tech giant's entry into the foldable market with the iPhone Duo marks a definitive break from the awkward tall-and-narrow proportions popularized by competitors. By adopting a balanced 1:1.4 aspect ratio and an ultra-thin profile, Apple has solved the clunkiness that long plagued folding devices. Yet the sheer financial barrier—starting near $2,000 before local taxes and exchange rates push it well past 2 million won in Korea—turns what should be pure tech celebration into a serious financial calculation for consumers.

Beneath the gleaming titanium and cutting-edge silicon lies a deeper tension between convenience and surveillance. Features like the Apple Watch's Live Rewind—which continuously buffers ambient audio to generate text transcripts—offer incredible accessibility benefits, but force users to weigh convenience against privacy anxiety. As smartphones increasingly demand that we trust them to verify reality, authenticate truth via "Apple Reference Image" metadata, and record our daily lives, the cost of innovation is no longer just monetary. It requires trusting devices with the very boundaries of our personal lives.
