Sam Altman’s South Korea Blitz: What OpenAI Wants from Samsung and SK

November 01, 2026

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman made a high-stakes visit to South Korea on October 31, meeting top leadership at Samsung Electronics and SK Group to secure next-generation AI memory chips and push for deeper enterprise AI integration.

  • What happened: Altman held a strategic session with over 50 Samsung executives and met SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won over lunch.
  • What was discussed: High-bandwidth memory (HBM) supply, AI data center (AIDC) infrastructure, and company-wide AI transformation (AX).
  • The symbolic gift: Altman presented Samsung leaders with a 13-button Codex keyboard worth about $220 (300,000 KRW), highlighting a shift from traditional QWERTY inputs to dedicated AI controllers.
  • Who is affected: Global tech supply chains and Korea's top two conglomerates, whose memory semiconductor profits surged 15-fold over the past year amid the generative AI explosion.

Securing the Hardware Backbone for OpenAI's Infrastructure

Altman's whirlwind trip underlines South Korea's indispensable role in the global AI race. With domestic memory semiconductor profits jumping 15-fold over the past year, big tech's expansion hinges on Korean hardware manufacturing. OpenAI's ambitious infrastructure plans—including massive AI data centers and custom chip projects—cannot proceed without securing high-bandwidth memory (HBM). Lunch discussions with SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won focused on securing reliable HBM supply, including discussions around 900,000 units, alongside joint efforts on energy-efficient data center design with SK Hynix, South Korea's primary HBM provider.

Forcing an Enterprise AI Pivot Inside Korea’s Tech Giants

Beyond component procurement, Altman delivered a sharp wake-up call to Samsung’s leadership during a session titled "The Next 10 Years Changed by AI." Addressing over 50 division presidents, he emphasized that competitive advantage now depends on how rapidly and deeply an organization embeds AI into its core workflow. Samsung responded by committing to an aggressive AI transformation across research, manufacturing, and marketing. Executives participated in hands-on exercises using Samsung's internal database, with mandatory AI training planned for all employees by next February.

The broader challenge for Korean tech giants is avoiding the trap of remaining mere hardware suppliers. While selling millions of HBM chips delivers strong short-term earnings, long-term leverage remains with platform operators like OpenAI. As global markets increasingly scrutinize AI capital expenditure and return on investment, Samsung and SK must use their current hardware dominance to negotiate deeper strategic stakes in the evolving global AI ecosystem.
