South Korea’s Ruling Party Faces Backlash Over Unsubstantiated Cult Allegations

October 20, 2026

Leadership of South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea is facing mounting internal pressure to either present concrete evidence supporting allegations of religious cult ties within its ranks or issue an official apology by the end of the month.

  • The controversy: Accusations dubbed "anti-Lee Shincheonji"—alleging that internal rivals of President Lee Jae-myung colluded with Shincheonji, a controversial fringe religious group—have sparked fierce factional infighting despite a lack of verified documentation.
  • The leadership on the spot: Party leader Kim Min-seok is under fire for allowing rumor-driven divisions to fester, with critics demanding he provide clear proof or step forward to take political responsibility.
  • Paralyzed parliamentary audits: The National Assembly's annual government audits have been derailed by personal attacks against a provincial governor, sidelining pressing issues such as local government budget deficits and depleted funding for frontline firefighters.
  • Executive missteps: Observers warn that the administration of President Lee Jae-myung is aggravating public fatigue by responding to swirling controversies via emotional social media retorts rather than transparent, institutional briefings.

From Policy Scrutiny to Factional Witch Hunts

The annual parliamentary audit is designed as a constitutional check on state spending, an urgent task at a time when municipal treasuries across the country face severe revenue shortfalls and public safety budgets are squeezed. Instead, proceedings have degenerated into mudslinging. By focusing on personal grievances and smearing party dissidents with accusations of ties to Shincheonji, lawmakers have wasted public resources on unsubstantiated conspiracy theories rather than rigorous policy oversight.

Weaponizing religious affiliations to purge internal opponents is a dangerous regression. The public witnessed the corrosive fallout of religious and occult scandals during the tenure of Yoon Suk-yeol, which eventually led to his impeachment, removal from office, and imprisonment. Having seen the catastrophic damage unchecked factionalism and shadow influences can inflict on state governance, the ruling party should be setting a higher standard of transparency rather than replicating the exact tactics of guilt by association.

The Need for Governance Over Grievance

For President Lee's administration, maintaining public trust requires disciplined, formal communication. Dismissing scrutiny through combative posts on social media channels only invites unnecessary conflict and signals administrative instability to voters already struggling under high living costs. Running a country demands institutional rigor rather than internet sparring.

The Democratic Party leadership must bring this spectacle to an end. If there is documented proof of external religious interference, it must be laid out cleanly before the public. If not, the leadership owes its members a clear apology, allowing the government to pivot away from internal witch hunts and return to addressing the bread-and-butter economic issues facing ordinary citizens.
