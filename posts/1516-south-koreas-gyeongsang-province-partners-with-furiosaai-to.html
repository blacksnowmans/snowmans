South Korea’s Gyeongsang Province Partners with FuriosaAI to Break Nvidia’s Grip on Manufacturing

October 27, 2026

North Gyeongsang Province is launching a major initiative to verify and deploy domestic AI semiconductors within its industrial heartland, aiming to reduce reliance on expensive foreign hardware and revitalize the region’s manufacturing sector.

  • North Gyeongsang Province and Gyeongsan City signed a strategic agreement with FuriosaAI, South Korea’s leading "fabless" (design-only) AI semiconductor firm, to establish a real-world verification hub for homegrown chips.
  • The project moves away from expensive, energy-hungry GPUs (Graphics Processing Units) dominated by Nvidia, focusing instead on NPUs (Neural Processing Units) that are up to ten times more power-efficient for specific AI tasks.
  • Konan Technology, a prominent Korean AI software provider, will join the effort to digitize the Pohang Steel Industrial Complex, bringing "AI Transformation" (AX) to the country’s traditional heavy industry center.
  • Local manufacturers will gain access to a "GPU-NPU hybrid" cloud farm, allowing smaller firms to utilize high-performance AI computing for quality control and energy management without massive upfront investments.

The End of the Nvidia Monopoly?

For South Korean manufacturers, the "Nvidia tax"—the high cost and long lead times associated with US-made chips—has become a significant barrier to digital transformation. By turning Gyeongsangbuk-do (North Gyeongsang Province) into a massive "testbed," the provincial government is providing FuriosaAI with the critical real-world data needed to prove that domestic NPUs can outperform global alternatives in industrial settings. While GPUs are versatile, the specialized NPUs being tested are designed specifically for "inference," the process of running AI models, making them ideal for the low-latency, 24/7 environment of a smart factory.

This initiative represents a shift toward "technology sovereignty." If successful, the project will create a localized ecosystem where Korean-designed chips power Korean-owned factories. Beyond the hardware, the province is building an integrated software framework that allows these chips to communicate seamlessly with existing industrial systems. This "bottom-up" innovation strategy ensures that the technology is refined by the engineers on the factory floor, rather than being imposed as a theoretical solution from a laboratory.

Restoring the R&D Ladder

This aggressive push into AI infrastructure is also a calculated political move to restore South Korea’s research momentum. The current administration under President Lee Jae-myung has prioritized "regional-led balanced development," providing the budget and autonomy for provinces to partner directly with tech leaders. This stands in sharp contrast to the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment. During the time of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the scientific community faced devastating budget cuts as R&D funding was targeted under the guise of clearing out "cartels," a move many experts say stunted the growth of the domestic semiconductor industry.

By establishing these verification hubs, the government is effectively repairing the "ladder of innovation" that was dismantled in previous years. The goal is to turn the Gyeongsan and Pohang clusters into a global standard for "AI Transformation" in manufacturing. If the hybrid infrastructure—utilizing both foreign GPUs for training and domestic NPUs for operation—proves cost-effective, it could serve as a blueprint for industrial zones worldwide, securing a future for South Korean tech that is independent of global supply chain whims.
