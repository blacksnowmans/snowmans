Lee Jae-Myung’s Reshuffle Draws Warning Over Pro-US Bias in a Multipolar World

November 24, 2026

Critics warn that President Lee Jae-myung’s recent foreign policy reshuffle stacks South Korea’s security team with pro-Washington figures just as a landmark summit between the US, China, and Russia signals the arrival of a multipolar world order.

  • Cabinet Reshuffle: Hong Ihk-pyo has been nominated to lead the Ministry of Unification—the government agency handling inter-Korean affairs—though analysts question whether he can effectively balance the influence of National Security Advisor Wi Sung-lac.
  • Diplomatic Pivot: Experts criticize the departure of foreign policy autonomy advocates in favor of a Washington-centric team, warning Seoul may concede national interests during policy disputes with the US.
  • Global Realignment: A historic meeting of US, Chinese, and Russian leaders underscores the waning of single-superpower dominance, giving Moscow diplomatic leverage and encouraging North Korea to strengthen its nuclear stance.
  • Strategic Imperative: Veteran foreign policy observers urge South Korea to move beyond single-minded alliance politics and pursue pragmatic multi-vector diplomacy to retain initiative on Korean Peninsula issues.

A Security Lineup Tilted Toward Washington

The nomination of Hong Ihk-pyo as Unification Minister brings solid academic and practical credentials, including a doctorate in North Korean studies and past advisory roles during the progressive Roh Moo-hyun administration. However, experts voice concern over the wider reshuffle within the National Security Council (NSC). With figures favoring diplomatic autonomy—such as former ministers Chong Dong-young and Lee Jong-seok—sidelined, the foreign policy apparatus has reorganized heavily around pro-US channels. Former ambassador Lee Kyeong-ryeol and Kwangwoon University professor Kim Hee-gyo warn that an overly dependent security team risks compromising diplomatic independence, potentially choosing submission over firm negotiation whenever friction with Washington arises.

Navigating a Shift in the Global Power Balance

This internal realignment comes alongside major international shifts. According to experts such as Hankuk University of Foreign Studies professor Che Sung-hoon, the recent US-China-Russia summit demonstrated that the post-Cold War era of exclusive US dominance is yielding to a multipolar reality. Russia secured tangible gains from the meeting—establishing groundwork for nuclear control post-New START, leveraging energy security, and breaking out of diplomatic isolation. Observing this shift, North Korean leader Kim Jong-un is exploiting the weakened US position by reinforcing ties with Beijing and Moscow while escalating nuclear capability.

Former Unification Minister Jeong Se-hyun argues that South Korea cannot afford to remain trapped in single-minded reliance on Washington while regional dynamics fracture. Pointing to Japan's shrinking influence at the recent Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) forum as a cautionary tale, analysts emphasize that Seoul must actively build three-party or multilateral dialogue frameworks. Without pragmatic flexibility and the courage to shape its own diplomatic agenda, the Lee administration risks leaving the fate of the Korean Peninsula entirely in the hands of foreign powers.
