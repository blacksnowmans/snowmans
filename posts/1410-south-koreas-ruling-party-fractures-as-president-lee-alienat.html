South Korea’s Ruling Party Fractures as President Lee Alienates His Progressive Base

October 22, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is facing intense backlash from his core progressive supporters as internal factional warfare and a police raid on a prominent liberal media figure expose deep rifts within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK).

  • Internal civil war: Factional infighting has erupted inside the ruling DPK over alleged religious cult involvement and covert political maneuvers during internal party elections.
  • Raid on ally media: Police executed a sudden, heavy-handed raid on Lee Jong-won, head of the pro-progressive YouTube news channel Sisa Tapa, sparking accusations of targeted suppression against friendly commentators.
  • Prominent intellectual revolts: Influential liberal writer Rhyu Si-min publicly accused President Lee of "deceiving the public" by reneging on core prosecutorial reform promises.
  • Volatile approval ratings: President Lee's approval rating crashed from 84% in July down to 37% in August, remaining unstable at 66% in October amid growing frustration over his outreach to moderates at the expense of his core base.

Alienating the Core for a Phantom Moderate Majority

President Lee Jae-myung and DPK senior leadership under Rep. Kim Min-seok have championed a "structural majority theory"—a strategy designed to expand the ruling party's appeal toward moderate and center-right voters. However, critics argue this pivot actively alienates the grassroots supporters who sustained the liberal movement through past candlelit rallies. Internal discord escalated as rival factions within the DPK publicly traded allegations regarding systematic infiltration by the controversial Shincheonji religious group, while party leaders refused to back a formal law enforcement investigation into the claims.

Compounding the frustration, President Lee's personal social media activity ignited widespread anger among his base. The president amplified posts from accounts known for relentless attacks against veteran progressive figures, including former Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae and commentator Rhyu Si-min. Rather than uniting the party, Lee's actions are widely seen as empowering toxic internet sub-factions while pushing away long-standing political allies.

Police Raids and Broken Promises Trigger Backlash

Tensions boiled over on October 15—the very day President Lee formally appointed Kim Ji-yong as head of the Serious Crimes Investigation Office. Police deployed riot units to execute a sweeping search and seizure against Lee Jong-won, head of the liberal YouTube outlet Sisa Tapa. Officers seized public broadcast archives and whistleblower documents detailing alleged illegal campaign activity by internal DPK factions, prompting charges that state authority was weaponized to scrub evidence damaging to party insiders.

In response, high-profile liberal intellectual Rhyu Si-min issued a scathing public critique, warning that President Lee was abandoning the core tenets of prosecutorial reform in favor of political expediency. Historical precedent shows that Korean political coalitions fail when leaders destroy their own foundation to pursue outside voters. If progressive voters respond with a "silent boycott"—withholding donations, campaigning, and turnout—the ruling party risks a severe defeat in upcoming national elections.
