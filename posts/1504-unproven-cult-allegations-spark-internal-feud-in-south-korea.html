Unproven Cult Allegations Spark Internal Feud in South Korea’s Ruling Party

October 26, 2026

Senior leaders in South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) are facing fierce criticism for stoking internal factional conflict by pushing unproven allegations of ties between rival lawmakers and a controversial religious sect.

  • What happened: During the DPK’s leadership convention, party figures including Supreme Council members Han Min-soo and Kim Min-seok alleged that anti-Lee Jae-myung factions formed a secret alliance with Shincheonji, a controversial religious group, but have failed to present promised evidence.
  • What it changes: Despite setting up a party task force that publicly claimed to have secured physical proof of systematic infiltration, leadership has kept the final report hidden, raising accusations of political fear-mongering.
  • Who is affected: DPK leadership, targeted lawmakers such as Jung Chung-rae, and President Lee Jae-myung, whose ruling party risks squandering its political momentum on internal witch-hunts.

Political Fear-Mongering Without Proof

During the DPK's convention, senior party figures ignited a political firestorm by framing the internal leadership race as a battle against a secret "anti-Lee, factionalist, and Shincheonji" alliance. The accusation carried immense volatility among party supporters. However, despite bold promises to unveil hard evidence once the preliminary primaries concluded, party leadership has repeatedly punted on transparency. Sensational accusations were lobbed at internal rivals, but no verifiable data followed.

The situation turned more bizarre after the election of the party leadership, when a dedicated task force announced it had secured "substantial physical evidence" of organized group enlistment by the sect. Yet not a single page of that report has been made public. Holding onto findings while dropping vague hints behind closed doors suggests the investigation was designed to pressure political opponents rather than uncover the truth. If real evidence of illegal infiltration exists, immediate disciplinary or legal action is the standard response; withholding it looks like a desperate bluff.

A Self-Made Trap for Party Leadership

This reliance on vague insinuations has backed leaders like Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok into a logical corner. By raising dark hints of a "cover-up" during official meetings without presenting court-ready evidence, leadership appears trapped between escalating the drama and lacking the proof to back it up. Furthermore, expanding these unsubstantiated claims into targeted attacks against fellow high-profile figures like Lawmaker Jung Chung-rae crosses from healthy political competition into self-destructive factional warfare.

With South Korea attempting to stabilize governance under President Lee Jae-myung following the historic impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, internal unity within the ruling party is essential. If party leaders possess real proof of a cult conspiracy, they must lay it out before party members immediately. If they do not, those who raised these baseless claims must apologize and step down. Public political institutions must be run on facts and accountability, not conspiracy theories.
