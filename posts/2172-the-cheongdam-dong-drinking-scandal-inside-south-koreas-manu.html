The Cheongdam-dong Drinking Scandal: Inside South Korea's Manufactured Investigation

November 10, 2026

Leaked police records from the infamous "Cheongdam-dong drinking scandal" reveal a classic staged investigation, where investigators built a narrative to protect powerful figures rather than pursue the truth.

  • Police selected a tiny, physically impossible venue—a room cafe called Tike—fourteen days before questioning even began, despite it lacking CCTV and credit card records.
  • The real matching venue, an exclusive club called Ia, was entirely scrubbed from the police records to shield high-profile figures.
  • Then-Justice Minister Han Dong-hoon avoided providing simple alibi data, instead using lawsuits to brand the claims as fake news.

The scandal, which originally centered on allegations that then-President Yoon Seok-yeol and top ministers spent a late-night drinking session with lawyers and cello players, has taken a darker turn. Rather than investigating the claims, police fed the venue name to a cellist during a hurried, two-hour interrogation. Crucial telecommunications data, including a suspicious 7-minute gap in cell tower records for a key figure, mysteriously vanished before forensic analysis could be completed.

What makes the case a textbook example of political cover-up is the timeline of collusion between authorities and conservative media. Conservative daily Chosun Ilbo published a front-page exposé declaring the cellist a liar less than 12 hours after her brief police interrogation—before digital forensics were even processed. Key figures who provided alibis for the night later received notable career boosts under Yoon Seok-yeol's administration, while whistleblowers and opposition politicians faced heavy defamation lawsuits.
