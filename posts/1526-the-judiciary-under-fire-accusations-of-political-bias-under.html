The Judiciary Under Fire: Accusations of Political Bias Under Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae

October 27, 2026

Critics are sounding the alarm over South Korea’s judiciary, claiming the court system under Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae has abandoned impartial legal standards to serve as a political tool against the current administration of President Lee Jae-myung.

  • Controversial Acquittals: Recent rulings, including the high-profile acquittal of former Minister Park Sung-jae, are being slammed as "pre-determined" outcomes that disregard substantial incriminating evidence.
  • Sentencing Leniency: Critics point to a pattern of reduced sentences for figures involved in internal rebellion cases, suggesting a double standard that favors conservative interests.
  • Political Signaling: Lawmakers from the ruling Democratic Party—South Korea's center-left governing party—argue that conservative judges appointed during the tenure of the now-impeached and imprisoned former president, Yoon Seok-youl, are using their positions to check the current government’s reform agenda.
  • Calls for Reform: Prominent voices, including Democratic Party lawmakers Yang Bu-nam and Park Joo-min, are demanding systemic reforms to ensure judicial neutrality and curb what they describe as "judicial politicking."

A Judiciary or a Political Weapon?

The core of the frustration lies in the perception that the judiciary has entered the "political arena." In the case of former Minister Park, observers argue that the court ignored a mountain of circumstantial evidence, effectively writing the verdict before examining the facts. When judges dismiss evidence without logical explanation to achieve a desired conclusion, the court ceases to be a bastion of the rule of law and instead becomes a platform for "political theater."

This is further complicated by the legacy of the previous administration. Many of the judges currently in influential positions were appointed under Yoon Seok-youl. Critics argue these judges are operating under the guise of "legal stability" to protect established interests and undermine the current administration led by the Democratic Party. To the eyes of many citizens, this represents a regression to a time when the courts served as a "handmaiden" to power, rather than an independent arbiter of justice.

The Erosion of Public Trust

The impact of these rulings is a sharp decline in public confidence. If the judiciary is seen as merely a "leaning scale" that provides safe passage for allies and harsh penalties for political opponents, the moral authority of the law evaporates. Public sentiment suggests that justice is no longer about the law itself, but about the specific ideology of the judge presiding over the case.

Democratic Party lawmakers like Yang Bu-nam are pushing for urgent reforms, including stricter accountability for judicial conduct and more transparent appointment processes. The argument is clear: judicial independence is not a license for judges to act on personal political whims, but a mandate to provide fair judgment within the bounds of the law. Without fundamental reform to restore neutrality, the current leadership risks being remembered as presiding over a "dark age" of a politicized judiciary that ultimately betrayed its constitutional duty.
