South Korea’s Ruling Bloc Hits Trouble as Core Support Slides

October 12, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) and President Lee Jae-myung are facing a sharp erosion in public approval, driven by escalating intra-party feuds and mounting public frustration over combative messaging.

  • Core base unravels: Backing for the ruling camp among self-identified progressives plunged 14.0 percentage points in a single week, falling from 73.5% to 59.5%.
  • Leadership clashes internally: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok sparked backlash after publicly clashing with reformist figures inside his own party, including Gyeonggi Province Governor Choo Mi-ae and Representative Kim Yong-min, over high-profile appointments.
  • Presidential golf outing sparks outcry: President Lee drew intense fire from the conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) after visiting the Taereung golf course over the same weekend that a shopping center ceiling collapse in Daegu injured 38 people.
  • Opposition rebounds: With negative assessments of President Lee’s performance climbing to 61.4% and the DPK slipping to 39.9%, the conservative People Power Party ticked up to 35.4%.

Unforced Errors from Party Leadership

The sudden drop in core support reflects growing voter fatigue with self-inflicted wounds inside the ruling camp. Controversy over judicial agency appointments quickly escalated into public infighting when party leader Kim Min-seok took to public platforms to criticize outspoken party colleagues instead of containing the dispute. Facing criticism from within that his unrestrained public commentary was fracturing party unity, the Democratic Party leadership abruptly announced a "media diet," cutting back Kim's morning broadcasts and unscripted public statements.

Silencing the microphone, however, treats the symptom rather than the disease. The pushback against Kim was not just about the volume of his appearances, but an increasingly lecturing tone that alienated reform-minded party members who expected genuine debate rather than party-line enforcement.

Defensive Posture Breeds Backlash

At the presidential level, the administration’s handling of recent crises has compounded the damage. President Lee’s weekend round of golf during the Daegu ceiling collapse handed an immediate target to the conservative People Power Party, which seized on the incident as hypocrisy. During Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's term in office, the then-opposition Democratic Party had repeatedly condemned presidential golf outings during emergencies as dereliction of duty. While the presidential office stated that President Lee received updates and issued disaster-response directives while at the course, the optics of leisure during a domestic emergency struck a nerve.

Compounding this vulnerability is President Lee’s tendency to take directly to social media to push back against critics. When an administration in power appears more focused on relitigating disputes online and lecturing the public on why critics are mistaken, it signals defensiveness rather than steady leadership. Reversing this downward trajectory will require the ruling camp to lower its combative posture, stop treating public criticism as a misunderstanding to be corrected, and shift focus to tangible governing results.
