Packing for the Camino: A 500-Mile Journey of Minimalism

September 07, 2026

  • The mission: A traveler is embarking on the 800km (approx. 500-mile) Camino de Santiago, starting in Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, France.
  • The philosophy: The trip serves as a meditative exercise in minimalism, focusing on "lightening the load" both physically and metaphorically.
  • Essential gear: Success relies on weight distribution, using a 36-liter Deuter pack, lightweight sleeping gear, and strategic clothing choices for multi-day cycles.
  • Sustainability: The traveler emphasizes the importance of responsible tourism, urging fellow pilgrims to avoid disposable goods that contribute to waste along the historic route.

The Camino de Santiago is far more than a long walk; for many, it is a rite of passage. After a six-year delay—compounded by the pandemic—the author is finally setting out. Preparation for the trek isn't just about athletic readiness; it’s a masterclass in downsizing one's life into a single backpack. By sticking to a strict rotation of two sets of clothing and utilizing organizational tools like compression pouches, the traveler aims to minimize morning setup time and reduce the physical burden of the journey.

Beyond the practicalities of ponchos and blister kits, the preparation highlights the social nature of the trek. Whether it's high-tech additions like an Apple AirTag or low-tech essentials like safety pins serving as makeshift clothespins, the gear reflects a mix of personal support and ingenious improvisation. The author notes that the most valuable item in the pack is the "heart"—the emotional support from friends and the intention to support charitable causes through their spending.

The Paradox of the Pilgrimage

The core lesson of the Camino seems to be that we require far less to survive than modern life leads us to believe. By intentionally choosing a life of restriction for the duration of the 500-mile trek, the traveler finds a sense of liberation. This is not a trip for the sake of luxury, but a deliberate encounter with one’s own limits. As the author prepares to cross into Spain with roughly $220 (200,000 KRW) in cash and a backpack full of well-wishes, the emphasis remains on the "aesthetic of emptying"—shedding the unnecessary to make room for the experience itself.
