Police Raid on Progressive YouTuber Exposes Bitter Rift Inside South Korea’s Ruling Party

October 25, 2026

A sudden police raid targeting prominent progressive commentator Lee Jong-won has blown open a fierce factional battle within South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), bringing allegations of presidential election meddling and backroom prosecutorial deals into the open.

  • What happened: Police executed search warrants against Lee Jong-won, head of the pro-liberal YouTube outlet Sisa Tapa, over defamation claims linked to reports of an alleged secret deal between the government and senior prosecutors over criminal indictments.
  • Who is affected: The probe directly entangles DPK Supreme Council member Lee Sung-yoon, while putting party leader Kim Min-seok and the presidential office of President Lee Jae-myung under fire for silencing critical media voices.
  • What it changes: Rather than containing the story, the raid has mobilized grassroots progressive skepticism, intensifying accusations that the administration is purging party rivals and softening promised reforms.

A Risky Crackdown on Friendly Media

The search of Lee Jong-won’s residence and studio was ostensibly launched to investigate defamation regarding leaked claims that senior officials sought dropped indictments in exchange for watered-down prosecution reforms—an explosive rumor first voiced by former journalist Hong Sa-hoon. Yet the decision to deploy physical search warrants against a public broadcaster has immediately backfired. Investigative reporter Jang In-soo publicly warned that investigators risk uncovering conclusive evidence of backroom maneuvering rather than disproving it, turning an intimidation tactic into a potential liability for the ruling bloc.

Compounding the controversy, the police Anti-Corruption and Public Crime Investigation Unit has pressed forward with investigating sitting lawmaker Lee Sung-yoon over the same remarks, even after designated government figures like Minister Jung Sung-ho stated publicly that they had no intention of filing criminal charges. To many inside the liberal base, using specialized investigative squads to police political speech looks uncomfortably similar to the prosecutorial excesses seen during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, South Korea's disgraced former leader who now sits in prison following his impeachment.

Factional Purges and Supporter Backlash

The raid has catalyzed broader grievances regarding heavy-handed control by President Lee Jae-myung’s inner circle. Critics point to a pattern of unilateral party interference, recalling how Senior Presidential Secretary for Political Affairs Hong Ik-pyo reportedly pressured party heavyweight Jung Chung-rae three times to drop out of the leadership race to clear a path for current leader Kim Min-seok. Similar backroom interventions—such as the top-down promotion of Seongdong District Mayor Jung Won-oh during the Seoul mayoral primary—previously sparked voter apathy and handed victory to the conservative opposition.

For the Kim Min-seok leadership, this growing confrontation risks shattering the party's core coalition. Progressive voters who rallied through freezing winter street protests expect structural reforms of the judiciary and bold policymaking, not the deployment of state investigators against left-wing commentators. If the leadership continues to treat internal dissent as insubordination while offering vague appeals to "trust the process," it risks alienating the passionate grassroots base required to sustain the administration's broader legislative agenda.
