South Korean Opposition Erupts Over Seoul Mayor's Acquittal

October 27, 2026

The acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon in his appellate trial has triggered a fierce political firestorm, with the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK)—the party of President Lee Jae-myung—accusing the judiciary of political bias and threatening radical systemic reforms.

  • Judicial Backlash: The DPK has formally denounced the acquittal as a "get-out-of-jail-free card" for the powerful, arguing that the court ignored evidence linking the Mayor to the ongoing "Myung Tae-kyun gate" scandal.
  • Proposed AI Judges: DPK lawmaker Choi Min-hee has proposed legislation to introduce AI judges for politically sensitive cases to bypass what the party calls the "subjective bias" of human judges.
  • Impeachment Threats: High-ranking DPK officials are now actively discussing the potential impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, signaling a major escalation in the conflict between the legislative and judicial branches.
  • Strategic Pivot: The party is shifting toward a "two-track" strategy: focusing on "National Safety Month" in November while simultaneously pushing for a parliamentary investigation into the judiciary’s decision-making processes.

A Crisis of Trust in the Judiciary

The DPK’s aggressive response is fueled by a belief that the judiciary has become a vestige of the era under the impeached former leader, Yoon Seok-youl, who is currently incarcerated. Party leaders argue that the court is protecting conservative political figures, essentially acting as a shield for those who once held power. By framing the acquittal as the "politicization of the court," the DPK is attempting to delegitimize the ruling, moving the goalposts from a simple legal dispute to a broader crusade for judicial transparency.

The proposal to deploy AI judges is a stark, symbolic ultimatum. While AI may not be a practical fix for complex legal doctrine, the DPK’s push for it highlights the depth of their distrust. They are essentially telling the public that if the judiciary refuses to function impartially, it has forfeited its right to be the sole arbiter of justice. This rhetoric is designed to keep the pressure on the courts while the party navigates its own legislative agenda.

The Path to Impeachment and Systemic Reform

The prospect of impeaching Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae marks a significant escalation. While the conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), has condemned these moves as "insane" and an attack on the rule of law, the DPK remains undeterred. They argue that the independence of the judiciary is meant to protect the law, not to provide cover for political allies or ignore established evidence.

For the DPK, this is not just about one mayor or one case; it is about rooting out what they describe as "systemic corruption" lingering in the legal system since the transition of power to President Lee Jae-myung. Whether this leads to genuine reform or simply creates a deeper constitutional crisis remains to be seen, but the message from the ruling party is clear: they are prepared to use every parliamentary tool available to reshape a judiciary they view as fundamentally compromised.
