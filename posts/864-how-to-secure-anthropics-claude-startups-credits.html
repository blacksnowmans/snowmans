How to Secure Anthropic’s "Claude Startups" Credits

October 09, 2026

South Korean startups looking to slash their mounting AI overhead can now tap into Anthropic’s “Claude Startups” program, which offers significant credits and subscription waivers for early-stage companies.

  • The Offer: Eligible startups receive one year of free access to the "Claude Team" plan (up to 5 premium seats, worth about $625) and $1,000 in API credits.
  • Eligibility: Companies founded within the last five years or those that have received funding in the last two years are eligible, even without venture capital backing.
  • The Catch: Applicants must register a credit card to join, creating a risk of automatic subscription charges after the 12-month free period ends.
  • Strategic Execution: Approval can take as little as three minutes if applicants align their corporate email domain and technical profile precisely with Anthropic's guidelines.

Navigating the Application Process

The barrier to entry is lower than many founders assume. You do not need a history of venture capital investment to qualify. However, Anthropic is strict about administrative details. You must use a corporate domain email (generic Gmail or Naver addresses will be rejected), and you must set up your "Claude Console" organization—the developer management portal—before applying. If you use a personal account during this setup, you risk disqualification.

When drafting your application, avoid generic fillers. Successful applicants report that providing a concrete, technical use case—such as building a Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool—significantly improves approval odds. Once approved, the benefits are not applied automatically; you must manually log into the member portal to "claim" the team plan and API credits. Note that the $1,000 in API credits expires within 180 days, so timing your application to match your product's development cycle is crucial.

Managing Risk and Maximizing Value

While the program provides a massive cushion for software-focused teams, founders should remain vigilant about the "hidden" financial risks. Because a credit card is required at sign-up, failing to track the one-year anniversary could result in an unexpected billing cycle. Furthermore, South Korean startups must ensure their tax information is correctly input to avoid unnecessary 10% value-added tax (VAT) charges that are difficult to claw back once processed.

In the current economic climate under the Lee Jae-myung administration, where startups continue to navigate capital constraints and high costs, these global AI partnerships function as a vital lifeline. However, the true benefit lies in strategic usage: allocate the premium seats to your most active developers and utilize the API credits for automated background tasks rather than manual chatbot queries. By treating the AI tools as a technical resource rather than just a subscription perk, founders can effectively "lock in" productivity gains while insulating themselves from the eventual transition to paid pricing.
