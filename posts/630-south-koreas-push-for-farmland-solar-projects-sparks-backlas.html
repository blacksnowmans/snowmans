South Korea's Push for Farmland Solar Projects Sparks Backlash

October 03, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s aggressive farmland inspection and solar conversion drive threatens rural livelihoods and food security while offering no practical support for farmers.

  • What happened: The administration has launched sweeping inspections of agricultural land under the banner of eradicating speculators, while simultaneously promoting the conversion of farmlands into solar energy installations.
  • Who is affected: South Korean farmers, who face increased regulatory anxiety, lack of basic farming infrastructure, and the potential loss of their land to energy projects.
  • What it changes: Instead of addressing rural depopulation and labor shortages, current policies risk turning productive agricultural zones into neglected industrial sites—exemplified by solar projects stalled due to a lack of grid connections.

Misguided Priorities Disguised as Reform

The government's heavy-handed focus on policing farmland echoes archaic, top-down administrative campaigns rather than modern agricultural strategy. While President Lee frames these measures as a crusade against speculators, critics argue the policies treat farmers as potential lawbreakers rather than the backbone of national food security. In regions like Cheonan and rural villages such as Wolpyeong, abandoned land sits neglected while newly installed solar panels sit idle as expensive scrap metal because the local power grids cannot transmit the electricity they generate.

Pushing green energy conversion without laying down basic infrastructure demonstrates a troubling disconnect between policymakers in Seoul and the realities of the Korean countryside. Rather than consulting farming communities on pressing issues like rising labor costs and depopulation, the administration has doubled down on sweeping slogans and superficial targets. Until the government builds the necessary grid capacity and consults actual agricultural workers, these initiatives will remain out-of-touch projects that sacrifice long-term food sovereignty for political optics.
