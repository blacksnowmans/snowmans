South Korea’s Political Arena Shaken by Seoul Mayor’s Trial and Fierce Parliamentary Audits

October 24, 2026

South Korea’s political landscape reached a boiling point as a live-broadcast appellate court ruling for Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon collided with fierce partisan battles over presidential power and judicial independence during parliamentary audits.

  • Mayor Oh’s political fate: Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon of the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) faced an appellate verdict over alleged political financing violations, a case with major ramifications for his future presidential ambitions and municipal leadership.
  • Impeachment rhetoric over law enforcement: The opposition PPP accused the administration of President Lee Jae-myung of trying to control investigative bodies by allowing the chief of the Serious Crime Investigation Agency to attend cabinet meetings, claiming it constituted grounds for presidential impeachment.
  • Pressure on the judiciary: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) drew opposition fire over discussions regarding the potential impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, drawing sharp criticism from the PPP and imprisoned former leader Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • Policy friction and labor backlash: Heated exchanges erupted over flight disruptions at Jeju Airport tied to presidential travel, while South Korea's two main labor confederations launched protests against government-backed regional economic legislation.

Judicial Battles Hijack National Governance

Legal battles have once again overwhelmed policy discussions in Seoul. Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s appellate trial regarding allegations of off-the-books campaign survey payments served as a focal point for both major parties. While the PPP maintained Oh’s innocence, the ruling DPK called for strict legal accountability. Tensions within the conservative camp were also on display, as Reform Party lawmaker Lee Jun-seok publicly distanced himself from rival conservative figures, criticizing political maneuvers reliant on "legal technicians"—a bitter nod to the contentious legal warfare that characterized politics during the presidency of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who currently remains in prison.

At the National Assembly, parliamentary audits devolved into arguments over constitutional boundaries. Opposition lawmakers lashed out at the presence of the Serious Crime Investigation Agency head at cabinet meetings, framing it as executive overreach. DPK lawmakers counter-argued that transparent administrative oversight was far better than the behind-the-scenes control exercised during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure. Simultaneously, DPK proposals to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae triggered warnings from opposition figures that weaponizing impeachment against the judiciary threatens the separation of powers.

Policy Clashes Amid Growing Public Disillusionment

Beyond the legal battles, everyday governance faced immediate friction. In the National Assembly's transport committee, opposition lawmakers slammed presidential travel controls for causing widespread flight delays at Jeju Airport, mockingly labeling the airport authority a "presidential takeoff office." DPK representatives dismissed the claims as political posturing, attributing delays to severe weather and existing air traffic bottlenecks. Outside parliament, major labor unions gathered to demand the withdrawal of the administration's Special Mega-Region Industry Act, arguing that corporate tax and regulatory breaks in high-tech sectors come at the expense of workers' rights.

Amid these controversies, President Lee Jae-myung presided over a national safety meeting, declaring a "war on violent crime" and ordering a shift toward proactive prevention. According to recent Gallup Korea poll figures, President Lee’s approval rating stood at 40% (with 50% negative assessment), while party support sat at 37% for the ruling DPK and 28% for the opposition PPP. Crucially, non-affiliated voters accounted for 27%, signaling widespread public weariness with persistent partisan warfare and judicial disputes.
