Democratic Party Members Mount Legal Challenge to Suspend Leader Kim Min-seok Over Cult Allegations

November 07, 2026

Aggrieved members of South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea are preparing criminal charges and an injunction to suspend party leader Kim Min-seok, alleging he spread false claims about religious cult infiltration during the leadership race to sway the election.

  • The core grievance: Critics accuse Kim of violating the Political Parties Act by fabricating allegations that the controversial Shincheonji religious sect was interfering in the leadership race to smear internal opponents like lawmaker Jung Cheong-rae.
  • The legal campaign: A group of party members, represented by attorney Lee Jae-il, is gathering 1,000 verified Democratic Party members to file an injunction seeking Kim's suspension from office alongside criminal complaints.
  • The broader stakes: The feud comes as the ruling party pushes a special counsel bill targeting fabricated indictments, while conservative rival People Power Party faces a looming appellate verdict against ousted leader Yoon Suk-yeol that could force the repayment of 39.7 billion won (around $28 million) in state election reimbursements.

Fabricated Cult Smears and Political Parties Act Violations

The controversy stems from Kim's radio remarks in July, where he suggested there was credible evidence of Shincheonji involvement in the party's convention and promised public revelations that never materialized. Shincheonji, a secretive religious group widely viewed with suspicion in South Korea, is frequently weaponized in domestic political rhetoric. Kim's critics allege the maneuver was a deliberate ploy to brand internal critics and rival party members as cult sympathizers, dividing party ranks for over four months.

Organizers argue these claims violate Article 52 of South Korea's Political Parties Act, which bans the publication of false facts intended to influence party primary outcomes—an offense punishable by up to three years in prison or fines up to 6 million won. Beyond the criminal complaint and civil injunction, the dissident faction plans to file disciplinary petitions with the party's internal ethics tribunal seeking Kim's expulsion, citing digital forensic evidence examined by the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency.

Internal Fractures Threaten Crucial Budget Season

The mounting civil revolt highlights growing exhaustion among core progressive supporters toward the party's current leadership. The Democratic Party leadership has prioritized a special prosecutor bill to probe alleged prosecutorial abuses, but rank-and-file organizers argue the push is poorly timed. Forcing the measure through the National Assembly risks stalling vital upcoming budget negotiations and alienating members who feel smeared by their own party apparatus.

At the same time, South Korean politics is bracing for seismic rulings against jailed former leader Yoon Suk-yeol, whose appellate campaign finance case could financially cripple the main conservative People Power Party. With high-stakes national and international shifts underway, the rebellion against Kim reveals a base increasingly unwilling to overlook dirty internal political maneuvers in the name of party unity.
