Anthropic Files for Historic $100 Billion Nasdaq IPO

October 27, 2026

Anthropic, the developer of the Claude artificial intelligence model and the primary rival to OpenAI, has officially filed for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker "ANT," targeting a record-shattering $100 billion valuation.

  • Record-Breaking Ambition: A $100 billion capital raise would surpass the valuation of Elon Musk’s SpaceX, potentially marking the largest IPO in history.
  • The Price of Intelligence: Financial disclosures reveal a stark contrast: $16.3 billion in revenue for the first half of the year against a staggering $80.7 billion net loss.
  • Public Market Litmus Test: This move serves as the ultimate trial for the "AI bubble," forcing the industry to prove its long-term profitability to public investors for the first time.
  • Korean Impact: The massive capital infusion is expected to drive demand for high-end memory chips from South Korean giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, the world's leading semiconductor manufacturers.

The Astronomical Cost of the AI Arms Race

The financial disclosures in Anthropic’s S-1 filing have sent shockwaves through both Wall Street and the Korean tech sector. The company’s revenue growth is explosive, but its "burn rate" is even more so—spending nearly five dollars for every dollar earned. These losses are primarily driven by the "Nvidia tax": the astronomical cost of AI chips, massive electricity consumption, and cloud infrastructure fees. For the Lee Jae-myung administration—the current government led by South Korea's liberal Democratic Party—these numbers serve as a sobering warning regarding the country's own "AI sovereignty" goals and the sheer capital required to compete in the global generative AI market.

The listing of "ANT" represents a pivot from private venture capital hype to the cold reality of quarterly earnings reports. For years, AI firms have operated in a vacuum of private funding where technical vision was the primary currency. Now, Anthropic must convince public shareholders that its massive losses are a temporary bridge to a profitable future. If the company fails to provide a clear roadmap to break-even, this IPO could transition from a "coronation" of a new tech king to a catalyst for a broader market correction in the technology sector.

A Reality Check for Korean Tech and Markets

While the IPO takes place in New York, the ripples will be felt acutely in Seoul. South Korea’s semiconductor industry—the backbone of its export economy—stands to benefit from Anthropic’s plans to reinvest IPO proceeds into more infrastructure. However, for domestic tech firms like Naver and Kakao that are attempting to build their own large language models (LLMs), the $80.7 billion loss figure is a "cruel reality check." It suggests that the top-tier AI game is increasingly becoming a playground only for those with bottomless pockets and a high tolerance for debt.

"The era of 'AI liquidity parties' is over. Anthropic is stepping onto a stage where technical brilliance must finally be matched by fiscal sustainability, or the entire sector risks a major cooling period."

The success of this IPO will likely determine the timeline for other AI startups to go public. For Korean investors and policymakers, the focus will remain on whether this massive capital injection translates into sustained orders for advanced HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) chips, or if it simply highlights the unsustainable nature of current AI development costs.
