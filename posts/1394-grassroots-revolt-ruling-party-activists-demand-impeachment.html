Grassroots Revolt: Ruling Party Activists Demand Impeachment of South Korea's Chief Justice

October 22, 2026

Outraged by court rulings that slashed prison sentences for participants in Yoon Suk-yeol’s abortive December 3 insurrection, grassroots activists from South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) have launched street protests demanding the immediate impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.

  • Protests target party leadership: Demonstrators wrapped in silver emergency blankets—dubbed the "Kisses Brigade"—staged overnight candlelit sit-ins outside DPK headquarters in Seoul's Yeouido district, demanding that lawmakers immediately table an impeachment motion against the top judge.
  • The sentencing trigger: The fury follows an appellate court decision drastically reducing the sentence of former Justice Minister Park Sung-jae—a key accomplice in Yoon Suk-yeol’s martial law decree—from 25 years down to 9 years in prison.
  • Internal party rift: Veteran lawmaker Park Jie-won and other reformists publicly condemned the DPK leadership around lawmaker Kim Min-seok for excessive caution, arguing that indecisiveness is depressing the government's approval ratings.
  • Core legislative goal: Protesters and party hardliners insist that President Lee Jae-myung’s reform drive will stall unless the party uses its legislative majority to rein in the judiciary and completely strip prosecutors of direct investigative powers.

A Boiling Backlash Against Judicial Double Standards

The immediate catalyst for the street demonstrations is what progressive voters view as a systemic double standard inside the South Korean judiciary. An appellate panel cleared former Justice Minister Park Sung-jae of several abuse-of-power charges, shaving 16 years off his original 25-year sentence for aiding the illegal martial law decree. For the DPK base, this leniency toward actions that threatened constitutional democracy stands in glaring contrast to the unyielding severity applied to progressive figures—most notably the four-year prison sentence handed to Chung Kyung-shim, wife of former Justice Minister Cho Kuk, over a forged academic commendation.

Protesters argue that the judiciary under Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae is using the banner of "judicial independence" as a shield for conservative power networks while blocking systemic reform. With senior officials from Yoon Suk-yeol’s time in office receiving reduced penalties or acquittals, grassroots supporters contend that the courts have forfeited institutional trust, leaving constitutional impeachment by the legislature as the only remaining remedy.

Leadership Timidity and the Window for Reform

The demonstrations also expose deep friction within the ruling party itself. While President Lee Jae-myung presses ahead with his domestic reform agenda, party elders like Park Jie-won have openly rebuked floor leader Kim Min-seok and the leadership for dragging their feet under the guise of "prudence." Activists and reform-minded lawmakers warn that excessive hesitation during the honeymoon period of the administration repeats the mistakes of past progressive governments that held legislative majorities but failed to overhaul the legal system.

Beyond impeaching the Chief Justice, the grassroots movement is demanding a definitive end to prosecutorial overreach by completing the separation of investigative and prosecutorial powers—specifically by creating a dedicated indictment agency and stripping prosecutors of supplementary investigation rights. For the demonstrators encamped outside party headquarters, the message to party leadership is clear: the current legislative majority was granted to enforce accountability, and failing to act against the judicial establishment now risks paralyzing the administration down the road.
