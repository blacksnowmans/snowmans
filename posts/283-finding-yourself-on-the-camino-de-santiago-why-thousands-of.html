Finding Yourself on the Camino de Santiago: Why Thousands of South Koreans Are Walking 800 Kilometers

September 07, 2026

For many South Koreans weighed down by high-pressure jobs, urban stress, and constant notifications, the historic Camino de Santiago has become the ultimate escape to rediscover a lost sense of self.

  • The Journey: The most popular route starts in Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, France, taking travelers across the Pyrenees and 800 kilometers (about 500 miles) through Spain.
  • Who Is Walking: Around 60,000 people begin their trek at the French border office annually, with a notable surge of South Korean travelers seeking deep personal reflection.
  • The Reality Check: The physical toll is immense—bringing blistering heat, severe muscle pain, and the growing threat of overtourism on local communities.
  • The Core Shift: Walkers intentionally trade fast-paced modern schedules for a simpler daily rhythm: walking at sunrise, eating when hungry, and sleeping when dark.

Shedding Status for the Open Road

At the pilgrim office in Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, travelers receive a "credential"—a pilgrim passport that replaces their real-world identities, job titles, and academic backgrounds with a single new title: pilgrim. This document requires walkers to travel strictly on foot rather than by bicycle, acting as a physical commitment to slow down the pace of life. Along the way, iconic scallop shells serve as quiet signals of solidarity, helping strangers connect with a simple "Buon Camino" greeting.

Yet, this romanticized trek carries a darker side. The boom in popularity has triggered severe overtourism, leaving quiet villages dealing with trash and strained local resources. True modern pilgrimage requires more than just collecting passport stamps; it demands an ethical mindfulness that respects the communities hosting the travelers. For those who brave the physical limits, the blisters and mountain climbs strip away superficial social masks, offering a rare, painful, but deeply necessary confrontation with one's true self.
