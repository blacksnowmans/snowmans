Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon Faces Growing Corruption Scandal Over Han River Water Bus Project

October 19, 2026

Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon is facing mounting allegations of high-level corruption and administrative cover-ups surrounding his flagship "Hangang Bus" water transit initiative, following revelations of illegal broker kickbacks exceeding 1 billion won ($750,000) and suspicious municipal funding transfers.

  • What happened: An unqualified firm with zero shipbuilding experience was awarded the 15 billion won ($11 million) contract to construct Seoul’s new river ferries after brokers solicited bribes, while an unregistered shell company pocketed 330 million won ($250,000) before suddenly shutting down.
  • Financial anomalies: Seoul Housing and Communities Corporation (SH Corp), a municipal public entity meant to build affordable public housing, inexplicably granted 80 to 90 billion won ($60–67 million) in loans to support the ferry project.
  • Cover-up accusations: Mayor Oh—a major figure in the main opposition conservative People Power Party—claims he has no memory of receiving detailed internal bribery reports, while city officials are accused of purging documents after submitting a stripped-down audit request to state authorities.
  • Who is affected: Seoul taxpayers and daily commuters, whose funds were funneled through dubious brokers while being left with potentially unsafe, hastily built vessels on the Han River.

A Rush for Political Trophies over Safety and Legality

The scandal surrounding the Hangang Bus illustrates a persistent structural problem in South Korean local governance: pushing rushed, high-profile infrastructure projects to pad a politician's national profile. Mayor Oh appears to have prioritized creating a visible political achievement over basic safety and administrative due process. When standard shipbuilders refused to take on the project due to unrealistic deadlines and strict safety demands, the city bypassed its initial list of 45 qualified candidates and handed the deal to Gadeok Heavy Industries—a firm without proper shipbuilding yards or technical history, allegedly brought in through broker connections.

Suppressed Audits and Institutional Misconduct

Equally damaging are the allegations of deliberate administrative manipulation. When Seoul's internal audit chief directly briefed Mayor Oh on a 320 million won ($240,000) rebate involving recorded calls and specific names, the Mayor reportedly dismissed the finding. Later, the city government filed an audit request to the Board of Audit and Inspection (BAI), South Korea's top state auditor, but deliberately erased all mentions of bribery from the single-page submission. When the BAI dismissed the vague request, Mayor Oh’s office used the dismissal to publicly claim full exoneration, prompting an unusually angry public rebuttal from the BAI itself.

With SH Corp diverting tens of millions of dollars from housing developments into water ferries and whistleblowers reporting internal directives to destroy evidence, the project has turned into a serious legal vulnerability for the Seoul Mayor. As police investigations accelerate under President Lee Jae-myung's administration, Mayor Oh faces rising pressure to explain how billions of taxpayer won vanished into paper companies while safety checks were systematically ignored.
