South Korea Passes Special Counsel Bill to Probe "Fabricated Indictments"

November 08, 2026

The National Assembly has passed a contentious bill to investigate allegations of prosecutorial overreach and evidence fabrication during the years Yoon Suk-yeol was in power, sparking immediate street protests from the opposition.

  • Special Counsel Bill Passed: The legislation targets alleged political targeting and judicial manipulation by prosecutors during the tenure of Yoon Suk-yeol 씨, who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment.
  • Opposition Rallies: The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition party, launched large-scale street protests near Gyeongbokgung Station, with leadership calling for the impeachment of President Lee Jae-myung.
  • New Justice Minister Nominee: President Lee nominated Lee Yong-cheol, the current head of the Defense Acquisition Program Administration (DAPA), as Justice Minister to lead the final stages of prosecutorial reform.
  • "Seongnam Line" Controversy: The PPP has labeled the nominee a "Seongnam Line" figure, alleging ties to the Daejang-dong land development scandal, a claim the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) dismisses as "baseless black propaganda."

A Judicial Reckoning or Political Warfare?

The passage of the special counsel bill marks a new peak in the friction between the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) and the opposition PPP. The DPK argues that the bill is a constitutional necessity to restore judicial integrity. They claim that during the previous administration, the prosecution was weaponized to target political rivals through "forced indictments" and "manufactured evidence." By establishing a massive investigative team of over 300 personnel, the government intends to root out what it describes as the "politicized prosecution" legacy.

The opposition, however, views this as a defensive maneuver. PPP leaders, including former chairman Han Dong-hoon and representative Jang Dong-hyeok, have characterized the bill as a "privilege law" designed to invalidate the ongoing trials of current government figures. The rhetoric in the streets has turned exceptionally sharp; during the Gyeongbokgung rally, PPP leadership explicitly used the word "impeachment" against President Lee, accusing him of undermining the constitutional order to protect himself from the law. This shift from parliamentary debate to "plaza politics" suggests a total breakdown in legislative cooperation.

Reform vs. Personnel Scandals

President Lee’s selection of Lee Yong-cheol for the Ministry of Justice is a strategic move to place a non-prosecutor at the helm of the legal system. The administration's goal is to finalize a new criminal justice framework that shifts investigative powers away from the prosecution to specialized agencies like the Major Crimes Investigation Office. However, the "Seongnam Line" narrative pushed by the PPP—linking the nominee to the President’s former tenure as Mayor of Seongnam—threatens to overshadow the policy goals of the appointment. The PPP claims Lee Yong-cheol was a "hidden architect" of the Daejang-dong project, while the DPK maintains his advisory roles predated the project's conception.

While the domestic front is dominated by these battles, the administration is also navigating a shifting global landscape. The recent victory of the Democratic Party in the U.S. midterm elections has created a check on "Trump-style" unilateralism, potentially impacting discussions on nuclear submarine technology and the transfer of wartime operational control. President Lee has sought to balance these security concerns with pragmatic economic diplomacy, recently attending the APEC summit to open new markets for Korean agricultural exports. Nevertheless, the success of his domestic agenda remains tethered to whether the special counsel can produce definitive evidence of prosecutorial abuse or if it will be dismissed as a political tool.
