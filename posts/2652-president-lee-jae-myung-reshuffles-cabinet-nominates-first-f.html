President Lee Jae-myung Reshuffles Cabinet, Nominates First Female Intelligence Chief

November 24, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung announced a significant mid-term cabinet reshuffle on November 23, nominating the country’s first female intelligence chief and replacing four ministers in a strategic move to consolidate power and refresh his administration's policy goals.

  • Kang Kum-sil, a reform-minded former Justice Minister, has been tapped to lead the National Intelligence Service (NIS), South Korea's primary spy agency.
  • The reshuffle replaces the heads of the Ministry of the Interior and Safety, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Ministry of Unification, and the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism.
  • The appointments signal a pivot toward "practical administration" and a tightening of the President’s inner circle within key agencies.
  • The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, has denounced the move as a "revolving-door" reshuffle of loyalists.

A Reformist Shift in Intelligence and Security

The nomination of Kang Kum-sil to lead the NIS is the most provocative element of this reshuffle. As the first woman nominated for the post, her appointment is intended to signal a "democratic transition" for an agency historically criticized for political interference. President Lee appears to be betting on Kang’s reputation as a principled reformer to modernize the NIS and ensure it remains focused on national security rather than domestic politics. If confirmed, she is expected to oversee a major structural overhaul of the agency.

The overhaul of the foreign policy and security team—specifically at the Foreign and Unification Ministries—suggests the Lee administration is seeking more flexibility in its dealings with Washington and Pyongyang. Lim Woong-soon, a career diplomat with deep experience in U.S. relations, takes over the Foreign Ministry, while Hong Ihk-pyo, a three-term lawmaker known for his expertise in inter-Korean economic cooperation, will lead the Unification Ministry. This pairing suggests a strategy of maintaining a robust alliance with the U.S. while simultaneously seeking new "breakthroughs" in stalled North-South relations.

Political Friction and the "Loyalist" Label

Domestic governance is also being reinforced with the appointment of Han Jun-ho to the Ministry of the Interior and Safety. Han is a high-profile lawmaker from the ruling Democratic Party of Korea and a long-time confidant of the President. His move to a ministry that oversees the police and national elections has drawn sharp fire from the opposition. Critics argue that placing a "core loyalist" in charge of the interior risks compromising the political neutrality of the nation’s administrative and law enforcement arms.

The timing of this reshuffle coincides with a period of intense judicial and legislative friction. While the President moves to refresh his cabinet, the Democratic Party is also pushing for the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, accusing the judiciary of unfair rulings. This broader context suggests that the cabinet reshuffle is not just about policy efficiency, but also about fortifying the executive branch against a hostile opposition and a skeptical judiciary. For the Lee administration, the success of these appointments will depend on whether they can translate "loyalist" energy into tangible improvements in public safety and the economy.

Key Nominees at a Glance

  • National Intelligence Service: Kang Kum-sil (Former Justice Minister)
  • Interior and Safety: Han Jun-ho (Two-term Lawmaker, DPK)
  • Foreign Affairs: Lim Woong-soon (Career Diplomat)
  • Unification: Hong Ihk-pyo (Three-term Lawmaker, DPK)
  • Culture, Sports and Tourism: Kang Yu-jung (Lawmaker and former presidential spokesperson)
