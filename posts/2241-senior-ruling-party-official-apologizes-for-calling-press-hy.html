Senior Ruling Party Official Apologizes for Calling Press "Hyenas"

November 12, 2026

Choi Min-hee, a member of the Supreme Council of the ruling Democratic Party, has issued a formal apology after disparaging photojournalists as a "pack of hyenas" seeking to manufacture political discord.

  • The Incident: Choi posted photos of journalists waiting to cover a National Assembly event in a private Telegram chat with supporters, labeling them "hyenas" and accusing them of trying to film "discord-sowing" footage.
  • Media Backlash: The Korea Press Photographers Association and the Korea Video Journalists Association issued a joint condemnation, characterizing the remarks as an insult to the profession and threatening legal action for defamation.
  • Internal Friction: The gaffe sparked rare public criticism from within her own party, with fellow Democratic Party lawmaker Kim Woo-young accusing Choi of favoring partisan YouTube influencers over institutional media.
  • Political Fallout: The People Power Party, South Korea’s main conservative opposition, seized on the comments to paint President Lee Jae-myung’s administration as hostile to a free and independent press.

The Shift Toward Partisan Media Echo Chambers

The controversy highlights a growing friction between the Democratic Party leadership and the mainstream press. While political tension with the media is common, Choi’s decision to vent her frustrations in a private Telegram group for "die-hard" supporters illustrates how top officials are increasingly retreating into digital echo chambers. By framing professional photojournalists—whose job is to document the movements of public officials—as predatory animals, Choi signaled a worldview where any unvetted coverage is treated as a malicious act of sabotage.

Internal critics like Representative Kim Woo-young were quick to point out that this rhetoric is counterproductive. Kim noted that while the party leadership often embraces large-scale pro-government YouTube channels that provide uncritical support, they have become increasingly dismissive of traditional outlets. This "media hatred" not only alienates the press corps but also undermines the Democratic Party’s broader legislative goals regarding media reform, as the public begins to view such policies through the lens of partisan retaliation rather than democratic improvement.

Damage Control and the Death of the Group Chat

Choi’s apology, delivered a week after the initial comments, was a necessary retreat to prevent the scandal from overshadowing President Lee Jae-myung’s legislative agenda. Standing at the National Assembly’s press center, she admitted her "expression was excessive and inappropriate." However, the sincerity of the apology was questioned by the press corps, as her initial reaction to the leak was not remorse, but anger that someone within the private chat had "leaked" her comments to the outside world.

Following the apology, the Telegram group where the comments originated was abruptly shut down. Administrators claimed it had become "unmanageable," but the move is widely seen as an attempt to erase the trail of similar rhetoric. While the chat room is gone, the incident has left a lasting bruise on the relationship between the ruling party and the journalists who cover them. For a government that prides itself on democratic transparency, labeling the very people who provide that transparency as "hyenas" is a difficult narrative to walk back.
