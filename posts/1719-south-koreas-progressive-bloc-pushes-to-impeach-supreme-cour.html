South Korea's Progressive Bloc Pushes to Impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Amid Scandals Over Seoul Mayor

October 30, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) and its progressive ally, the Rebuilding Korea Party, are escalating a high-stakes drive to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, alleging systemic judicial bias following the controversial acquittal of conservative Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon.

  • What happened: An appellate court overturned a previous ruling to acquit Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, a heavyweight in the main opposition People Power Party (PPP), in an illicit campaign-funding case tied to political broker Myung Tae-kyun, sparking outrage over the court's reasoning that Oh "would not have broken the law."
  • The new allegations: Mayor Oh faces separate criminal complaints over the city's "Hangang Bus" river ferry initiative, involving allegations of a 320 million won (about $240,000) kickback paid to an unvetted shipbuilder and accusations that Oh misled the city council about state audit findings.
  • A ticking clock: The statute of limitations on Oh’s election-law charges expires in early December, putting intense pressure on prosecutors to decide on an indictment.
  • What it changes: Progressive lawmakers plan to bypass the PPP to initiate a parliamentary inquiry while drafting an impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho to suspend his authority before upcoming election-related trials.

Judicial Double Standards and the Case Against the Chief Justice

Critics argue that Chief Justice Cho’s judiciary has operated as a shield for conservative figures. The appellate court’s acquittal of Mayor Oh drew fierce condemnation from legal observers for relying on subjective assumptions rather than material evidence—especially after lower courts convicted intermediaries involved in the transactions. While witness testimony from broker Myung Tae-kyun was accepted in cases clearing former president Yoon Suk-yeol during Mr. Yoon's tenure, judges selectively dismissed the exact same witness's credibility to absolve Oh.

For the progressive coalition, the ruling represents an intolerable politicization of the bench. The Rebuilding Korea Party is demanding that the DPK move quickly to formalize an impeachment motion alongside a parliamentary probe. By suspending Chief Justice Cho's duties, lawmakers hope to neutralize what they view as a judicial establishment hostile to the current administration of President Lee Jae-myung and protect upcoming election-law rulings from partisan influence.

The Hangang Bus Fallout and Coalition Discipline

Mayor Oh's legal troubles extend beyond campaign finance. His signature Hangang Bus project awarded contracts to Gadeok Heavy Industries, a firm with no prior shipbuilding experience. City council inquiries uncovered circumstantial evidence of a 320 million won kickback, alongside claims that internal documentation intended for investigators was destroyed. Oh's public claims that the Board of Audit and Inspection formally dismissed the case were contradicted by official records, prompting the DPK to file complaints for spreading false information under the Public Official Election Act.

With the December deadline to indict Oh fast approaching, the ruling DPK is also managing internal discipline. The party is holding a contest for head of its Women's Committee—a key post carrying automatic membership on the powerful candidate nomination committee for future municipal elections. While an unexpected race between two-term lawmaker Moon Jeong-bok and freshman Kim Nam-hee stirred talk of factional friction, the leadership is moving quickly to preserve internal unity to keep its legislative offensive focused on the judiciary and City Hall.
