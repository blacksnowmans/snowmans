Why Gold is Spiking: The Physics and Limits Behind the Metal

September 27, 2026

Gold prices are hitting record highs not just because of market anxiety, but because the Earth literally keeps most of its gold buried out of human reach.

  • Cosmic abundance: Earth actually holds enough gold to coat its entire surface 50 centimeters (about 20 inches) deep, but gravity sank almost all of it into the planet's core and mantle during its molten early days.
  • Mining constraints: Miners are running out of high-grade ore, forcing them to dig deeper for lower concentrations, which drives up extraction costs exponentially.
  • Unique properties: Unlike silver or iron, gold forms a microscopically thin oxide layer that protects it from decay, explaining its thousands-year-old status as an uncorrupting store of value.

We treat gold as a purely financial instrument, but its high price is ultimately a collision between human desire and hard physical limits. The gold we trade today represents only the microscopic fraction left on Earth's thin outer crust. As easily accessible deposits dry up, mining operations require deeper shafts, heavier equipment, and vastly more energy to unearth far lower-grade rock.

Beyond economics, the fascination with gold taps into our obsession with permanence in a decaying world. While other metals react with oxygen and rust away, gold's stubborn chemical stability keeps it shining for millennia. It is less a human invention of value and more a rare cosmic gift that happens to defy the elements.
