Why League of Legends Icon Faker Turned Down a $24M Blank Check to Stay in Korea

October 16, 2026

  • What happened: Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok has repeatedly rejected multi-million dollar offers—including a reported $20 million-plus (about $24 billion won) annual salary from Chinese and North American teams—preferring to stay with his South Korean esports organization, T1.
  • What it changes: Faker has not only remained a "one-club man" in an industry known for aggressive player poaching, but he also secured a partial ownership stake in T1 in 2020 and extended his contract through 2029.
  • Who is affected: The move protects the competitive health of the LCK (League of Legends Champions Korea) and cements Faker's status as a foundational pillar of South Korean cultural soft power.

More Than Money: Defending Korean Esports

When Chinese capital swept through Korean esports in 2014, buying out entire rosters with offers two to five times higher than domestic salaries, a 17-year-old Faker chose to stay put. Turning down astronomical sums has become a recurring theme in his career. Former T1 CEO Joe Marsh confirmed that Chinese teams tabled offers reaching $20 million a year during the 2021 free agency window, yet Faker opted to prioritize his personal growth, his relationship with his team, and his identity as a representative of Korean esports.

By refusing to chase the highest bidder, Faker insulated the domestic league from total financial drain, allowing Korean teams to remain competitive on the global stage. Observers note that his modest lifestyle—reportedly spending very little on daily living expenses despite his earnings—keeps him grounded in a way that makes multi-million-dollar temptations irrelevant. In an era where athletes frequently hop teams for short-term financial gains, Faker’s loyalty is viewed by supporters as a rare defense of public, cultural value over raw capitalism.

From Franchise Player to Co-Owner

Recognizing that standard salary increases would eventually fail to capture his value, T1 broke new ground in 2020 by offering Faker a stake in the team as a part-owner alongside a multi-year contract extension. This unique structure treats the legendary mid-laner not merely as an employee, but as an institutional partner whose career will eventually transition into team management. With his latest contract extension keeping him signed through 2029, Faker is on track to spend his entire professional playing career under a single banner.

Ultimately, Faker's choice to stay in Korea reflects a philosophy centered on legacy rather than accumulation. While critics of modern professional sports often point to runaway commercialization as a corrupting force, Faker's career suggests that individual purpose and institutional loyalty can still prevail. For international observers watching the global expansion of competitive gaming, his journey remains a masterclass in how an athlete can transcend the sport itself.
