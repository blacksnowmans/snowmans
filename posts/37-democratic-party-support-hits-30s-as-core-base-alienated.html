Democratic Party Support Hits 30s as Core Base Alienated

September 24, 2026

The South Korean Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country’s main liberal opposition party, is facing a deepening crisis as its support ratings drop into the 30% range—a decline driven by the alienation of its core base in a misguided attempt to capture centrist voters.

  • Falling Numbers: Recent polling by Gallup Korea shows DPK support at 39%, the first time it has dipped below the 40% threshold since last October.
  • Failed Strategy: Party leadership, including Representative Kim Min-seok, has prioritized "pragmatic expansion" toward moderate-conservative voters, effectively distancing the party’s traditional, loyal supporters.
  • Policy Missteps: Internal party analysis points to failures in real estate and financial policy (specifically regarding stock market instability) as the primary catalysts for the public’s loss of confidence.
  • Internal Conflict: Public spats between senior figures like Kim Min-seok and former leader Jung Chung-rae have exposed deep factional rifts, further eroding public trust.

The Illusion of "Outer Expansion"

The DPK’s recent strategy has been to broaden its political reach, but the result has been a "worst of both worlds" scenario. While the party has failed to attract new, moderate voters, it has simultaneously triggered an exodus of its base. Critics note that the party’s core supporter engagement has plummeted—some reports suggest response rates from the base have cratered from 50% to roughly 17%. By treating its most loyal supporters as a group to be "taught" rather than "partnered with," the leadership has alienated the very people who built the party’s foundation.

This detachment is exacerbated by a toxic political climate fueled by social media algorithms. Echo chambers, often reinforced by extreme partisan content on YouTube, have turned internal dissent into a taboo. Critics of the party’s current direction are frequently dismissed with "Shincheonji frames"—a derogatory smear suggesting they are outside infiltrators or cult-affiliated—rather than being engaged as legitimate party members raising valid concerns. This defensive, insular posture has left the leadership trapped in a "metaverse" of its own making, oblivious to the fact that nearly 70% of voters in their 20s and 30s now feel the party does not understand their reality.

A Warning to the Leadership

The current situation mirrors the downfall of previous conservative administrations, where arrogance and a refusal to accept "bitter medicine" led to political isolation. The DPK is now facing a structural challenge: it has prioritized factional hegemony and power dynamics over tangible improvements in the lives of citizens. As the party heads toward future local and national elections, the "golden time" for course correction is rapidly disappearing.

True reform will not come from blind praise or the silencing of critics, but from the courage to listen to the 17% of core supporters who have quietly turned away. Unless the leadership steps out of its digital echo chamber and addresses the economic anxieties of the public, it risks turning a temporary polling slump into a lasting electoral defeat.
