Seoul’s 91-Week Price Streak Masks a Growing Crack in the Real Estate Market

November 07, 2026

While Seoul apartment prices have statistically risen for 91 consecutive weeks, the market is fracturing as the prestigious Gangnam districts face significant price drops and southern satellite cities overheat.

  • Gangnam’s "invincibility myth" is wavering, with flagship luxury apartments seeing price drops of over $1 million (1.4 billion won) in single transactions.
  • The "Semiconductor Belt" is surging, as southern Gyeonggi Province cities like Bundang and Suwon see record highs driven by industrial development and proximity to major tech hubs.
  • Construction costs have spiked 38% in a year, making redevelopment projects in poorer districts financially impossible and freezing the new housing market.
  • The rental crisis is worsening, with Jeonse (Korea’s unique lump-sum deposit rental system) prices rising for 88 weeks as supply dries up.

The Gangnam Decoupling and the "Peak Theory"

For decades, the "Gangnam 3"—the wealthy southern districts of Gangnam, Seocho, and Songpa—were considered immune to market downturns. That confidence is shaking. At Raemian One Bailey, a landmark luxury complex in Seocho, a medium-sized unit recently sold for about $4.1 million (5.7 billion won), a staggering $1 million drop from its peak of $5.1 million (7.1 billion won). This isn't an isolated incident; small and mid-sized apartments across the elite districts are seeing 5% to 10% price adjustments as buyers hit their credit limits.

Market analysts are increasingly pointing to a "Real Estate Peak." While 61% of Seoul residents still expect prices to rise, the actual ability to purchase has hit a wall. The current Lee Jae-myung administration has signaled a firm departure from the deregulation-heavy era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, whose policies are now blamed for fueling the household debt bubble. President Lee has explicitly stated that the "myth of real estate invincibility" must be broken, and with tighter loan regulations in place, the market is entering a forced "diet" phase.

A Tale of Two Cities: Bundang vs. the Outskirts

While the capital’s center cools, a different fever is burning in the southern suburbs. Bundang, a major satellite city, recently recorded the highest price growth in the country. This is driven by the "Semiconductor Belt" effect—the massive investment in chip manufacturing clusters in nearby Yongin and Pyeongtaek. Investors are flocking to these areas, betting on long-term demand from high-income tech workers. This "balloon effect" shows that liquidity is not disappearing but is instead concentrating in areas with guaranteed industrial growth.

In contrast, the "No-Do-Gang" area (the northern districts of Nowon, Dobong, and Gangbuk) is facing a bleak reality. Residents hoping for urban renewal are finding that the "contribution costs" for redevelopment now exceed the actual value of their homes. Because private apartment construction costs have jumped nearly 40% in a single year, many projects have simply ground to a halt. This has created a brutal hierarchy: those in the southern tech corridor see their assets grow, while those in older northern districts are trapped in depreciating, aging buildings they cannot afford to fix.

The current market is no longer a "rising tide" that lifts all boats. It is a fragmented landscape where credit restrictions and construction inflation are punishing the middle class while luxury hubs face a long-overdue correction.

The Rental Bottleneck

The most immediate threat to stability is the Jeonse market. As buying becomes too expensive or risky, residents are staying in rentals, pushing the average Seoul Jeonse price to over $520,000 (728 million won). In areas like Sanggye and Bukahyeon, where large-scale redevelopment is displacing thousands of families, rental prices are effectively "whatever the landlord asks." For the average worker, the dream of homeownership is being squeezed from both sides: purchase prices are too high to reach, and rental deposits are too high to allow for savings.
