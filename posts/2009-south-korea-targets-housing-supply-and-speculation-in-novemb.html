South Korea Targets Housing Supply and Speculation in November Real Estate Push

November 06, 2026

South Korea's government is pushing to accelerate Seoul housing supply and relax select property taxes while launching a heavy-handed crackdown on market speculators as price growth slows.

  • What happened: Land Minister Hong Ji-sun urged faster urban redevelopment to boost Seoul housing supply, while the ruling Democratic Party debated easing comprehensive real estate holding taxes for single-home owners who do not live in their properties. Meanwhile, President Lee Jae-myung announced a third wave of government crackdowns on real estate crimes.
  • What it changes: The policy shifts signal a delicate balancing act—attempting to relieve tax pressure on bona fide homebuyers while shutting down speculative market manipulation.
  • Who is affected: Prospective homebuyers in Seoul, non-resident single-home owners facing tax burdens, and property speculators targeted by upcoming government investigations.

Pushing Supply While Balancing Tax Relief

Recent data points to a three-week cooling trend in Seoul apartment prices, which Minister Hong Ji-sun has framed as an encouraging sign of market stabilization. However, officials recognize that sustainable stability depends entirely on securing adequate housing inventory. The central government is pressing the Seoul Metropolitan Government—which holds critical licensing authority—to cooperate on speeding up redevelopment projects and cutting through red tape.

At the same time, ruling party lawmakers from Seoul held a strategy meeting to address public grievances over heavy tax burdens. The discussions focused on easing holding taxes for single-home owners who live outside their primary property due to work, alongside potential revisions to long-term holding deductions. With a decision expected within the month, the ruling party faces the challenge of granting relief to genuine homebuyers without accidentally inviting speculative demand back into the market.

Cracking Down on Market Manipulation

Alongside supply and tax adjustments, the administration is turning its sights on illegal market activity. President Lee Jae-myung issued a stern warning that the era of profiting from real estate crime is over, confirming that a third major government crackdown will soon begin. Authorities plan to target behaviors like fake contract reporting, price manipulation, and gift tax evasion that squeeze out ordinary buyers and young Koreans.

Ultimately, stabilizing South Korea's housing market requires more than fluctuating statistics. If government measures successfully weed out speculative cartels while expanding practical housing options, it may finally offer everyday citizens a fairer path to homeownership.
