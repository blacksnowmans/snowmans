South Korean Ruling Party Official Faces Ethics Probe and Legal Charges After Calling Journalists 'Hyenas'

November 13, 2026

A senior lawmaker of South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea is facing an internal ethics probe and criminal defamation charges after publicly calling reporters covering the National Assembly a "pack of hyenas."

  • What happened: Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee sparked intense political and media backlash by verbally abusing press corps members and claiming in a broadcast that President Lee Jae-myung opposed the impeachment of Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • Official response: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok issued a public apology on November 12 and ordered an immediate investigation by the party's ethics inspection unit.
  • Legal escalation: The Korea Press Photographers Association announced it will file a formal defamation complaint against Choi with the police on November 16.
  • Political impact: The Presidential Office quickly rejected Choi’s claims regarding Yoon Suk-yeol's impeachment, while the main opposition People Power Party demanded Choi's expulsion from the ruling party.

A Sharp Fall for a Former Media Reform Activist

Choi’s remarks have struck a particularly sensitive nerve in South Korean politics due to her history as a prominent media rights activist before entering parliament. By directing crude hostility toward press corps members performing their reporting duties, she faces widespread criticism for betraying the very principles she once championed. The controversy worsened when she claimed during a broadcast that President Lee Jae-myung opposed the impeachment of Yoon Suk-yeol. The Presidential Office issued a strong statement of regret just 40 minutes later, refuting her claims and distancing the administration from her comments.

Political and Legal Fallout for the Democratic Party

The swift intervention by party leader Kim Min-seok highlights the severe risk Choi’s conduct poses to the ruling Democratic Party of Korea. By launching a formal ethics audit, the party leadership is attempting to contain damage as the conservative People Power Party accuses the ruling majority of arrogance and hostility toward the press. The upcoming criminal complaint by photojournalists represents a rare legal step by press groups against a sitting senior lawmaker, threatening to turn Choi into a persistent political liability for President Lee’s legislative agenda.
