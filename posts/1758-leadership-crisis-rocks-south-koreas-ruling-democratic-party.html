Leadership Crisis Rocks South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party

October 31, 2026

The Democratic Party (DP), South Korea’s ruling liberal party under President Lee Jae-myung, is facing a severe leadership crisis as internal infighting and a sharp drop in approval ratings fuel rumors of a transition to an emergency interim committee.

  • Leadership under fire: Critics within the party are calling for the resignation of the current leadership, led by Kim Min-seok, as internal disagreements over judicial reform and policy agendas paralyze decision-making.
  • Support bleeding: Recent polls show the DP’s approval rating plummeting—down to roughly 35%—with significant defections among its core base, particularly voters in their 40s and progressives who are shifting their support to the Rebuilding Korea Party (a smaller, more hardline liberal party).
  • Judicial standoff: A high-stakes clash between the presidential office and Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae over judicial appointments has stalled the government’s promised judicial reforms, creating a political bottleneck.

A Party at War with Itself

The DP is currently paralyzed by disputes over how to handle sensitive issues, including an investigation into the Shincheonji religious sect and the potential impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. Senior party figures, including former National Assembly Speaker Woo Won-shik, have publicly rebuked the leadership, warning that the party is squandering its political mandate on petty factional squabbles rather than focusing on the livelihoods of citizens.

The situation has become so volatile that rumors of a transition to an "Emergency Response Committee" (a temporary leadership body used when a party faces an existential crisis) are circulating. Leaked comments from influential party insiders have even disparaged the current leadership as "incompetent," signaling a deep-seated loss of confidence that may force a major reorganization if the internal friction is not resolved immediately.

Defections and the "Alternative" Vote

The electoral fallout is significant. Recent polling data indicates a massive decline in support, with the DP losing nearly 15 percentage points among its traditional progressive base and a 17 percentage point drop among voters in their 40s. Many of these voters are migrating to the Rebuilding Korea Party.

Public intellectual Rhyu Si-min has diagnosed this as a "discontent shift" rather than a true ideological split. Voters are not necessarily abandoning liberal values, but are using the Rebuilding Korea Party as a vehicle to express their frustration with the Lee Jae-myung administration’s perceived policy failures and internal dysfunction. For the ruling party, this is a clear warning: if the leadership continues to blame external factors rather than addressing its own inability to govern, the trend of losing its base to alternative liberal parties will only accelerate.

The government must now pivot from internal power struggles to tangible policy results. If the Lee administration continues to operate under the shadow of the "demonization frames" used against it by conservatives and the prosecution, it will remain in a defensive crouch. To survive, the party must reconcile its internal factions, focus on economic stability, and pursue judicial reform through legislative precision rather than emotional, partisan confrontations that do little to restore public trust.
