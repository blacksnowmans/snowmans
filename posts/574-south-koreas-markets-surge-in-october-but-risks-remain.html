South Korea's Markets Surge in October, But Risks Remain

October 02, 2026

  • South Korea's tech-heavy Kosdaq index jumped 4.48% on October 1, marking a dramatic rebound after a brutal September sell-off.
  • Foreign net selling slowed significantly to about $350 million (500 billion won), easing immediate pressure on local equities.
  • Semiconductor equipment suppliers and biotech stocks led the rally, fueled by a delay in short-selling rules and strong global demand for AI-related hardware.
  • Retail investors remain vulnerable to high leverage, volatile trading conditions, and Telegram investment scams using deepfakes.

A Relief Rally Rooted in Tech and Semiconductor Demand

South Korean markets kicked off the fourth quarter with a sharp rebound, as the Kosdaq surged 4.48% and the broader Kospi gained 1.95%. The rally brought much-needed relief to retail investors following a painful third quarter marked by heavy losses. Foreign investors reined in their aggressive sell-offs, dropping daily offloading to around $350 million. While high exchange rates and lingering market volatility remain concerns, the breadth of the rally—with over 1,200 stocks advancing—suggests the market has built short-term defensive resilience.

Driving this momentum are South Korea's powerhouse semiconductor supply chain and a temporary reprieve on short-selling regulations. Global demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure, high-bandwidth memory (HBM), and enterprise SSDs has accelerated equipment and materials spending, turning domestic component makers into core beneficiaries. Meanwhile, pharmaceutical and biotech stocks bounced back after regulators delayed the enforcement of strict short-selling rules that would have severely impacted R&D-heavy firms.

Governance Battles and Scams Threaten Retail Investors

Despite the upbeat market indicators, structural risks and corporate governance issues continue to trouble minority shareholders. Controversy surrounds SK Hynix, South Korea's second-largest chipmaker, over plans to list its U.S. subsidiary Solidigm, which retail investors fear will dilute their share value. The debate highlights growing pressure on corporate boards to prioritize shareholder loyalty over internal restructuring.

At the same time, retail investors are facing a wave of sophisticated financial fraud. Online scammers are deploying AI-generated deepfakes of prominent market commentators to lure victims into fraudulent Telegram chat rooms promising guaranteed stock returns. Regulators and analysts emphasize that sustainable returns in South Korea's volatile equities depend on careful analysis of corporate fundamentals—such as surging R&D costs in biotech and actual supply chain positioning—rather than speculative shortcuts.
