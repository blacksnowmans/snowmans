The ETF Scandal and the Crisis of Leadership in South Korea’s Main Opposition

September 17, 2026

South Korea’s main opposition party, the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), is facing a severe political storm as falling approval ratings expose deep internal fractures and questionable policy decisions. The party’s leadership is currently reeling from accusations of “imperial” conduct and the emergence of a leaked government document regarding high-risk financial products.

  • Policy Failure: A leaked document confirms that the office of the Prime Minister, under then-leader Kim Min-seok, pushed for the approval of high-risk "2x leverage" Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) despite unified warnings from financial experts and major brokerages.
  • Financial Fallout: Reports indicate that 75–80% of investors in their 20s who bought these specific products have suffered massive losses, contributing to a sharp decline in the party’s support among young voters.
  • Internal Authoritarianism: Party leadership has been accused of bypassing consensus, specifically by unilaterally announcing a reduction in the threshold for parliamentary negotiation groups (from 20 to 15 seats) without consulting other opposition parties.
  • Political Infighting: Critics within the party, including senior figures like Choo Mi-ae, are facing aggressive pushback from the leadership, deepening the perception of a party more focused on internal purges than national governance.

The "Imperial" Leadership and the ETF Debacle

The DPK’s current crisis is not merely a result of bad luck, but a consequence of a top-down leadership style that ignores expert warnings and stifles internal dissent. The leaked YTN report on the leverage ETF meeting is particularly damaging; it shows that the financial sector explicitly flagged these products as dangerous, yet the party leadership pushed them forward under the guise of financial innovation. When these investments wiped out the savings of young, inexperienced traders, the party’s refusal to take responsibility—instead blaming market volatility—severely eroded the trust of its core support base.

The party’s internal atmosphere has become defined by a "culture of silence." Lawmakers, fearing repercussions during the candidate nomination process—which many suspect is being manipulated through a veneer of "system-based" fairness—have largely chosen to remain quiet rather than challenge the leadership. This fear-based loyalty is preventing any meaningful course correction, turning the DPK into a shadow of the more robust, debate-driven party it once claimed to be.

Analysis: The Price of Avoiding Accountability

The DPK’s plummeting approval ratings, now hovering in the low 30% range, are the direct result of a disconnect between the party’s power-hungry behavior and the reality faced by ordinary citizens. By prioritizing internal control over transparent communication, the leadership has created a structural failure that no amount of political maneuvering can fix.

If the party continues to prioritize "whipping" its own members into submission over addressing the economic devastation caused by its policy failures, it risks losing the moral authority required to lead. The history of Korean politics shows that true reform only comes when leaders stop worrying about their own job security and start confronting the hard truths of their own failures. Until the DPK moves past its internal blame game, it will remain trapped in a downward spiral of its own making.
