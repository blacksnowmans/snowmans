A Pilgrimage for the Soul of Korean Democracy

November 09, 2026

Around 1,000 citizens traveled to the rural homes of former progressive leaders this weekend, signaling a growing nostalgic yearning for political dignity amidst frustration with the current administration.

  • The "Walking the Path of Democracy" event saw a massive turnout at Bongha and Pyeongsan villages, the respective homes of former Presidents Roh Moo-hyun and Moon Jae-in.
  • Participants expressed deep mourning for the late Lee Hae-chan, a legendary strategist and former Prime Minister of the Democratic Party of Korea (the current ruling party), whose absence is felt in today’s fractured political landscape.
  • The gathering highlighted a sharp contrast between the warm reception for former President Moon and the public's disappointment with President Lee Jae-myung’s recent social media conduct.
  • The event serves as a reminder of the "Bongha spirit"—a commitment to justice and anti-authoritarianism—that remains the bedrock of South Korean progressive identity.

The Enduring Legacy of Roh and Moon

The pilgrimage began at the gravesite of Lee Hae-chan, often considered the "intellectual giant" of modern Korean progressivism. For many attendees, the visit was bittersweet. There is a prevailing sense among the base that if Lee were still active, the Democratic Party would not be struggling with its current lack of direction. This sentiment quickly transitioned into a journey to Bongha Village, the final resting place of Roh Moo-hyun. Despite its remote location and poor public transit, Bongha remains a spiritual center for those who value Roh’s "foolish" persistence against corruption and the "prosecutorial dictatorship."

At Pyeongsan Village, the atmosphere shifted from mourning to healing. Former President Moon Jae-in, now a quiet bookstore owner, was met with thunderous cheers. The sight of Moon—peacefully retired and beloved by the public—stands in stark contrast to the fate of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was impeached, removed from office, and is currently serving time in prison. For the citizens present, Moon represents a model of how power should be handled and, eventually, gracefully relinquished.

A Warning to the Current Leadership

While the event was a celebration of past leaders, it carried a heavy subtext for the present. Many participants expressed disappointment in President Lee Jae-myung. While the crowd was busy honoring the dignity of his predecessors, President Lee reportedly posted a message on X (formerly Twitter) that many interpreted as "petty" or "triggered" by the attention Moon was receiving. For a sitting president to appear insecure regarding the popularity of a retired predecessor suggests a lack of the "grand leadership" that defined the Roh and Moon eras.

The event was organized by Sisa Tapa, a prominent progressive citizen media group led by Lee Jeong-won. This network of independent media and citizen activists is increasingly seen as a vital counterbalance to mainstream narratives. As the ruling party navigates internal scandals and the ongoing fallout from the Yudong-gyu case (a key figure in a major land development scandal), the base is looking backward to find the moral compass they feel is missing in the current administration. The pilgrimage was not just a trip down memory lane; it was a strategic gathering of citizens preparing to defend democratic values in a turbulent political era.
