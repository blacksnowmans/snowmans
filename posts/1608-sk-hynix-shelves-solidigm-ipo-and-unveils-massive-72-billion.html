SK Hynix Shelves Solidigm IPO and Unveils Massive $72 Billion Shareholder Payout

October 28, 2026

South Korean memory chip titan SK Hynix has halted the public listing of its US enterprise storage subsidiary Solidigm, choosing instead to fund growth internally while rolling out an unprecedented 100 trillion won (about $72 billion) shareholder return package through fiscal 2026.

  • Solidigm listing aborted: SK Hynix withdrew the pre-IPO and initial public offering process for Solidigm, its AI data center storage and SSD business, citing severe financial market volatility and sector-wide semiconductor stock pullbacks that threatened proper valuation.
  • Unprecedented 100 trillion won roadmap: The company pledged to return at least 100 trillion won (about $72 billion) by fiscal 2026, earmarking over 30 trillion won (about $21.6 billion) for cash dividends and more than 60 trillion won (about $43.2 billion) for share buybacks and retirements.
  • Record quarterly dividend: Shareholders will receive 20,000 won (about $14.40) per share this quarter—a 14 trillion won (about $10 billion) total outlay—comprising a 10,000 won base dividend and an equal 10,000 won special dividend sharing profits from high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and AI demand.
  • Aggressive share cancellations: A 40 trillion won (about $28.8 billion) treasury stock buyback completed last week will be canceled immediately, alongside an additional 10 trillion won cancellation scheduled for this quarter.

Prioritizing Balance-Sheet Fundamentals Over Market Dilution

Shelving Solidigm’s public debut is a pragmatic retreat from an unforgiving equity market. Rather than rushing an IPO during tech-sector turbulence and accepting an unfavorable valuation, SK Hynix is opting to back its AI storage expansion through internal cash flow and balance-sheet strength. The move avoids equity dilution while keeping Solidigm’s future growth tied entirely to the parent group’s valuation.

This self-funded approach requires exceptional cash-generation capability. SK Hynix is wagering that its operational momentum in AI components—particularly HBM—is resilient enough to bankroll heavy capital expenditures without leaning on external equity markets.

A Radical Antidote to the 'Korea Discount'

The 100 trillion won capital return strategy represents a departure from South Korea’s traditional corporate playbook of hoarding cash reserves. Backed by a firm commitment to distribute more than 50% of free cash flow (FCF), SK Hynix is directly linking windfall earnings from the artificial intelligence boom to cash payouts and stock cancellations.

The firm has also established unusually strict corporate governance lines around its buybacks. Out of a planned 20 trillion won share repurchase this quarter, only the 10 trillion won destined for permanent cancellation will count toward the 100 trillion won pledge, while a separate 10 trillion won earmarked for employee compensation is excluded. Amid market skepticism and structural issues lingering from Yoon Suk-yeol's tenure, this aggressive redistribution program establishes a new standard for Korean capital allocation, showing foreign investors that excess corporate profits can indeed reach shareholder pockets.
