South Korea Honors UN Korean War Veterans with Global 'Turn Toward Busan' Tribute

November 12, 2026

On November 11 at 11:00 AM, South Korea and 22 partner nations paused for a synchronized one-minute moment of silence facing the UN Memorial Cemetery in Busan to honor the 1.95 million international troops who served during the 1950–1953 Korean War.

  • What happened: South Korea commemorated the International Memorial Day for UN Veterans through nationwide silent prayers accompanied by sirens, an ROK Air Force aerobatic flypast, and blue lighting displays across Seoul’s central Gwanghwamun Square.
  • Key proposal: Lee Joong-keun, chairman of major property developer Booyoung Group and head of the UN Association of Korea, urged the government to restore UN Day (October 24) as an official national holiday.
  • Who is affected: Surviving war veterans, families of the fallen, diplomatic envoys from 22 participating UN nations, and South Korean citizens engaging in memorial events nationwide.

Veteran Diplomacy as a Pillar of National Security

The annual "Turn Toward Busan" event is far more than a routine ceremony—it represents one of South Korea's most vital forms of soft power and veteran diplomacy. By honoring the 22 nations that dispatched nearly two million troops to defend the country, Seoul actively reinforces the historical foundation of its foreign alliances. The presence of foreign diplomats alongside official appreciation luncheons hosted by the Ministry of Patriots and Veterans Affairs demonstrates how honoring past sacrifice reinforces modern diplomatic bonds.

Civic and corporate involvement added emotional weight to this year's proceedings. A 92-year-old combat veteran laid flowers at the graves of fallen comrades in Busan, while local veterans' offices organized peace walks and community art education programs for veterans' spouses. Simultaneously, an academic seminar marking the sixth anniversary of the late General Paik Sun-yup—South Korea's renowned wartime commander—focused on preserving the historical legacy of the UN coalition as a core security asset for the nation.

Institutionalizing History: The Push to Restore UN Day

The proposal by Booyoung Group Chairman Lee Joong-keun to reinstate UN Day on October 24 as a public holiday highlights a push to formalize national remembrance. South Korea previously observed UN Day as a legal holiday to recognize international support during its darkest hour, but the designation was later revoked. Restoring it would institutionalize civic education for younger generations, ensuring they understand that South Korea's transition from war-torn ruins to a global economic powerhouse was made possible by international solidarity and sacrifice.
