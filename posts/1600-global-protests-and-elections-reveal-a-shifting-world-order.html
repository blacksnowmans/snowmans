Global Protests and Elections Reveal a Shifting World Order

October 28, 2026

From razor-thin parliamentary exits in Israel to a masterclass in digital campaigning in Brazil and widespread unrest in France, citizens worldwide are pushing back against political stagnation—and traditional media is missing the story.

  • Israel's high-stakes election: Early exit polls show an ultra-tight race between anti-Netanyahu and pro-Netanyahu blocs, with wartime leadership hanging in the balance.
  • Brazil's digital upset: Veteran politician Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva pulled off a stunning presidential comeback in just three weeks by leveraging WhatsApp and youth-led social media strategies.
  • Misleading media frames: International coverage of French protests often reduces complex social unrest and safety-net debates to simple narratives of violence and property destruction.
  • The rise of Gen Z activism: From Indonesia to Europe, young voters facing high unemployment and bleak economic futures are taking to the streets, signaling a global revival of student-led political movements.

What links these disparate global events is a profound disconnect between citizens and ruling systems. In Brazil, Lula's victory proved that abstract political rhetoric fails against concrete anxieties over safety and the cost of living; voters respond only when politics speaks directly to their daily survival. Similarly, the wave of student-led demonstrations sweeping across multiple continents highlights how economic precarity is driving a new generation to reclaim political agency through digital networks rather than traditional party politics.

Yet, as these grassroots movements demand systemic change, global media coverage frequently flattens complex realities into sensationalized soundbites. Whether misrepresenting the nuanced makeup of French street protests or reducing systemic welfare debates to simple budget deficits, mainstream reporting often obscures the actual grievances of ordinary people. For international observers, looking past the media noise to understand these underlying economic and social pressures is essential for making sense of where global politics is heading next.
