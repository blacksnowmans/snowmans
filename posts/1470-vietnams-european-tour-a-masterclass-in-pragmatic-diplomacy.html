Vietnam’s European Tour: A Masterclass in Pragmatic Diplomacy and a Lesson for Seoul

October 26, 2026

Vietnamese General Secretary and President To Lam has concluded a landmark tour of Europe, securing approximately $21.5 billion (20 billion Euros) in potential investment while upgrading diplomatic ties with Denmark, Belgium, and the European Union to "strategic partner" status.

  • Diplomatic Elevation: Vietnam successfully upgraded its relations with Denmark, Belgium, and the EU, signaling its transition from a regional Southeast Asian player to a global strategic partner.
  • Economic Windfall: The tour opened doors to an estimated 30 trillion KRW ($21.5 billion) in resources and investment, specifically targeting green energy, high-tech manufacturing, and logistics.
  • Strategic Autonomy: By strengthening ties with Europe, Hanoi is utilizing "Bamboo Diplomacy" to balance its position between the U.S. and China, creating a third pillar of economic support.
  • Tech Transition: New agreements on intellectual property and joint R&D with the EU indicate Vietnam’s shift away from being a low-cost assembly hub toward becoming a high-value industrial economy.

The Logic of "Bamboo Diplomacy" in a Multipolar World

Vietnam is executing a sophisticated strategy of "strategic autonomy." By courting the EU, Hanoi is deliberately reducing its over-reliance on any single superpower. The focus on Denmark’s offshore wind expertise and Belgium’s logistics infrastructure demonstrates a clear intent to modernize the national economy through specialized European technology. This is not merely about trade; it is about institutionalizing European standards within Vietnam to ensure long-term competitiveness and avoid the "middle-income trap" that plagues many developing nations.

For South Korea, Vietnam’s ascent is both a blueprint and a competitive challenge. Under the current administration of President Lee Jae-myung and the Democratic Party (South Korea's liberal ruling party), Seoul has returned to a pragmatic "national interest first" foreign policy. This approach stands in stark contrast to the previous era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment—whose administration was frequently criticized for a rigid, "value-based" diplomacy that often alienated major regional partners. Vietnam’s success reinforces the idea that in a multipolar world, national interest must supersede ideological alignment.

Economic Shifts and the Korean Response

The $21.5 billion investment pipeline is a staggering figure, representing a significant portion of Vietnam’s annual foreign direct investment (FDI) targets. However, the more critical takeaway is the focus on "Green Diplomacy." As Vietnam adopts European environmental and technical standards, it is positioning itself to navigate the EU’s upcoming Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). This move ensures that Vietnamese-made goods remain competitive in the lucrative European market while the country undergoes a domestic energy transition.

Korean companies, which have long treated Vietnam as a primary manufacturing base, must now prepare for a Vietnam that is more technologically advanced and environmentally regulated. The Lee Jae-myung administration’s domestic emphasis on RE100 (the global initiative to use 100% renewable energy) aligns well with Vietnam’s new direction. This provides a unique window for a "triangular cooperation" model between Europe’s technology, Korea’s ICT expertise, and Vietnam’s growing industrial base. In an era where technology is synonymous with national security, Vietnam’s clever maneuvering serves as a reminder that diplomatic flexibility is the ultimate currency.
