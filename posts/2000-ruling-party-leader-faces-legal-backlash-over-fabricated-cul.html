Ruling Party Leader Faces Legal Backlash Over Fabricated Cult-Smear Claim

November 06, 2026

Democratic Party of Korea leader Kim Min-seok has admitted that his high-profile primary campaign claims accusing rival faction members of forming a secret alliance with the controversial Shincheonji cult were entirely fabricated, sparking criminal complaints and calls for his resignation.

  • What happened: Three months after winning the leadership race of South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), Kim Min-seok privately confessed to party lawmakers that an internal investigation found zero evidence supporting his campaign allegations against internal opponents.
  • What it changes: Spreading false accusations during an internal primary to derail competitors violates Article 52 of South Korea's Political Parties Act, carrying potential penalties of up to five years in prison or fines of up to 10 million won (about $7,500).
  • Who is affected: Kim faces growing pressure from primary rivals like lawmaker Chung Chung-rae, party ethics committees, and grassroots members filing police complaints and seeking an injunction to suspend his leadership duties.
  • The political damage: The scandal exposes the DPK to intense charges of political hypocrisy, as the party previously demanded strict criminal accountability for similar campaign falsehoods committed by opposition figures like Yoon Suk-yeol.

A Democratic Breach Masked by a Closed-Door Apology

Kim’s allegations were not minor campaign rhetoric. During the DPK leadership convention, he weaponized fears of factional betrayal by claiming rivals were conspiring with "anti-Lee" (anti-President Lee Jae-myung) forces and Shincheonji—a secretive religious group widely distrusted by the South Korean public. This framing heavily influenced primary voters. Attempting to dismiss the manufactured smear with a single, private apology behind closed doors to lawmakers—rather than addressing the victimized candidates and the voting members who were misled—has only infuriated the party’s grassroots base.

The scandal has now spilled into the legal arena. Supreme Council member Lee Sung-yoon, a former senior prosecutor, has publicly called for an official probe into whether Kim violated party election laws. Meanwhile, party members are filing criminal complaints and preparing injunctions to suspend Kim's authority. Compounding Kim’s troubles, claims by his allies that the party verified member religious affiliations have raised separate legal risks under South Korea’s Personal Information Protection Act regarding the unlawful handling of sensitive personal data.

For President Lee Jae-myung’s ruling party, which built its platform on democratic reform and judicial fairness, the fallout poses a fundamental challenge. The DPK spent years criticizing figures like Yoon Suk-yeol over campaign falsehoods and strict legal accountability. If party leadership shields Kim by treating a severe primary election offense as a minor misstep, it risks stripping the party of its moral authority and damaging internal party democracy from within.
