Democratic Party Internal Chaos: The "Shincheonji" Infiltration Scandal

November 11, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party (DP)—South Korea's dominant liberal party—is facing a severe internal crisis as allegations that the Shincheonji religious sect infiltrated the party have backfired, sparking a bitter factional war and calls for the resignation of party leader Kim Min-seok.

  • What happened: Party lawmaker Lee Sung-yoon reviewed the internal task force report on the Shincheonji infiltration claims and publicly dismissed it as groundless, demanding an apology from Kim Min-seok.
  • The "0.4%" Reality: The investigation revealed that only 0.4% of members were linked to the group, a figure too small to support claims of an organized "takeover," leading critics to call the probe a politically motivated "alibi" exercise.
  • Leadership Crisis: Following the backlash, Kim Min-seok abruptly canceled all scheduled public appearances, leaving the party’s leadership in a state of paralysis.
  • Grassroots Backlash: Party members have filed official petitions demanding disciplinary action against Kim, signaling that the divide is no longer limited to high-level factional infighting.

A Party Fractured by its Own Probe

The controversy centers on whether Shincheonji members organized a mass entry into the party to influence internal primaries. However, Lee Sung-yoon’s blunt assessment that the report contained "nothing of substance" has turned the investigation against its architects. By weaponizing data that ultimately proved inconclusive, the leadership has inadvertently handed the conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), a massive tactical advantage.

The PPP is now seizing the moment, calling for a special counsel investigation into the matter. This puts the ruling party in a precarious position: they are being attacked by the opposition for an "infiltration scandal" that many of their own lawmakers believe was manufactured by their own leadership. The situation has descended into a "frame war" where the party’s scientific approach to governance is being sacrificed for factional interests.

Leadership in Peril

Kim Min-seok’s decision to go silent and cancel his schedule has only fueled rumors of a deeper leadership crisis within the administration of President Lee Jae-myung. In the vacuum left by his silence, frustration is boiling over among party members, who view the internal strife as a betrayal of the party's democratic values and a distraction from the urgent policy needs of the nation.

The irony is profound: in an attempt to project strength against external threats, the DP has created an internal nightmare that threatens its unity. As the party grapples with these calls for discipline and accountability, it faces a critical choice: continue to fight over the 0.4% or acknowledge the miscalculation, apologize, and return to the business of running the country. Failure to resolve this quickly risks alienating the party's core support base and undermining the legislative agenda of the Lee Jae-myung government.
