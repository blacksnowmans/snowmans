South Korea's Weekend Golf Controversy Overshadows Daegu Mall Collapse

October 11, 2026

South Korean politics has once again descended into a fierce partisan row after the opposition revealed that President Lee Jae-myung played golf on the same day a shopping mall ceiling collapsed in Daegu, injuring dozens.

    What happened: Ju Jin-woo, a lawmaker from the conservative People Power Party, released evidence showing President Lee at the state-run Ta릉 Golf Course on the day of the Daegu structural collapse. The government's defense: The presidential office confirmed the golf outing but insisted Lee managed the crisis remotely, receiving briefings and ordering rescue efforts from the course. The real fallout: While politicians trade blows over executive optics and weekend leisure, critics argue both sides are exploiting a structural safety tragedy for political point-scoring.

Optics, Outrage, and the Politics of Crisis Management

The timing of the revelations has handed South Korea's conservative opposition a ready-made weapon. By releasing detailed photographic and video evidence of presidential motorcades and security vehicles at the golf course, the People Power Party framed the president as detached and indifferent while elderly citizens were being rushed to hospitals in Daegu. The opposition piled on secondary jabs regarding family presence and budget use, turning what should have been a sober discussion about disaster response into a messy, partisan spectacle.

Yet the presidential office's defensive posture reveals a persistent political anxiety in Seoul. Breaking protocol to issue a swift explanation, officials stressed that Lee was briefed immediately on the Daegu accident and directed the national police chief to prioritize rescue operations. While answering questions about a leader's weekend movements is exhausting, it underscores how heavily South Korean politics relies on performative empathy. When a disaster strikes, the public demands not just operational competence behind the scenes, but absolute visual solidarity from the top.

Beyond the Fairway: The Real Crisis in Infrastructure

Lost beneath the noise of the golf course controversy is the actual tragedy: a 1994-built commercial building in Daegu where a senior citizens' goods promotional event turned into terror when a ceiling collapsed, sending 18 people to the hospital. Korea's disaster management manuals are theoretically comprehensive, yet the country remains trapped in a reactive cycle where structural decay and safety blind spots in aging buildings are only scrutinized after people bleed.

Ultimately, this episode exposes the fatigue of South Korea's polarized political ecosystem. The opposition weaponizes tragedy to land blows on a progressive administration, while the government fights to protect its image rather than wholly owning the public disconnect of a presidential golf outing during a national scare. Until both sides move past cheap point-scoring, the real victims of systemic neglect will continue to pay the price.
