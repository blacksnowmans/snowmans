Seoul Housing Crisis Deepens: 90 Weeks of Price Hikes Meet 80,000 Unsold Units

November 01, 2026

  • 90-Week Streak: Seoul apartment prices have risen for 90 consecutive weeks, hitting record highs for the year.
  • The Inventory Paradox: Despite surging prices, a "transaction cliff" has left nearly 80,000 unsold properties stranded on the market as buyers face strict lending caps.
  • Political Clash: The conservative People Power Party blames the liberal administration led by President Lee Jae-myung, while critics point to lingering bubbles from the impeached and imprisoned former leader, Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • Supply Push: The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport is accelerating efforts to break ground on 31,000 homes in Seoul this year.

Seoul’s housing market is trapped in a bizarre paradox: prices keep climbing to historic records even as transactions grind to a halt. Prospective buyers are effectively frozen out by strict loan regulations and sky-high valuations, leaving property owners stubbornly holding out for peak prices. This standoff has jammed the pipeline, creating a mounting backlog of nearly 80,000 listings across the capital that simply refuse to sell.

The political fallout is fierce. The opposition People Power Party is hammering the Lee Jae-myung government over the affordability crisis, arguing that housing ladders are being kicked away from everyday citizens. Yet this framing conveniently ignores the cheap-credit policies of the Yoon Suk-yeol era that inflated the bubble in the first place. Fixing a distorted market while managing public anxiety has turned into a high-stakes political tightrope walk for the administration.

Ultimately, the current standoff exposes the deep vulnerability of South Korea's real estate obsession. While the government pushes for public-led developments and faster housing starts, speculative capital continues to huddle in safe-haven assets like Gangnam apartments, widening the gap with neglected provincial markets. Until housing is treated as a place to live rather than a speculative lottery ticket, these policy band-aids will do little to heal the underlying social strain.
