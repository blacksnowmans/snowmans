Civil War in Korea’s Ruling Party: Task Force Disbanded Amid Disciplinary Dispute

November 19, 2026

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), South Korea’s ruling party, has plunged into internal turmoil following the abrupt disbanding of a key media task force and a fierce factional fight over the discipline of Representative Choi Min-hee.

  • The DPK leadership dissolved the Media Public Interest Task Force on November 18, a move widely seen as an attempt to distance the party from controversial remarks made by its chair, Representative Choi.
  • Choi is facing disciplinary pressure after she described her critics as a "pack of hyenas," a comment that triggered a petition for punishment signed by 55 first-term DPK lawmakers.
  • Party heavyweights have revolted against the leadership, with prominent figures like Jung Chung-rae and former Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae accusing Party Representative Kim Min-seok of using the incident for a "political hit."
  • Legislative momentum is at risk as the sudden collapse of the task force stalls the Lee Jae-myung administration’s primary efforts to reform the country's media landscape.

Factional Warfare Masked as Discipline

This conflict is not merely a disagreement over a "slip of the tongue"; it is a battle for influence within the ruling party. By disbanding the task force, the DPK leadership signaled a strategic retreat to protect the party’s image among moderate voters. However, this decision has backfired by exposing deep fractures between the party’s leadership and its most vocal senior members. The "hyena" comment has become a lightning rod for broader grievances regarding how the party handles internal dissent and public criticism.

The counter-offensive from senior members has been remarkably blunt. Representative Jung Chung-rae publicly questioned the timing of the disciplinary push, suggesting that it was a coordinated effort to weaken certain factions. Meanwhile, Choo Mi-ae has turned the tables on the party leadership, suggesting that the ethics committee should investigate Party Representative Kim Min-seok’s own conduct before judging Choi. This level of public acrimony among the party's top brass suggests that the "unity" typically demanded under the Lee Jae-myung administration is beginning to fray at the edges.

A Leadership Crisis for the Ruling Party

The DPK’s ethics committee is now trapped in a "no-win" situation. If the party imposes a harsh penalty on Choi, it risks a full-scale revolt from the party’s core activist base and senior leaders who view her as a "warrior" for their cause. Conversely, if the party issues a mere slap on the wrist, it will appear to be protecting its own, validating the criticisms of the 55 freshman lawmakers who argue that such rhetoric damages the party's national standing.

For the Lee Jae-myung government, this infighting is a costly distraction. The dissolution of the Media Public Interest Task Force effectively kills the momentum for legislative reforms that the party has spent months preparing. As the ruling party consumes itself in a proxy war between "reformists" and the leadership, the actual work of governance is being pushed to the sidelines. The outcome of Choi’s disciplinary hearing will likely determine whether the DPK can regain its footing or if it will slide further into a cycle of internal recrimination.
