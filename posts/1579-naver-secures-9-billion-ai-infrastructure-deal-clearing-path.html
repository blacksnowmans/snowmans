Naver Secures $9 Billion AI Infrastructure Deal, Clearing Path for Nvidia Stake

October 28, 2026

    South Korea's top tech firm Naver has partnered with global asset manager Brookfield for a $9 billion (about $12 trillion KRW) AI infrastructure agreement.
  • The massive deal fulfills the final prerequisite for Nvidia to acquire a stake worth roughly $1.5 billion in Naver, making the U.S. chip giant Naver's third-largest shareholder.
  • The partnership enables Naver to launch a 1GW-class "AI Factory" project, securing massive computing power and physical infrastructure for large-scale AI operations.
  • By tying capital, infrastructure, and hardware together, Naver is pivoting from a domestic search portal into a globally competitive AI infrastructure provider.

The 12 Trillion Won Bet on Physical Infrastructure

Naver's deal with Brookfield marks one of the largest single technology infrastructure investments in South Korea's history. In an era where AI dominance is dictated by physical computing capacity rather than software algorithms alone, Naver has bypassed software-only strategies to secure the raw power required for real-time AI processing.

By locking down a 1GW power-scale infrastructure—roughly equivalent to the output of a nuclear reactor—Naver ensures it won't face the severe hardware and energy bottlenecks plaguing competitors worldwide. Brookfield's willingness to deploy capital validates South Korea's operational and technical capabilities on the global stage, proving that local tech leaders can attract Tier-1 international alternative investment capital.

Cementing the Nvidia Alliance and the Sovereign AI Dilemma

The successful infrastructure pact removes the final hurdle for Nvidia, led by Jensen Huang, to finalize its roughly $1.5 billion equity investment in Naver. Securing Nvidia as its third-largest shareholder gives Naver an immense strategic advantage in securing high-demand graphics processing units (GPUs) and solidifying its position in the global AI supply chain.

However, this heavy reliance on foreign capital and American semiconductor giants raises important questions regarding "Sovereign AI." As Naver integrates deeply with global capital and hardware ecosystems, maintaining local data sovereignty and independent technological leadership under the current Lee Jae-myung administration will require careful balancing. While the financial backing and infrastructure firepower are unprecedented, Naver's long-term success will ultimately depend on its ability to translate this raw capacity into proprietary, high-value software services without losing its independent edge.
