Economic Volatility Stalls President Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Ratings Despite Administrative Wins

November 23, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung’s overall job approval rating held virtually flat last week despite major diplomatic summits and domestic judicial rulings, as economic anxiety from a sharp stock market selloff erased mid-week gains.

  • What happened: President Lee’s approval rating fluctuated by just ~0.1 percentage points across both telephone and automated surveys, peaking at 38.4% on Thursday before a Friday drop in the KOSPI index checked momentum.
  • Administrative success: 65% of respondents positively evaluated the government’s management of the Suneung (South Korea’s national college entrance exam), led by strong approval from voters in their 30s over effective anti-cheating emergency text alerts.
  • Divergent party polls: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea fell 4 percentage points in telephone interview polls but rose 2 points in automated response system (ARS) surveys, while the minor progressive Cho Kuk Innovation Party hit 10.4% in ARS polling.
  • Leadership headwinds: Job performance ratings for Democratic Party Chair Kim Min-seok declined over the past month, remaining below the 70–80% approval levels past party leaders enjoyed among core supporters.

Economic Anxiety Outweighs Diplomatic Momentum

According to poll analysis by Lee Taek-soo, head of polling agency Realmeter, and political commentator Park Si-young, President Lee Jae-myung’s rating stayed flat because opposing forces cancelled each other out. The positive momentum generated by President Lee’s attendance at the ASEAN and APEC summits was undercut by domestic economic shocks and sensitive judicial rulings. When the benchmark KOSPI stock index plunged below the 5,000-point mark on Friday, it immediately halted a Thursday surge toward the 40% threshold. For many working-class South Koreans—particularly younger professionals—shrinking portfolio balances superseded high-level foreign diplomacy, demonstrating how sensitive public sentiment remains to real-economy indicators.

Public Praises Governance over Party Friction

In contrast to tense political metrics, public satisfaction with basic public administration proved remarkably strong. The government’s seamless operation of the high-stakes Suneung exam earned a 65% approval rating, with voters in their 30s reacting especially favorably. Initial public skepticism toward using emergency notification text messages turned into praise after a strategically timed third text alert successfully helped detect exam cheaters. This outcome highlights a growing demand among South Korean voters for pragmatic administrative execution over political theater.

Meanwhile, party preference polling revealed a split landscape. While the combined support of the Democratic Party and the Cho Kuk Innovation Party climbed back above 50%, the ruling party faces clear communication challenges. The drop in telephone interview ratings alongside falling approval for Democratic Party Chair Kim Min-seok suggests that moderate voters are adopting a wait-and-see approach, leaving the administration reliant on maintaining tight, practical management of public services.
