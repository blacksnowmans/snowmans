South Korea Faces Massive Exposure to Potential 2027 U.S. AI Bubble Burst

November 27, 2026

South Korea’s export-reliant economy faces acute vulnerability to any crash in the U.S. artificial intelligence boom, which economists warn could trigger severe domestic asset deflation around 2027.

  • What happened: Economists warn that an unsustainable U.S. tech rally—where U.S. stock market capitalization has reached an all-time high of 382% of GDP—leaves South Korea uniquely exposed to global market shocks.
  • What it changes: U.S. Big Tech firms have spent roughly $1.9 trillion on AI infrastructure against just $970 billion in revenue, raising fears of corporate insolvencies if monetization fails to outpace rising debt costs.
  • Who is affected: South Korean chipmakers and household investors bear the brunt, as U.S. data center cutbacks directly hit memory exports while falling asset prices threaten to further freeze Korea's sluggish domestic consumption.

The Supply-Chain Vulnerability of the KOSPI

Whenever U.S. tech stocks stumble, the KOSPI—South Korea’s main stock index—suffers disproportionately sharp drops. Analysis from Barclays highlights a key structural reason: much of the U.S. data center boom is built on imported components. Consequently, if U.S. tech giants scale back capital expenditure, the direct hit to U.S. GDP will be muted by reduced imports, while Asian semiconductor suppliers like South Korea and Taiwan absorb the primary shock.

While U.S. equity wealth has expanded by $35.4 trillion since the AI surge began, a sharp divergence has opened between hardware suppliers and software adopters. South Korea's SK Hynix and U.S. chipmakers like Nvidia have turned real profits selling high-bandwidth memory and processors, but end-user firms—including Amazon, Microsoft, and OpenAI—remain deep in the red on AI services. If persistent inflation keeps U.S. interest rates elevated, rising capital costs could overwhelm these loss-making AI investments before they become profitable.

Asset Deflation and the 2027 Risk Horizon

Economists like Choi Baeq-keun warn that a single high-profile Big Tech bankruptcy could trigger an AI bubble collapse around 2027, with potential financial spillovers ballooning toward $2 trillion in bad loans. Unlike during the 2008 global financial crisis, when China’s massive stimulus rescued global growth, Beijing is currently trapped in a structural slowdown, leaving the international economy without a traditional buyer of last resort.

For South Korea, an AI downturn carries grave domestic risks. Korean households are heavily exposed to stock and real estate values. If paper wealth erodes alongside semiconductor exports, the resulting asset deflation will drag down consumer spending, creating a volatile financial and political landscape for South Korea around 2027.
