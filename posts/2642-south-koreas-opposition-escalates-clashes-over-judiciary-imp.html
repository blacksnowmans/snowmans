South Korea’s Opposition Escalates Clashes over Judiciary Impeachment as Stock Risks Rise

November 23, 2026

South Korea’s main opposition party has launched aggressive street protests in Daegu against President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, framing the ruling party's push to impeach the Supreme Court Chief Justice as an unconstitutional power grab.

  • Opposition Rallies Against Impeachment: People Power Party (PPP) leader Jang Dong-hyeok condemned the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) for seeking to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, calling the effort "treasonous," while minor opposition Reform Party leader Lee Jun-seok also denounced the move as an attack on judicial independence.
  • Economic Stability Warnings: PPP floor leader Jung Jum-sik warned of potential stock market turbulence ahead of Monday's market opening, urging the government to prioritize financial stabilization over political feuds.
  • Local Realignment Moves: Reform Party leader Lee Gi-in announced a strategic move to run in Yongin to anchor the party in southern Gyeonggi Province's tech belt, while municipal officials lobbied the Assembly for local council legislation and budget increases.
  • Global Extremism Escalation: International far-right rhetoric surged, highlighted by Germany’s Alternative for Germany (AfD) calling for the deportation of up to 2.6 million foreigners and former Israeli military leadership condemning West Bank settler attacks as "Jewish terrorism."

Judicial Clashes Dominating Domestic Agenda

The political standoff in South Korea has reached a boiling point. By mobilizing a large outdoor rally in Daegu—the traditional heartland of South Korean conservatism—the main opposition People Power Party is seeking to rally public outrage against President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea. The DPK’s push to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae has drawn rare consensus across opposition lines, with both conservative and third-party leaders accusing the ruling coalition of attacking the judiciary to delay legal proceedings. In response, ruling party lawmakers have dismissed the opposition's street rallies as destructive political theater.

At the same time, economic volatility is emerging as a central battleground. Opposition floor leaders are tying market uncertainty directly to the administration’s focus on judicial fights, attempting to pressure the government on economic governance and financial stability ahead of upcoming market sessions.

Extremism and Structural Shifts Beyond Seoul

Beneath the national friction, minor political forces are reshuffling their territorial strategies. The Reform Party's push into Yongin signals a deliberate campaign to secure a foothold in key high-tech suburban districts in southern Gyeonggi Province. Simultaneously, regional leaders are pressing ahead with practical demands, visiting the National Assembly to secure local development budgets and advocate for decentralized council laws amidst the chaos in Seoul.

This domestic polarization aligns with a broader global trend of political radicalization. From AfD’s radical deportation plans in Germany to far-right election momentum in France, the UK, and Spain, populist rhetoric is challenging democratic norms. Combined with escalating violence by extremist groups in the Middle East, political discourse both inside Korea and internationally is increasingly being driven by confrontational, hardline factions.
