'Night of Seoul': A New Documentary Captures South Korea’s Martial Law Crisis

November 18, 2026

A new 72-minute documentary titled Night of Seoul offers a raw look at the dramatic events inside and outside South Korea's parliament during the December 3 martial law declaration, connecting the recent crisis directly to historical struggles for democracy.

  • What happened: Directed by veteran editor Cho Chul-young, Night of Seoul documents the tense hours of December 3 when military troops stormed the National Assembly (South Korea's parliament) following a surprise martial law decree by Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • Historical resonance: The film draws explicit parallels to the traumatic memories of the 1980 Gwangju Uprising—a pivotal pro-democracy movement brutally suppressed by a military junta—highlighting how ordinary citizens stepped up to prevent past state violence from repeating itself.
  • Global reach: Promoted by political commentator Park Si-young, the film is screening internationally in cities including Seattle, Sydney, San Diego, Taipei, and Brussels as part of a global "K-Democracy" campaign.
  • What’s next: Cho spent three months editing the rough cut and is already planning a sequel focused on the massive street protests that followed the crisis.

Documenting the Night Democracy Was Tested

When news of the martial law decree first broke on a Tuesday evening, director Cho Chul-young was at home preparing to watch television. Like many South Koreans, his initial reaction was disbelief—assuming the news was a rumor or a absurd mistake by Yoon Suk-yeol. However, vivid footage of soldiers breaking into the National Assembly, blaring helicopters, and citizens using fire extinguishers to hold off troops quickly made the grim reality undeniable. Cho was called into his editing suite the next morning to synthesize the overwhelming wave of footage.

Rather than relying on sensationalism, the 12-and-older rated film focuses heavily on the unnamed citizens and lawmakers who placed their bodies in front of armed troops to ensure parliament could convene and vote down the decree. Featuring a score by prominent film composer Jo Sung-woo, the documentary forces viewers to confront how fragile South Korea's democratic institutions proved to be when challenged by power.

From National Trauma to a Global Message

Journalist Jang In-soo notes in the film that the chaotic aftermath—including the roller-coaster legal proceedings surrounding Yoon Suk-yeol, who was arrested, released, and re-arrested before his eventual impeachment—must not be hastily swept under the rug. Instead, the documentary urges South Koreans to thoroughly examine the structural vulnerabilities that allowed martial law to be declared in the first place.

By taking Night of Seoul overseas to diaspora communities and international audiences, the filmmakers aim to frame South Korea's experience as a universal lesson. As democratic norms face pressure around the globe, Cho hopes the film serves as a testament to the power of civic resistance, demonstrating that democracy is sustained only through active memory and collective action.
