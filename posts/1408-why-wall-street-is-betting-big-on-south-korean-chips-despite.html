Why Wall Street Is Betting Big on South Korean Chips Despite Local Panic

October 22, 2026

Global investment bank Nomura has reaffirmed its bullish stance on South Korea's semiconductor giants, forecasting a structural memory chip shortage through at least 2028 driven by unrelenting AI infrastructure demand.

  • Ambitious price targets: Nomura maintained strong "Buy" ratings, setting target prices of 670,000 won (about $500) for Samsung Electronics, the world's top memory manufacturer, and 4.7 million won (about $3,500) for SK Hynix, South Korea's second-largest chipmaker.
  • Structural supply bottlenecks: Expanding advanced semiconductor packaging and fabrication facilities takes three to five years, locking in tight supply for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and server DRAM.
  • Local market disconnect: South Korea's benchmark Kospi index remains hyper-volatile near the 5,600 mark as exhausted retail investors panic-sell into minor rallies to recover principal.
  • Governance headwind: Foreign institutional investors have dumped SK Hynix shares over fears that a planned U.S. listing of its NAND flash subsidiary, Solidigm, will dilute parent company value.

Fears vs. Fundamentals in the Chip Cycle

The stark divide between Wall Street’s multi-year optimism and Seoul’s battered stock market underscores a deep psychological fatigue among Korean investors. Worn down by choppy markets, local retail traders are treating small price bounces as exit opportunities rather than holding for long-term fundamental growth. Self-inflicted corporate governance issues have only worsened market sentiment—most notably SK Hynix’s push to separately list its lucrative Solidigm unit on the Nasdaq, an action that revives old fears of parent company equity dilution.

Global Tech Spend Guarantees Demand

Despite local anxiety, global fundamentals tell a far more constructive story. Earnings reports from U.S. technology giants demonstrate that the high-performance memory supercycle is far from over. Meta's aggressive capital spending on AI infrastructure recently pushed its free cash flow into negative territory, while Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang warned that a mere three-month delay in data center construction burns through $10 billion (about 13 trillion won) in missed revenue. With Big Tech racing to build AI facilities at any cost, South Korea's memory duopoly holds an indispensable bottleneck position that virtually guarantees robust cash generation for years to come.
