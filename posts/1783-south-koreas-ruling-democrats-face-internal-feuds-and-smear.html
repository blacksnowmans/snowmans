South Korea’s Ruling Democrats Face Internal Feuds and Smear Campaigns Against Reformers

October 31, 2026

Despite consolidating power under the Lee Jae-myung administration following Yoon Suk-yeol’s impeachment, the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is being shaken by bitter internal friction and smear campaigns targeting prominent reformist lawmaker Jung Chung-rae.

  • Internal backlash against reformists: Rivals within the DPK have begun labeling reform-minded leadership members as a narrow personal clique around Jung ("pro-Jung"), a framing supporters argue is designed to stall systemic party innovation.
  • From hero to target: Widely praised within the party when he spearheaded the impeachment drive against Yoon Suk-yeol as chair of parliament's Legislation and Judiciary Committee, Jung has faced sharp attacks and wild rumors from within his own camp since seeking party leadership.
  • Realignment pressure: Stagnant poll numbers have led several DPK lawmakers to urge a formal merger or close strategic alliance with the Rebuilding Korea Party, a staunchly progressive minor party led by Cho Kuk.
  • Grassroots intervention: Highly engaged rank-and-file party members are taking to community forums and public events to debunk internal disinformation, defending reformists against top-down factional sabotage.

From Battlefield Vanguard to Internal Target

The sudden shift in how Jung Chung-rae is treated illustrates the cutthroat nature of South Korean party politics. During the constitutional crisis triggered by Yoon Suk-yeol’s illicit emergency martial law declaration, Jung was lauded as the DPK's frontline fighter, using his post as Legislation and Judiciary Committee chairman to dismantle the administration's defenses. However, the moment internal power struggles began ahead of leadership races, intra-party rivals reframed his sharp political edge as a liability. The resulting friction has unleashed coordinated character assassination, including a bizarre rumor claiming Jung was closely related to the leader of the notorious JMS religious cult—a smear so baseless that even the conservative daily The Chosun Ilbo debunked it by cross-checking family clan records.

Stagnation Fears and the Grassroots Defense

The party's underlying anxiety stems from flatlining support ratings. Although the DPK successfully transitioned into the ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung, lawmakers worry that the window for meaningful governance and reform will close without swift political momentum. This urgency has fueled serious debate over joining forces with Cho Kuk's Rebuilding Korea Party, giving the progressive bloc a stronger mandate to deliver on legislative pledges.

Rather than letting backroom factions dictate terms, however, the party’s rank-and-file are asserting control. Many supporters view the DPK not as a flawless institution, but as the only viable safeguard for South Korea's democracy following the democratic near-collapse under Yoon. By independently refuting fake news and pushing back against factional labels, these grassroots members are functioning as a corrective force, proving that reform within the ruling party is increasingly driven from the bottom up.
