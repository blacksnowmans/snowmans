Beyond the Affair: Why Nvidia’s Board Refuses to Bring Back Jensen Huang

November 22, 2026

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was ousted not merely over an extramarital affair, but after a year of systematically misleading the board, alleged corporate self-dealing, and two decades of severe workplace abuse coming to light.

  • What happened: Nvidia’s board forced out founder Jensen Huang after discovering he concealed an affair for a year and attempted to allocate 40,000 premium H200 AI chips to a firm tied to his mistress, while whistleblowers revealed a 20-year history of verbal abuse and family nepotism.
  • The pushback: Donald Trump and Elon Musk publicly demanded Huang’s return to preserve American tech leadership, but interim CEO Colette Kress and the board firmly ruled out his reinstatement.
  • Market fallout: Nvidia’s stock plunged 14% in a single day and 23% over a week, triggering $1.76 billion in asset liquidations as the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) launched an accounting investigation into "missing" Singapore sales and circular financing schemes.
  • What’s next: Compounded by an 11.5% defect rate in its flagship RTX 5090 GPUs, Nvidia faces its first major trial without Huang during its upcoming earnings report on November 17.

Governance Meltdown Behind the AI Boom

For years, stellar financial performance shielded Nvidia’s leadership from scrutiny. However, Huang's sudden removal has laid bare an autocratic management culture where personal relationships allegedly dictated resource allocations and staff endured relentless verbal abuse. Whistleblower reports describing extreme workplace hostility, alongside criticisms of family members wielding unchecked influence, have painted a stark picture of imperial corporate control behind Silicon Valley's top AI chipmaker.

More troubling for global investors is the expanding SEC accounting probe. Regulators are examining whether Nvidia engaged in round-tripping—investing capital into startups on the condition that they purchase Nvidia chips—to artificially inflate revenue numbers. Combined with questions surrounding revenue routed through Singapore, market trust in Nvidia’s core financials has taken a heavy blow.

Can Nvidia Transcend Its Founder?

The rift between prominent figures like Trump—who advocate for Huang's return—and Nvidia's board highlights a fundamental tension between founder charisma and corporate governance. While political and industry allies argue that Huang’s strategic relationships are irreplaceable in the AI race, the board is gambling that Nvidia’s underlying engineering talent and dominant CUDA software ecosystem can sustain momentum without him.

The upcoming November 17 earnings call will serve as the ultimate test of this transition. If interim leadership can project stability and address accounting concerns directly, Nvidia may successfully pivot from a founder-dependent empire to an institutionally managed market leader. If they falter, the fallout could ripple across the global tech supply chain.
