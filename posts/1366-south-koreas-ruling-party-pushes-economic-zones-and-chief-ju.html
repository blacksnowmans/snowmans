South Korea's Ruling Party Pushes Economic Zones and Chief Justice Impeachment Amid Gaffe Fallout

October 21, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is balancing a high-stakes legislative push for regional industrial development and an aggressive campaign to impeach Chief Justice Jo Hee-de, even as leadership scrambles to contain public fury over a lawmaker's disparaging remarks about firefighters.

  • Gaffe containment: Senior DPK leaders, including Supreme Council Member Kim Min-seok and Floor Leader Han Byung-do, publicly apologized and opened internal ethics inspections after party lawmaker Boo Seung-chan claimed the national fire service "drains state finances dry."
  • Unusual dual-track legislation: To back President Lee Jae-myung’s regional development agenda, the DPK took the rare step of tabling two versions of a mega-regional special zone bill at once—one exempting companies from the statutory 52-hour weekly work cap and one retaining it.
  • Judicial clash: The party confirmed plans to move toward impeaching Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de after the parliamentary audit season wraps up, preparing joint strategy sessions with the minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party despite fierce opposition from the conservative People Power Party (PPP).
  • Local oversight and welfare: The DPK formed an investigative panel to probe potential safety defects and fiscal waste in conservative Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s "Hangang Bus" river ferry project, while successfully pressing the welfare ministry to conduct a nationwide survey of roughly 17,800 vulnerable disabled households.

A Pragmatic Compromise on Labor Rules

The DPK’s decision to submit two contrasting bills for the same economic initiative reflects a delicate tightrope walk. President Lee's administration needs flexible labor regulations to lure cutting-edge manufacturing and technology investments away from the congested Seoul capital region. However, watering down South Korea's strict 52-hour workweek cap risks alienating organized labor—represented by the country's two largest union confederations—and progressive allies like the Rebuilding Korea Party, which immediately demanded the exemption clause be scrapped.

By filing both versions simultaneously, the ruling party is attempting to shift the battlefield from ideological deadlock to committee-level bargaining. Floor Leader Han is betting that parliamentary debate can forge a middle ground between corporate demands for flexibility and labor demands for worker protections without stalling the regional revitalization agenda entirely.

Judicial Pressure Undercut by Unforced Errors

At the same time, the DPK is escalating institutional warfare against the judiciary. Accusing Chief Justice Jo of political bias and compromised judicial independence, the party is laying the groundwork for an unprecedented impeachment vote alongside allied progressives. The main opposition PPP has decried the move as outright intimidation of the courts. Pushing ahead will consume massive political capital and further inflame partisan gridlock in the National Assembly.

That high-pressure strategy makes careless blunders by backbench lawmakers particularly damaging. Rep. Boo’s dismissive remarks about frontline emergency responders hit a raw nerve with a public that holds firefighters in high regard. For a ruling party trying to project competence and defend sweeping governance reforms, having to repeatedly bow in apology for basic discipline failures diverts attention and saps momentum right when legislative scrutiny is peaking.
