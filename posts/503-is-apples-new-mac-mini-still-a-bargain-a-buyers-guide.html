Is Apple's New Mac Mini Still a Bargain? A Buyer’s Guide

September 30, 2026

The latest Mac Mini—featuring the M6 and M5 Pro chips—has left many potential buyers paralyzed by choice, as a significant price hike forces users to rethink whether a compact desktop is still the "value king" it once was.

  • What happened: Apple has restructured its Mac Mini lineup, with base prices jumping from about $650 (₩890,000) for the previous generation to roughly $1,100 (₩1.49 million) today.
  • The dilemma: Upgrading memory and storage on a base M6 model quickly pushes the price into M5 Pro territory, a phenomenon local fans call "the addiction of adding just a little more" (an endless upgrade cycle).
  • The performance gap: While the M6 offers a massive 59% jump in multi-core performance over the M4, the M5 Pro remains roughly 50% faster than the M6 in heavy professional tasks like 4K video rendering and local AI model processing.
  • The takeaway: For most, the base M6 is plenty, but professionals running heavy AI agents or complex video workflows should skip the mid-range upgrades and invest directly in the M5 Pro "class" of hardware.

The "Class" System: Why Specs Don't Always Win

A common trap for buyers is assuming the latest chip is always faster. In real-world testing, an older M2 Max Mac Studio actually outperformed the new M5 Pro in 4K video exports. This highlights a critical reality: hardware "class" often beats chip generation. The memory bandwidth and physical architecture of a "Max" series chip provide a ceiling that software optimization simply cannot overcome.

This is even more apparent in the era of local AI. If you are running Large Language Models (LLMs) like Qwen3 locally, the M5 Pro handles complex, long-context requests significantly faster than the M6. For developers or data analysts who need to keep AI agents running constantly, the M5 Pro’s superior memory bandwidth is not just a luxury—it is a necessity for maintaining a productive workflow.

The Hidden Cost of the AI Race

It is easy to blame Apple for the price increases, but the reality is more systemic. The global surge in demand for high-performance memory chips has created a volatile supply chain. Even a tech giant like Apple is feeling the pressure, and these costs are inevitably passed down to the consumer. We have moved into an era where even the "entry-level" desktop is being marketed as an AI workstation, necessitating higher memory baselines that drive up prices across the board.

Before you hit the checkout button, stop and weigh your actual pain points. If you are currently using an M4-series device and aren't hitting bottlenecks, there is no reason to upgrade. If you are coming from an older Intel-based Mac, the M6 is an excellent starting point, provided you don't over-customize it. Ultimately, the best way to navigate this market is to ignore the "upgrade addiction" and calculate exactly how much your wait time is worth in dollars and cents.
