LG, Defense Ministry, and Startups Lead South Korea's Rapid Push Into Physical AI

November 21, 2026

South Korea’s technology landscape is shifting rapidly from screen-based algorithms to "physical AI," as major conglomerates, government bodies, and startups integrate artificial intelligence directly into robotics, industrial machinery, and defense hardware.

  • Corporate realignment: LG Group, one of South Korea's largest industrial conglomerates, overhauled its executive leadership to put physical AI and hardware-linked AI infrastructure at the front and center of its future business strategy.
  • Defense and disaster deployment: South Korea's Ministry of National Defense showcased urban tactical operations using Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs), while tech firm Bon AI secured a 10.75 billion won (~$7.7 million) state project to field wildfire-detection drones powered by domestic system semiconductors.
  • Global market momentum: Robotics submissions at the CES Innovation Awards jumped 74% year-over-year, alongside global funding moves like Indian startup Humin Labs securing $47 million to harvest physical human action data for robot training.
  • Industrial automation: Local startups are solving complex field challenges, with robotics firm XYZ applying physical AI visual and tactile sensors to sort irregular, deformable items like packaged bread in logistics hubs.

From Software Screens to Industrial Machinery

The turn toward physical AI reflects a major shift in how Korean industrial leaders view artificial intelligence. For years, corporate strategy focused heavily on software interfaces and generative models. LG Group's organizational changes signal that manufacturing powerhouses are moving to embed AI into physical production, consumer electronics, and advanced components. By reorganizing research arms directly around commercial hardware units, Korean industrial groups aim to secure market dominance before global competition around autonomous manufacturing settles.

Tactical Utility and Public Safety Testbeds

South Korea's public sector is serving as a crucial testing ground for physical AI capabilities. Facing severe demographic contractions and military manpower shortages, the Ministry of National Defense is accelerating the adoption of autonomous UGVs for urban search and tactical missions. Simultaneously, state-backed field projects—such as Bon AI’s on-device processing drones—demonstrate how edge computing allows real-time disaster monitoring even in remote areas lacking network connectivity.

This dual momentum from industrial conglomerates and government-sponsored field tests shows that physical AI in South Korea has moved past theoretical demonstration. As logistics firms, material suppliers like Wacker, and local municipalities build out regional hubs and specialized hardware, Korea is attempting to position its manufacturing base as an essential hardware platform for the AI era.
