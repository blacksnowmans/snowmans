How Climate Crisis and Broken Systems Shattered the American Dream for a Popular Korean Creator

October 09, 2026

Popular South Korean YouTube creator Oliver쌤 (Oliverseam) and his wife have published a new book, American Survival, exposing the brutal realities of immigration that shatter the glossy myth of the American Dream.

  • What happened: The couple built a home in rural Texas in 2016 using family loans equivalent to about $70,000 (100 million won), only to watch desertification, vanishing groundwater, and extreme weather like the 2021 Texas freeze force them to abandon it eight years later.
  • The broader reality: The book highlights collapsing U.S. middle-class metrics, skyrocketing mortgage rates, and a notorious healthcare system that failed the author's late father-in-law, who was misdiagnosed with pancreatic cancer.
  • Who is affected: Aspiring immigrants, climate migrants fleeing natural disasters, and middle-class families trapped by soaring living costs and debt.
  • The irony: After escaping climate disaster and a predatory financial system in Texas to move to freezing Minnesota, the authors later returned to South Korea, only to witness another form of grueling survival: children trapped in intense cram school and academic competition until dawn.

When the American Dream Meets Desertification and Debt

The journey detailed in American Survival is a far cry from the leisurely immigrant lifestyle many imagine. With virtually no credit history upon arriving in Texas, the couple relied on high-risk family debt and grueling round-the-clock video editing work to survive. But nature proved even less forgiving than the banking system. Severe groundwater depletion, driven by regional overdevelopment, turned their lush rural Texas haven into an unsustainable drought zone.

Compounding these environmental shocks was America's notoriously punitive healthcare maze. Without accessible primary care—where patients face rigid bureaucracy and months-long waits just to see a specialist—the authors' family suffered a devastating personal loss when a relative's serious symptoms were brushed off by a primary physician until it was too late.

A Universal Story of Resilience

What makes this book resonate isn't just the catalog of American institutional failures—from crumbling water tables to predatory medical billing—but the raw depiction of human endurance. The authors' migration from Texas to Minnesota, driven purely by the desperate need for clean water and water security, underscores how climate change is actively redrawing where and how humans can live.

Ultimately, American Survival offers a sobering corrective to idealized notions of moving abroad. Whether navigating the crunch of American capitalism or returning to South Korea's grueling academic pressure cookers, the book argues that survival today requires ordinary people to band together, accept life's inevitable tragedies, and carve out small pockets of warmth against harsh systemic odds.
