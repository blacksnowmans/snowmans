SoftBank’s Dropped $1 Trillion Bid for SK Hynix Signals HBM’s Extreme Value

November 22, 2026

SoftBank Chairman Masayoshi Son reportedly sounded out Wall Street financial institutions to fund a staggering $1 trillion acquisition of South Korean chipmaker SK Hynix, though SoftBank has officially confirmed the ambitious plan has been shelved.

  • What happened: SoftBank explored an unprecedented $1 trillion (1,350 to 1,400 trillion won) buyout of SK Hynix, South Korea's premier memory chip manufacturer, approaching major Wall Street banks to gauge financing possibilities.
  • Why it stalled: Wall Street responded with extreme caution due to high global interest rates and financial risk, while strict regulatory hurdles over semiconductor national security and antitrust laws made global approval nearly impossible.
  • The official stance: SoftBank formally declared that while it briefly reviewed the proposal in the past, it has completely halted considerations and is not actively pursuing the deal.
  • Who is affected: Global big tech and memory chip makers, as the move highlights how high-bandwidth memory (HBM) suppliers have transformed into vital strategic assets in the artificial intelligence race.

Behind SoftBank’s Mega-Deal Vision

Son’s audacious plan aimed to consolidate a complete AI hardware supply chain. SoftBank already owns chip design firm ARM and is investing heavily in AI infrastructure. However, cutting-edge AI processors and large language models require high-performance memory to function effectively. By acquiring SK Hynix—the global leader in High Bandwidth Memory (HBM)—SoftBank sought to control everything from chip architecture to memory manufacturing, securing a vertical monopoly over the hardware driving the AI boom.

The deal quickly ran into insurmountable economic and geopolitical barriers. A $1 trillion price tag—equivalent to nearly half the market capitalization of South Korea’s entire stock market—proved far too large for Wall Street lenders to finance. Furthermore, SK Hynix serves as a core profit driver for its parent company, SK Group, which had no intention of selling. Even if capital were raised, the South Korean government treats advanced semiconductor technology as a critical national security asset and would not permit a foreign takeover. Antitrust regulators in the US, EU, and China would have similarly blocked the combination of ARM and SK Hynix on monopoly grounds.

Why HBM Memory Has Become a Strategic Asset

Although the acquisition ended as a defunct scenario, the episode sends a clear signal to global markets regarding SK Hynix’s strategic importance. Memory makers, long treated as cyclical commodity suppliers, are now viewed by international tech conglomerates as indispensable partners—or targets—essential to solving AI supply chain bottlenecks.
