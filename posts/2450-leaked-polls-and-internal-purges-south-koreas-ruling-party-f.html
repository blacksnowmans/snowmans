Leaked Polls and Internal Purges: South Korea’s Ruling Party Faces "80-Seat" Meltdown

November 17, 2026

Leaked internal polling suggests the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) could see its parliamentary presence collapse to just 80 seats in the next election, potentially paralyzing the administration of President Lee Jae-myung.

  • Approval ratings have reportedly dipped into the 20% range, a psychological floor that has triggered alarm bells within the party leadership and the Presidential Office.
  • Projections of an "80-seat disaster" are circulating, echoing the party’s 2008 wipeout when it was reduced to 81 seats and spent years in the political wilderness.
  • Party leader Kim Min-seok has ordered a direct internal inspection of Choe Min-hee, a member of the party’s supreme council, a move critics call a heavy-handed purge to enforce loyalty.
  • Factional infighting is threatening the legislative agenda of the Lee Jae-myung government, specifically its signature prosecution reform and economic stability measures.

The Shadow of 2008 and the 20% Approval Floor

The DPK is currently haunted by the number 81. In the 2008 general election, the liberal predecessor to the DPK was decimated, securing only 81 seats and losing control of the legislative agenda for a decade. According to reports from the political YouTube channel "Media Must Change to Change," internal data now suggests a repeat of this catastrophe. While external polls often fluctuate, internal party data—which often uses more precise "virtual number" sampling provided by telecommunications firms—is being treated with much greater gravity by DPK insiders.

This is not merely a matter of prestige. For the Lee Jae-myung administration, a failure to secure a robust majority would effectively end its governing mandate. Without a dominant lead in the National Assembly, the administration’s core policy goals—ranging from housing stability to the finalization of prosecution reforms—will likely stall. The fear within the party is that a "lame duck" period could begin early, turning the DPK into a "long-term opposition party" if it cannot regain public trust before the next vote.

Disciplinary "Direct Inspections" and the Nomination War

Amidst this electoral anxiety, a power struggle between DPK leader Kim Min-seok and Representative Choe Min-hee has broken into the open. Kim’s decision to order a "direct inspection" of Choe is a significant escalation. Unlike a standard review by the party’s ethics committee, a direct order from the party leader signals an intent to impose a heavy penalty. This move is being interpreted by many in the party as a warning shot to those who might challenge the current leadership during the upcoming candidate nomination process.

The timing of this internal crackdown is particularly risky. While the leadership argues that "discipline and unity" are required to overcome falling approval ratings, the optics suggest a party more interested in internal purges than addressing the cost-of-living crisis. For voters in their 30s—a crucial swing demographic—the sight of the ruling party fighting over internal disciplinary procedures while ignoring economic pressures is a recipe for further disillusionment. If the DPK cannot shift the conversation from internal "loyalty tests" back to governance, the 80-seat projection may become a self-fulfilling prophecy.
