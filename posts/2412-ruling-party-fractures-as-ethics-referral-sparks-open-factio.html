Ruling Party Fractures as Ethics Referral Sparks Open Factional Warfare

November 17, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has plunged into severe internal turmoil after party leader Kim Min-seok referred Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee to the party's Ethics Tribunal for disciplinary review, disregarding her high-profile public apology.

  • Public Apology Rejected: Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee issued a dramatic 90-degree bow during a leadership meeting, blaming a "memory error" for controversial remarks that damaged President Lee Jae-myung's standing, but party leadership pressed forward with formal ethics proceedings.
  • Factional Clash Erupts: Tensions between internal party factions boiled over in open leadership meetings, accompanied by counter-accusations involving external religious groups and leaked private chatrooms containing severe personal attacks against Choi.
  • Lawmakers Divided: A group of first-term Democratic Party lawmakers known as "Deomincho" attempted to collect signatures demanding Choi's heavy punishment, drawing sharp pushback from colleagues who warned against pressuring the independent tribunal.
  • Plummeting Support: Public fallout from the escalating internal feud pushed the ruling Democratic Party’s approval rating down to a record low, according to recent broadcast polling reports.

An Apology Fails to Stem Disciplinary Action

Choi’s dramatic bow before party leadership was meant to contain days of damaging media coverage over her controversial statements. By attributing her remarks—which critics argued undermined President Lee Jae-myung’s authority and identity—to a simple memory lapse, Choi sought to appease both the administration and rank-and-file members. However, the icy atmosphere inside the leadership meeting made it clear that apologies would not suffice. Party leader Kim Min-seok immediately reinforced procedural discipline, insisting that the party's Ethics Tribunal conduct an independent investigation strictly according to party rules.

Kim’s decision to pursue formal sanctions rather than a political compromise signals a push to enforce internal discipline, even at the risk of escalating factional friction. Measures under consideration include extending election candidacy bans for those who evade internal audits from five to ten years. Yet with the tribunal expected to reach a decision as early as next week, the strict approach has only intensified backroom hostility.

Leaked Chats and Polling Slump Signal Deepening Crisis

The dispute has rapidly spiraled beyond policy disagreements into outright character assassination. Reports of a leaked private chatroom used by party staff revealed explicit death threats and abusive language directed at Choi, sparking outrage among female lawmakers and rival factions. Meanwhile, junior lawmakers remain split over whether to formally demand Choi’s resignation or allow the ethics process to take its course without political pressure.

For the ruling party, the timing could not be worse. As the administration tries to push through key legislative priorities, open warfare within leadership has badly damaged its public standing. With party approval ratings sinking to record lows, the Democratic Party faces a growing crisis of governance that an ethics ruling alone may not easily resolve.
