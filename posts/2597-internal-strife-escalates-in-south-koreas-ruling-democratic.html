Internal Strife Escalates in South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party Over Impeachment Remarks and Factional Feuds

November 21, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has plunged into severe internal turmoil after party leadership launched an ethics investigation against Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee over controversial private comments regarding President Lee Jae-myung.

  • What happened: Party leader Kim Min-seok referred Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee to the party ethics tribunal after she reportedly claimed in a private meeting that President Lee had previously opposed Yoon Suk-yeol’s impeachment to win moderate voters.
  • The retaliation: Rivals within the party—including former governor Choo Mi-ae and allies of former party chief Jung Chung-rae—demanded Kim Min-seok face political accountability first over unproven smear allegations he raised during the party's leadership election in August.
  • Who is affected: Pro-Jung supreme council members boycotted official leadership meetings to hold a separate press conference, while the Presidential Office issued a formal statement rejecting Choi's claims as completely groundless.
  • What it changes: The clash has exposed deep mistrust over how party discipline and candidate nomination systems are handled, drawing in National Assembly Speaker Woo Won-shik and senior party elders as reluctant mediators.

Factional Warfare Escalates Into Ethics Weaponization

The rift within the Democratic Party of Korea, South Korea’s ruling party, highlights how easily off-the-record missteps can ignite full-scale factional warfare. The crisis began when reports surfaced that Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee told local party members that President Lee Jae-myung—during his time as party chief—had initially opposed impeaching Mr. Yoon in order to attract moderate voters. When party leader Kim Min-seok immediately ordered an ethics probe into Choi, three supreme council members allied with former party leader Jung Chung-rae boycotted the official leadership meeting in protest, holding a rogue press conference instead.

To Choi’s defenders, the leadership’s swift disciplinary action is a double standard meant to shield Kim Min-seok. Critics point out that Kim himself previously spread unproven allegations of a secret alliance involving a controversial religious group during his leadership campaign against Jung Chung-rae, offering only a brief apology months later. With media-cited surveys showing over half of respondents demanding Kim’s resignation over those campaign allegations, Choi's faction argues that Kim’s past tactics caused far greater political damage than a misspoken remark at a private meeting.

Presidency and Party Elders Caught in the Crossfire

The escalating dispute forced the Presidential Office to intervene directly. Senior presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung issued a public message expressing deep regret and flatly denying Choi’s account, clarifying that President Lee consistently advocated for Mr. Yoon's impeachment. While intended to set the record straight on the president’s stance, the presidential statement inadvertently bolstered Kim's disciplinary push, further alienating the party's pro-Jung wing.

Meanwhile, efforts by party elders—including National Assembly Speaker Woo Won-shik—to broker peace by calling on both sides to exercise restraint have drawn sharp backlash from party supporters. Commentators view such calls for compromise as cowardly fence-sitting that avoids addressing root responsibilities. With legislative ethics bodies historically failing to discipline members impartially, many within the ruling party now view ethics probes not as genuine tools for self-cleansing, but as factional weapons deployed to secure dominance over upcoming candidate nominations.
