South Korea Overhauls Daycare Enrollment as November Admission Season Looms

October 27, 2026

South Korea is streamlining its notoriously complex childcare enrollment systems just as regional education offices prepare to kick off next year's kindergarten and daycare admissions in November.

  • Unified system: The government is rolling out an integrated application process, sparing parents from juggling separate, complicated portals for daycare versus kindergarten.
  • Upcoming deadlines: Regional education offices—including in Ulsan and South Gyeongsang Province—will begin priority admissions for the next school year starting November 2.
  • Expanding public options: Local governments are boosting public-backed childcare, such as Gyeongbuk adding 18 new "public-type" private daycares and Iksan expanding its parent-transparent "open daycare" network.
  • Grassroots strain: Despite administrative upgrades, local childcare centers face severe financial pressure from falling birth rates, soaring heating and cooling bills, and lagging teacher compensation.

For parents in South Korea, securing a spot in a reputable daycare or kindergarten has long felt like a high-stakes competitive sport. The push toward a unified, one-stop application portal is a welcome administrative fix that cuts down on redundant paperwork and administrative errors. It is a practical step forward in a country where balancing work and family is already an uphill battle.

Yet these digital conveniences risk masking a deeper crisis in South Korea's childcare ecosystem. As the country's birth rate continues to plunge, private and community daycare operators are fighting for survival against shrinking enrollments and skyrocketing utility costs. While local governments are stepping up by expanding public-private hybrid centers and transparent "open daycares" to reassure parents, centers on the ground warn that without direct financial relief for heating bills and staff wages, administrative tweaks will not be enough to keep their doors open.
