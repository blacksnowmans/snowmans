AI Tech Boom Masks a $1.6 Trillion Debt Bomb

October 28, 2026

While US markets are rallying on the back of explosive artificial intelligence growth, a massive $1.65 trillion mountain of off-balance-sheet debt is quietly accumulating among Big Tech leaders.

  • Tech Surge: The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged 3.6% on Wednesday, October 28, driven by aggressive AI infrastructure spending from companies like Nvidia and Broadcom.
  • Meta’s Strategic Raise: Meta successfully raised $25 billion through a hybrid share offering, choosing to pay a 6.5% dividend to investors rather than taking 9% bank loans.
  • Off-Balance-Sheet Risks: Five major tech giants—Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and Oracle—now carry roughly $1.65 trillion (about 2,190 trillion KRW) in hidden liabilities related to data center leases and chip orders.
  • Geopolitical Relief: Oil prices eased slightly after Mojtaba Khamenei, a prominent figure in the Iranian leadership, signaled a potential desire to end the "neither war nor peace" status quo.
  • K-Shaped Recovery: US economic data shows a widening gap; while consumer confidence is at a five-month high, the housing market remains frozen by high interest rates.

The High Cost of the AI Arms Race

The current market rally is fueled by a "move fast and break things" mentality reminiscent of the late 90s. Tech leaders are pouring billions into AI infrastructure to avoid falling behind, but the financial mechanics behind this growth are increasingly precarious. The $1.65 trillion in off-balance-sheet debt—an eightfold increase over the last four years—represents future obligations for data centers and AI processors that haven't even been delivered yet. While CEOs argue that the resulting technology will justify the cost, capital markets are beginning to show signs of hesitation as the weight of this debt grows.

Meta’s recent funding round provides a masterclass in creative capital management. By issuing a mix of common and preferred shares, they bypassed traditional high-interest bank debt. This kept their balance sheet looking "cleaner" while securing $125 billion in total demand. However, this strategy relies entirely on shareholder trust. If the AI "dream" fails to translate into immediate earnings, the 6.5% dividend promised to preferred shareholders could become a heavy anchor rather than a clever shortcut.

Shifting Hegemony and Currency Volatility

Beyond Silicon Valley, the global financial landscape is shifting. The Chinese Yuan is showing unexpected strength, and China has now overtaken Japan as the second-largest holder in the US Treasury market. For export-heavy economies like South Korea, this multi-polarization of currency power is a double-edged sword. While it reduces absolute dependence on the US dollar, it introduces new layers of volatility that traditional hedging strategies are not equipped to handle.

The "K-shaped" nature of the current economy means that while the Nasdaq celebrates, the average consumer is struggling. Housing prices continue to slide, and manufacturing remains stagnant. The broader market is essentially riding on the coattails of five or six tech giants. If the AI bubble reaches an inflection point where the cost of debt exceeds the rate of innovation, the correction will not be limited to tech; it will pull down a global economy that has become dangerously reliant on a single sector’s success.
