South Korea Implements Drastic Penalties for AI Cheating on College Entrance Exam

November 19, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party and the government have announced a sweeping crackdown on AI-assisted cheating for the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT), introducing a “one-strike-out” policy that includes a 10-year ban on taking the exam and up to five years in prison.

  • Aggressive Penalties: Cheating with AI or high-tech devices now carries a 10-year ban from future exams and potential criminal prosecution, including prison sentences.
  • Institutional Consequences: University students caught facilitating cheating for others face immediate expulsion and the revocation of their admission.
  • Whistleblower Incentives: Authorities are finalizing a plan to offer up to 100 million won (about $71,000) for reports leading to the discovery of organized cheating rings.
  • Increased Oversight: 8,400 additional proctors will be deployed across testing centers to monitor for sophisticated, AI-driven devices.

A Shift Toward Zero Tolerance

The Democratic Party, South Korea’s governing party, and the administration of President Lee Jae-myung moved with urgency just one day before the exam to address growing public anxiety over tech-enabled fraud. By classifying the use of AI in exams not just as a disciplinary breach but as a criminal offense, the government intends to shift the societal perception of "cheating" to that of a serious crime. The proposed "Anti-Cheating Act" aims to close legal loopholes that have allowed high-tech gadgets to evolve faster than educational oversight.

However, the rapid rollout of these measures—specifically the plan to retroactively apply the 10-year ban starting with this year’s exam—has triggered debate among legal experts. Critics point to the constitutional principles against retroactive punishment, arguing that while the intent to preserve the sanctity of the CSAT is clear, the implementation risks being challenged in court. Furthermore, the 11th-hour nature of the announcement has drawn criticism from parents and students who feel the government’s reactive stance highlights a decade-long failure to address the proliferation of micro-surveillance and communication devices.

Balancing Security and Fair Access

While the crackdown focuses on the integrity of the exam, the government is also facing pressure to ensure that these measures do not disproportionately affect vulnerable groups. Local officials have raised concerns that strict AI-usage bans could inadvertently penalize students with disabilities who rely on assistive technologies. In response, authorities have pledged to develop clear guidelines to distinguish between illegal cheating devices and legitimate medical or educational aids.

Ultimately, the effectiveness of these measures will depend on the government's ability to move beyond temporary, headline-grabbing solutions. As the Democratic Party pushes for long-term legislative reforms, the public remains focused on whether the state can genuinely secure the exam process without infringing on the rights of students or relying on erratic policy shifts that emerge only on the eve of the test.
