Internal Feuds and Sectarian Smears Roil South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party

November 08, 2026

Former lawmaker Choi Kang-wook has exposed explosive behind-the-scenes infighting within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), revealing that party leader Park Sun-won privately pleaded for mercy after launching baseless rumors linking party rivals to a controversial fringe sect.

  • The smear: During a high-stakes party primary, DPK Supreme Council member Park Sun-won leveled unverified allegations connecting internal rivals to Shincheonji, a secretive fringe religious movement widely viewed in South Korea as a cult, citing crowd-sourced web encyclopedias as his evidence.
  • The confession: Former lawmaker Choi Kang-wook disclosed phone logs showing Park privately begging him to drop the issue the following day, admitting he had no proof and was merely floating claims fed to him by shadowy party figures.
  • The backlash: High-profile progressive commentators, including author Rhyu Si-min, publicly rebuked Park for debasing political norms, while critics pointed to violations of the National Assembly’s ethical standards that continue to go unpunished.
  • Who is affected: The ruling DPK faces intensifying scrutiny from its base over opportunists who posture as staunch defenders of President Lee Jae-myung’s administration while sabotaging party solidarity for personal advancement.

Opportunism Behind the Mask of Presidential Loyalty

The Shincheonji dispute illustrates a cynical survival strategy inside Yeouido, Seoul’s legislative district. Candidates deploy reckless black propaganda to eliminate competitors in primary races, only to grovel behind closed doors when their claims collapse under public scrutiny. Park’s plea to Choi to "save him" demonstrates how internal power struggles are conducted without conviction, ideological grounding, or factual verification.

This tactic closely mirrors past factional sabotage within the ruling camp. When party representative Jung Cheong-rae proposed an opposition-unifying merger, rival factions launched smear campaigns falsely claiming the move defied the presidential office's agenda. In both instances, political operatives hid behind the supposed authority of President Lee Jae-myung, only to quietly retreat into silence once the truth emerged.

The Rising Cost of Campaign-Trail Reformers

The revelation highlights a broader systemic failure within the governing party: candidates who campaign as fierce champions of judicial and prosecutorial reform often become legislative obstacles once safely in office. Key figures who vigorously demand reform before the cameras routinely backtrack, tone down party bills in committee, or vanish when decisive votes are cast.

Meanwhile, attempts by veteran politicians such as Rep. Park Jie-won to stifle internal critique in the name of safeguarding the Lee Jae-myung administration only deepen the problem. Shielding careerist lawmakers from voter accountability degrades the ruling party's moral authority. Unless the DPK establishes mechanisms to discipline legislators who manufacture slander, factional infighting will continue to hamstring the government’s reform agenda from within.
