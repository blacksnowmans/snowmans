Black Snowman Brief

A Leaked Notebook Exposes Internal Rifts in South Korea’s Main Opposition

September 10, 2026

A caught-on-camera moment in the National Assembly has blown the lid off the increasingly bitter power struggles within the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country’s main liberal opposition party. A notebook held by Kim Min-seok, a senior DPK official, has revealed a calculated list of potential candidates for the Seoul mayoral race, signaling a move by the party’s pro-Lee Jae-myung faction to consolidate power and sideline internal rivals.

  • The Reveal: The notebook, clearly captured by cameras, contained a list of names for future Seoul mayoral candidates, including senior party figures like Chung Chung-rae and Woo Won-shik.
  • The Strategy: Analysts interpret the list as a "political hit list" designed to force potential rivals into a difficult, high-stakes race—essentially setting them up for a fall to solidify the pro-Lee faction's control.
  • The Fallout: The revelation has alienated traditional DPK supporters, who feel the party is prioritizing internal purges and "political engineering" over public service and democratic consensus.

The Anatomy of a Political Maneuver

The display of the notebook is widely viewed as a deliberate, high-level maneuver. By placing the names of party stalwarts like Chung Chung-rae—who has publicly disavowed any interest in the Seoul mayorship—on the list, the leadership is effectively marking its territory. For critics, this is a classic "poisoned chalice" tactic: if a non-aligned or rival politician is forced into a losing race, the blame for the defeat can be pinned on them, clearing the path for the dominant faction to cement its grip on the party apparatus.

The incident has already sparked public backlash, with Chung Chung-rae hitting back on social media, accusing the leadership of "cruel" political gamesmanship. Meanwhile, National Assembly Speaker Woo Won-shik’s appearance on the list has fueled speculation that he is maneuvering for a future Prime Minister appointment, drawing parallels to past political precedents where Assembly speakers transitioned into the executive branch. This suggests that the party is no longer functioning as a collaborative entity, but rather as a collection of shifting interests focused on personal survival and succession.

Eroding Trust and Fading Support

The DPK’s internal obsession with power dynamics is taking a toll on its base. Traditional supporters, particularly in key regions, feel increasingly sidelined and branded as "anti-Lee" simply for questioning the current leadership's direction. This disconnect is exacerbated by administrative scandals, such as the recent controversy over a policy video produced by an individual previously known for using derogatory language toward former President Moon Jae-in. The party’s refusal to act decisively on such issues has fostered a culture of bureaucratic inertia and arrogance.

Current polling reflects this growing disillusionment. Recent surveys indicate that the President’s approval ratings are struggling, with a significant portion of the public feeling that there is no positive policy direction to support. By focusing on internal squabbles and the calculated removal of political rivals, the DPK is squandering its mandate. Unless the party shifts its focus back to the lived concerns of the public and abandons its "small-minded" power plays, the arrogance currently on display will likely serve as the catalyst for its own undoing in future elections.
