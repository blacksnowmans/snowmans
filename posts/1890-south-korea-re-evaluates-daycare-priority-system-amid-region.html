South Korea Re-evaluates Daycare Priority System Amid Regional Disparities

November 03, 2026

South Korea’s Ministry of Education is launching a nationwide investigation into the priority scoring system for daycare admissions to determine whether automatic extra points given to multicultural families create inequities for working parents and multi-child households.

  • Scoring System Review: The Ministry of Education will evaluate if current multicultural preference policies disproportionately disadvantage other vulnerable groups, considering shifts toward income- and need-based criteria.
  • Admissions Pitfalls: As applications open for the 2027 academic year, officials warned parents that using the online portal’s "bookmarking" feature does not constitute a completed registration.
  • Stark Infrastructure Gap: Public childcare access shows extreme regional disparity, with Seoul’s affluent Seocho District opening its 100th public daycare while remote locations like Yokji Island have no childcare facilities at all.
  • Integration Progress: Local authorities are rolling out unified early education programs, such as survival swimming lessons in Sejong, under the national initiative to merge kindergarten and daycare oversight.

Balancing Fairness in Daycare Admissions

The Ministry of Education’s decision to review preferential daycare points for multicultural households touches on a sensitive issue in South Korean family policy. Originally designed to assist social integration, critics argue that the uniform point system grants priority regardless of a household's actual economic situation or language proficiency. In competitive urban districts, this has left dual-income working parents and families with multiple children stuck on long waiting lists. Reforming the system to weigh actual care deficits and income levels rather than broad demographic categories is a necessary step toward genuine fairness.

At the same time, parents navigating the newly opened admissions cycle for the 2027 academic year face administrative hurdles. Municipal authorities have reported recurring instances of parents mistaking a portal "save" or "bookmark" option for a final submission, inadvertently losing their place in the fierce race for public daycare spots. As competition remains intense in major cities, clearer digital guidance is urgently needed.

The Growing Urban-Rural Childcare Divide

The current landscape highlights a glaring contrast in South Korea's childcare infrastructure. While Seoul's Seocho District recently celebrated opening its 100th municipal daycare through public-private partnerships in major residential developments, rural island communities such as Yokji Island in South Gyeongsang Province lack a single operating daycare. Rapid rural depopulation has triggered a wave of facility closures, leaving remote families completely cut off from basic early childhood care.

Following budget cuts and policy turbulence during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's term, the Lee Jae-myung administration has pushed to restore public childcare accountability and integrate early childhood education systems. However, bridging the gap between thriving metropolitan hubs and disappearing rural communities remains a formidable challenge. Ensuring that public funding prioritizes non-viable rural regions will be essential to guarantee equitable childcare access nationwide.
