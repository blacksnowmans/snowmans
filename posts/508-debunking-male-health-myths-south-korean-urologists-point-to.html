Debunking Male Health Myths: South Korean Urologists Point to Data on Prostate Care and Performance

September 30, 2026

Contrary to popular online abstinence trends, regular ejaculation—at least 21 times a month—can reduce prostate cancer risk by nearly 30%, according to urological research highlighted in South Korea.

  • What happened: Medical evidence shared by South Korean urological sources debunks widespread myths regarding sexual frequency, anatomical expectations, and dietary supplements.
  • Why it matters: Prostate cancer is rising rapidly in South Korea—projected to become the country's second most common male cancer by 2034—driven in part by sedentary lifestyle habits.
  • What the data shows: Anatomical girth is routinely more significant than length for partner perception, while popular performance fixes like caffeine and oral L-arginine offer little benefit for erectile function.
  • Who is affected: Men navigating sexual health concerns, those considering costly cosmetic surgeries, and prospective fathers preparing for conception.

The Science of Prostate Care and Physical Habits

A global study of over 150,000 men cited by urologists reveals that ejaculating 21 or more times per month lowers prostate cancer incidence by roughly 30% compared to ejaculating four to seven times. While extreme claims promoting strict abstinence circulate on online forums, medical professionals warn that prolonged restriction allows waste and inflammatory substances to accumulate, potentially triggering congestive prostatitis. Beyond ejaculation frequency, sedentary behavior poses a major threat: sitting for extended periods directly compresses the prostate. Taking a short walking break every hour can restore over 90% of local blood flow and reduce strain.

Targeted exercise also plays a critical role in managing premature ejaculation and pelvic health. Urologists emphasize that proper Kegel exercises require both contraction and controlled relaxation of the pelvic floor muscles, rather than continuous tightening. When paired with lower-body workouts and cardio, pelvic muscle training significantly improves control and vascular health.

Anatomy Realities, Performance Stress, and Sperm Quality

Anatomical anxiety remains widespread, but historical data from South Korean military studies places the average erect length at approximately 13.4 centimeters (about 5.3 inches). Medical experts note that girth matters far more than length for partner satisfaction, advising against unnecessary enlargement surgeries that cost between 3 million and 6 million won (about $2,200 to $4,500) and carry risks of complications like lymphedema or nerve damage. Furthermore, childhood obesity during puberty can halve male hormone secretion, causing buried penis conditions that are best addressed through weight management rather than surgical intervention.

For young men experiencing performance issues, roughly 70% to 80% of erectile dysfunction cases in their 20s and 30s are psychogenic, driven by performance anxiety rather than physical defects. Quick fixes like pre-encounter caffeine actually constrict blood vessels and worsen dysfunction, while oral L-arginine is largely metabolized in the gut and liver before taking effect. Finally, couples planning pregnancy should note the 74-day sperm development cycle; lifestyle choices made 100 days prior directly impact sperm quality, and hair-loss medications like finasteride should be managed carefully due to potential impacts on motility and fetal safety.
