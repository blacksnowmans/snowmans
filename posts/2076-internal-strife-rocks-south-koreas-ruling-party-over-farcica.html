Internal Strife Rocks South Korea’s Ruling Party Over "Farcical" Cult Allegations

November 07, 2026

Kim Min-seok, the chairman of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), is facing intense backlash and calls for his resignation after presenting what fellow lawmakers describe as "farcical" and outdated evidence attempting to link a political rival to a controversial religious sect.

  • The Accusation: Kim presented "secret" documents to DPK lawmakers alleging that former party leader Jung Chung-rae has ties to Shincheonji, a religious group widely regarded as a cult in South Korea.
  • Flimsy Evidence: The "proof" consisted of expired member lists from 2015–2021 and bizarre claims, such as the color of a person’s clothing and the design of a local government logo resembling the sect’s symbols.
  • The Whistleblower: Investigations revealed that the key informant, a Shincheonji elder named Guk Yong-ho, is a core member of a 2,800-person private chat room dedicated to supporting Kim Min-seok’s political career.
  • Political Fallout: Lawmakers within the DPK have expressed "deep humiliation," accusing Kim of using "dictatorship-era" smear tactics to manufacture a scandal against his own party colleagues.

A "Secret" File of Outdated Data and Fashion Critiques

The controversy erupted during a private general assembly of the DPK, the party currently led by President Lee Jae-myung. Kim Min-seok attempted to frame the disclosure as a major security revelation, allowing lawmakers to view the documents for only 30 minutes under tight security. However, those who saw the files reported that the data was "stale," dating back to the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol (who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment). The "evidence" failed to show any current or meaningful link between Jung Chung-rae and the religious group.

The absurdity of the claims reached a peak when Kim’s team cited "fashion choices" as proof of cult membership. They argued that a local businessman was a Shincheonji member because he "frequently wears the sect's colors." Furthermore, Kim’s camp claimed the official logo of the Mapo District Office—a major administrative district in Seoul and Jung’s political stronghold—was suspicious. In reality, that logo was designed by brand expert and former lawmaker Sohn Hye-won and has no connection to any religious organization. Lawmakers leaving the session were heard saying that if the presentation was a joke, it wasn't funny, and if it was serious, it was grounds for Kim's resignation.

The "Self-Staged" Plot and Moral Bankruptcy

The most damaging aspect of the scandal is the revelation that the entire narrative may have been a "self-staged" political hit. The primary whistleblower, Guk Yong-ho, was not an independent witness but an active participant in a massive KakaoTalk chat room used to coordinate support for Kim Min-seok. This chat room also reportedly included other high-profile DPK figures like Park Sun-won and Lee Un-ju. The fact that Kim used a member of his own support group to feed "tips" to media outlets, only to then use those reports as "official evidence" in a party meeting, suggests a coordinated effort to bury a political rival.

This incident marks a low point for the DPK’s internal politics. While the Lee Jae-myung administration focuses on national reform, Kim Min-seok appears to have reverted to the "politics of fear" and character assassination. By weaponizing the public’s inherent distrust of Shincheonji without substantive proof, Kim has not only defamed a colleague but also undermined the procedural integrity of the ruling party. Critics argue that if the DPK is to maintain its moral authority, it must treat this "internal cancer" of fake news and manufactured scandals with the same severity it applies to its political opponents.
