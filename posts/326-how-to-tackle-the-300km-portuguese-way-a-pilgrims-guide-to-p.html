How to Tackle the 300km Portuguese Way: A Pilgrim’s Guide to Packing Light

September 07, 2026

  • Prioritize weight: Aim for a pack under 7kg (approx. 15 lbs) to prevent injury and prioritize comfort over carrying "just in case" gear.
  • Blister defense is survival: A multi-layer sock strategy, proper gaiters, and immediate medical care for blisters are essential to finishing the journey.
  • Flexible planning: Don’t over-schedule your daily distances; let your body dictate your pace to avoid chronic strain.
  • Financial ease: Budget about 1 Euro per kilometer, utilizing local ATMs and essential apps rather than carrying excessive cash.

The Santiago de Compostela pilgrimage—the historic network of paths leading to the Cathedral of Santiago de Compostela in Spain—is as much a test of logistics as it is a spiritual journey. Having walked 800km (about 500 miles) in 2019, I learned that success isn't about romanticizing the path; it’s about mechanical survival. Your feet are your only transport, and your backpack is your weight-bearing burden.

For my upcoming 300km trip on the "Portuguese Way" (a popular, slightly shorter route through Portugal and Spain), I have overhauled my gear. Gone are the heavy hiking poles and the "everything but the kitchen sink" mentality. By pairing a lightweight, 420g sleeping bag with a minimalist approach to electronics and clothing, I have trimmed my pack to 7kg. The goal is simple: if it doesn't serve a daily survival purpose, it doesn't make the cut.

Managing the Physical Toll

The most common reason pilgrims cut their trip short is not a lack of willpower, but simple physical breakdown. Ignoring pain to meet a daily "goal" of 25km (approx. 15 miles) is a recipe for plantar fasciitis or knee injuries. I have adopted a "listen to the body" rule: if the body needs rest, you take it. The journey is not a race for a completion certificate; it is a process of clearing out internal clutter by clearing out external baggage.

Preparation for the terrain also means preparing for the environment. I’ve budgeted 450 Euros (approx. $500) for the trip, relying on local ATMs to keep cash on hand low. For navigation and connectivity, I secured 40GB of data via Movistar, a major Spanish telecommunications provider, for about 13,000 KRW (approx. $15). In the blistering midday sun, the strategy is to start at 7:00 AM and aim to finish 70% of the day’s trek before noon.

Ultimately, the pilgrimage is about the "Buen Camino"—the communal spirit shared between walkers. Whether you are staying in albergues (low-cost hostels specifically for pilgrims) or navigating the Meseta plateau, the path is a space for connection. My advice to those preparing: pack light, respect your feet, and remember that the most important thing you find on the road isn't a stamp in your passport—it's the ability to find contentment in the simplicity of a single step.
