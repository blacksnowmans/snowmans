Anthropic’s IPO Warning Cements South Korea’s Memory Grip on AI

October 28, 2026

Anthropic’s initial public offering filing has formally identified high-bandwidth memory (HBM) supply bottlenecks from South Korea’s Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix as a core operational risk, confirming that Korean memory makers now wield structural leverage over the global artificial intelligence boom.

  • The bottleneck admitted: Anthropic cited acute shortages of cutting-edge HBM alongside high concentration at contract chipmaker TSMC as existential threats, conceding that server deployments cannot proceed without sustained allocations from Korean producers.
  • Historic earnings, muted equities: Projected combined quarterly operating profits for Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix touched a staggering 184 trillion won (around $135 billion) amid HBM4 momentum, yet domestic stock valuations lag due to capex exhaustion and investor fatigue over shareholder returns.
  • Demand horizons to 2035: SK Hynix’s order pipeline reportedly stretches years ahead, while surging enterprise demand and future robotics workloads threaten to extend tight supply conditions through 2027.
  • A diverging technology race: While US competitor Micron Technology plans to bypass traditional 4F² DRAM cell layouts in favor of wafer-to-wafer bonding and 3D DRAM, Samsung and SK Hynix are maintaining a dual-track approach to protect high-volume yields.

From Commodity Cyclical to Strategic Choke Point

The disclosures in Anthropic’s listing paperwork confirm a structural transformation in enterprise computing. In previous hardware cycles, memory semiconductors were treated as fungible commodities subject to painful pricing gluts and sudden downturns. Today, specialized memory has become the primary physical barrier to scaling frontier AI models. With even dominant consumer tech giants like Apple reportedly being turned away on bulk discount requests, Korean fabrication lines have emerged as the market's ultimate price setters.

The Profit Paradox and the 2027 Hurdle

Despite South Korea's total HBM exports surpassing 50 trillion won (about $37 billion), domestic equity investors face a striking disconnect. While operational profits reach historic milestones, capital markets are demanding concrete dividend distributions and buybacks rather than perpetual capital spending. Executives, however, cannot afford to pause: sustaining leadership requires pouring astronomical sums into next-generation HBM4 packaging, yield stabilization, and the upcoming transition from planar shrinking to vertical 3D DRAM architectures.

The tension will only intensify as frontier computing expands beyond data centers into humanoid robotics and physical edge systems. Unless alternative fabrication ecosystems reach commercial scale, global AI model developers will remain hostage to Korean production schedules for the foreseeable future.
