Kim Hyun-ji: The Shadow Power Behind South Korea's President

October 06, 2026

Kim Hyun-ji, a lifelong aide to South Korean President Lee Jae-myung, has emerged as the most powerful and controversial figure in the current administration, despite spending decades avoiding the public eye.

  • Who she is: A former low-level civic activist who has worked alongside President Lee for 28 years, moving from local 성남 (Seongnam) grassroots campaigns to the center of national power.
  • What changed: Her ascent from administrative roles to high-level presidential office has triggered fierce political battles, with opposition lawmakers accusing her of dodging legislative oversight.
  • Why it matters: Leaks and internal party slips have exposed a deep rift between official presidential staff and Kim's inner circle, fueling public distrust over invisible influence in government staffing.

Kim’s political journey began in 1998 as a junior clerk for a civic group in Seongnam where Lee—then a local lawyer—was active. Over the next two decades, she managed local administrative projects and stood by Lee through contentious legal battles, including a 2004 dispute over a municipal hospital that resulted in a fine for Lee. When Lee became Seongnam mayor and later Gyeonggi provincial governor, Kim followed as a core strategist, eventually entering the presidential Blue House as Chief of General Affairs before moving to head the First Presidential Private Secretariat.

Despite her extensive administrative footprint, Kim has consistently drawn fire for dodging legislative scrutiny. Opposition parties have repeatedly criticized her strategic lateral moves—such as shifting roles right before national audits—as a shield to avoid parliamentary questioning. Controversies, including a leaked open mic from a former Democratic Party official who casually remarked that "the hiring line is Kim Hyun-ji," have only deepened public suspicion that official titles mask her true, unchecked sway over government personnel and policy.
