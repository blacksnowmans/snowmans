South Korea Expands Lifelong Learning and AI Training for Middle-Aged Workers

October 28, 2026

    Local governments and public bodies across South Korea are ramping up practical re-employment and digital training programs for middle-aged and older adults.
  • Seoul’s Guro district and the Seoul 50 Plus Foundation are directly linking adult education courses to actual job placements.
  • Regional education offices, such as in Sejong, are offering hands-on tech classes covering artificial intelligence, robotics, and AI agents for older job seekers.
  • Provinces like Gyeongsangnam-do and Jeollabuk-do are establishing dedicated centers to handle career transitions and local retention outside the capital region.
  • Critics argue that local governments must move beyond superficial festivals and vanity projects to ensure these programs deliver measurable employment outcomes.

South Korea is aggressively overhauling its adult education infrastructure as the country confronts rapid demographic aging and intense technological disruption. No longer viewed as mere leisure or hobby classes, lifelong learning has become an economic survival mechanism. Public agencies and local governments are racing to bridge the digital divide for older generations who face the dual challenge of post-retirement income gaps and a fast-digitizing job market.

While local authorities like Guro-gu and regional education offices are rolling out innovative curricula—ranging from basic digital literacy to advanced AI tool handling—experts emphasize that execution is everything. Policymakers must ensure that state-backed training pipelines translate directly into sustainable jobs rather than ending up as short-term bureaucratic checkboxes. Building a robust public safety net for second-half careers is essential to overcoming regional economic stagnation and fixing the public infrastructure neglected during the turbulent years of the disgraced former administration under imprisoned ex-leader Yoon Seok-yeol.
