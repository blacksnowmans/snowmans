Inside South Korea's Ruling Party Infighting and Falling Approval Ratings

September 08, 2026

  • Senior presidential secretary Hong Ik-pyo has dismissed rumors of a government ban on politicians appearing on specific progressive media channels, reaffirming President Lee Jae-myung’s core philosophy of political unity.
  • South Korea's ruling Democratic Party is facing severe internal friction, fueled by aggressive online attacks from radical supporters targeting dissenting voices within their own camp.
  • President Lee Jae-myung’s administration is battling sliding approval ratings driven by a mix of political instability, policy missteps—such as past tax and ETF controversies—and tone-deaf public communication.
  • The Blue House is weighing a shift toward direct communication from the president to bypass polarized online channels and rebuild trust with mainstream voters.

South Korea's progressive ruling circle is currently caught in a toxic loop of falling approval ratings and vicious internal warfare. As online political commentators and hyper-partisan YouTubers increasingly dictate the terms of engagement, mainstream politicians find themselves weaponizing language against their own allies rather than focusing on governance.

The situation highlights a widening chasm between the pragmatic demands of running a country and the radicalized echo chambers of core party supporters. When political figures spend more time labeling and mocking fellow party members than debating policy, it poisons the public arena and alienates ordinary voters who simply want competent economic management.

Ultimately, the Lee Jae-myung administration's political fortunes will depend on its ability to enforce internal discipline, shed its defensive posture, and return to the basics of inclusive leadership. Without a deliberate shift toward policy substance and measured dialogue, the ruling camp risks self-destruction from within.
