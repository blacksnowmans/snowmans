Progressive Rebuilding Korea Party Gains Momentum as Ruling Democratic Party Struggles with Internal Feuds

November 17, 2026

South Korea’s minor progressive Rebuilding Korea Party has broken the 10 percent polling threshold on a strong reform mandate, exposing strategic hesitation and internal friction within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea.

  • Polling Surge: The Rebuilding Korea Party reached 10.1% overall—and 17.5% among voters in their 50s—in a recent EveryResearch poll, driven by its uncompromising stance on reform.
  • Diplomatic Row: Tokyo denied entry to former party leader Cho Kuk despite an official university invitation, relying on a reported 200-page dossier targeting South Korean figures focused on historical justice and sovereignty over Dokdo.
  • Impeachment Push: The Rebuilding Korea Party is pushing to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae over judicial conduct during Yoon Suk-yeol's term, while ruling party leadership hesitates with calls for further investigation.
  • Internal Party Friction: Democratic Party of Korea leader Kim Min-seok triggered backlash after ordering an ethics probe against lawmaker Choi Min-hee without prior formal procedures, followed by pressure from freshman lawmakers.

Diplomatic Backlash and the Case for Progressive Competition

The entry refusal of Cho Kuk in Japan underscores Tokyo’s ongoing practice of blacklisting South Korean political figures who take a firm stance on historical justice and territorial sovereignty over Dokdo (islets claimed by Japan as Takeshima). Rather than adopting a low profile, the Lee Jae-myung administration must maintain diplomatic reciprocity and demand a formal explanation from Tokyo to protect South Korean representatives and sovereign dignity abroad.

Domestically, the minor party's double-digit polling reflects strong demand among voters in their 50s for clear-cut reform. Premature floating of merger ideas by Democratic Party of Korea (DPK, South Korea's ruling party) leadership only risks diluting the minor party's distinct identity and confusing supporters. The broader progressive bloc operates best through healthy competition: the Rebuilding Korea Party setting a sharp reform agenda, and the ruling DPK using its majority to deliver legislative results.

Judicial Accountability and Ruling Party Missteps

Tensions between the two progressive parties are equally evident in their approach to judicial reform. While the Rebuilding Korea Party calls for the immediate impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae for impeding judicial integrity during Yoon Suk-yeol's term, DPK leadership remains stuck on proposing legislative inquiries. Hesitating when sufficient cause for constitutional action exists risks squandering momentum for real judicial oversight.

At the same time, the ruling DPK is dealing with self-inflicted internal conflict. DPK leader Kim Min-seok’s sudden ethics inspection of Representative Choi Min-hee—amplified by a joint statement from 55 freshman DPK lawmakers demanding her resignation—violates procedural fairness and compromises the independence of the party's ethics tribunal. To maintain public trust and secure stable governance, the ruling party must halt internal purges and focus on coherent, unified reform.
