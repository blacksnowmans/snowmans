Ruling Party Civil War: The High-Speed Purge of Choi Min-hee

November 17, 2026

A high-speed disciplinary probe against a top Democratic Party official has ignited a fierce internal power struggle between factions supporting Party Representative Kim Min-seok and those loyal to former party leadership.

  • Choi Min-hee, a member of the ruling Democratic Party’s Supreme Council, faces potential expulsion for claiming President Lee Jae-myung once opposed the impeachment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • The Blue House (the presidential office) issued a rare, swift rebuttal, labeling Choi’s remarks "groundless" just 90 minutes after media reports surfaced on November 12.
  • Party Representative Kim Min-seok ordered an ethics probe only 40 minutes after the Blue House statement, forwarding the case for formal punishment within 72 hours.
  • Allies of Choi argue the move is a political frame-up designed to distract from Kim’s own recent scandals involving false accusations regarding a controversial religious sect.

A Slip of the Tongue or a Political Opportunity?

The core of the dispute involves remarks Choi allegedly made during a private meeting in Namyangju, a city east of Seoul. According to leaked transcripts, Choi suggested that President Lee Jae-myung had initially been against the impeachment of his predecessor, Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. In the current political climate—where Mr. Yoon is a disgraced figure currently in prison—suggesting the President was ever sympathetic to him is a serious blow to the administration's "pro-reform" image. Choi has since apologized, calling the remark a "memory lapse" regarding the specific timing of the impeachment movement.

However, the unprecedented speed of the party’s response has raised eyebrows. While the Democratic Party leadership claims it is merely "establishing discipline," the faction loyal to former leadership sees a blatant double standard. They point to Representative Kim Min-seok’s own recent conduct: during the party leadership race, Kim accused rivals of forming a "secret alliance" with Shincheonji, a religious sect often viewed with suspicion in Korea. Despite admitting on November 3 that there was no evidence for this claim, Kim faced no internal disciplinary action. For Choi’s allies, the three-day turnaround on her probe looks less like justice and more like a factional purge.

The Battle for Party Control

This internal friction comes at a sensitive time for Representative Kim, who is approaching his 100th day in office. Critics suggest he is using the Choi incident to consolidate power and "cleanse" the party of opposing voices before the next election cycle. The tension has already paralyzed the party's top decision-making body; several Supreme Council members boycotted a meeting on November 13 to protest what they called the "malicious framing" of Choi. They argue that by turning a private conversation into a public scandal, Kim is effectively staging a "coup" against the party members who elected Choi to her post.

The outcome of this probe will be a litmus test for the Democratic Party of Korea (the country's main liberal ruling party) and its internal democracy. If the Ethics Committee (the party’s internal disciplinary body) moves toward a heavy-handed punishment, it could trigger a wider revolt among the grassroots base. Many local party members in Namyangju have already signed petitions supporting Choi, arguing that the leadership is ignoring the context of her remarks to score factional points. For a party currently holding the presidency, this level of public infighting suggests that the "united front" under President Lee is increasingly fractured by internal rivalries.
