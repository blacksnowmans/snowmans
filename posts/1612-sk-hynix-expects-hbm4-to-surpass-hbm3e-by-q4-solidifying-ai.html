SK Hynix Expects HBM4 to Surpass HBM3E by Q4, Solidifying AI Memory Lead

October 28, 2026

SK Hynix expects its next-generation High Bandwidth Memory, HBM4, to overtake its current flagship HBM3E in both shipment volume and revenue share by the fourth quarter of this year.

  • What happened: SK Hynix, South Korea's leading memory chipmaker, revealed in response to investor inquiries that its HBM4 production yields have exceeded internal targets, prompting the company to raise its yield goals while reporting that HBM3E also surpassed mature yield benchmarks.
  • The pipeline: The chipmaker confirmed it already delivered HBM4E samples to customers in the second quarter and is actively developing specialized "custom HBM" tailored to individual client workloads.
  • What it changes: Rather than an extended transition between generations, SK Hynix is forcing an aggressive generational crossover, demonstrating mastery over difficult advanced packaging processes.
  • Who is affected: Global AI accelerator customers, including Nvidia, secure faster delivery of denser next-generation memory, while rival chipmakers face an even wider technological gap in the AI supply chain.

Process Yields and the Custom Memory Shift

The core bottleneck in High Bandwidth Memory has never been just raw fabrication; it is the brutal complexity of back-end packaging. Stacking thinned DRAM dies, bonding micro-bumps, and running Known Good Stated Die (KGSD) tests frequently ruin margins if treated as disconnected manufacturing steps. SK Hynix attributed its yield outperformance to managing the entire back-end pipeline as a single integrated flow. Exceeding planned yields on HBM4 before full volume deployment gives the company both cost leverage and pricing power in upcoming contract negotiations.

At the same time, the memory business is shedding its identity as a standardized commodity market. By distributing HBM4E samples in the second quarter and co-designing custom HBM solutions for hyperscalers, SK Hynix is embedding itself directly into its clients' multi-year silicon roadmaps. Customization makes memory sticky: once a client tunes their proprietary AI architecture to a specific supplier's bespoke memory interface, switching suppliers becomes prohibitively expensive.

The Strategic Takeaway

Past memory cycles were defined by commodity price wars and sudden gluts. HBM has flipped that dynamic into a high-margin, high-precision engineering race where speed dictates market control. By pushing for a fourth-quarter crossover and proving stable yields ahead of schedule, SK Hynix is ensuring that competitors chasing its HBM3E footprint will find the goalposts moved before they can catch up.
