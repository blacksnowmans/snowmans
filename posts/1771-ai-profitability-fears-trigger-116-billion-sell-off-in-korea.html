AI Profitability Fears Trigger $1.16 Billion Sell-off in Korean Chip Giants

October 31, 2026

Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix saw their share prices crater on October 30 as the "Alphabet Shock" fueled global skepticism over AI returns, despite memory chip prices reaching record highs.

  • Massive Capital Flight: The KOSPI, South Korea’s benchmark stock index, tumbled 3.48% as foreign investors dumped roughly $1.16 billion (1.6 trillion won) in a single day.
  • Chip Giants Hammered: SK Hynix, a critical supplier of High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) for AI processors, plunged over 7%, while industry leader Samsung Electronics fell more than 3%.
  • Profitability Doubts: The rout followed earnings from Alphabet, Google’s parent company, which sparked fears that the massive capital expenditures (CAPEX) required for AI might not yield immediate returns.
  • Price Paradox: The sell-off occurred even as PC DRAM fixed-contract prices hit an all-time high of $27.50 in October, driven by a supply squeeze.

The Disconnect Between Markets and Factories

The current market behavior represents a massive "risk-off" move by global funds rather than a reflection of local business fundamentals. While investors are panicking about the long-term sustainability of the AI boom, production lines in Korea are struggling to keep up with demand. Samsung and SK Hynix have shifted so much capacity toward AI-specific server chips that standard PC DRAM is now in a severe shortage, with prices expected to rise another 13% to 18% in the fourth quarter.

The "Alphabet Shock" has effectively hit a nerve in the market, amplifying fears that the AI investment cycle is peaking. However, the Lee Jae-myung administration and local industry experts view this as a temporary sentiment-driven correction. While companies like Intel have seen catastrophic drops due to weakness in the traditional PC market, Korean manufacturers are heavily hedged toward the high-end AI infrastructure that Big Tech firms are still fighting to control.

The Chinese Threat and Future Outlook

A more structural threat is emerging from China. CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies), China’s leading DRAM manufacturer, reported a staggering 613% year-on-year revenue increase in the third quarter, reaching approximately $37.7 billion (269 billion yuan). As Samsung and SK Hynix pivot their resources to HBM and premium DDR5 chips, Chinese firms are aggressively filling the vacuum in the "legacy" or general-purpose memory market. This creates a two-front challenge: defending the high-end against Western skepticism while fending off Chinese expansion in commodity chips.

Ultimately, the record-high prices for memory products suggest that the industry's engine is still running hot. The divergence between record-breaking profits and collapsing stock prices is a classic market anxiety. For the Democratic Party-led government, maintaining the stability of the semiconductor sector—which forms the backbone of the Korean economy—will depend on whether these firms can maintain their technological lead in HBM3E and enterprise-grade storage (eSSD) before the projected AI investment plateau in 2028.

"The extreme gap between record-breaking DRAM prices and plummeting stock prices is an illusion created by market impatience. The noise of the stock market must be separated from the reality of the production line."
