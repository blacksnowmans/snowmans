Micron’s "Perfect" Earnings and the Reality of the Memory Market

October 02, 2026

Memory chip giant Micron Technology’s latest earnings report has silenced skeptics regarding AI demand, proving that the semiconductor sector remains the beating heart of the South Korean stock market.

  • Record-breaking results: Micron reported $54.2 billion in revenue, handily beating market expectations of $51.0 billion, with an impressive 87% adjusted gross margin.
  • AI Infrastructure: Sales for AI data centers surged 11-fold compared to the previous year, underscoring the structural demand for high-performance chips.
  • Market Reaction: Despite the stellar performance, some investors fixated on a minor projected dip in margins (from 87% to 86.25%), labeling the peak in a narrative of "Peak-ron"—a cynical take that ignores the company’s massive growth trajectory.
  • Impact on Korea: As the global memory market's "little brother," Micron's success bodes well for South Korean titans Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, suggesting their own fundamentals are stronger than bearish sentiment implies.

Beyond the Earnings: The 2031 Horizon

The core takeaway from Micron’s report isn't just this quarter's success, but the company's explicit confidence in long-term demand. While analysts were once worried about the immediate future, industry leaders and global investment banks are now projecting that the AI-driven demand cycle will remain robust through at least 2031. The semiconductor industry is moving away from the "boom-and-bust" cycles of the past; chips are now the essential fuel for a global digital economy.

For South Korean investors, the focus must shift. With astronomical earnings already expected, the market’s next test for Samsung and SK Hynix is their commitment to shareholder returns. As large-scale stock buyback programs (a key support for stock prices) approach their conclusion in mid-October, there is some anxiety regarding short-term supply and demand. However, this is simply a transition back to a market driven by core fundamentals. The era of relying on artificial price supports is ending; it is time for these companies to prove their value through consistent shareholder-friendly policies.

Global Risks: Oil and the "Shadow" Credit System

Outside of the semiconductor sector, the global landscape remains volatile. While U.S.-led "ship-to-ship" transfers of crude oil have helped global exports recover to about 80% of pre-conflict levels, this is an expensive, inefficient, and temporary fix. With China expected to return to the global market as a major buyer, oil prices face renewed upward pressure. Any further escalation by Iran—especially if they perceive the current "bypass" operations as a loss of control—could lead to direct attacks on regional energy infrastructure, once again destabilizing the global supply chain.

Domestically, a darker issue persists in the shadow of the financial sector. The Saemaul Undong Cooperatives—a network of community-based financial institutions under the loose supervision of the Ministry of the Interior and Safety—are reportedly still forcing third-party joint guarantees on subcontractors. In the world of Project Financing (PF), if a construction firm cannot meet lending requirements, they often coerce smaller subcontractors into signing these guarantees under the threat of losing their contracts. It is a predatory practice that exploits small businesses, leaving them vulnerable to the collapse of real estate projects they never truly controlled.
