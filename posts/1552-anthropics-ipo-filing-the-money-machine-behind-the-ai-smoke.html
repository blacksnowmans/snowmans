Anthropic’s IPO Filing: The "Money Machine" Behind the AI Smoke and Mirrors

October 27, 2026

Anthropic’s S-1 filing has shattered the "AI bubble" narrative, revealing a business model with explosive revenue growth and elite-tier profit margins that suggest generative AI is becoming a massive cash generator.

  • Revenue surge: Quarterly revenue skyrocketed from $370 million in early 2025 to approximately $16.8 billion by Q3 2026.
  • Elite margins: The company maintains a 85% gross profit margin on its core inference services, proving the underlying business is highly profitable.
  • Accounting illusions: A headline-grabbing $80.7 billion (approx. 106 trillion won) net loss is primarily due to non-cash accounting valuations, not a lack of cash flow.
  • Supply chain security: Long-term infrastructure commitments provide a "revenue floor" for partners like SK Hynix (the world’s leading AI memory maker) and Samsung Electronics.

Unmasking the 85% Margin "Money Machine"

For months, the global market has been haunted by "AI fatigue," with skeptics questioning if companies like Anthropic could ever monetize their expensive technology. The 289-page SEC filing puts those fears to rest. While the surface-level operating margin appears slim at 8%, the gross profit margin (GPM) sits at a staggering 85%. This means that once the massive cost of training a model is finished, the actual cost of selling "tokens" to businesses is remarkably low. Anthropic is essentially a high-margin software business disguised as a capital-intensive hardware play.

The perceived "losses" are a matter of conservative accounting. Unlike some firms that spread out development costs over years, Anthropic expenses its massive R&D—nearly $10 billion in the first half of 2026—immediately. This is similar to a K-pop agency spending heavily on trainees; the "loss" is actually an investment in a future star. Once the "frontier" models like Claude move from the training phase to the usage phase, the profit transition will be exponential. For Korean investors heavily exposed to NVIDIA and SK Hynix, this filing is the strongest evidence yet that the demand for AI infrastructure is backed by real, high-margin revenue.

Accounting Noise and Governance Risks

The $80 Billion Illusion

Media headlines focused on a "106 trillion won" ($80.7 billion) net loss, but the reality is less dire. Approximately $72 billion of that figure consists of non-cash "valuation losses" on convertible bonds. As Anthropic’s private valuation soared, the accounting value of the debt held by early investors also rose, which must be recorded as a loss under U.S. GAAP rules. In reality, Anthropic even recorded a modest operating profit of $974 million in Q2 2026. The company is far from a sinking ship; it is a victim of its own success in the eyes of accountants.

The "Founder Control" Catch

Despite the stellar financials, the filing reveals significant governance risks. The seven co-founders have consolidated 50.1% of the voting power through a dedicated "Founder LLC." Additionally, a "Long-Term Benefit Trust" holds the power to appoint a majority of the board to ensure the AI remains "safe for humanity." While this may be noble, it leaves public shareholders with almost no say in how the company is run. This "dual-class" structure is common in Silicon Valley but remains a point of contention for institutional investors who prefer standard corporate oversight.

The transparency provided by this filing is a breath of fresh air for the markets, particularly compared to the economic volatility seen during the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol (the former president who was impeached and is currently imprisoned). Under the current President Lee Jae-myung, the focus has shifted toward fostering a more stable and transparent capital market. Anthropic’s move to "open the books" aligns with this global trend toward clarity, providing a clear roadmap for the AI infrastructure sector that fuels much of South Korea’s export economy.
