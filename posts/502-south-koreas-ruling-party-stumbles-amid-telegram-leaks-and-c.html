South Korea’s Ruling Party Stumbles Amid Telegram Leaks and Cabinet Scandals

September 30, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party faces a mounting political crisis, driven by a leaked Telegram message involving a top party official, repeated cabinet appointment failures, and a widening rift with its core base.

    What happened: Leaked Telegram messages from Democratic Party Chairman Kim Min-seok revealed internal discussions about party reform and appointments just before a high-profile ministerial nominee resigned, triggering an ongoing fifth cabinet failure for the administration.
  • What it changes: President Lee Jae-myung’s approval ratings have slipped into the 30% range, and the ruling party’s lead over the conservative opposition has vanished within the margin of error.
  • Who is affected: The administration, core liberal supporters feeling alienated by leadership arrogance, and moderate voters drifting toward the conservative People Power Party.

Leaked Messages and Cabinet Failures Expose Ruling Party Disarray

The leak of Chairman Kim Min-seok’s Telegram exchange laid bare an internal disconnect within the Democratic Party just as the administration's latest ministerial nominee, Kim Seung-won, was forced to step down. Instead of addressing the scandal or explaining the breakdown in vetting, party leadership chose silence. This wall of quiet has alienated loyal supporters who previously formed the bedrock of the party’s electoral victories, while centrist voters have grown wary of perceived legislative overreach.

Adding to the friction, prominent liberal figures and commentators have begun publicly rebuking the administration's tone-deaf responses. Pundit Rhyu Si-min used a "stone in a shoe" metaphor to criticize how minor internal nuisances and hardline factions are hobbling the president’s path forward. Rather than accepting the critique, Chairman Kim responded with dismissive wordplay, arguing that small stones can grow into giant boulders—a defensive maneuver that did little to soothe public frustration.

Arrogance Over Strategy Alienates the Democratic Base

The core vulnerability of the current liberal strategy lies in a dangerous political gamble: neglecting the party's core base—often referred to as the jip-tokki, or "house rabbits"—in a blind pursuit of centrist voters. While expanding political reach is necessary for any governing party, treating long-term loyalists as a captive audience guarantees backlash when policies falter. President Lee's recent rhetoric regarding judicial reforms and dismissals of constructive criticism have further alienated the very people who act as the administration's political shield during crises.

Recent polls from agencies like Gallup Korea show that the conservative opposition is closing the gap not because of their own stellar performance, but because the ruling party is actively mismanaging its coalition. When leadership responds to plummeting approval and public policy missteps with semantic debates rather than genuine reflection, they risk losing the foundational trust required to govern effectively.
