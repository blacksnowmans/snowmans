Rhyu Si-min Sounds the Alarm for President Lee Jae-myung

November 02, 2026

Rhyu Si-min, a heavyweight intellectual and key influencer for South Korea’s liberal camp, has issued a stark warning to the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, arguing that the government is facing a structural crisis of isolation despite being the ruling power.

  • What happened: Rhyu Si-min used a high-profile broadcast to analyze the falling approval ratings of President Lee, which have stagnated at around 37% while disapproval has soared past 60%.
  • What it changes: This marks a shift from blind defense to "strategic criticism" by the liberal elite, signaling that the President’s traditional base is losing patience with his current leadership style.
  • Who is affected: The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country’s ruling liberal party, and its internal factions who are increasingly divided between "loyalist" fanatics and moderates.
  • The "Lifeboat" Effect: Rhyu noted that the rising popularity of the Rebuilding Korea Party—a minor liberal party led by former Justice Minister Cho Kuk—acts as a "lifeboat" for DPK supporters who are disappointed with the President but cannot bring themselves to join the conservative opposition.

A Warning Against the "Echo Chamber"

Rhyu Si-min’s intervention is significant because he has spent years as one of President Lee’s most effective public defenders. However, he now argues that the President’s most aggressive supporters are actually his greatest liability. Rhyu warned that these "pro-Lee speakers" are isolating the President by attacking anyone within the liberal camp who offers a different opinion, labeling them as "traitors" or "betrayers." According to Rhyu, this narrow-mindedness is shrinking the President’s political ground, turning him into a leader of a faction rather than a leader of the nation.

Rhyu dismissed claims from conservative media that he has turned "anti-Lee." Instead, he described his critique as "loyal advice" (chung-eon). "If I were truly anti-Lee, I wouldn't stop at this level of criticism," Rhyu stated, implying that his goal is to save the administration from the same fate as his predecessor. He compared the current situation to the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was eventually impeached and is now serving time in prison, warning that a failure to read the public's "mood of reversal" could lead to a similar collapse in legitimacy.

Integrity as a Political Tool

To bolster the weight of his advice, Rhyu has maintained a distance from professional politics and financial gain. During the broadcast, which drew over 1.5 million views, Rhyu refused his appearance fee and directed approximately $25,000 (35 million won) in viewer donations to be used for program production and supporter events rather than his own pocket. By positioning himself as a full-time writer rather than a political operative, Rhyu aims to provide a moral compass for a base that feels increasingly alienated by the government’s policy stagnancy and internal bickering.

His analysis suggests that President Lee must move beyond "policy results" and address the "people and path" of his administration. Rhyu’s "three layers" of polling analysis—People, Path, and Policy—argues that even if policies are sound, the public will reject them if they do not trust the "people" delivering them or the "path" the government is taking. For a ruling party currently facing a motivated People Power Party (the main conservative opposition), Rhyu’s message is clear: broaden the tent or watch the supporters drift away to smaller, more radical alternatives.
