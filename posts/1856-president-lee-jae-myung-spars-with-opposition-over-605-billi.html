President Lee Jae-myung Spars with Opposition Over $605 Billion Budget

November 02, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung has ignited a fierce political confrontation with the conservative opposition by using a blunt Buddhist metaphor to dismiss criticisms of his recent diplomatic tour, just as the National Assembly begins debating a record-breaking 821 trillion won (about $605 billion) budget.

  • President Lee Jae-myung fired back at the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main conservative opposition party, regarding his overseas trips by stating, "To the eyes of a pig, only pigs are visible."
  • The administration has submitted a massive 821 trillion won (about $605 billion) budget for next year, prioritizing R&D and social safety nets, which the PPP has vowed to cut by over $22 billion (30 trillion won).
  • Internal divisions within the PPP are deepening as leadership figures like Rep. Jang Dong-hyeok explicitly ruled out the return of Han Dong-hoon, a key associate of the imprisoned Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • While the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) defends the President’s "diplomatic exhaustion"—citing reports of him receiving IV drips and suffering nosebleeds—the opposition dismisses these accounts as mere media play to justify "tax-wasting" trips.

The "Pig" Metaphor and the Diplomacy War

The President’s choice of words—invoking a famous proverb suggesting that one's perception of others reflects their own nature—marks a significant escalation in rhetoric. By labeling the opposition's criticism as "pig-like," Lee is signaling a refusal to engage in what the DPK views as petty political sabotage of national interests. The ruling party argues that unlike the "diplomatic disasters" of the Yoon Suk-yeol era, Lee’s grueling schedule is a necessary response to a volatile global supply chain and security environment. For Lee's supporters, this directness is a refreshing departure from traditional political hedging; for the opposition, it is proof of a "distorted" view of state affairs.

The PPP, led by figures like Rep. Jeong Jeom-sig, has countered by attacking the President’s personal conduct, even mockingly suggesting his fatigue was due to late-night social media use rather than diplomatic hard work. This race to the bottom in political discourse highlights a total breakdown in bipartisanship. The DPK has responded by reminding the public of past scandals where Yoon Suk-yeol reportedly brought his own alcohol on the presidential plane, framing the current PPP attacks as blatant hypocrisy from a party that failed to maintain basic decorum while in power.

A Record Budget and Conservative Purges

The 821 trillion won (about $605 billion) budget is the administration’s primary tool for implementing its "Basic Society" vision. By focusing on R&D and energy transition, the DPK aims to jumpstart a sluggish economy. However, the PPP’s threat to slash $22 billion from the proposal sets the stage for a grueling winter session. The opposition's "populism" label is a standard play, but it lacks teeth when they simultaneously struggle with internal identity crises and a fringe element still obsessed with debunked election fraud conspiracies regarding early voting.

Perhaps most telling is the PPP’s internal realignment. Rep. Jang Dong-hyeok’s firm "no" to Han Dong-hoon’s return suggests a desperate attempt to excise the ghost of the Yoon Suk-yeol administration from the party’s future. By pivoting toward figures like Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, the conservative leadership is trying to transition from "pro-prosecutor" brawling to "policy-oriented" governance. Yet, as long as the party entertains far-right rhetoric and refuses to acknowledge the legitimacy of the current administration’s diplomatic efforts, their path back to public trust remains obstructed by the very "pig-eyed" cynicism they accuse the President of harboring.
