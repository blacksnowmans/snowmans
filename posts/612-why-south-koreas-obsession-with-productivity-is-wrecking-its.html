Why South Korea's Obsession with Productivity Is Wrecking Its Sleep

September 10, 2026

  • The core issue: South Korea's hyper-fast culture, late-night smartphone use, and constant pressure to stay productive have fueled a national sleep crisis.
  • The science: Dreams are not premonitions or mystical messages, but the brain's data-optimization and creative problem-solving mechanism.
  • The genetic factor: True "short sleepers" — people who function perfectly on minimal rest due to a rare genetic mutation — make up just 1% of the global population and cannot be replicated by willpower.
  • The fallout: Obsessing over rigid sleep schedules or trying to force unnatural habits creates severe stress, while tech addiction and artificial lighting continue to shatter natural circadian rhythms.

South Korea's relentless pace has turned rest into a luxury and sleep into an afterthought. From dawn-to-dusk work culture to late-night scrolling fueled by the world's fastest digital networks, the nation's collective bedtime is shrinking. While sleep-aid apps and wellness supplements are booming into a massive commercial market, the underlying fix isn't chemical — it's cultural.

Society often treats sleep as a negotiable asset or a sign of weakness, harking back to industrial-era attitudes where downtime was viewed as wasted time. Yet large-scale health data reveals that irregular sleep patterns and chronic deprivation dramatically elevate risks for cognitive decline and dementia. Treating sleep as an enemy to conquer, rather than a biological necessity to respect, is a losing battle against human biology.
