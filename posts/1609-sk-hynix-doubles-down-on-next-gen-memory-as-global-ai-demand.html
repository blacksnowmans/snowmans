SK Hynix Doubles Down on Next-Gen Memory as Global AI Demand Proves Durable

October 28, 2026

SK Hynix, South Korea’s second-largest memory chipmaker and a crucial supplier to global artificial intelligence hardware leaders, is accelerating its fourth-quarter production of high-margin memory products to capture surging, long-term AI infrastructure spending.

  • Ramping next-gen DRAM: SK Hynix is prioritizing high-value products, including 10-nanometer-class fifth-generation DDR5, LPDDR5X for AI servers, and HBM3E, while aiming to reach full production capacity at its M15X fabrication plant by the end of the year.
  • Expanding advanced NAND: The company plans to raise 321-layer NAND flash to over 50% of its domestic domestic production capacity in the fourth quarter, driving shipments of high-capacity enterprise solid-state drives (eSSDs) for data centers.
  • Diversifying customer base: Demand for AI memory is broadening beyond a few Big Tech hyperscalers to include "sovereign AI" investments by national governments and corporate on-premises deployments.
  • Solidifying financial returns: Global cloud service providers are registering 30% to 80% year-over-year revenue growth alongside expanding contract backlogs, demonstrating that infrastructure spending is generating real commercial returns.

Beyond the Hype: Why the AI Chip Rally Has Legs

Skepticism surrounding the sustainability of AI capital expenditures ignores the structural shift taking place across global data infrastructure. Capital expenditure growth among global cloud service providers is projected to exceed 60% between 2022 and 2027, driven by an AI infrastructure market expanding at a 40% annual rate through 2030. Earlier market concerns over reliance on a small cluster of tech conglomerates are receding as national governments launch sovereign AI initiatives and enterprises build out dedicated private systems.

SK Hynix’s Q4 Execution Strategy

For SK Hynix, seizing this structural shift requires expanding its competitive advantage beyond DRAM into high-performance storage. Converting more than half of its domestic NAND production to 321-layer technology reflects a broader realization: modern AI workloads require fast storage to complement raw compute power. High-capacity enterprise SSDs are becoming essential for managing the sheer scale of training and inference data.

By accelerating the ramp-up of its M15X fab and preparing for the transition toward next-generation HBM4, SK Hynix is positioning itself to lead the next memory cycle. As AI technology transitions from software tools to physical applications like robotics and autonomous systems, advanced memory hardware will remain the indispensable backbone of global tech infrastructure.
