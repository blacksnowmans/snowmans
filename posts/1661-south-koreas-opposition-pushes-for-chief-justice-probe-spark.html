South Korea's Opposition Pushes for Chief Justice Probe, Sparking Constitutional Clash

October 29, 2026

South Korea’s political landscape is plunging into a severe institutional crisis as the ruling Democratic Party pushes to launch a parliamentary investigation into Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-de, triggering fierce resistance from the conservative opposition.

    What happened: Building on recent legal actions including the additional detention of Kim Keon-hee, the liberal Democratic Party is fast-tracking a parliamentary inquiry into the judiciary aimed at early December. The core conflict: The ruling party argues the judiciary must be held accountable for past decisions, while the conservative People Power Party condemns the move as an assault on the separation of powers and a form of intimidation against the courts. New flashpoints: Tensions escalated further following allegations that ruling party lawmakers checked the Chief Justice's phone replacement history, prompting opposition accusations of illegal surveillance. Who is affected: The standoff threatens to paralyze legislative work and deepens public fatigue as both sides double down on partisan warfare rather than economic or livelihood issues.

A Risky Showdown Over Judicial Independence

The latest confrontation exposes a dangerous blurring of lines between South Korea's legislative and judicial branches. For the Democratic Party, the push for a parliamentary probe is framed as a necessary reckoning to correct what it views as a compromised and unaccountable court system. Having secured momentum from high-profile legal rulings, the ruling bloc appears convinced that backing down now would surrender their leverage for sweeping judicial reform.

Yet the strategy carries heavy political risks. Internal divisions are already showing, with some moderate voices within the ruling party urging caution to avoid a severe voter backlash. Recent polling indicating that over 50% of the public opposes impeaching the Chief Justice suggests that the relentless attacks on the judiciary risk alienating crucial moderate voters who are growing weary of endless political bickering.

Stalemate and the Search for Meaningful Reform

For the conservative People Power Party, defending the judiciary is an existential baseline. Labeling the ruling party's actions as "legislative bullying" and a violation of the constitutional separation of powers, the opposition has fought back with maximalist rhetoric. However, the opposition's defensive posture also leaves little room for constructive engagement, reducing the entire discourse to a zero-sum turf war.

As the December target date for the parliamentary investigation report approaches, the risk of a hollow, partisan circus looms large. If the inquiry proceeds without genuine evidentiary backing or descends into a toothless spectacle, it will achieve little beyond deepening public cynicism. Democracy requires holding powerful institutions accountable, but when oversight mechanisms are weaponized purely for political survival, the foundational principles of governance are the ultimate casualties.
