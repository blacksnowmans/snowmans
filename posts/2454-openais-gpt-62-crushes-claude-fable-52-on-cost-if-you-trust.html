OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Crushes Claude Fable 5.2 on Cost, If You Trust the Benchmarks

November 18, 2026

OpenAI’s new GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol models match or beat Anthropic’s flagship Claude Fable 5.2 on major agentic benchmarks at less than a tenth of the price, overturning the long-standing trade-off between AI capability and API cost.

  • What happened: On Agents’ Last Exam v1.1, GPT-6.2 Astra scored 77.4% for $4.47 and the lighter GPT-6.2 Sol hit 73% for $1.32, both beating Claude Fable 5.2’s 72.8% at $60; Astra also led Terminal-Bench Science 0.1 at 77.1% ($7.15) versus Fable 5.2’s 73.3% ($45).
  • Pricing shift: GPT-6.2 Astra trims input and output token prices by 20% from GPT-6 Astra and introduces a 60% discount on cached inputs, while GPT-6.2 Sol increases how much work subscribers can complete within existing paid plans.
  • Where Anthropic held on: Claude Fable 5.2 still edged out Astra on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0.1 (64.6 points to 62.1), though that 2.5-point lead cost $26.64 compared with Astra’s $4.
  • Who is affected: Engineering teams building multi-step AI agents can now route routine tasks to $1-tier models and run repeated verification loops without blowing up their monthly bills.

Why the numbers need a reality check

The collapse in inference costs is real, and it changes how software teams architect AI products. When multi-step reasoning loops fall from tens of dollars to a few bucks, developers no longer have to treat every API call like scarce fuel—they can use GPT-6.2 Sol as a daily workhorse and reserve Astra for complex edge cases. With major model release cycles shrinking from six to twelve months down to mere weeks, hardcoding a product around a single expensive flagship model is rapidly becoming a liability.

Still, buyers should read these leaderboards with healthy skepticism. Vendor-selected benchmarks frequently diverge from everyday performance: Terminal-Bench Science 0.1 is an early-stage test, and startup Datacurve recently revealed with its DeepSWE benchmark that the widely used SWE-bench Pro misgrades roughly 32% of submissions due to flawed verifiers—and even left git commit histories exposed so older models could look up answers.

The bottom line for buyers

Even if real-world gaps prove narrower than the launch charts suggest, OpenAI has shifted the industry’s center of gravity from raw capability to unit economics. Rather than chasing whichever model holds a two-point lead on a public leaderboard this month, companies should build model-agnostic pipelines and benchmark GPT-6.2 against their own internal data before switching.
