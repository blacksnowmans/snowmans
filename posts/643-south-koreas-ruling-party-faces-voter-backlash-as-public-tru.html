South Korea’s Ruling Party Faces Voter Backlash as Public Trust Plummets

September 10, 2026

    South Korea's ruling Democratic Party faces a severe voter backlash and a sharp drop in presidential approval ratings, driven by core supporters feeling used and discarded.
  • President Yoon Suk Yeol's successor, President Lee Jae-myung, has seen his administration's approval ratings hit a "dead cross" after five consecutive weeks of decline.
  • Longtime progressive supporters report feeling alienated and attacked by party insiders and hardline pro-Lee factions.
  • Young voters and critics accuse the party of abandoning its moral high ground for raw, unmasked political ambition and opportunism.
  • Internal party elections have exposed deep fractures between factions, overshadowing the administration's policy agenda.

The Crisis of Betrayal Among Core Supporters

The greatest threat to a political movement is not a failed policy, but the emotional alienation of voters who realize they were used as mere tools for someone else's ambition. South Korea's liberal base, which spent years and personal fortunes helping the Democratic Party take power, now finds itself targeted by its own party's inner circle. Dedicated supporters who question the administration's direction are swiftly branded as internal enemies or anti-Lee factions, drawing sharp attacks on social media and party platforms.

This erosion of trust has dealt a heavy blow to the party's foundation. Where there was once a shared sense of democratic idealism, there is now bitter disillusionment. The party's new online community spaces, meant to foster grassroots democracy, have instead devolved into battlegrounds where aggressive fandoms hurl insults and division. As approval ratings plunge across all age groups—most notably among voters in their 40s, 50s, and 60s—the ruling camp's failure to address basic economic pain, such as tightening real estate markets, only fuels public anger.

The Rise of Raw Ambition and Youth Cynicism

Young South Koreans, long critical of political hypocrisy, view the Democratic Party's current trajectory with chilling cynicism. While voters once expected progressive leaders to maintain at least a veneer of moral duty and reformist ideals, they now see a party consumed by naked opportunism and fierce loyalty competitions. Politicians seeking party entry or favorable nominations fall silent in the face of internal misconduct, signaling that careerism has thoroughly replaced conviction.

When a reform-minded political force sheds its ideological wrapping and abandons its supporters for the spoils of power, the resulting void is filled with alienation. Unless the ruling party looks past factional infighting and addresses the root causes of its declining public trust, it risks a harsh political reckoning from an electorate that feels utterly deceived.
