Ruling Democratic Party Opens Impeachment Discussions Against Chief Justice

October 17, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party has officially put the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae on the table, escalating an unprecedented confrontation between the legislative majority and the judiciary.

  • What happened: Senior leaders of the Democratic Party formally initiated discussions to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, while simultaneously launching parliamentary audit attacks against conservative Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon.
  • What it changes: The move shifts the ruling party’s judicial reform push from verbal pressure into tangible political action, threatening a major constitutional showdown over judicial independence.
  • Who is affected: The judiciary, President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, and the main conservative opposition People Power Party, which called the proposal an illegal attempt to shield power.

An All-Out Clash with the Judiciary

The Democratic Party’s decision to entertain impeaching Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae reflects deep-seated frustration within the party and its base over alleged trial delays and political bias. Ruling party leadership, led by figures like Kim Min-seok, argued that structural imbalances within the court system inherited from Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s former administration can no longer be ignored. By making impeachment an explicit option, the party is signaling that it intends to push structural judicial reform to its limit.

The main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) reacted immediately, accusing the ruling majority of waging an unconstitutional campaign against the separation of powers. PPP Representative Jeong Jeom-sig warned that introducing an impeachment motion against the head of the judiciary would trigger severe public backlash and create political instability for President Lee Jae-myung’s government. Despite the defense from conservative lawmakers, the Democratic Party maintains that restoring public trust in the courts requires drastic action.

Broadening the Offensive to Municipal Power and Local Politics

The judicial gambit is part of a broader political offensive by the Democratic Party during the ongoing parliamentary audit season. In Seoul, party leadership targeted conservative Mayor Oh Se-hoon over alleged kickbacks and cover-ups surrounding his signature "Hangang Bus" river transit initiative, demanding full investigations and witness testimony. Meanwhile, party leadership traveled to Busan to mark the 47th anniversary of the historic Bu-Ma Democratic Protests, promising constitutional recognition for the movement alongside regional development projects to win over southern voters.

By pairing high-profile constitutional fights with targeted local messaging and everyday legislative proposals—such as crackdowns on illegal medical clinic operators—the Democratic Party is trying to consolidate its dominance. However, recent polling shows voters demand high ethical standards alongside executive accountability, making this aggressive multi-front push a high-stakes test for the ruling party’s momentum.
