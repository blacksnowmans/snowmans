Leaked Audio Sparks Ethics Probe and Factional Feud Inside South Korea’s Ruling Party

November 19, 2026

A leaked recording of confidential remarks by senior lawmaker Choe Min-hee has ignited an ethics investigation and exposed deepening factional friction within South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea.

  • What happened: A 70-minute audio recording from an October closed-door meeting surfaced in mid-November, showing Supreme Council member Choe Min-hee making inaccurate claims about President Lee Jae-myung, questioning party leader Kim Min-seok's leadership, and touching on unverified religious endorsement tips.
  • What it changes: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok launched an immediate ethics audit after media reports broke, triggering intense party debate over whether leadership intentionally sat on the recordings for weeks.
  • Who is affected: The ruling party’s leadership faces a trial of authority as rival factions clash over internal discipline, procedural fairness, and political loyalty ahead of Kim’s 100-day benchmark.

Distorted Accounts and Public Apologies

The controversy stems from an October 16 closed-door gathering in Namyangju, where Choe Min-hee claimed that President Lee Jae-myung had previously opposed impeaching Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who was later impeached and imprisoned—and sought a negotiated resignation alongside former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo. Choe further alleged that lawmaker Park Jie-won advised this approach. Both the presidential office and Rep. Park swiftly refuted the claims as entirely baseless. Choe issued a public apology citing a lapse in memory, but senior party members argued that her remarks directly undermined the political legitimacy of the president.

Beyond the presidential comments, the recording revealed sharp internal friction regarding party strategy and leadership. Choe criticized Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok over stagnant 30% approval ratings near his 100-day mark, suggested merging with the minor opposition Rebuilding Korea Party, made harsh assessments of presidential appointees, and raised unverified tips alleging religious groups like Shincheonji supported Kim during leadership contests.

Timeline Disputes and Leadership Dilemmas

The central political battle now centers on when party leadership became aware of the audio. Records show that informants delivered files to Kim Min-seok’s office and key aides as early as October 19. However, Kim ordered an ethics audit on November 12, just 40 minutes after media reports surfaced. Former party leader Jung Chung-rae and internal critics publicly questioned whether Kim delayed action until public leaks forced his hand, raising doubts over the party's reporting hierarchy and crisis management.

This ethics probe has transformed Supreme Council meetings into a battleground over discipline and loyalty. While hardline lawmakers demand severe penalties to restore party order, others accuse the leadership of lacking procedural fairness and ignoring due process. As the Democratic Party navigates these internal rifts, the fallout threatens to hamper leader Kim Min-seok’s momentum and paralyze the ruling party’s internal cohesion.
