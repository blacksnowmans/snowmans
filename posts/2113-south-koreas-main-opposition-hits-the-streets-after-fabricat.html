South Korea’s Main Opposition Hits the Streets After "Fabricated Indictment" Bill Passes

November 08, 2026

South Korea’s political deadlock has shifted from the halls of parliament to the streets of Seoul as the main opposition party launches a massive protest campaign against a controversial new investigative law.

  • The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, held large-scale rallies near Gyeongbokgung Station to protest the passage of the "Special Prosecutor Act on Fabricated Indictments."
  • PPP Chairman Jang Dong-hyeok called for the impeachment of President Lee Jae-myung, labeling the law a "legislative coup" and a "jailbreak law" designed to protect the President from legal scrutiny.
  • The ruling Democratic Party (DP) defended the bill as a necessary tool to investigate prosecutorial abuses and "fabricated" cases that occurred during the administration of the now-imprisoned Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • Amid the tension, Lee Jun-seok, a prominent lawmaker for the minor Reform Party, announced he will seek criminal punishment for a popular history lecturer who accused him of forging his academic credentials.

The Return of Street Politics

The PPP’s decision to abandon the National Assembly for the "asphalt" of central Seoul marks a breakdown in parliamentary cooperation. By using high-voltage rhetoric—accusing the government of a "legislative rebellion"—the opposition is attempting to mobilize its core base against a law they cannot stop through votes. This move reflects a broader trend where the main opposition, finding itself sidelined in the legislature, relies on public demonstrations to challenge the legitimacy of the Lee Jae-myung administration.

This "street politics" carries significant risks for the country’s stability. While it may consolidate the conservative base, it leaves critical national issues, such as the budget and welfare reforms, in a state of paralysis. For the PPP, the focus has shifted entirely to a defensive posture, framing any attempt to investigate past prosecutorial conduct as a direct threat to the constitutional order. However, critics argue that relying on inflammatory slogans in the town square is a poor substitute for the policy-driven leadership expected of a major political party.

A Reckoning with the Yoon Era

At the heart of the conflict is the "Special Prosecutor Act on Fabricated Indictments." To the ruling DP, the law is essential to cleaning up the "political prosecution" culture they claim flourished under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure. Since Mr. Yoon’s impeachment and imprisonment, the DP has pushed for a thorough investigation into how the state’s investigative powers were allegedly used to target political rivals. They argue that judicial justice cannot be restored without a transparent review of these cases.

Conversely, the opposition views this as a "self-pardon" for the President, intended to derail legal proceedings. This clash illustrates the deep-seated distrust in the Korean justice system, which has become the primary battlefield for political warfare. As the PPP calls for the President’s impeachment and the DP prepares for a special probe into the previous administration's actions, South Korean politics is increasingly defined by a cycle of retribution rather than governance. This climate of extremism is also reflected internationally, as populist movements in the U.S. and Europe grapple with similar shifts in voter sentiment following recent elections.
