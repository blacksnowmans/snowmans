Anthropic’s Stumbling IPO and Nvidia SEC Probe Trigger Global Tech Sell-Off, Hitting Korean Markets

November 22, 2026

Anthropic’s record-breaking Nasdaq debut failed to sustain a market-wide AI rally, triggering a global technology sell-off exacerbated by a U.S. SEC accounting probe into Nvidia and steep losses for South Korean investors.

  • What happened: Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 and surged 17% on its first trading day before plunging 8.5% on day two, while Nvidia crashed 14% in a single session to $136 following reports of a U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) accounting investigation.
  • Market fallout: The Nasdaq fell 5.3% in a single day, erasing $4 trillion in market capitalization, while an AI prediction model raised the probability of an AI bubble collapse to 58%.
  • Impact on Korea: South Korea's benchmark Kospi index broke below the 5,000 level, while Korean retail investors—known locally as "Seohak gaemi" (investors purchasing overseas stocks)—face massive paper losses after buying over 800 billion won (about $570 million) worth of Nvidia shares this month alone.
  • What changes: Market focus has abruptly shifted from top-line valuation hype to corporate profitability, putting intense scrutiny on "circular financing" loops between chip suppliers and AI startups.

From IPO Hype to Circular Finance Scrutiny

The euphoria surrounding Anthropic's landmark public debut faded remarkably fast. Although its initial surge briefly pushed its market capitalization into the $1 trillion club and created seven new mega-billionaires among its founders, investor sentiment soured on day two. Markets reacted negatively to news that much of the raised capital—between 110 trillion and 161 trillion won (about $80 billion to $115 billion)—was designated for purchasing AI chips, paying data center rent, and clearing existing debts. This circular money flow raised serious doubts about Anthropic's core profitability, particularly as tech giants like Apple move toward proprietary AI ecosystems.

Compounding the market anxiety, reports that the SEC is investigating Nvidia over its accounting practices triggered a severe market correction. The inquiry centers on potential revenue inflation through "circular AI financing"—where Nvidia funds AI startups that subsequently use those capital injections to buy Nvidia GPUs. Following Jensen Huang’s exit and the transition to an interim leadership structure under Colette Kress, Nvidia’s stock plunged 14% to a 17-month low, cutting its valuation nearly in half from its peak and pushing its market cap rank down to fourth place.

Korean Markets and Retail Investors Face the Fallout

The contagion spread immediately to South Korean markets, hitting both retail traders and heavyweights in the semiconductor supply chain. South Korea’s "Seohak gaemi" retail investors, who heavily backed Nvidia’s dip this month with over 800 billion won ($570 million) in net purchases, now face trillions of won in unrealized losses as support levels dissolved.

The domestic market felt an immediate ripple effect. Benchmark chipmakers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix came under severe pressure due to their direct exposure as primary suppliers of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips to Nvidia. Even telecom leader SK Telecom saw its stock dragged down by broader market skepticism, despite domestic brokerages valuing its equity stake in Anthropic at 5 trillion won (about $3.6 billion). As Wall Street re-evaluates the real returns on AI infrastructure, South Korea's tech ecosystem faces a difficult period of valuation adjustment.
