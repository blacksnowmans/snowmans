US Trade Pressure: South Korea’s Diplomatic Balancing Act

September 10, 2026

South Korea is facing intense pressure from the United States to increase financial and military commitments, with reports suggesting that Washington is leveraging trade agreements to demand massive new investments and troop deployments.

  • Unprecedented Investment Demands: Following recent G20 meetings, US officials are reportedly pushing for over $350 billion (approx. 470 trillion won) in new investments, despite South Korea having already finalized a $350 billion investment package last year.
  • Energy Project Risks: The US is pressuring Seoul to take on unprofitable Alaska LNG (liquefied natural gas) projects that major energy firms like ExxonMobil and BP previously abandoned due to high costs.
  • Semiconductor Leverage: The US is reportedly using the potential for unfavorable trade treatment as leverage to dictate the future of key South Korean firms like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix.
  • Military Deployment Deadline: Reports indicate the US has set an 11-month deadline for South Korea to commit troops to the Strait of Hormuz to fill a security vacuum created by American force reductions.

The High Cost of the US-ROK Alliance

The diplomatic climate between Seoul and Washington has become increasingly strained as the US ties security cooperation to economic concessions. Industry Minister Kim Jeong-gwan has faced internal criticism for failing to push back against these demands, which many characterize not as reasonable negotiations between allies, but as unilateral exploitation. The demand to fund economically unviable energy projects is particularly contentious, as it forces South Korean companies to shoulder the risk for a project that global energy giants have already deemed a loss-maker.

Beyond economics, the intersection of trade and national security is causing alarm. US officials are reportedly attempting to intervene in the use of land for South Korean semiconductor projects, citing the Status of Forces Agreement (SOFA)—the treaty governing US military personnel stationed in South Korea—to claim the land for potential air base use. This persistent encroachment, coupled with the pressure to deploy troops to the Middle East, suggests a strategy aimed at maximizing US gains at the direct expense of South Korean sovereignty.

An Uncertain Future for "Allied" Diplomacy

The current situation raises a fundamental question: at what point does a strategic alliance become a liability? For years, South Korea has prioritized its relationship with the US, but the "give-only" nature of these current demands is testing the limits of public patience. The insistence on further massive investment—beyond what is already straining the capacity of domestic firms—threatens to sap the long-term growth potential of South Korea’s tech sector.

From my perspective, this is a crisis of diplomatic self-respect. While the US-ROK alliance is crucial for security, it cannot be a one-way street of exploitation. The South Korean government faces a delicate challenge: it must move beyond a passive, reactive posture and demonstrate the courage to say "no" when the costs of compliance undermine national interest. If Seoul continues to accept these unreasonable demands as "achievements," it risks not only economic damage but the erosion of the very sovereignty it seeks to protect.
