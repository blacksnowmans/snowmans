South Korea’s Judiciary Eyes Yoon Conviction to Setup Trap for President Lee

November 04, 2026

As South Korea’s judiciary prepares a potential conviction for imprisoned former leader Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, political analysts warn the court is abandoning Yoon to establish legal precedents meant to target sitting President Lee Jae-myung.

  • Financial crisis for the opposition: A final guilty verdict could force the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main conservative opposition party, to reimburse up to 43.4 billion won (about $32 million) in state-funded presidential election expenses.
  • Calculated judicial pivot: Under Chief Justice Jo Hee-de, the Supreme Court of Korea appears ready to drop Mr. Yoon to project an image of judicial impartiality while building legal ammunition against the current administration.
  • Distraction from governance: Entrenchment in political defense is beginning to sap President Lee’s policy momentum, delaying key economic and technology initiatives like the government’s artificial intelligence task force.

Judicial Strategy and Opposition Bankruptcy

The potential conviction of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol presents an existential threat to the conservative People Power Party. Under South Korean election law, if a candidate is retroactively convicted of serious campaign-related violations, their party must return public funds disbursed to reimburse election costs. Facing a repayment demand of up to 43.4 billion won (about $32 million), the PPP would face immediate financial insolvency and deep internal division over whether to completely sever ties with Mr. Yoon.

However, the broader maneuver extends beyond party finances. The judiciary, under Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de, seems eager to drop its defense of Mr. Yoon. By convicting the disgraced figure, the court creates a veneer of political independence. In reality, this move is a calculated trade-off: sacrificing Mr. Yoon allows the court to establish restrictive judicial precedents regarding political speech and campaign conduct that can immediately be weaponized against President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea.

Weaponized Precedents and Governance Drain

This tactic aims to drag President Lee into the same legal quagmire. Should the judiciary rule that certain past statements or actions by Mr. Yoon during his campaign constitute criminal offenses, conservative prosecutors and opposition politicians will demand that identical legal standards be applied to President Lee’s own past statements. This calculated symmetry threatens to embroil the presidency in endless litigation, turning legal rulings into tools of political sabotage.

The primary casualty of this judicial warfare is national governance. With the administration forced to devote significant energy to countering legal probes, critical policy initiatives are slipping through the cracks. Strategic projects—including national artificial intelligence task forces created early in President Lee’s term—have quietly stalled. As South Korea faces intense global competition in advanced technologies, the political obsession with judicial warfare risks draining the country’s momentum when focus on economic growth is needed most.
