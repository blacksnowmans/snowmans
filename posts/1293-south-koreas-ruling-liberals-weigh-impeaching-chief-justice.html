South Korea’s Ruling Liberals Weigh Impeaching Chief Justice Amid Audit Clashes

October 19, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is escalating its push to reform the judiciary by putting the potential impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae on the table, even as party leadership faces sudden political headwinds over an awkward protocol misstep on the campaign trail.

  • Judicial Showdown: Ruling DPK lawmakers and allied progressive figures, including Cho Kuk of the minor opposition Rebuilding Korea Party, are demanding a swift impeachment vote against Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae, arguing the courts have overstepped their constitutional bounds with biased rulings.
  • Conservative Pushback: The conservative main opposition People Power Party (PPP) fiercely denounced the move, accusing the ruling party of taking the country's judicial independence hostage to distract from its own record during ongoing parliamentary audits.
  • Protocol Backlash: DPK leader Kim Min-seok was forced to issue a rapid public apology after an unscripted stage greeting interrupted a live performance by pop singer HYNN at a local festival in Sunchang, sparking criticism over entitled political behavior.
  • Audit Gridlock: As the annual parliamentary audit reaches its halfway mark, clashes deepened over national security disputes—specifically accelerating the transfer of wartime operational control (OPCON) from Washington—and calls to reinvestigate past conservative figures.

A Risky Constitutional Gamble

The push to impeach the sitting Chief Justice represents a high-stakes escalation in the DPK’s drive to dismantle what party stalwarts view as entrenched judicial biases left over from the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president removed via impeachment and currently imprisoned. Ruling party leaders argue that the National Assembly has a constitutional duty under Article 65 to rein in an unchecked judiciary. Yet pulling the trigger on an impeachment motion against the nation’s top judge is an aggressive gambit that hands the PPP an easy target, allowing conservatives to frame the move as partisan overreach designed to intimidate the courts.

Ground-Level Blunders and Electoral Realities

The DPK’s broader strategic focus centers on expanding its regional foothold, notably in southeastern swing districts around Busan’s Nakdong River belt ahead of future electoral cycles. However, high-minded reform rhetoric can quickly be undone by unforced retail errors. Party leader Kim Min-seok’s awkward intrusion onto a concert stage at the Sunchang Fermented Sauce Festival handed conservatives a prime narrative: that the ruling party has grown out of touch and overbearing. While Kim’s swift apology limited the immediate fallout, the episode highlights how quickly ground-level missteps can complicate ambitious legislative agendas.
