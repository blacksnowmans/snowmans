South Korea's Live-Commerce Briefing: Deep Discounts, AI Efficiency, and K-Beauty's Global Rise

November 11, 2026

South Korea’s e-commerce landscape is moving at breakneck speed, driven by aggressive year-end price wars, explosive growth in AI-generated video production, and surging global demand for Korean beauty and lifestyle brands.

    Coupang's aggressive push: South Korea's retail giant Coupang launched its "Black Daily Necessities Week," offering over 20,000 items with prices starting at an eye-catching 1,990 won (about $1.40) to lock in household essentials shoppers. AI cuts video costs by over half: Tests by AI video solution VCAT and Naver Shopping Live—the live-streaming arm of South Korea's dominant search engine—revealed that AI-generated streams slashed production costs by 59% while achieving 1.6 times higher sales efficiency. K-Beauty wins abroad: Korean skincare and cosmetics continued their global winning streak, highlighted by Missha’s BB cream placing at a British beauty award and the "Ginseng Cleansing Oil" by brand Beauty of Joseon taking home an Allure Beauty Award in the US. Curated live streams drive instant sales: Premium grocery platform Market Kurry reported a 34% rise in third-quarter revenue alongside a 63% jump in operating profit, proving that curated trust directly boosts live-commerce conversion rates.

The Two Forces Reshaping Retail: Deep Discounts vs. AI Production

The current retail environment presents a clear dichotomy. On one end, legacy e-commerce giants like Coupang are waging a war of attrition, using ultra-low prices on basic household goods to capture consumer loyalty. On the other end, brands are rethinking how they reach audiences. High-consideration products—such as Continental Tires offering up to 47% off during a Naver live stream—prove that live commerce is no longer just for cheap impulse buys, but a tool to educate consumers and remove purchasing friction.

Meanwhile, the rapid adoption of artificial intelligence in live broadcasting signals a major operational shift. When automated video creation cuts expenses by 59% without sacrificing sales performance, it levels the playing field for smaller merchants who previously could not afford high production overhead. However, this automation raises the stakes for human creators: as AI takes over standard product pitches, human hosts must rely on raw authenticity, like beauty creators selling out inventory in minutes through unscripted storytelling, to truly move products.

Global Expansion and the Need for Localized Trust

As domestic markets mature, Korean brands are aggressively targeting overseas territories through localized live-streaming and social commerce. From fashion brand Nick & Nicole partnering with Taiwanese platforms to leverage star power, to the explosive rise of TikTok Shop in Southeast Asia—where Vietnam alone recorded over 100 million online purchases in three months—the boundaries of retail are increasingly digital and borderless.

Yet, winning global awards or riding short-form video trends is only the first step. To maintain momentum in crowded overseas markets, Korean exporters must build direct, community-driven communication channels rather than relying solely on traditional export models. In an era where AI can generate a broadcast in seconds and platforms can slash prices by half, the ultimate currency for any brand remains the consumer's trust.
