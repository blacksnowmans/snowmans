The Illusion of "Push-Button" AI Wealth: Inside South Korea’s High-Priced Side-Hustle Trap

September 01, 2026

  • Aggressive online advertisements claiming ordinary office workers can make millions of won a month in an hour using AI are mostly lures for expensive, low-quality lectures.
  • Consumer complaints in South Korea regarding online side-hustle courses nearly quadrupled from 11 cases to 42 cases over a single year.
  • Experts warn that true content creation requires deep planning and heavy labor, and relying on AI for automated riches often leaves participants out of pocket while enriching course sellers.

South Korean social media is currently flooded with advertisements promising easy riches: watch a tired office worker generate thousands of dollars a month with just a single click of an AI tool. These pitches lean heavily on fake dashboard screenshots and fabricated success stories, capitalizing on deep societal anxieties. With traditional wages stagnating and living costs rising, the promise of effortless "copy-paste" wealth has hooked countless hopeful workers looking for a financial lifeline.

Yet, the reality behind these automated promises is far more grim. What the advertisements omit is the grueling labor required behind the scenes—prompt engineering, subscription fees, platform restrictions, and low copyright yields that render the actual return on investment negligible. Rather than a shortcut to financial freedom, the trend has become a booming market for overpriced courses costing upwards of 3 to 4 million won (thousands of dollars). In this ecosystem, the only reliable way to make money off AI is to sell courses teaching others how to make money off AI.

Selling Fantasy to the Financially Stressed

The explosive growth of these predatory courses highlights a darker side of South Korea's hyper-competitive economy. When people feel that honest, hard work no longer guarantees upward mobility, desperation sets in. Shady operators exploit this exact vulnerability, repackaging basic AI tools as magical money printers. When students inevitably fail to see results, course vendors typically blame the victim's lack of effort rather than the product's hollow curriculum.

True artificial intelligence is an incredible productivity enhancer, capable of reducing video editing times and sparking fresh ideas. However, treating AI as a replacement for genuine human creativity, narrative depth, and critical thought is a recipe for failure. Audiences quickly tune out formulaic, machine-generated noise. For readers navigating the digital economy, the lesson is simple: AI is a powerful assistant, not a business model. Until people stop looking for shortcut fairy tales, they will remain data laborers working solely to line the pockets of internet marketers.
