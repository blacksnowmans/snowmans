Morgan Stanley Rattles Korean Chip Giants with 2028 "Earnings Cliff" Warning

November 11, 2026

  • What happened: Global investment bank Morgan Stanley slashed its price targets for Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, with SK Hynix facing a significant cut of up to 38% due to long-term supply concerns.
  • The core issue: Analysts are warning of a potential "earnings cliff" by 2028, driven by an aggressive global expansion of semiconductor production capacity that could lead to a massive supply glut.
  • Market impact: The move has sparked intense debate over whether the current artificial intelligence-driven boom is sustainable, casting a shadow over the future of South Korea's chip-dependent economy.

The "Grim Reaper" of Semiconductors Strikes

Morgan Stanley’s recent report has sent shockwaves through the Seoul stock market, leading some to dub the firm the "Grim Reaper" of the semiconductor industry. While Samsung Electronics reported a robust quarterly operating profit of about $77 billion (107 trillion won) largely fueled by AI memory demand, the report shifts focus away from current gains to future profitability. The firm argues that the current race to build new fabrication plants (fabs) across the industry will inevitably result in oversupply once these facilities come online around 2028, mirroring the cyclical, boom-and-bust nature of the memory market. This warning highlights a critical vulnerability in the South Korean economy. The reliance on chip exports is so profound that even the Bank of Korea’s interest rate policies are often dictated by the health of the semiconductor sector. With the current administration under President Lee Jae-myung—who took office following the impeachment and imprisonment of former leader Yoon Seok-yeol—looking to stabilize growth, the prospect of a market downturn presents a major policy headache.

Survival in a Post-Boom Reality

For companies like Samsung Electronics, the challenge is twofold. Internally, the firm is struggling with a "deformed" business structure where semiconductor profits mask the weakening performance of its mobile and home appliance divisions. To survive a potential 2028 slump, Samsung must evolve into a comprehensive solutions provider rather than relying solely on memory chip cycles. Externally, the industry is caught in a geopolitical crossfire. As the U.S. and China continue to tighten regulations, South Korean firms face the difficult task of navigating a supply chain that is increasingly fragmented. While some analysts maintain optimistic price targets, the reality is that the era of blind growth is over. The "earnings cliff" warning is a stark reminder that in the volatile world of semiconductors, the transition from a supplier’s market to a buyer’s market could arrive faster than anticipated. For investors, the focus must shift from short-term quarterly results to the long-term technical "moats" and supply chain agility that will define the winners of the next decade.
