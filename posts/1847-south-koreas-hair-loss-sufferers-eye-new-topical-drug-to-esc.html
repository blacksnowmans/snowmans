South Korea’s Hair Loss Sufferers Eye New Topical Drug to Escape Severe Side Effects

November 02, 2026

South Koreans dealing with male pattern baldness are increasingly seeking targeted topical treatments and cosmetic alternatives after suffering severe physical and psychological side effects from traditional oral medications.

  • What happened: Patients in South Korea are pushing back against severe side effects—including deep depression and numbness—caused by popular oral finasteride treatments, often exacerbated by dangerous practices like splitting high-dose prostate medication to cut costs.
  • What is changing: Clascoterone, an FDA-approved topical treatment developed by an Italian pharmaceutical firm, is scheduled to launch in South Korea in 2025, marking the first fundamentally new pharmacological approach to hair loss in over 30 years.
  • How it works: Instead of lowering male hormone levels across the entire body, Clascoterone targets androgen receptors locally at the hair follicle, drastically reducing the risk of systemic complications such as depression, brain fog, and sexual dysfunction.
  • Who is affected: Millions of South Koreans managing hair loss, alongside a growing industry of alternative solutions ranging from scalp concealers to customized hairpieces.

The Hidden Costs of Oral Medications and the Rise of Alternatives

For decades, male pattern baldness treatment in South Korea has relied heavily on oral finasteride drugs like Propecia (1mg). To trim healthcare costs, many patients resort to splitting 5mg Proscar pills—designed for prostate enlargement—into smaller portions. Medical experts like pharmacist Jeong Jae-hoon warn that breaking pill coatings leads to imprecise dosing and erratic hormone fluctuations. Public figures such as Cho Sang-hyun, CEO of the Korean hairpiece brand We Can Do It, have candidly shared how six months of oral medication led to overwhelming depression and physical numbness. Psychiatrist Dr. Choi Myung-ki notes that the psychological toll of hair loss often amplifies chemical drug side effects, leading many patients to seek alternative paths like hairpieces or scalp concealers, which dermatologist Dr. Lee Seon-min notes are safe when properly washed.

A Paradigm Shift in Medical Care and Social Perception

The upcoming 2025 domestic release of Clascoterone represents a major shift from systemic hormone suppression to localized targeted therapy. By blocking follicle receptors directly, the topical application allows patients to maintain normal blood hormone levels while protecting their hair. This technology arrives alongside a broader cultural transformation in South Korea, where open discussion about hairpieces and balding is replacing secrecy and shame. Under President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, public discourse has increasingly framed hair loss care as an area requiring accurate health information and social welfare support rather than individual insecurity, empowering patients to prioritize overall quality of life alongside hair restoration.
