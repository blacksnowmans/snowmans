Global Shifts: US Midterms, Middle East Stand-offs, and Turkey's Trade Push

November 03, 2026

As global political and economic fault lines shift, incoming projections for the 2026 U.S. midterm elections and ongoing Middle East conflicts are setting up a volatile landscape that will directly impact South Korea's economy and security.

  • U.S. Midterm Shift: Polls and analyses indicate a nearly 80% chance that the U.S. Democratic Party could capture both the House and Senate in 2026, driven by rising voter fatigue with Donald Trump's platform even in Republican strongholds like Texas and Kansas.
  • Middle East Re-alignment: U.S. President Donald Trump has reportedly rebuffed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's push to resume airstrikes on Iran, while internal anti-Netanyahu pressure from Israeli Arab parties opens a potential window for negotiated de-escalation.
  • South Korea-Turkey Trade Realignment: During a recent state visit by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan to meet South Korean President Lee Jae-myung, Ankara pressed for revisions to the bilateral free trade agreement to address a glaring 9-to-1 trade deficit.

The convergence of shifting American legislative power and pragmatic global diplomacy underscores how quickly traditional alliances are being tested. President Lee Jae-myung’s administration faces the delicate task of balancing economic exposure—such as South Korean automakers and steelmakers operating in Turkey—while navigating the ripple effects of a potential Democratic "blue wave" in Washington that could constrain unilateral U.S. foreign policy.

At the same time, developments in Europe and the Middle East offer sharp reminders of the fragility of democratic institutions and the limits of hard power. From Poland's bruising judicial reforms aimed at undoing the populist, nationalist legacy of the Law and Justice party, to Turkey leveraging its drone-manufacturing prowess and nuclear energy partnership options, global players are doubling down on transactional, interest-driven foreign policy. For South Korea, steering a steady course through these geopolitical crosscurrents will require moving past ideological posturing and anchoring national strategy firmly in economic pragmatism.
