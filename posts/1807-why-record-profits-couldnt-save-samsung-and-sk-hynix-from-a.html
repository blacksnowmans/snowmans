Why Record Profits Couldn't Save Samsung and SK Hynix from a Market Rout

November 01, 2026

  • The Paradox: South Korea's chip giants Samsung Electronics (the world's largest memory chip maker) and SK Hynix posted massive, record-breaking earnings—including an operating profit of about $77 billion (107 trillion won) for Samsung—yet saw their share prices plunge 6% and 8% respectively.
  • The Trigger: The sell-off was sparked by the "Alphabet shock" and a broader Wall Street tech rout, dragging down the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and triggering a massive $1.16 billion (1.6 trillion won) foreign capital flight from the local market.
  • The Fear: Investors are ignoring current balance sheets out of fear that the AI memory boom has hit its "peak-out" point, overshadowing SK Hynix's surging high-bandwidth memory (HBM) sales and shareholder return plans.
  • The Internal Crisis: While SK Hynix leans into its AI memory dominance with technological reshuffles, Samsung faces internal turbulence, labor friction, and growing pressure over lagging competitiveness in advanced foundry and HBM sectors.

Logic has taken a backseat to panic in Seoul. When companies report stellar earnings only to watch their valuations crater, it signals that the market is no longer pricing in what is happening today, but what it fears tomorrow. For foreign investors watching South Korea's bellwether equities, the decoupling of fundamentals and stock prices highlights how tightly tied the local market remains to global macro anxieties.

The core issue is that chips are no longer treated as isolated cyclical commodities; they are proxies for the entire artificial intelligence trade. When US tech sentiment wobbles due to bubble fears, Korean chipmakers take the direct hit, regardless of how many advanced memory chips they manage to ship. Until global macroeconomic stability and clear visibility on AI monetization return, record profits alone will not be enough to stop investors from running for the exits.
