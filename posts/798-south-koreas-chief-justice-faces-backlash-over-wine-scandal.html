South Korea's Chief Justice Faces Backlash Over Wine Scandal and Political Ambitions

October 07, 2026

South Korea's top judge, Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, is facing intense scrutiny following parliamentary audit revelations regarding hundreds of dollars in official expense spending on luxury wine and meals, alongside accusations that the judiciary is overstepping its constitutional role to harbor political ambitions.

  • The Audit Revelations: Legislative auditors revealed that 26 bottles of wine were charged to official expense accounts under Chief Justice Cho's administration, prompting sharp questioning from lawmakers.
  • Defensive Stance: Cho faced criticism for offering evasive answers during the audit, claiming he never touched the credit cards or drank the wine, while appearing visibly uncomfortable under questioning about luxury dining expenses.
  • Political Fallout: Critics argue that the judiciary, under Cho's leadership, is displaying signs of political overreach and defying the democratic mandate of President Lee Jae-myung's administration.
  • Key Legal Battles: Discussions during the audit also touched on potential arrest warrants related to figures like Kim Keon-hee, highlighting tension between the courts and prosecutors.

The controversy centers on whether South Korea's highest judicial authority is misusing public funds while harboring political aspirations reminiscent of past judge-turned-politicians. During the parliamentary audit, lawmakers pressed Cho on the 26 bottles of wine purchased with official funds, despite his claims of abstinence and ignorance regarding the transactions. For critics, the incident underscores a broader detachment among senior judicial figures who enjoy lavish meals at taxpayer expense while ordinary citizens struggle with inflation.

Beyond the expense account scandal, the confrontation at the audit exposed deeper institutional friction. Opposition lawmakers and progressive critics accused the judiciary's leadership of attempting to build an independent political base in opposition to President Lee Jae-myung's administration. Observers noted that Cho's defensive reactions to questions about presidential ambitions reveal an alarming erosion of the boundary between the non-partisan judiciary and active political maneuvering, threatening the checks and balances essential to South Korea's democracy.
