Packing for the Camino: What South Korean Travelers Take on the 800-Kilometer Pilgrimage

September 07, 2026

Packing for Spain's Camino de Santiago is not just about travel gear; it is a calculated exercise in survival, where every item in a 10.7-kilogram (23.5-pound) backpack represents the physical toll a traveler is willing to endure.

  • The Weight Limit: Keeping the pack under 20% of body weight is essential to prevent injury during daily multi-kilometer hikes.
  • The Bedbug Defense: Using plastic bags inside backpacks creates noise that deters thieves and keeps pests at bay.
  • Specialized Gear: Toe-separating socks (Injinji) and trekking poles are treated as non-negotiable tools to prevent plantar fasciitis and joint damage.
  • Survival Add-ons: Seasoned travelers pack emergency rations like Korean seasoned red pepper paste (gochujang) and pepper spray for remote sections.

Gear as Armor Against the Trail

For many South Koreans taking a career break to tackle the 800-kilometer (500-mile) pilgrimage, preparation goes far beyond a romantic notion of walking in nature. Stories of K-pop stars like Akdong Musician’s Lee Su-hyun suffering from plantar fasciitis on the trail underscore a harsh reality: physical pain and bedbugs are constant threats. Consequently, packing lists are treated as defensive armor rather than vacation checklists. Osprey 68-liter backpacks serve as mobile homes, while clever additions like loud bells and plastic bag linings act as multi-layered security systems against European hostel pests and theft.

Every item carried reflects hard-earned practical experience. Minimalist wardrobes layered for fluctuating temperatures, blister-preventing petroleum jelly, and high-SPF sunscreens are prioritized over fashion. Electronics like bone-conduction headphones allow hikers to listen to directions or music while remaining alert to traffic and footsteps behind them. Ultimately, looking at what these travelers pack reveals a mindset that views the journey not as a leisurely holiday, but as a grueling test of endurance where poor preparation translates directly into physical suffering.

The Mental Weight of Letting Go

In my view, the ritual of packing for the Camino is as much about confronting personal psychology as it is about physical logistics. Everyone learns the hard way that items packed with a hesitant "just in case" quickly transform into instruments of torture on steep mountain passes. The best packing lists are born from brutal honesty about what a human body can handle over weeks of continuous walking.

What makes these Korean travel preparations compelling is the deep respect shown to the environment and the physical limits of the walker. The journey forces a minimalist philosophy: you cannot carry everything, and you must learn to rely on what is essential. Long before the physical blisters heal, the true lesson of the pilgrimage begins with the willingness to shed excess weight, both inside and outside the backpack.
