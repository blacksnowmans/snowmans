South Korea's Democratic Party Faces Deepening Factional Crisis as Approval Ratings Plunge

September 03, 2026

  • South Korea's liberal Democratic Party, currently in power, has seen its approval ratings drop by 4.3% while President Lee Jae-myung's positive ratings fell sharply by 8.4%.
  • Discontent is particularly severe in Seoul, where disapproval ratings have surged to nearly 70.2%, alongside a jump in middle-of-the-road voter alienation.
  • Internal infighting, polarizing factional behavior, and growing doubts over the fairness of the party's ethics watchdog have sparked warnings of a potential political collapse from within.

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party is facing a severe reality check following a wave of internal factionalism and plummeting public support. Despite earlier optimistic promises from party leadership under Chairman Kim Min-seok to quickly boost public approval, the party is instead grappling with a profound loss of trust among its core base and independent voters alike. Accusations of internal gatekeeping, mutual backstabbing, and a compromised internal ethics system have turned what was supposed to be a period of consolidation into a bitter factional war.

The core of the backlash centers on toxic internal dynamics, notably divisive party groupings and harsh public spats among prominent figures like lawmaker Park Sun-won targeting former party leader Cho Kuk. Critics argue that instead of focusing outward against political opponents, the party has retreated into an exclusionary mindset where anyone outside a favored inner circle is treated as an enemy. Compounding these fractures are rising doubts about the integrity of the Democratic Party's ethics committee, which critics accuse of operating with a dangerous double standard—treating loyalists with leniency while aggressively targeting internal dissent.

This is no longer a routine dip in the polls; it is a clear warning that unearned complacency and factional arrogance have completely disconnected the leadership from ordinary citizens. If the party continues to mistake genuine internal criticism for betrayal, the current slump will not be a temporary setback, but the beginning of a much deeper political decline. Restoring basic democratic norms and honest debate within the party is no longer optional—it is a matter of survival.
