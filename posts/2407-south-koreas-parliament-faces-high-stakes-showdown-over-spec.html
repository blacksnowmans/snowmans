South Korea’s Parliament Faces High-Stakes Showdown Over Special Counsel Bill and Budget Cuts

November 17, 2026

South Korea’s political deadlock reached a crisis point ahead of a pivotal November 19 parliamentary session, with the ruling Democratic Party and the opposition People Power Party colliding over a special counsel bill, deep budget slashes, and internal party feuds.

  • What happened: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DP) is pushing to pass a special counsel bill targeting alleged "fabricated prosecutions," while forcing through budget cuts against the judiciary and the Presidential Office.
  • The opposition reaction: The main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) staged rallies outside the Presidential Office, demanding Prime Minister Han Duck-soo veto the special counsel bill if passed.
  • Judicial pressure: DP lawmakers eliminated 324 million won (around $230,000) allocated for Supreme Court justice allowances, while the minor opposition Rebuilding Korea Party launched a sit-in demanding the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.
  • Ruling party infighting: The DP is facing severe internal friction as a group of junior lawmakers launched a petition demanding formal discipline against Representative Choi Min-hee, despite her public apology to President Lee Jae-myung.

Multi-Front Battles in National Assembly Committees

The confrontation has paralyzed legislative business across key parliamentary committees. In the Legislation and Judiciary Committee, ruling party lawmakers stripped executive allowances for Supreme Court justices—a move the opposition PPP condemned as overt intimidation of the judiciary. Meanwhile, the House Steering Committee descended into shouting matches over the Presidential Office’s draft budget, where conservative lawmakers demanded full transparency and total slashes to presidential special activity funds. With the opposition accusing the administration of subverting judicial independence and the DP pushing to secure legislative momentum for President Lee Jae-myung's agenda, the November 19 plenary session has become an uncompromising flashpoint.

Factional Fractures and Political Smokescreens

Compounding the legislative standoff is deepening unrest inside the ruling camp. A formal 90-degree bow of apology to President Lee by DP lawmaker Choi Min-hee failed to quell internal discontent. A group of first-term DP lawmakers, known as "Deo Mincho," initiated a signature drive seeking to suspend her party membership rights, sparking sharp pushback from pro-Lee loyalists who warned against damaging party unity. At the same time, National Assembly Speaker Cho Jeong-sik’s proposal to launch a constitutional amendment advisory committee was flatly rejected by the PPP, which dismissed the initiative as a transparent distraction to cover up the ruling party's judicial risks.

With both major parties consumed by internal factional struggles and retaliatory legislative tactics, routine budget reviews and public welfare measures have been pushed to the background. Unless party leadership can contain internal ruptures and establish a baseline for negotiation, public frustration with the legislative standstill is likely to intensify.
