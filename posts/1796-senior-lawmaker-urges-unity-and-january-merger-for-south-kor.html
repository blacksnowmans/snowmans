Senior Lawmaker Urges Unity and January Merger for South Korea's Ruling Party

November 01, 2026

Senior lawmaker Jung Chung-rae of South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea called for internal party unity on October 31 following controversy over liberal commentator Rhyu Si-min's recent remarks, while floating a January deadline to merge with the minor allied Rebuilding Korea Party.

  • Calls for Internal Tolerance: Responding to sharp criticisms surrounding public commentator Rhyu Si-min, Jung urged party members to embrace diverse viewpoints, comparing internal differences to the distinct factions within Korea’s historical independence movement.
  • 100-Day Leadership Benchmark: Jung set a clear 100-day deadline to evaluate the performance of party leadership under Kim Min-seok, hinting that he may step forward into a key political role after assessing the outcome.
  • January Merger Proposal: To streamline political momentum under President Lee Jae-myung and prepare for upcoming local elections, Jung proposed completing a full merger with the Rebuilding Korea Party no later than January.
  • Political Context: Although the Democratic Party’s approval ratings recently surged by 10 percentage points following the impeachment and removal of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the ruling party faces continued pressure from the conservative opposition People Power Party over internal scandals and lingering party infiltration allegations.

Navigating Internal Friction in the Post-Yoon Era

The recent friction within South Korea's ruling coalition highlights the delicate balancing act required to keep the progressive camp united under President Lee Jae-myung. Following controversial remarks by political writer Rhyu Si-min, party heavyweights moved quickly to control potential fallout. While lawmaker Park Ji-won publicly appealed to Rhyu to exercise self-restraint to prevent burdening the administration, Jung Chung-rae advocated a more conciliatory approach. Jung argued that internal debate is natural and that maintaining broad unity is essential for passing key reform legislation and warding off attacks from opposition rivals.

Setting Timelines for Party Leadership and Realignment

Beyond immediate damage control, Jung’s comments signal a strategic roadmap for party governance and upcoming elections. By establishing an explicit 100-day evaluation period for leadership under Kim Min-seok, Jung put current party officials on notice to deliver tangible legislative results. He also signaled his readiness to step up and raise his own political banner depending on how the next three months unfold, cementing his influence among the party's core base.

Crucially, Jung's proposal for a January deadline to absorb the Rebuilding Korea Party aims to consolidate the progressive bloc ahead of local elections. While some party insiders caution that a swift merger could complicate efforts to appeal to moderate voters, supporters argue that a unified front is necessary to fully dismantle the political legacy of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol and secure a stable legislative majority behind President Lee's national agenda.
