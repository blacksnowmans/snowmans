South Korea Faces a Reckoning Over AI Regulation and Global Information Warfare

October 05, 2026

  • Deepening domestic divisions: Public opinion in South Korea is tightly split on whether to accelerate AI development by minimizing regulation or to urgently apply brakes, with stark gender and generational divides among young adults.
  • Overlooked global threats: While international experts gather at events like a recent Lithuania conference to tackle multi-billion-dollar psychological and cognitive warfare operations, South Korean political leadership remains largely disengaged.
  • Economic and security risks: Observers warn that treating information warfare and advanced AI strictly as domestic political distractions risks reducing the country to an information colony.

South Korea finds itself caught between two fast-moving global realities: the breakneck, unregulated expansion of artificial intelligence and the invisible escalation of cross-border information warfare. Recent local polling illustrates a society deeply unsettled by the pace of technological change. While some argue for minimal oversight to maintain tech competitiveness, others demand strict safety controls, reflecting an anxiety that current ethical frameworks cannot keep up with machine intelligence.

This unease is especially pronounced among South Korea’s twenty- and thirty-somethings, where young men and women view the risks and rewards of tech-driven disruption through sharply different lenses. Yet, while the public wrestles with these survival-level economic anxieties, the nation's political establishment remains fixated on internal bickering. Observers point out that international cognitive warfare—spanning deepfakes, disinformation, and psychological ops—has evolved into a massive economic and geopolitical industry, leaving South Korea dangerously vulnerable to external manipulation while its leaders look the other way.
