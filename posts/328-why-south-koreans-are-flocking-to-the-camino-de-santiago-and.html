Why South Koreans are flocking to the Camino de Santiago, and what they wish they'd known first

September 07, 2026

  • The Trend: A Korean traveler who recently completed the 34-day, 800-kilometer (about 500 miles) Camino de Santiago pilgrimage shares a brutally honest survival guide.
  • The Costs: Total expenses for the journey ran about €1,525 (roughly $1,700), excluding flights and transit.
  • The Gear: Proper preparation—specifically lightweight backpacks, broken-in footwear, toe socks, and daily applications of petroleum jelly—can mean the difference between a spiritual awakening and a forced march of blisters.
  • The Takeaway: Beyond the physical strain, the pilgrimage offers weary modern workers a rare, stripped-down community of mutual respect that feels increasingly absent back home.

For many South Koreans in their 30s navigating high anxiety, economic uncertainty, and intense social pressures, the Camino de Santiago has become a coveted escape. But romanticizing the 800-kilometer Spanish trek without proper preparation is a fast track to misery. Veteran pilgrims emphasize that physical survival on the trail requires meticulous planning—from keeping total pack weight under 10% of your body weight to ditching unnecessary tech like laptops entirely.

Survival on the trail relies heavily on digital tools and street smarts. Pilgrims lean on apps like Camino Ninja for offline GPS navigation and WhatsApp to coordinate with local hostels and restaurants where English fails. Yet, despite the trail's reputation as a sanctuary of pure goodwill, petty theft remains a persistent hazard: gear left outside or unattended vanishes quickly. Beyond the physical blisters and unexpected costs, returning travelers argue that the true value of the roughly $4,000 total investment isn't just the cardboard completion certificate—it's rediscovering a basic human empathy that corporate life in Seoul rarely affords.
