President Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Plunges to 35% as Ruling Party Faces Grassroots Backlash

October 26, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating has dropped to roughly 35% against a 62.3% negative rating in a recent Realmeter survey, signaling deepening cracks within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea’s core base amid legislative paralysis and internal party discord.

  • Plunging Support: The recent Realmeter poll reveals that disapproval of the Lee administration has climbed past 62%, fueled by voter fatigue over economic livelihood issues and party infighting.
  • Troubling By-Election Deficit: In an upcoming parliamentary by-election in Gangneung—vacated after the disqualification of conservative lawmaker Kweon Seong-dong—the Democratic Party is trailing the main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) by 29.8% to 52.0%.
  • Fandom Media Backlash: Viewership on pro-Democratic YouTube channels such as Minti TV has dropped by more than half, reflecting open disaffection from traditional base voters who are rebelling against party leadership decisions.
  • Policy Implementation Gaps: Unresolved administrative disputes—most notably the central government's failure to shoulder the 90% local fiscal burden for newly nationalized firefighters—continue to erode the administration's reputation for competent governance.

Cracks in the Partisan Media Stronghold

The decline in Lee’s numbers is particularly damaging because it is driven not just by swing voters, but by disaffection among the Democratic Party's core loyalists. Digital public engagement metrics reveal a clear trend: live viewership on prominent progressive political channels has cratered from peaks of 55,000 down to the low 20,000s. Paradoxically, streams saw audience bumps when party leader Kim Min-seok was absent, reflecting a voter base actively signaling discontent with the party's current messaging rather than offering uncritical support.

Internal recriminations and distractions have compounded voter exhaustion. Lawmakers have spent weeks clashing over unverified claims regarding fringe religious groups and staging public disputes, prompting President Lee to openly express frustration with the pace of legislation, remarking how "stifling" the National Assembly has become. For a base that expected aggressive institutional reforms following the political turbulence of recent years, the current legislative deadlock feels like squandered momentum.

Electoral Warnings and Policy Fatigue

The 22-point deficit in the Gangneung by-election highlights the administration's shrinking electoral coalition beyond its progressive bastions. While Gangneung is traditionally conservative, a deficit of this scale demonstrates that independent and moderate voters are currently unresponsive to the Democratic Party's framing, punishing the ruling party for neglecting practical kitchen-table issues.

The controversy surrounding emergency service budgets exemplifies these broader governance bottlenecks. Despite public celebrations when municipal firefighters were reclassified as national civil servants, local municipal governments still shoulder 90% of the operational costs. With 77.7% of respondents in a Realmeter survey insisting that the central government must fund emergency services directly, prominent regional figures like Governor Choo Mi-ae have openly demanded fiscal structural reforms. Unless the ruling party pivots away from factional disputes and delivers tangible legislative outcomes on public services and the economy, the administration risks entrenching a hostile governing majority ahead of the next national electoral tests.
