South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Rating Climbs to 37.9%

September 30, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung has seen a slight recovery in his approval rating, snapping a recent downward trend, according to the latest data from Realmeter, a prominent domestic polling agency.

  • The Numbers: President Lee’s approval rating rose 3.1 percentage points to 37.9% for the fourth week of September.
  • The Trend: While still below the 40% threshold, the uptick marks a critical inflection point after a period of steady decline.
  • The Context: The survey, conducted among 1,508 adults, highlights a polarized political environment where the public remains cautious rather than fully supportive.

A Fragile Rebound

In South Korea’s highly sensitive political climate, a 3.1% jump is significant, yet it hardly signals clear sailing for the administration. The rise suggests that the public’s frustration may have hit a temporary ceiling, allowing the government a narrow window to regain momentum. However, political analysts warn that this is a "precarious equilibrium"—any policy misstep or mishandled crisis could easily send these numbers back into a tailspin.

The core challenge for President Lee is that 37.9% reflects a "wait-and-see" sentiment among the undecided majority rather than a surge of hardened loyalty. For the administration, the task ahead is to convert this incomplete positivity into a more robust mandate. This will require moving beyond rhetoric and addressing the tangible economic anxieties—inflation, housing, and job insecurity—that continue to weigh heavily on the average South Korean household.

Policy and Personnel: The Path Forward

Political commentators have been quick to note that numerical stability is not a substitute for effective governance. If the President fails to demonstrate decisive leadership, this rebound will be remembered as nothing more than a statistical blip.

The Critical Role of Personnel

A recurring theme in the domestic debate is the necessity of "personnel reform." Critics argue that if the President’s inner circle remains insular or detached from public reality, no amount of policy adjustments will suffice. The administration is being urged to embrace a broader range of voices and prioritize expertise over political loyalty. In a system where presidential power is immense, the composition of the cabinet and advisory team serves as the ultimate litmus test for whether the government is truly interested in course-correction or merely in protecting its political turf.

Ultimately, the President’s success will depend on whether he can prove that his administration is responsive to the lived realities of citizens. As the political landscape remains volatile, the coming months will reveal whether this 37.9% is a foundation for reform or a final gasp before further stagnation.
