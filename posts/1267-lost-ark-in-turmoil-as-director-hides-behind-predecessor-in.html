Lost Ark in Turmoil as Director Hides Behind Predecessor in Crisis Stream

October 18, 2026

Smilegate RPG’s flagship title Lost Ark has plunged into a leadership crisis after current game director Jae-hak Jeon sent former chief Keum Kang-sun to face an angry player base in his place during an emergency live broadcast.

  • What happened: Facing fierce backlash over raid design and systemic neglect, current director Jae-hak Jeon avoided the camera and sent former director Keum Kang-sun—who stepped down to work on Smilegate's upcoming project "Orpheus"—to absorb criticism on a surprise livestream.
  • The breaking point: Player outrage boiled over following severe class balance failures in the latest end-game raid, "Mordum," which left multiple classes effectively barred from group finder parties, alongside rapid hyperinflation of in-game gold.
  • The content slump: Fans are rebelling against uninspired recycled content, restrictive character-bound reward systems, and the indignity of Korean players having to import raid strategies from North American and Chinese servers.
  • Who is affected: South Korea's core MMORPG community, streamers, and Smilegate RPG, whose premier live-service franchise is hemorrhaging player trust and active users.

A Total Collapse of Executive Accountability

The broadcast was intended to soothe a burning community, but sending Keum back to the podium backfired entirely. While Keum remains a beloved figure credited with reviving Lost Ark during previous slumps, he no longer holds the operational authority to tune class numbers or rewrite development roadmaps. Keum admitted on air that Jeon had visited him confessing that he struggled to set the game's direction and feared facing the community due to "misunderstandings." For players, watching a current leader push a former executive forward as a human shield represents an abdication of duty.

Communication in live-service gaming only works when the person holding the microphone also holds the power to act. Keum could offer little more than vague emotional appeals to calm the chat, even remarking that he would simply take the insults himself. Rather than projecting confidence, the spectacle underscored a leadership vacuum at the very top of one of South Korea's biggest gaming properties.

Scale Without Direction

The controversy exposes deep technical and design rot in Lost Ark’s current lifecycle. Despite Smilegate expanding its core development personnel from around 15 to 57, content delivery has slowed while quality control has deteriorated. Boss mechanics have grown increasingly punishing without accounting for class archetypes, forcing players to abandon long-standing characters. Meanwhile, recycled encounters with inflated statistics have replaced meaningful progression, and the value of in-game gold has collapsed to roughly 34,000 to 35,000 gold per 2,750 won (about $2.00), wiping out the economic incentive to play.

As past missteps across South Korea's domestic gaming industry have demonstrated, hiding behind legacy figureheads and dodging accountability is a recipe for user exodus. By relying on administrative excuses rather than facing the community directly, Lost Ark’s current leadership risks permanently breaking the social contract that made the game a global success.
