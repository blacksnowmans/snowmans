South Korea's Ruling Party Shaken by Claims of Cult Infiltration and Internal Purges

October 16, 2026

Kim Min-seok, leader of South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea, has publicly alleged that the controversial religious sect Shincheonji systematically infiltrated the party's August 17 convention to manipulate primary results, sparking fierce accusations of illegal surveillance and manufactured political purges.

  • Mass Infiltration Allegations: Kim announced that a party review task force verified significant, coordinated memberships and collected testimonies showing Shincheonji—a fringe, secretive religious group—meddled in the party's internal leadership races.
  • Data Privacy and Legal Exposure: The revelation immediately raised legal alarms over how party administrators, lacking statutory investigative authority, managed to acquire and cross-check private religious rosters against the party's membership registry.
  • Factional Power Play: Political observers suspect the move is an orchestrated maneuver backed by President Lee Jae-myung to purge outspoken figures like Representative Jung Chung-rae under the guise of an organizational clean-up.
  • Calls for External Investigation: Facing backlash over party autocracy and double standards, lawmakers face growing demands to hand the case to the Serious Crime Investigation Agency or state prosecutors ahead of the upcoming legislative elections.

A Dangerous Precedent and a Political Boomerang

The Democratic Party frequently attacked the conservative establishment during the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who was later impeached and is now imprisoned—over alleged links to Shincheonji and other religious movements, framing such ties as unconstitutional breaches of church-state separation. By confirming that Shincheonji members penetrated its own base, the ruling party faces an acute political boomerang. Crucially, without a transparent explanation of how private religious registries were matched with party rolls, the leadership leaves itself exposed to accusations of unlawful data acquisition and internal intimidation.

Consolidating Power Under President Lee Jae-myung

A disclosure of this magnitude from the ruling party leader cannot happen in a vacuum. It almost certainly reflects the tacit approval, if not the direct directive, of President Lee Jae-myung. Observers suggest that loyalists like Kim Min-seok are using the Shincheonji taint as a convenient bludgeon to sideline influential party insiders—chief among them Jung Chung-rae—who could otherwise contest the administration's tight grip over the party apparatus.

Rather than strengthening procedural democracy, labeling intra-party competitors as sectarian stooges risks driving away centrist voters and alienating core supporters. With general elections drawing near, the conservative opposition People Power Party is already seizing on the chaos, framing the ruling camp's self-proclaimed crisis as clear evidence that internal self-policing has failed and that external law enforcement must intervene.
