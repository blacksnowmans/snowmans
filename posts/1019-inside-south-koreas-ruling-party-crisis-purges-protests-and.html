Inside South Korea's Ruling Party Crisis: Purges, Protests, and the Battle for the Democratic Party's Soul

October 12, 2026

South Korea’s progressive ruling party is tearing itself apart over an internal crackdown on dissent that critics say mimics the authoritarian tactics of the past.

  • The Crackdown:
    •  South Korea's Justice Ministry, led by Justice Minister Yoon Ho-jung, has ordered a disciplinary probe into senior prosecutor Lim Eun-jung for publicly criticizing the administration, a move critics denounce as "politics of fear."
  • Party Infighting:
    •  Gyeonggi Province Governor Choo Mi-ae has publicly lashed out at the Democratic Party leadership for demanding her confidential schedule, accusing party bosses of launching political witch-hunts against veteran figures.
  • The Ideological Rift:
    •  Critics inside and outside the liberal camp argue that President Lee Jae-myung has hijacked the Democratic Party (the People Power Party's main conservative rival), sidelining its foundational reformist values to consolidate personal power.
  • Who Is Affected:
    •  Reform-minded prosecutors, veteran liberal politicians, and rank-and-file party members who feel alienated by a leadership prioritizing loyalty over democratic norms.

The Irony of Prosecutorial Purges Under a Progressive Government

For decades, South Korea’s progressive camp fought tooth and nail against opaque prosecutorial power and the suppression of internal whistleblowers. Yet, the current disciplinary investigation into senior prosecutor Lim Eun-jung—known long before for speaking truth to power—turns that legacy on its head. Justice Minister Yoon Ho-jung has defended the probe by framing Lim's critical remarks as impermissible "political action." Labeling honest dissent as a political threat under the guise of institutional reform is a bitter pill for voters who rallied against past conservative excesses, particularly under impeached former leader Yoon Seok-yeol, who is currently imprisoned.

This heavy-handed approach goes beyond one prosecutor. Tensions have boiled over between the party leadership and veteran figures like Gyeonggi Governor Choo Mi-ae, who publicly criticized the Democratic Party for weaponizing routine administrative requests to undermine independent local leaders. Observers note that these moves reflect a broader consolidation of power by loyalists tied directly to President Lee Jae-myung, alienating core party veterans who trace their political lineage back to former presidents Kim Dae-jung, Roh Moo-hyun, and Moon Jae-in.

A Party at a Crossroads

The core grievance among critics is that the current administration is treating the Democratic Party less like a democratic institution and more like a personal political vehicle. By demanding silence, suppressing internal critics, and prioritizing personal loyalty over debate, the leadership risks alienating the very citizens who brought them to power. As public unease grows over what many are calling a betrayal of democratic principles, the test ahead is whether party members and the public can reclaim the party's true identity before irreversible damage is done.
