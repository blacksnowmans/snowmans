Lee Jae-myung’s Administration Slips Into Dead-Duck Territory Amid Foreign Policy Rumors and Political Backlash

October 12, 2026

  • South Korea's liberal administration, in power for just over a year, is facing severe governance fatigue, struggling to fill key roles like the presidential chief of staff, while unverified rumors of an imminent overseas troop deployment circulate in political circles.
  • President Lee Jae-myung has drawn heavy criticism for invoking former progressive presidents Roh Moo-hyun and Park Won-soon on social media to defend his administration's combative online interactions and block critics.
  • The unfolding political crisis threatens to alienate the public further, as critics accuse the administration of dodging domestic scrutiny and economic discontent through rhetorical defenses and historical comparisons.

Barely fifteen months into his term, South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is confronting a sharp loss of political momentum. With key cabinet positions repeatedly turned down and vacancies unfilled, political insiders in Yeouido are openly whispering about a "dead-duck" presidency. Rather than addressing these structural hurdles directly, the administration has increasingly relied on defensive rhetoric, pulling historical figures into the public discourse to deflect mounting criticism.

A primary flashpoint has been Lee’s active presence on X (formerly Twitter), where he frequently engages in real-time spars with critics. To justify blocking hostile accounts and pushing back against negative sentiment, the president has invoked the legacy of late progressive President Roh Moo-hyun and late Seoul Mayor Park Won-soon, framing his combative communication style as a continuation of their struggles. However, critics argue that using past leaders as a shield exposes an unwillingness to absorb legitimate public dissent, reducing presidential communication to defensive posturing.

Troop Deployment Rumors and the Danger of Historical Parallels

Compounding the administration's political woes are unconfirmed intelligence rumors circulating in political circles regarding an imminent overseas troop deployment. Critics draw parallels to the Roh Moo-hyun administration's controversial decision to dispatch troops to Iraq in the 2000s, which cost billions of won (over 700 billion KRW) and drew fierce domestic backlash. Invoking Roh's precedent to justify modern military actions appears designed to preempt opposition scrutiny, but lawmakers and civil society groups warn that utilizing foreign policy decisions to mask domestic weakness is a high-stakes gamble.

The reliance on political deflection highlights a deeper vulnerability within the ruling Democratic Party, where many lawmakers remain silent while a core faction attempts to manage the narrative. As public fatigue deepens amid economic pressures, the administration’s strategy of leaning on historical shields risks accelerating its political isolation.
