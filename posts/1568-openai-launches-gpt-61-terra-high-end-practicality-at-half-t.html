OpenAI Launches GPT-6.1 Terra: High-End Practicality at Half the Price

October 28, 2026

OpenAI has released GPT-6.1 Terra, a mid-tier model designed to slash workplace automation costs in half compared to its higher-end sibling Sol—provided users avoid the hidden trap of excessive reasoning settings.

  • A Four-Tier Lineup: OpenAI has organized its offerings into four distinct tiers—Astra, Sol, Terra, and Luna—with Terra priced at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, exactly half the price of GPT-6.1 Sol and roughly one-quarter the cost of Anthropic’s Opus 5.5.
  • Practical Workplace Gains: In routine administrative benchmarks such as the Agent's Last Exam (ALE)—which tests blueprint conversion and record-keeping—Terra scored 58.5 at $0.66 per task, outperforming Sol’s 56.5 at $1.45 while keeping hallucination rates down to 5.0%.
  • Coding and Control Limits: Terra hits a clear ceiling on advanced software engineering; on Terminal-Bench 4.0, Sol running on medium reasoning easily beat Terra pushed to its maximum reasoning setting at almost half the cost ($1.02 vs. $1.84).
  • Enterprise Security Credential: Despite its mid-tier status, Terra achieved the top "CRITICAL" cybersecurity risk rating under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, inheriting the vulnerability detection and payload analysis safeguards previously reserved for flagship models.

The "Reasoning Diet" and the Token Paradox

The biggest pitfall with Terra is assuming a half-price token fee automatically cuts enterprise bills in half. Terra features six granular levels of reasoning effort—from "none" to "maximum"—and every step spent thinking accumulates directly as billable output tokens. When confronted with difficult logic, cheaper models tend to deliberate longer and write more verbose responses. In benchmark testing, Sol completed an evaluation run using 67 million tokens, whereas Terra consumed 130 million tokens on the exact same challenge, completely erasing its 50% unit-price advantage.

Matching the Tier to the Task

Terra is not an Astra or Sol replacement for heavy engineering; it is a dedicated workhorse for high-volume enterprise pipelines. Integrated into ChatGPT Work and Codex, it handles repetitive paperwork, automated invoice data entry, form processing, and visual document inspection with equal or superior accuracy to higher models at a fraction of the budget. For multi-step desktop orchestration and complex script architecture, OpenAI explicitly maintains that Astra and Sol remain necessary.

The lesson for companies deploying generative AI is that default reliance on the most expensive flagship model is an outdated and wasteful habit. The decisive factor in AI ROI is no longer picking the most capable model on paper, but engineering precise pipelines where model tiers and reasoning dials are matched strictly to the complexity of the workflow.
