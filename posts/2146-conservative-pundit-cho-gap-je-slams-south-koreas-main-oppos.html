Conservative Pundit Cho Gap-je Slams South Korea's Main Opposition Over Far-Right "Conspiracies"

November 09, 2026

Veteran conservative commentator Cho Gap-je has issued a scathing critique of the People Power Party (PPP) leadership, warning that their reliance on far-right conspiracy theories makes them easy prey for the ruling Democratic Party.

  • Internal Conservative Fractures: Cho Gap-je, a legendary figure in conservative media, attacked PPP leaders like Jang Dong-hyeok and Jeong Jeom-sik, claiming their failure to purge election-fraud conspiracy theorists ensures they will never defeat the Democratic Party.
  • Shifting Political Chessboard: While the main opposition struggles with its identity, Kim Chol-geun of the Reform Party—a minor centrist-conservative group—has moved his candidacy to the strategic hub of Yongin to target younger voters.
  • Past Corruption Surfacing: New reports have surfaced regarding a far-right YouTuber’s sister who held a "side job" for profit while employed in the Presidential Office during the term of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president who was recently impeached and imprisoned.
  • Global Far-Right Violence: The briefing notes a chilling rise in global extremism, including a mass shooting in Kraków, Poland, carried out by a suspect motivated by far-right ideologies, resulting in six deaths.

The PPP’s Struggle to Escape the "Far-Right Trap"

The critique from Cho Gap-je represents a significant moment of self-reflection within the conservative camp. By labeling the party leadership as "stupid far-right" being fed to "clever far-lefts," Cho is highlighting a strategic paralysis. The People Power Party (PPP), South Korea's main conservative opposition, remains haunted by the legacy of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol. Instead of offering policy alternatives to President Lee Jae-myung’s administration, the PPP has largely retreated into outdoor rallies and inflammatory rhetoric, labeling special counsel bills as "jailbreak laws."

This reliance on "YouTuber politics"—where high-decibel conspiracy theories about rigged elections drive engagement—is alienating moderate voters. While the PPP blames recent volatility in the KOSPI (the benchmark Korean stock index) on the current government's policies, their own internal chaos and refusal to accept procedural democracy make them a difficult sell for the general public. As Cho Gap-je noted, a party that cannot distinguish between constitutional order and conspiracy theories is fundamentally unfit for governance.

Extremism as a Global Business and Security Threat

The briefing highlights that far-right extremism is not just a Korean political hurdle but a dangerous global trend. In Europe and the US, this brand of politics has become a lucrative, albeit deadly, business. For instance, US far-right figure Laura Loomer reportedly receives about $5,000 (7 million KRW) per post to spread political agitation. This "hate-for-profit" model mirrors the situation in Korea, where certain influencers thrived under the previous administration, even gaining entry into the Presidential Office—the equivalent of the White House—under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

The consequences of this rhetoric are increasingly violent. The tragic shooting in Poland and the disruption of the Diwali festival in Seattle by far-right "hooligans" demonstrate that when mainstream parties fail to distance themselves from radical elements, it emboldens domestic terrorism. For the PPP to regain relevance in the Lee Jae-myung era, it must decide whether it wants to be a functioning legislative body or a platform for the fringes of the internet. Without a clean break from the "impeachment-era" conspiracies, the Korean right risks becoming a cautionary tale of political obsolescence.

"Stupid far-right elements are simply food for the clever far-left. With these faces, the PPP will never win, even if they were born again." — Cho Gap-je, conservative commentator.
