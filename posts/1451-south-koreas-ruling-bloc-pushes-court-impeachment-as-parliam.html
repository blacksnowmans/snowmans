South Korea’s Ruling Bloc Pushes Court Impeachment as Parliamentary Audits Devolve into Viral Theater

October 25, 2026

South Korea’s National Assembly has plunged into bitter conflict as the ruling Democratic Party floated impeaching Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, while lawmakers across the aisle sidelined critical policy oversight in favor of algorithm-driven social media stunts.

  • Judiciary in the crosshairs: Following a high-stakes appellate ruling, members of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea suggested impeaching Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, prompting the main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) to accuse the ruling bloc of an unconstitutional assault on judicial independence.
  • Rise of ‘Shorts politics’: The annual parliamentary audit of state affairs, traditionally designed to scrutinize government policy and budgets, degraded into theatrical, staged screaming matches designed to harvest viral clips on YouTube Shorts and TikTok.
  • Conservative identity dispute: Ruling Democratic Party leaders demanded the PPP dismiss controversial Korea Communications Commission head Lee Jin-sook over far-right sympathies or take down party portraits of the late Kim Young-sam, the conservative president celebrated for dismantling military influence in the 1990s.
  • Global unrest: The domestic turmoil unfolded against a backdrop of acute economic frustration worldwide, illustrated by massive cost-of-living and anti-far-right protests across France that shut down the Louvre and drew 70,000 riot police.

The Perils of Algorithmic Partisanship

The push by ruling-party lawmakers to impeach the head of the supreme court signals a dangerous escalation in how Seoul treats the judiciary. Rather than functioning as a final arbiter of legal disputes, the bench has been transformed into an extension of the political battlefield. Threatening impeachment over judicial decisions subverts constitutional checks and balances. When judicial rulings are routinely treated as political hit jobs, the baseline public trust required for the rule of law dissolves.

This institutional decay is being accelerated by the incentives of social media. The parliamentary audit is meant to rigorously assess state spending, structural inequality, and persistent economic pressures. Instead, it has been hollowed out by "Shorts politics," where lawmakers engineer one-minute outbursts of outrage to mobilize hardline supporters and rake in political donations. Complex policy deliberations cannot survive inside an algorithmic echo chamber that rewards only humiliation and rage.

A Deepening Crisis of Democratic Norms

The simultaneous dispute over broadcasting chief Lee Jin-sook underscores the conservative opposition's ongoing crisis of direction. Since the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the People Power Party has struggled to disentangle itself from hard-right YouTube agitation and ideological revisionism. By defending figures associated with extreme historical revisionism, the party risks repudiating the democratic, reformist heritage established by conservative leaders like Kim Young-sam.

Seoul’s political class is prioritizing performative combat while ordinary households battle high living costs and economic stagnation. As seen in the explosive streets of France, ignoring basic bread-and-butter livelihood issues in favor of political extremism eventually invites severe public backlash. If legislative politics remains trapped between viral algorithms and institutional sabotage, South Korea's democratic foundations will face enduring structural strain.
