Ruling Party Factional Clash Deepens Over Ethics Probe and Unproven Cult Claim

November 27, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is engulfed in an escalating factional dispute over disciplinary fairness after party leader Kim Min-seok referred Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee to an ethics tribunal, prompting rivals to highlight Kim's own admission that his past convention claim of a secret alliance involving the Shincheonji religious group lacked evidence.

  • The Choi Min-hee referral: According to Money Today and TV Chosun, Kim ordered an internal ethics inspection on the 12th and referred Choi to the DPK Ethics Tribunal on the 15th over closed-door remarks alleging President Lee Jae-myung had opposed the impeachment of Yoon Suk-yeol while serving as party head.
  • The convention backlash: TV Chosun reported that Gyeonggi Province Governor Choo Mi-ae criticized Kim for acknowledging—four months after winning the August 17 leadership convention—that he had no proof for alleging an "anti-Lee, divisionist, and Shincheonji three-way secret alliance," arguing the tribunal must first weigh potential false-statement violations under Article 52 of the Political Parties Act.
  • Charges of double standards: At a Supreme Council meeting on the 27th, Choi and colleagues aligned with targeted lawmaker Jung Chung-rae—including Lee Seong-yoon and Han Min-soo—questioned why Choi's case accelerated while a disciplinary petition against Kim remains pending, with Jung warning that vague apologies without accountability invite future smear campaigns.
  • Spillover beyond the party: Mediation talks among 12 senior DPK lawmakers stalled without Kim or Jung attending, while police raided YouTuber Lee Jong-won on the 20th over separate allegations of rigging convention preference polls, underscoring how unresolved party disputes are spilling into criminal investigations.

What the Stand-off Means for DPK Leadership

The dispute appears to have trapped Kim in a difficult political bind. Commentators on the political YouTube channel Sisageongeon argued that heavily penalizing Choi risks uniting party members behind demands to discipline Kim over his unsubstantiated convention claim, whereas backing down could draw fierce criticism from pro-Lee commentators such as Lee Dong-young who have closely tracked the case. Conversely, the DPK leadership maintains that verifying remarks concerning the sitting president's past stance through the Ethics Inspection Office and Ethics Tribunal is a routine and necessary party procedure.

Beneath the personal rivalry lies a broader credibility problem for internal party justice mechanisms across South Korean politics. Critics on Sisageongeon questioned the Ethics Tribunal's independence by pointing to vice-chair Moon Jin-seok's own past influence-peddling controversy, while the DPK is also watching how judicial intervention shapes party autonomy after a Seoul court granted an injunction suspending the conservative opposition People Power Party's expulsion of former Supreme Council member Kim Jong-hyuk.

With grassroots activists planning a rally outside National Assembly Station calling for Kim's resignation and opposing Choi's punishment—alongside controversial demands to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-dae—the DPK's leadership faces mounting pressure from its base. Whether the party can resolve the competing petitions through transparent rules, rather than factional bargaining or external lawsuits, will serve as an early test of its governing cohesion under the Lee Jae-myung administration.
