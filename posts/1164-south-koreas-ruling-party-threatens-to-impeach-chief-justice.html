South Korea’s Ruling Party Threatens to Impeach Chief Justice Over Kim Keon-hee Warrant Delay

October 15, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has signaled it may launch impeachment proceedings against Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, accusing the nation's top court of deliberately stalling an arrest warrant review concerning Kim Keon-hee, the wife of imprisoned former leader Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

  • The ultimatum: Senior ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) lawmaker Kim Min-seok announced that the window for Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae's voluntary resignation has expired, warning that judicial foot-dragging on procedural assignments has crossed into unconstitutional territory.
  • The procedural dispute: The controversy centers on an arrest warrant re-appeal regarding Kim Keon-hee; ruling party officials argue that the customary seven-day window to process the case and assign it to a bench has been clouded by opaque delays.
  • Legislative leverage: DPK spokesperson Cho Gye-won confirmed the party is weighing all legal actions, including impeachment, timed to coincide with a forthcoming special counsel bill against allegedly fabricated indictments following the annual parliamentary audit.
  • Institutional impact: If pursued by the DPK-controlled legislature under President Lee Jae-myung's administration, the move would mark an extraordinary confrontation between parliament and the judicial leadership, testing the constitutional standards for removing top judges.

The 7-Day Window and Allegations of Courtroom Foot-Dragging

The immediate flashpoint is the procedural mechanism governing Supreme Court bench assignments. Following the delivery of case documents regarding Kim Keon-hee's arrest warrant, standard practice dictates the prompt filing of re-appeals and bench allocation within roughly seven days. For the DPK, the pace of assigning a presiding justice has become a litmus test. DPK lawmakers allege that the top court is affording undue protection to the former first lady, echoing what they describe as systemic delays that characterized the justice system during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s time in office.

Proponents of the push argue that judicial independence protects judges from external political pressure, not from the law itself. From this viewpoint, a Chief Justice who bends administrative procedures or shields sensitive figures from swift adjudication is committing a severe constitutional breach that meets the threshold for impeachment.

A High-Stakes Clash Between the Lee Administration's Allies and the Bench

This escalating confrontation is not an isolated procedural skirmish; it forms the opening salvo of a broader drive to overhaul the judiciary. Following the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the governing DPK under President Lee Jae-myung has aggressively challenged traditional legal elites. The push against Cho is designed to converge with upcoming special legislation targeting alleged prosecutorial overreach and manufactured indictments.

The political risk is immense. Removing a sitting Chief Justice requires proving grave constitutional violations rather than mere administrative disputes or ideological grievances. While the DPK frames this as an essential cleansing of an entrenched judicial hierarchy to restore public trust, critics see a dangerous precedent that could compromise the independence of the bench whenever politically sensitive rulings hang in the balance.
