Middle East Escalation Triggers Oil Spike and Supply Chain Shock

November 13, 2026

Escalating military tensions between Israel and Iran, compounded by U.S. President-elect Donald Trump’s refusal to negotiate with Teheran, are driving up global energy prices and ocean shipping costs, posing a direct threat to South Korea’s export-reliant economy.

  • Trump’s Hard Line & Iranian Retaliation: President-elect Trump’s declaration that he will not negotiate with Iran sent crude oil prices rising over 1%, while Teheran responded by showcasing underground "missile cities" and warning that strikes could reach U.S. bases in Europe.
  • Friction in Jerusalem: Strategic misalignments have surfaced between Trump—who favors a quick conclusion to hostilities—and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who seeks to eliminate Iranian threats while facing internal Israeli political disputes over military command authority.
  • Shipping Rate Surge: Heightened security risks in key maritime corridors like the Red Sea have sent global freight rates soaring, driving quarterly operating profits at HMM (South Korea’s flagship container shipping carrier) above 1 trillion won (about $720 million).
  • Spillover to Seoul: Rising import costs and ocean freight rates threaten to fuel domestic inflation in South Korea, prompting President Lee Jae-myung’s administration to closely monitor global supply chain bottlenecks and domestic price stability.

Geopolitical Brinkmanship Hits the Global Market

The diplomatic transition period in Washington has accelerated a dangerous game of military escalation. Trump’s signals of a revived "Maximum Pressure" strategy have effectively closed the door on near-term diplomatic compromise, prompting Teheran to showcase hardened subterranean military assets and widen its regional threats. Meanwhile, Israel’s leadership continues aggressive military operations despite domestic political debates over authorization processes and mounting international outcry over civilian casualties, including a Gaza hospital strike that left at least six dead and 20 injured.

Why South Korea Is on High Alert

For South Korea, conflict in the Middle East translates directly into elevated energy bills and supply chain friction. While local shipping giant HMM reaped windfall quarterly profits exceeding 1 trillion won (approx. $720 million) due to supply-constrained ocean freight rates, the broader macroeconomic picture is far less positive. Surging logistics expenses and volatile energy prices threaten to feed directly into domestic consumer prices, creating an immediate policy headache for the Lee Jae-myung administration as it works to protect domestic consumption and support Korean exporters.
