Nvidia Bets on South Korea as the Global Hub for "Physical AI"

November 10, 2026

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has declared South Korea the strategic epicenter for the next decade of "Physical AI," signaling a shift from digital software to AI that interacts directly with the physical world through robotics and industrial automation.

  • The Shift to Physical AI: At "Nvidia AI Day Seoul," the company moved beyond generative text to showcase AI integrated into factory lines, autonomous ships, and humanoid robots.
  • Key Partnerships: Industry leaders including Hyundai Motor, LG, Naver (Korea’s leading search and AI portal), and Doosan Robotics are collaborating with Nvidia to build a "real-world" AI ecosystem.
  • Sovereign AI Strategy: The event emphasized "Sovereign AI," a movement to ensure nations and companies maintain control over their own data and infrastructure while utilizing global technology.
  • Semiconductor Synergy: Nvidia is deepening ties with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and working with local startups like Rebellions to foster a domestic AI chip ecosystem.

From Code to the Factory Floor

The core message from Nvidia is that the next frontier of artificial intelligence is not found on a screen, but on the factory floor. By leveraging Korea’s massive manufacturing DNA—spanning cars, appliances, and shipbuilding—Nvidia aims to turn industrial hardware into "agentic" entities capable of making real-time decisions. For instance, Doosan Robotics is evolving its collaborative robots into industrial humanoids that can perceive and navigate complex environments, rather than just following pre-programmed paths.

This transition relies heavily on Nvidia Omniverse, a simulation platform that allows companies like LG to test manufacturing processes in a virtual environment before deploying them in reality. This "Digital Twin" approach reduces trial and error and maximizes efficiency in high-stakes environments like shipyards and biotech labs. For the Lee Jae-myung administration, which has prioritized a "competent economic state," these partnerships represent a concrete realization of industrial policy that was often seen as opaque or lacking during the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol.

Data Sovereignty and the Social Safety Net

While the technological showcase was impressive, the briefing also highlighted the geopolitical and social stakes of the AI race. The concept of Sovereign AI is particularly resonant in Korea. By partnering with Naver and SK Telecom, Nvidia is providing the hardware backbone for AI models that understand the specific linguistic and cultural nuances of Korea, ensuring that the country's digital "territory" remains under domestic control.

However, the rapid advancement of Physical AI brings significant social challenges. As robots become more capable of replacing human labor in dangerous or repetitive tasks, the gap between large conglomerates and small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) could widen. There is an emerging consensus that the government must provide public AI cloud infrastructure to prevent a "tech monopoly." Furthermore, as the Lee Jae-myung government pursues its vision of a "People-Centered" economy, the integration of technology must be balanced with social safety nets—such as job transition training—to ensure that the benefits of automation are not reserved solely for capital owners.

"Korea is the core of the physical AI journey for the next ten years." — Jensen Huang, Nvidia CEO

Ultimately, the partnership with Nvidia is a double-edged sword. It offers South Korea a seat at the head of the global AI table, but it also risks deep dependency on a single foreign provider. The challenge for the current administration and Korea’s tech giants will be to stand on the shoulders of this "AI giant" without losing the ability to walk on their own.
