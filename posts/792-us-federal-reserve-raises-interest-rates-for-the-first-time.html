US Federal Reserve Raises Interest Rates for the First Time in Three Years

September 17, 2026

  • The US Federal Reserve voted unanimously to raise its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to a range of 4% to 4.25%, breaking a freeze held since July 2023.
  • The move triggered turbulence across markets, pushing the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield back above 5% and driving the US dollar index past 100.
  • Stubbornly high inflation—which has outpaced the Fed's target for five consecutive years—and escalating Middle East geopolitical risks point toward a prolonged period of high borrowing costs.

The Federal Reserve has officially broken its long pause on monetary policy, delivering a 25 basis point rate hike that caught some investors flat-footed. While initial market losses were surprisingly modest given the severity of the pivot, the real shock came from the central bank's hawkish tone. Officials made it clear that after five years of above-target inflation, they are in no rush to ease up, shutting down hopes that this would be a one-off "insurance cut."

Bond markets reacted far more violently than equities. The 10-year Treasury yield punched back above 5%, a level that acts as the beating heart of global borrowing costs. When money stays this expensive for too long, the cracks multiply. While headline economic indicators look resilient, high-interest-rate pressures are already squeezing individual households and pushing vulnerable businesses toward bankruptcy. Compounding the economic strain, brewing conflicts in the Middle East—including attacks near Saudi Arabia and supply chain bottlenecks—are keeping energy prices stubbornly high, leaving the Fed with very few easy options.
