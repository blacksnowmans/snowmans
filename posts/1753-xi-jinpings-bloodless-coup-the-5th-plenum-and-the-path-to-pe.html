Xi Jinping’s Bloodless Coup: The 5th Plenum and the Path to Perpetual Rule

October 31, 2026

Chinese leader Xi Jinping has effectively cleared the path for a fourth term following a massive political purge of the military elite during the Communist Party’s latest high-level meeting.

  • The 5th Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee confirmed the expulsion of 33 top officials, including General Zhang Youxia, the military’s second-in-command and Vice Chairman of the Central Military Commission.
  • Official data revealed that a staggering 7.92 million party members have been disciplined under anti-corruption campaigns since Xi took power 14 years ago, a scale equivalent to the population of a small nation.
  • The meeting formally introduced "Xi Jinping Thought on Party Building," while notably leaving high-level vacancies unfilled to prevent any potential successors from emerging.
  • The purge of 16 generals and dozens of senior officials signals a shift from a national military to a private force subservient only to Xi, heightening the risk of military miscalculation in the Taiwan Strait.

A Military Reborn in Xi’s Image

The fall of Zhang Youxia is the most significant tremors to hit Beijing in years. Once considered a loyal ally, his removal—along with 16 other generals—proves that in the current climate, loyalty has an expiration date. By labeling even his closest military associates as "corrupt" or "undisciplined," Xi has sent a chilling message to the People’s Liberation Army (PLA): the only path to survival is absolute, unquestioning obedience. This is no longer a government focused on collective leadership; it is a cult of personality that has dismantled the internal checks and balances established after the Mao era.

This atmosphere of fear has paralyzed the Chinese bureaucracy. When nearly 8 million officials have been punished over 14 years, the resulting "zombie state" avoids taking initiative for fear of being the next target. This internal rot is particularly dangerous during China’s current economic downturn. Without officials willing to speak truth to power or suggest creative solutions to the property crisis, Beijing is flying blind. The focus has shifted entirely from governance to survival, with "Xi Jinping Thought" serving as the mandatory operating system for every civil servant.

The Geopolitical Toll of Absolute Power

For the administration of President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, this hardening of Chinese autocracy presents a grave security challenge. A Chinese military purged of its "realist" veteran commanders is a military more likely to engage in adventurism. Without senior voices to warn of the logistical nightmares or the risks of American intervention, the threshold for a move against Taiwan has dropped significantly. Seoul now faces an neighbor that is increasingly unpredictable and motivated by a hyper-nationalistic agenda designed to distract from domestic instability.

South Korea’s "pragmatic diplomacy" will be pushed to its limit. While the Lee government seeks to manage economic ties, the reality of a one-man regime in Beijing requires a much more robust alignment with democratic allies. We must remember that when a dictator faces no internal opposition, external aggression often becomes the only tool left to maintain legitimacy. The 5th Plenum was not just a meeting about party discipline; it was a declaration that China has abandoned the path of reform for a permanent state of political warfare.
