Lee Jae-myung’s "Sovereign Diplomacy" Bolsters Approval Amid Calls for Judicial Impeachment

October 19, 2026

President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating has stabilized at 41.4% as the South Korean public signals strong support for his assertive "sovereign diplomacy" toward the United States and his commitment to democratic legacies.

  • Approval Rebound: President Lee’s approval rating rose 2.0 percentage points to 41.4% this week, buoyed by his attendance at the 47th anniversary of the Buma Democratic Uprising.
  • Sovereign Diplomacy: Approximately 56% of South Koreans support Lee’s "sovereign and independent" foreign policy, which explicitly rejects "subservient" ties with Washington.
  • Judicial Crisis: Public distrust in the legal system has reached a tipping point, with over 51% of citizens supporting the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.
  • Conservative Fracture: In a major shift, more than 50% of supporters of the People Power Party (PPP), the main conservative opposition, now favor a criminal investigation into the "fake credentials" scandal involving PPP Representative Han Dong-hoon’s daughter.

A Shift Toward Diplomatic Independence

President Lee Jae-myung’s recent declaration that South Korea will no longer maintain a "subservient" relationship with the United States has resonated deeply with the electorate. By emphasizing "sovereign independence" during recent cabinet meetings, Lee has positioned his administration as the spiritual successor to the late President Roh Moo-hyun, who famously sought a more balanced relationship with Washington. This stance is particularly popular among voters weary of intense U.S. trade pressure and demands for increased investment in American domestic industries.

The administration’s focus on historical legitimacy has also played a key role in its current standing. By attending the 47th anniversary of the Buma Democratic Uprising—the 1979 pro-democracy protests in Busan and Masan that triggered the end of the Park Chung-hee dictatorship—Lee has reinforced the Democratic Party’s roots in the struggle against authoritarianism. This stands in sharp contrast to the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president currently serving a prison sentence following his impeachment, whose administration was often criticized for downplaying democratic milestones.

Cracks in the Opposition and Legal Distrust

The most striking takeaway from recent polling is the erosion of support for the conservative leadership. Representative Han Dong-hoon, once the high-profile Justice Minister under Mr. Yoon, is now facing a "fairness" crisis that mirrors the scandal that brought down his political rivals. The fact that a majority of his own party's supporters now call for a reinvestigation into his daughter's academic credentials suggests that the "pro-fairness" banner the conservatives used to prosecute the previous administration has now turned against them.

Simultaneously, the call for the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae highlights a systemic collapse of trust in the judiciary. For a majority of the public to support the removal of the head of the Supreme Court is nearly unprecedented in South Korean political history. This sentiment is fueled by a perception that the courts have remained a bastion of the old guard, resistant to the sweeping reforms promised by the Lee administration. As the Rebuilding Korea Party—a smaller, more radical liberal party led by Cho Kuk—continues to rise in the polls (now at 8.4%), the pressure on President Lee to pursue even more aggressive judicial and prosecutorial reforms will only intensify.
