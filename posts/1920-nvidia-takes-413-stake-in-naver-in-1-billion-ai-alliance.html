Nvidia Takes 4.13% Stake in Naver in $1 Billion AI Alliance

November 04, 2026

Nvidia has finalized a 1.34 trillion won (about $1 billion) investment in Naver, South Korea's dominant internet portal and search engine, making the U.S. chip giant its third-largest shareholder with a 4.13% stake ahead of a November 6 payment date.

  • What happened: Nvidia finalized a 1.34 trillion won (about $1 billion) capital increase in Naver, taking a 4.13% stake.
  • What it changes: Naver secures priority access to scarce Nvidia hardware like H100 and B200 chips while cementing its status as a hub for "Sovereign AI."
  • Global ripples: Nvidia is simultaneously demanding that cloud partners cap China-related transactions at 49% or less.
  • Who is affected: South Korean tech suppliers, such as semiconductor substrate maker Simmtech, which posted a 721.6% jump in Q3 operating profit.

A Geopolitical Turning Point for South Korean Tech

The multibillion-won deal marks a stark turnaround from the previous administration of ousted former leader Yoon석열, whose turbulent diplomacy and prosecutorial crackdowns left South Korea isolated from global tech giants. Under the current administration of President Lee Jae-myung, which prioritizes pragmatic technology diplomacy, Nvidia's direct injection of capital into South Korea's flagship IT company signals a strong vote of confidence in Naver's homegrown AI model, HyperCLOVA X.

For Naver, the partnership provides the financial and hardware backing necessary to build "AI factories" aimed at exporting sovereign AI solutions to emerging markets. For Nvidia, anchoring itself in South Korea offers a secure, geopolitically stable base in Asia as Washington ramps up its technological decoupling from Beijing. By requiring cloud partners to keep Chinese client shares under 49%, Nvidia is tightening the screws on alternative computing routes, making trusted non-Chinese partners like Naver increasingly valuable.

Supply Chain and Market Repercussions

The alliance is already cascading down South Korea's tech supply chain. Simmtech, a major semiconductor substrate manufacturer, reported a staggering 721.6% surge in third-quarter operating profit to 101.7 billion won (about $75 million), proving that Nvidia's ecosystem is generating real manufacturing profits for South Korean mid-tier firms. Meanwhile, infrastructure players like Otech Carrier are gaining Nvidia's certification for advanced data center cooling solutions essential for high-power AI workloads.

However, this deepening integration with U.S. capital carries complex trade-offs. As global supply chains fracture along geopolitical lines, South Korean tech companies must navigate stricter external demands while protecting their own operational independence. If managed well, the alignment with Nvidia could secure South Korea's position as an indispensable pillar of the global AI infrastructure—provided policymakers continue to foster an environment of practical economic cooperation rather than political interference.
