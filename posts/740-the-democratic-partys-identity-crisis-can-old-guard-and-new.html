The Democratic Party’s Identity Crisis: Can Old Guard and New Faction Coexist?

September 13, 2026

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country’s main liberal opposition, is facing a deepening internal rift as traditional party supporters clash with a new, aggressive faction associated with the "New Lee Jae-myung" movement.

  • What happened: A fierce debate has erupted over whether the "New Lee" movement—a faction aligned with party leader Lee Jae-myung—can reconcile with the party’s traditional base.
  • The core conflict: Critics argue the new faction has adopted "Ilbe-style" tactics (referencing a notorious far-right, vitriolic online community), characterized by "doxing," targetted harassment, and toxic rhetoric.
  • The risk: Observers warn that if the party fails to address these exclusionary practices, it risks losing its democratic legitimacy and alienating core voters.

A Culture of Toxicity?

The divide is no longer just about policy differences; it is about the fundamental identity of the Democratic Party. Critics within the party have labeled the "New Lee" supporters as an "outgroup that should be purged" rather than allies. They argue that this new faction’s methods—which prioritize silencing dissent through mockery and coordinated online attacks—are fundamentally at odds with the party's democratic values and history.

Most concerning to long-term supporters is the apparent complicity of some DPK lawmakers. There is growing evidence that elected officials, rather than curbing this extremist trend, are participating in or emboldening these tactics to maintain favor with the most vocal online base. This shift suggests that the party’s decision-making processes are being hijacked by an echo chamber of hate, effectively paralyzing the party's ability to act as a rational, public-facing organization.

The Path Forward: Reconciliation or Purge?

The Democratic Party is currently a litmus test for the future of Korean political discourse. The "New Lee" movement proponents push for a "chemical fusion" of these groups to build a larger power base, but many argue that such a union is actually a "deceptive alliance" that trades the party's integrity for short-term gain.

The reality is that a party built on a foundation of hate and "doxing" (coordinated targetting) is inherently fragile. If the DPK leadership continues to prioritize the approval of extreme online factions over the values of civil debate and democratic procedure, they risk permanent damage to the party’s reputation. True solidarity cannot exist on a foundation of vitriol; the party faces a critical juncture where it must choose between regaining its focus on common-sense politics or fully surrendering to a culture of reactionary exclusion.
