South Korea Market Briefing: Rate Shock, Chip Gear Resilience, and Toss Moves on E-Commerce

September 30, 2026

South Korean markets held their ground despite a massive $2.2 billion (about 3 trillion won) foreign sell-off driven by Federal Reserve rate hike fears, while structural reforms and tech shifts reshape the broader landscape.

    Foreign outflow shock: Aggressive foreign selling hit large-cap stocks like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, but corporate buybacks and institutional defense cushioned the drop. Semiconductor equipment boom: Surging global AI infrastructure demand and heavy facility investments in China and domestically are fueling strong rallies for chip equipment suppliers. Toss enters e-commerce: Super-app Toss partnered with fresh-food delivery firm Oasis to challenge Coupang's dominance in the domestic retail space. Structural reform hurdles: Proposed changes to capital markets face resistance as critics highlight lingering governance issues, such as majority shareholders protecting their own premiums at the expense of minority investors.

The resilience of South Korean chip equipment makers amid broad macroeconomic panic underscores a key divergence in the market. While foreign capital flees due to high US Treasury yields and a strong dollar, structural growth in artificial intelligence infrastructure is creating pockets of undeniable strength. Companies that supply advanced machinery to memory and foundry makers are defying broader market gravity because their order books are tied to multi-year technological upgrades rather than short-term sentiment.

At the same time, high-profile corporate maneuvers—such as Toss leveraging its 30 million users to push into grocery delivery—signal that the era of single-player dominance in Korean tech and retail is ending. Yet, until systemic governance flaws like unequal buyout terms for minority shareholders are addressed, foreign investors will likely view market reforms with skepticism, no matter how many headline-grabbing initiatives the government rolls out.
