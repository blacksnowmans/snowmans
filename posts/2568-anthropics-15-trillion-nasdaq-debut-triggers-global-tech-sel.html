Anthropic’s $1.5 Trillion Nasdaq Debut Triggers Global Tech Sell-Off and Slams South Korean Markets

November 21, 2026

Artificial intelligence pioneer Anthropic’s historic $1.5 trillion Nasdaq debut quickly turned sour on its second day of trading, sparking a global tech sell-off that sent South Korea’s benchmark Kospi index tumbling below 5,000.

  • What happened: Anthropic surged 17% on its November 20 debut following a 7-to-1 subscription frenzy, but plunged 10% on day two as investor focus abruptly shifted from hype to profitability metrics.
  • Impact on South Korea: South Korea's benchmark Kospi index plummeted over 5% to a 10-month low, while telecom giant SK Telecom fell 12% despite holding an Anthropic stake expected to yield a $3.6 billion (5 trillion won) valuation windfall.
  • Global tech contagion: Major tech heavyweights were hit hard as capital was reshuffled, sending Japan’s SoftBank down 16%, AMD down 7%, and Micron down 5%.
  • Windfalls and controversy: Anthropic employees netted an average of $42 million to $50 million (58 billion to 68 billion won) in equity value, while seven co-founders cashed out $280 million each amid public backlash over potential rollbacks to their original 80% charity pledge.

A Reality Check for the AI Bubble

The swift reversal in Anthropic’s share price highlights how rapidly public markets are demanding tangible financial returns from generative AI leaders. While private venture funding previously valued AI firms on technological breakthroughs, listing on the Nasdaq subjects these companies to strict quarterly earnings scrutiny. The second-day 10% drop reflects a broader market recalibration, signaling that public investors are unwilling to sustain record valuations without immediate evidence of monetization.

For South Korea, the event served as a stark reminder of the market's vulnerability to global liquidity drains. Foreign institutional investors dumped domestic equities to free up capital for the US mega-IPO, pulling liquidity from Seoul and dragging down beneficiary stocks like SK Telecom. Furthermore, domestic retail investors saw little benefit through local financial products: six Korean exchange-traded funds (ETFs) tracking Anthropic held minimal weightings due to regulatory caps on newly listed foreign equities, leaving only specialized funds—such as Woori Asset Management's US short-term bond IPO fund—to secure meaningful allocations.

The Wall Street Divide on Long-Term AI Value

Beyond the immediate market disruption, a sharp divide is emerging between short-term market momentum and long-term institutional bets. While day traders and funds rushed to exit AI positions during the second-day slump, major institutional investors moved in the opposite direction. Notable among them was investment firm Altimeter, which placed a massive $5 billion bet on Anthropic (ANT), projecting its valuation will double to $3 trillion within three years.

This stark divergence underscores the dual forces shaping the current tech sector. In the short term, Anthropic’s market entry acted as a liquidity vacuum, drawing capital away from hardware suppliers, chipmakers, and Asian equities. In the long term, however, institutional mega-cap funds remain firmly committed to backing foundational AI models, viewing initial market volatility as a temporary hurdle on the path to long-term dominance.
