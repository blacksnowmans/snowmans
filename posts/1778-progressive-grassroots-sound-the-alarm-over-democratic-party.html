Progressive Grassroots Sound the Alarm Over Democratic Party Infighting

October 31, 2026

At an offline gathering organized by the influential progressive channel Park Si-young TV, South Korea's core progressive supporters warned that deepening factional fractures within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea risk crippling President Lee Jae-myung’s reform agenda.

  • What happened: Thousands of grassroots activists and prominent commentators gathered for a live political retreat to confront rising internal turmoil inside the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) following the impeachment and imprisonment of Yoon Suk-yeol.
  • The core rift: Speakers strongly criticized establishment figures for attempting to marginalize newly mobilized supporters—often referred to as the "New Lee Jae-myung" base—warning that exclusionary politics will splinter the ruling coalition.
  • Targets of frustration: Attendees directed sharp criticism at complacent Democratic lawmakers who drag their feet on judicial and media reforms, as well as the judiciary under Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae for escaping democratic accountability.
  • The stakes: Invoking counsel from prominent liberal author and commentator Rhyu Si-min, speakers stressed that preserving the progressive camp’s unity is the only way to sustain President Lee's governing momentum against the conservative opposition People Power Party.

Governing Exposes Internal Fractures

Winning power has proved easier than managing it. While progressives maintained a disciplined, unified front during their successful push to remove Yoon Suk-yeol from office, governing has exposed bitter fault lines. The Democratic Party now controls both the presidency and a comfortable legislative majority, yet tension is surging between the party leadership under representative Kim Min-seok and newly energized grassroots supporters. Calls from some party insiders to simply discard this vocal insurgent base were met with outright outrage at the event, where organizers warned that political subtraction is suicide for a reformist government.

The gathering also underlines the outsized role YouTube commentary networks play in shaping contemporary South Korean politics. Outlets like Park Si-young TV operate less like standard news shows and more like ideological coordinating bodies that organize rallies, run informal polling, and hold lawmakers' feet to the fire. Frustration toward self-satisfied Democratic lawmakers who rest on their establishment perks rather than driving aggressive reform ran high throughout the discussions.

Burnout and the Call for Discipline

Behind the fiery rhetoric, the retreat revealed palpable political exhaustion. Multiple speakers acknowledged that fighting internal party battles is far more emotionally taxing than opposing the conservative People Power Party. Commentators actively urged burnt-out activists to step back and manage their rage—even recommending walks along Seoul's Yangjaecheon stream—to build the emotional stamina required for a protracted political cycle.

The ultimate verdict from the grassroots is straightforward: the ruling party cannot afford complacency or division. If the Democratic leadership isolates half of its active supporters to protect institutional insiders, it risks repeating historical mistakes and squandering President Lee's mandate from within.
