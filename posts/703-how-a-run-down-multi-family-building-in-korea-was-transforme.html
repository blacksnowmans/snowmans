How a Run-Down Multi-Family Building in Korea Was Transformed for $350,000

October 05, 2026

A dramatic architectural overhaul of a cramped, leaking multi-family house in an older urban neighborhood demonstrates how smart spatial design can turn a structural headache into a modern home.

  • What happened: A severely weathered multi-family building—plagued by chronic leaks, makeshift polycarbonate covers, and a total lack of parking—underwent a total gut renovation costing about $350,000 (450 million won).
  • What changes: The redesign replaced the chaotic exterior with a signature arched-window facade, carved out a functional two-car parking space from an impassable front yard, and reconfigured the interior to maximize an 18-pyeong (approx. 600 sq ft) footprint.
  • Who is affected: Both property owners and tenants benefit from sharply separated entryways, private floor plans, and resolved waterproofing issues that transform a blighted structure into high-value urban housing.

Solving Structural Nightmares: Parking and Façade Design

Old multi-family residential buildings in Korea often accumulate decades of ad-hoc patches—like plastic sheeting bolted onto brick walls to stop persistent leaks—resulting in a fragmented, eyesore exterior. In this project, designers stripped away the patchwork to unify the building envelope, featuring signature arched windows on the third floor that double as rain guards while improving natural light. Fixing the chronic parking crisis required even bolder structural surgery: relocating the main entrance to the side of the building to carve out a gentle ramp and a tandem two-car parking space complete with a wash-down tap.

Inside the compact 18-pyeong footprint, creative spatial engineering squeezed out every possible square inch of utility. Designers utilized dead space under the stairwells for a walk-in storage room, repositioned the kitchen layout to accommodate modern appliances and a large island counter, and added a clever movable staircase leading to a fully insulated rooftop room. By addressing deep-seated structural flaws—such as severe basement water leakage—rather than just applying cosmetic paint, the project highlights the heavy lifting required to revitalize aging urban real estate.

The Hidden Reality of Renovation Contracts

While the final reveal looks effortless, the reality of Korean home remodeling is fraught with legal and financial risks. Contractors and owners frequently clash over poor finishes, tile misalignments, and most dangerously, waterproofing failures that lead to hidden leaks and mold. Without meticulous contracts specifying defect liability periods and itemized business registration numbers, owners are often left footing massive repair bills or dragging unreliable builders through costly legal disputes. This transformation serves as both an aesthetic inspiration and a stark reminder that successful urban renewal requires rigorous legal and structural diligence.
