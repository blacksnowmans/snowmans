The Crisis of "Youth Politics" within South Korea’s Ruling Party

October 10, 2026

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the current ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung, is facing intense backlash as its "youth political" wing is being accused of abandoning reform to mimic the worst habits of the country’s old-guard political establishment.

  • What happened: High-profile young DPK figures are embroiled in scandals involving the unauthorized leakage of sensitive presidential personnel vetting data, hypocrisy regarding political ethics, and the use of inflammatory, "rage-bait" tactics on social media.
  • The controversy: Critics argue these individuals are not representing the interests of the youth, but are instead "political grifters" using the party platform to settle personal scores and build their own profiles.
  • Who is affected: The Democratic Party’s image among voters in their 20s and 30s is suffering, as the party's failure to cultivate genuine talent and reliance on toxic online influencers alienates a generation looking for practical policy solutions.

The "Grifter" Culture in Progressive Politics

The core of the issue lies in the behavior of certain young political influencers, such as Jeong Min-cheol, who have been accused of weaponizing internal party secrets for clout. Critics point out that while these figures preach reform, they frequently employ the same "old-school" authoritarian rhetoric they claim to oppose. They are often seen acting as "political wreckers"—using inflammatory memes and disinformation—rather than engaging in substantive policy debate.

This dysfunction is exacerbated by senior DPK officials, such as Representative Lee Hae-sik, who have publicly endorsed these controversial figures on social media. By validating individuals who have been caught peddling gossip and violating security protocols, the party is effectively signaling that it prioritizes loyalty and "dopamine-inducing" provocation over the professional standards required for governance. The result is a demoralized support base and a widening gap between the party and the real-world concerns of younger voters.

Beyond "Youth Identity Politics"

The trend of young, hyper-partisan influencers dominating the DPK’s youth platform is a warning sign of structural rot. Instead of fostering a new generation of leaders capable of addressing Korea's AI-driven economy, housing inequality, or the climate crisis, the party has allowed the space for "youth politics" to be occupied by opportunists who thrive on divisive culture wars.

If the Democratic Party wishes to maintain its relevance, it must stop treating youth politics as a marketing gimmick or a reservoir for partisan shock troops. The irony is stark: while these influencers claim to speak for the youth, they are the primary architects of the cynicism that drives young voters away from progressivism. Unless the party cuts ties with those who prioritize personal gain over public service, the DPK risks losing the intellectual and moral credibility necessary to lead the nation into the future.
