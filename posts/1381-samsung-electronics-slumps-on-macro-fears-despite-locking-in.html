Samsung Electronics Slumps on Macro Fears Despite Locking In 80% of Next Year's Memory Output

October 22, 2026

Samsung Electronics shares have tumbled under persistent macroeconomic gloom, even though the South Korean tech giant has already pre-sold more than 80% of its projected memory chip output for next year through long-term supply agreements.

  • Guaranteed order book: Samsung has locked in long-term contracts for roughly 70% of its standard DRAM and NAND production and virtually 100% of its high-bandwidth memory (HBM) capacity for next year with global hyperscalers like Microsoft, Google, and Meta.
  • Foundry recovery: The company's 4-nanometer foundry lines have reached near-100% capacity utilization, easing massive fixed-cost overhead and redirecting unmet demand toward its 5nm lines.
  • Narrowing the HBM gap: Samsung's share of the global HBM market jumped from 21% in the first quarter to 33% in the second quarter, clawing back territory from domestic rival SK Hynix.
  • Retail investor pain: South Korea's retail investors—often called "Donghak Ants"—are absorbing steep paper losses as foreign funds dump large-cap equities amid concerns over the trajectory of U.S. interest rates and tech-sector fatigue.

The Chasm Between Fundamentals and Stock Price

The current selloff in Samsung Electronics is a classic case of macroeconomic anxiety overriding corporate earnings reality. Market watchers have seized on market intelligence firm TrendForce's projections showing quarter-on-quarter DRAM contract price gains slowing toward the 10% range later this year—down from 90% in the first quarter—interpreting this moderation as a cyclical peak. In response, foreign and institutional investors have treated Korea’s bellwether stock as an easy exit ramp to trim macro risk, disregarding the unprecedented visibility of Samsung's forward book.

That panic ignores the shift in how memory chips are bought and sold. Unlike prior boom-and-bust cycles defined by glut and cutthroat price wars, today's AI-driven buildout is underpinned by long-term capacity reservations. With next-generation HBM4 average selling prices projected to surge between 100% and 150%, Samsung's profitability is shielded by locked-in pricing power rather than exposed to spot-market volatility.

Foundry Spillovers and Sector Tailwinds

On the non-memory front, Samsung faces an uphill climb against Taiwan's TSMC, which commands an overwhelming 72.5% market share against Samsung’s 5.9%. Yet TSMC's aggressive expansion in Tainan and tight production bottlenecks create an opening. With Big Tech customers facing capacity rationing and steep wafer price hikes in Taiwan, Samsung's stabilized 80% yield on 4nm wafers positions it as the only viable alternative supplier. The company is now preparing to test its second-generation 2nm process (SF2P) on its upcoming Exynos 2700 mobile chip, aiming to prove yield reliability for external fabless clients.

Korea's export heavyweights outside semiconductors are showing similar fundamental resilience. In pharmaceuticals, Celltrion Chairman Seo Jung-jin has reaffirmed the company's guidance of 600 billion won (about $440 million) in fourth-quarter operating profit, mounting an international sales push despite foreign exchange swings. Across both tech and bio, corporate balance sheets tell a far sturdier story than Seoul's volatile trading screens suggest.
