Park Se-ik Urges President Lee to Back a $70 Billion Market Stabilization Shield

November 17, 2026

At a recent capital markets policy forum, Chesley Investment Advisory CEO Park Se-ik urged President Lee Jae-myung to establish a 100 trillion won (about $70 billion) Stock Market Stabilization Fund to deter foreign speculative selling and narrow the persistent "Korea discount."

  • The proposal: Park argued to President Lee that past 10 trillion won (about $7 billion) stabilization funds—including one formed during the 2020 pandemic—were far too small for a market where the combined KOSPI and KOSDAQ capitalization nears 5,000 trillion won (about $3.6 trillion) and foreign funds can sell 10 trillion won in days.
  • How it works: Rather than sitting idle, the fund would use a "capital call" model backed by South Korea's five major financial holding groups and large asset managers, deploying capital only during steep plunges to signal overwhelming buying power to short-term speculators.
  • Pension linkage: Park proposed channeling part of South Korea's 500 trillion won (about $360 billion) in retirement pensions into bond-mixed stabilization funds run by major firms like Mirae Asset and Samsung Asset Management, holding safe cash-like assets normally and buying blue chips during 30%-plus drops.
  • Who is affected: Retail investors, domestic brokerages, major chipmakers like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, and foreign institutional traders would all face lower volatility if a sovereign-scale market floor is established.

Why Seoul's markets feel exposed

Park's pitch taps into deep frustration among Korean investors who view Seoul's bourse as overly exposed to derivatives-driven swings. Supporters point to the 2010 options expiry shock—when foreign traders dumped trillions of won in stocks at closing to net about $130 million (180 billion won) on put options, bankrupting a domestic asset manager before winning a 2023 Supreme Court acquittal—and to steep sell-offs following foreign reports such as Morgan Stanley's 2024 chip downturn warning. Modeling the fund on Taiwan's Ministry of Finance-backed National Stabilization Fund, which bought TSMC and Foxconn during tariff turmoil, advocates argue that curbing volatility could lift Korean stock valuations from a price-to-earnings ratio of 10 toward 15.

The catch with a state-backed floor

Whether South Korea's financial giants and policymakers will embrace the plan remains an open question. Free-market critics question why banks and regulators should intervene in equity pricing at all, and turning retirement pensions into a counter-cyclical market backstop carries fiduciary and political risks if a downturn lasts longer than expected. Still, with President Lee's administration looking for ways to tackle the Korea discount and protect domestic savers, Park's call highlights a growing push in Seoul to treat capital-market stability not just as a regulatory issue, but as an economic security priority.
