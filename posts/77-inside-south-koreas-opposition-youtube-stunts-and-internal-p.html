Inside South Korea's Opposition: YouTube Stunts and Internal Purges

September 03, 2026

South Korea's main opposition Democratic Party is waging an internal war where YouTube view counts and ethics panels are being weaponized for factional purges.

  • The Battle of the Channels: The Democratic Party’s official channel, "Minti," has seen explosive live-viewer spikes, but experts point out this is the result of coordinated "coordinate targeting" rather than organic growth.
  • The Jang Kyung-tae Trap: Lawmaker Jang Kyung-tae was nearly destroyed by a selectively edited media report alleging misconduct, but raw footage and text messages later proved he was the target of a deliberate setup.
  • Weaponized Ethics: Leaked Telegram messages from high-ranking party officials reveal that the party's ethics watchdog has devolved into a political slaughterhouse used to silence rivals and consolidate power.
  • Who is Affected: South Korea's political landscape, the credibility of the progressive opposition, and voters looking for genuine democratic alternatives as internal paranoia takes over.

YouTube Metrics as a Political Weapon

For outside observers, the obsession with South Korea's hyper-politicized YouTube ecosystem might seem trivial, but it serves as a raw pulse check for party dominance. When the Democratic Party launched "Minti" to rival dominant independent progressive channels like Kim Ou-joon's "News Factory," initial concurrent viewer numbers soared from 13,900 to 48,000. Yet, beneath these vanity metrics lies a more cynical reality. Traffic drops off instantly when streams end, and engagement patterns heavily suggest coordinated brigades rather than grass-roots enthusiasm. It is a classic case of artificial metric-padding designed to project strength in an ongoing internal power struggle.

Worse still, this obsession with optics has bled into reckless messaging. The channel recently stumbled into a controversy over an online symbol linked to far-right hate sites—a blunder excused as a simple formatting quirk, but perceived by the public as another sign of a sloppy, chaotic internal apparatus. When a major political party treats communication channels like battlegrounds to outmaneuver internal rivals rather than engage the public, the brand rots from the inside out.

The Anatomy of a Political Setup

Perhaps the most chilling revelation of this factional warfare is the treatment of Lawmaker Jang Kyung-tae. Initially smeared by heavily doctored footage that made him look like a predatory aggressor during an interaction, Jang was branded by public outrage before the full story came to light. Un-edited video and private text messages subsequently exposed a different reality: the woman in question initiated the contact, and Jang rebuffed her. Messages uncovered during the aftermath confirmed that Jang was the intended victim of an orchestrated hit job designed to politically assassinate him.

This is where the infighting moves from unsavory to dangerous. Leaked Telegram messages between senior party figures—bristling with words like "operation," "seizure," and "forces"—lay bare a party leadership consumed by paranoia and purge culture. An ethics inspection unit meant to maintain party discipline has instead been twisted into an instrument of intimidation. When a major political movement relies on entrapment and character assassination against its own members, it ceases to be a democratic alternative and begins to resemble a machine of fear. The numbers game on YouTube and the manufactured scandals in the hallways are just different fronts in the same destructive war.
