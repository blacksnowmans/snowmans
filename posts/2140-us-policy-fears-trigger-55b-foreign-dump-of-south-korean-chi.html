U.S. Policy Fears Trigger $5.5B Foreign Dump of South Korean Chip Giants

November 09, 2026

Foreign investors dumped a record 7.6 trillion won (about $5.5 billion) in Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix stock in just one week, panic-selling over U.S. election uncertainty and looming regulations on AI data centers.

  • Massive foreign retreat: Foreign ownership in Samsung Electronics, South Korea's largest conglomerate, dropped to a 17-year low, while memory rival SK Hynix plunged 10.44% in a single trading session.
  • Policy shockwaves: Democratic victories in U.S. state elections have fueled anxiety over modified CHIPS Act subsidies and shifting trade rules, threatening overseas investments committed during Yoon Suk-yeol's term.
  • AI "peak-out" anxiety: Anticipated 2028 U.S. environmental and power grid restrictions on AI data centers triggered sell-offs in U.S. tech leaders like Nvidia and AMD, dragging down South Korean High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) suppliers.
  • Retail investors step in: Korean retail traders and state pension funds are buying the dip, relying on the combined 329 trillion won ($240 billion) cash reserves of both chipmakers.

Political Volatility and the AI Regulatory Threat

The sudden exodus of foreign capital highlights growing fragility in South Korea’s semiconductor sector, which is caught between Washington’s shifting politics and global tech valuations. Democratic victories in key states like Texas and Indiana have cast doubt over U.S. federal subsidy timelines for new fabrication plants. Billions in investments pledged overseas during Yoon Suk-yeol's term now look vulnerable to policy resets, leaving President Lee Jae-myung’s administration scrambling to protect Korean supply chains against escalating U.S. protectionism.

At the same time, markets are aggressively pricing in long-term regulatory headwinds. Wall Street has grown increasingly wary of proposed 2028 U.S. regulations targeting the immense power consumption of AI data centers. If Big Tech is forced to throttle infrastructure expansion, demand for High Bandwidth Memory (HBM)—currently the primary growth engine for both Samsung and SK Hynix—could drop sharply. This fear caused severe pullbacks in Nvidia (-9.3%) and AMD (-17.3%), spilling over directly into Seoul's semiconductor index.

A Financial Buffer Against Market Panic

Despite the market turbulence, South Korea's memory titans possess an unprecedented financial safety net. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix hold a combined 329 trillion won (roughly $240 billion) in cash and liquid assets. This massive war chest ensures both companies can maintain heavy R&D investments and defend their market position even through a severe industry downcycle.

However, fundamental financial strength has so far failed to halt the foreign retreat. While domestic retail investors view recent price drops as a rare buying opportunity—recalling past cyclical rebounds—foreign institutions are using Korean chip equities as liquid instruments to quickly de-risk their global portfolios. Until Washington clarifies its tech policy and foreign capital flows stabilize, South Korea's semiconductor leaders face a volatile road ahead.
