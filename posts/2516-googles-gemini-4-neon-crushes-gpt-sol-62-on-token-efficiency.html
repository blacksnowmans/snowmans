Google’s Gemini 4 Neon Crushes GPT Sol 6.2 on Token Efficiency in Practical Benchmarks

November 19, 2026

Google’s new mid-tier Gemini 4 Neon delivers top-tier performance while using a fraction of the compute time and token consumption required by rival GPT Sol 6.2 across real-world 3D design, coding, and engineering tasks.

  • Vastly lower resource footprint: In complex 3D Blender rendering, interactive web builds, and CAD board layouts, Neon finished tasks in roughly half the time of Sol 6.2 while burning up to 95% fewer tokens.
  • Priced for production: Positioned between economy models and flagship frontier systems at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, Neon targets production pipelines requiring sustained execution without runaway API bills.
  • Strong security guardrails: The model demonstrates robust resistance to prompt injection attacks, making it viable for autonomous enterprise agents with system-level access.
  • Persistent Korean language weakness: Despite stellar spatial and coding logic, Neon still stumbles on nuanced creative writing in Korean, lagging behind Anthropic's Claude lineup.

Massive Token Savings Redefine the Developer Math

The gap between the two models in practical execution is striking. When tasked with generating an exploded assembly animation of Geunjeongjeon—the main throne hall of Seoul’s historic Gyeongbokgung Palace—in Blender, Gemini 4 Neon wrapped the build in 22 minutes and 32 seconds using 388,000 tokens. GPT Sol 6.2 consumed 44 minutes and burned through an astonishing 16 million tokens for a comparable result. Similar disparities emerged during CAD design for a USB controller, where Neon finished in 29 minutes using under 470,000 tokens, compared to Sol’s 56 minutes and 17 million tokens.

Historically, Gemini models earned a reputation for terminating runs early to conserve tokens, often sacrificing output quality in the process. Neon appears to have eliminated that penalty in technical tasks. Its structural layouts, physics handling, and interactive particle animations match or exceed competitors' outputs without requiring massive token dumps or repetitive code generation loops.

The Emerging Architecture: Frontier Brain, Neon Muscle

For engineering leads and independent studios managing API budgets, these metrics suggest an immediate shift in workflow architecture. Rather than relying on expensive frontier models to execute every micro-task, teams are adopting a tiered setup: deploy an elite orchestrator to plan system architecture, then offload the execution, logic scripting, and asset structuring to Gemini 4 Neon as a dedicated worker.

The clear exception remains localization and creative prose. In Korean fiction tests set around Seoul's Euljiro district, Neon produced stiff, grammatically awkward prose, underscoring that raw architectural reasoning does not automatically translate to linguistic nuance. For businesses building global workflows, Neon is an aggressive cost-cutter for technical backends, but editorial tasks still belong elsewhere.
