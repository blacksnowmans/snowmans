South Korea’s Ruling Party Pushes 10-Year Exam Ban for AI Cheating and Escalates Budget Battles

November 17, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) is moving to double the penalty for using artificial intelligence to cheat on the national university entrance exam to a 10-year ban, while simultaneously driving a broad slate of judicial, budget, and public safety initiatives.

  • Strict AI Cheating Penalties: Proposed legislation would ban students who use AI technology to cheat on the Suneung (College Scholastic Ability Test) from retaking the exam for up to 10 years or more, doubling the current five-year restriction.
  • Pressure on Supreme Court: DPK leadership called for a stern verdict in the upcoming November 19 Supreme Court trial of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo regarding insurrection-related charges.
  • 821 Trillion Won Budget Standoff: The DPK is clashing with the main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) over presidential office special activity funds within the proposed 821 trillion won (about $590 billion) national budget.
  • Livelihood and Local Reforms: The party is pushing to increase national government funding for long-term senior care to 80%, while opposing a proposal to abolish Jeju Island’s visa-free entry regime.

Tackling AI Cheating and Demanding Judicial Accountability

The ruling party's legislative blitz highlights growing concern over high-tech misconduct ahead of college admissions. By pushing to ban AI cheaters from the Suneung for at least a decade—and exploring retroactive application—the DPK aims to safeguard educational fairness in a rapidly shifting technological landscape. Concurrently, the party launched a dedicated Education Special Committee, chaired by lawmaker Kim Min-seok, to address AI-driven learning disparities and modernize national curriculum standards.

On the political front, the DPK is keeping intense pressure on the judiciary. Ahead of the Supreme Court's scheduled November 19 ruling on former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo, DPK leaders insisted that the courts must strictly punish those involved in insurrection actions to uphold constitutional order. The party warned that any lenient stance would betray public trust and undermine judicial justice.

Budget Battles and Public Safety Reforms

In the National Assembly Steering Committee, partisan friction escalated over executive spending. The opposition PPP demanded a complete wipeout of the presidential office’s special activity budget, while the ruling DPK argued for maintaining executive operational needs alongside transparent audits, urging the opposition to stop boycotting fiscal reviews for the 821 trillion won ($590 billion) budget package.

Beyond fiscal fights, the party rolled out targeted regional and social security bills. To protect local tourism, the DPK’s Jeju branch formally opposed calls to eliminate Jeju’s visa-free travel system. Meanwhile, new legislative proposals seek to raise central government coverage for long-term senior care costs to 80%, retroactively apply strict fire safety codes to older logistics warehouses, enable swift asset freezes on crypto accounts used in financial scams, and expand protection order boundaries up to 2 kilometers for victims of intimate relationship violence.
