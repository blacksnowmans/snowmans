South Korea’s New Anti-Corruption Agency Head Pick Sparks Backlash Over Past Scandals

October 06, 2026

  • President Lee Jae-myung’s nomination of Kim Ji-yong to head the newly established Serious Crime Investigation Agency has ignited fierce infighting within the ruling Democratic Party.
  • Critics, including ruling party lawmaker Park Eun-jung, accuse Kim of covering up a sexual misconduct case involving a former prosecutor during his past tenure in the prosecution service.
  • The controversy exposes deep factional fractures within South Korea's progressive camp, pitting reform-minded lawmakers against presidential loyalists.
  • The scandal threatens to undermine the administration’s core reform agenda and alienate its base ahead of upcoming political battles.

The bitter fallout over the nomination of Kim Ji-yong to lead South Korea's newly formed Serious Crime Investigation Agency has laid bare the mounting dysfunction within the ruling progressive camp. Rather than marking a clean break with past prosecutorial overreach, the pick has exposed the administration to charges of recycling figures connected to the very institutional misconduct it pledged to dismantle. Opposition within the ruling party has broken out into the open, signaling a dangerous disconnect between the presidential office and its own reform wing.

The controversy is fueled by grave allegations concerning Kim's past career, specifically claims that he ignored and buried complaints regarding a major sexual assault scandal involving a former prosecutor. While presidential loyalists and pro-government commentators rush to defend the nomination as an exercise of executive prerogative, critics argue that placing a figure dogged by cover-up allegations at the helm of a flagship anti-corruption body makes a mockery of reform. Compounding the political damage, reports of questionable parachute appointments at public firms—such as a heavily criticized executive pick for a Seongnam public enterprise—have reinforced public perception of cronyism.

Internal Fractures and the Cost of Unyielding Power

What makes this crisis particularly damaging for the administration is the public rupture it has caused among key figures who once championed judicial reform. Lawmakers like Park Eun-jung have openly challenged the presidential office, asking how an administration born of reform promises can justify sheltering figures tied to old bureaucratic abuses. Instead of engaging with substantive criticisms, critics charge that the presidential office and its allies have resorted to factional gatekeeping, treating policy disagreement as disloyalty and dismissing internal critics as extremists.

This heavy-handed leadership style risks isolating the presidency and reducing its inner circle to a circle of uncritical loyalists. When a government responds to minor controversies—such as a reporter losing a press pass during an overseas trip—with heavy-handed disciplinary actions and emotional public spats, it projects insecurity rather than strength. As loyalty is increasingly prioritized over competence and accountability, the administration is squandering its political capital on petty internal turf wars, alienating the very voters who brought it to power.
