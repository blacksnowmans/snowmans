How GoPro Lost the Action Camera Market and Sold Off a 90% Stake

October 05, 2026

  • What happened: Once valued at $12,000,000,000, action camera pioneer GoPro has agreed to sell a 90% stake to an optical components maker for $285 million, valuing the company at a tiny fraction of its peak.
  • Why it happened: Rapid smartphone camera improvements and a fierce onslaught by Chinese rivals like DJI and Insta360 battered GoPro's sales, compounding years of minor hardware updates, overheating issues, and failed expansions into drones and media.
  • Who is affected: Consumers have largely moved on to cheaper, higher-performing alternatives, while remaining shareholders face an uncertain pivot into B2B and defense markets using GoPro's remaining patents.

GoPro defined a category a decade ago, putting rugged cameras on the helmets and surfboards of travelers worldwide. Today, it is fighting for mere survival after three consecutive years of operating losses and plunging sales. By offloading 90% of its equity, the company is effectively admitting that its glory days as a standalone consumer hardware darling are over.

The fall is a classic lesson in the perils of technological complacency. While smartphone cameras grew powerful enough to handle casual travel vlogging, and competitors like DJI (which now commands over 60% of the global market) relentlessly improved stabilization and low-light performance, GoPro kept rolling out iterative upgrades plagued by old hardware bugs. Failed side ventures—like its recalled Karma drone—drained resources and focus from its core product.

Now, GoPro is attempting to pivot toward the defense sector and business-to-business infrastructure, leaning on its portfolio of roughly 2,500 patents and optical know-how. But in our view, branding this as an exciting tech evolution misses the point. Defense contracts and specialized optics require deep trust and flawless execution—qualities that a company struggling with consumer loyalty and basic hardware reliability will find hard to fake. Unless GoPro can prove it can build products that people actually want to buy again, this sale looks less like a triumphant reinvention and more like a quiet exit.
