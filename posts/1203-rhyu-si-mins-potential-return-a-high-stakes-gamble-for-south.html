Rhyu Si-min’s Potential Return: A High-Stakes Gamble for South Korea’s Ruling Progressives

October 16, 2026

High-profile intellectual Rhyu Si-min is facing mounting pressure to abandon his role as a commentator and join the ruling Democratic Party of Korea to spearhead a "reformist breakthrough" for the President Lee Jae-myung administration.

  • Strategic Realignment: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) and the Rebuilding Korea Party, a minor progressive party led by former Justice Minister Cho Kuk, are seeking a "chemical fusion" to accelerate reforms and consolidate the liberal power base.
  • The Rhyu Factor: Rhyu Si-min, a former cabinet minister and influential liberal writer, is viewed as a "game changer" whose entry into the DPK could fundamentally shift the party’s internal dynamics and public messaging.
  • Political Shield: Supporters argue that formal political participation is a necessary "survival strategy" for Rhyu to gain legal and organizational protection from remnant opposition forces and the prosecution.
  • Leadership Shift: Analysts envision Rhyu joining forces with aggressive reformists like lawmaker Choo Mi-ae to transform the DPK from a cautious administrative party into a proactive, "offensive" political machine.

Beyond Commentary: The Need for a Heavyweight "Speaker"

The current political climate suggests that the era of "elegant commentary" is over. While Rhyu Si-min has long provided intellectual direction for the liberal base from the sidelines, the DPK now requires a heavyweight "speaker" within its official ranks. Some observers have floated the idea of Rhyu joining the Rebuilding Korea Party, but strategic consensus points toward the DPK. While joining a minor party might boost local support, only a role within the ruling DPK—the "engine" of the current administration—is seen as having the mass to effectively drive the national agenda.

The goal is to move past the "dilemma of dispersion." If the DPK and the Rebuilding Korea Party begin to view each other as competitors, it creates an opening for the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, to regain ground. By bringing a figure like Rhyu into the DPK fold, the ruling bloc aims to create a unified front that combines the DPK’s massive organizational power with the "sharpness" and clarity that the liberal base currently craves.

Survival as Strategy in the Post-Yoon Era

Perhaps the most pressing argument for Rhyu’s return is defensive. Despite the transition to the Lee Jae-myung government, the "barbaric" political offensives seen during the era of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol—who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment—remain a threat. The source suggests that public intellectuals remaining outside the formal protection of a political party are easy targets for the prosecution and entrenched interests. Entering the "ring" of institutional politics provides a shield consisting of organized supporters and party immunity.

Ultimately, the call for Rhyu to return is about more than just one individual’s career; it is about the "atmosphere" of the ruling party. The DPK has often struggled to balance moderate appeal with the demands of its core supporters for clear, decisive reform. Rhyu Si-min’s ability to translate complex policy into the language of the public is seen as the missing piece for the Lee Jae-myung administration. By stepping onto the front lines, Rhyu would not only be defending himself but acting as a "catalyst" to ensure the government’s reform tasks are completed before the opposition can regroup.
