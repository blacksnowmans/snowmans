The Record of a Failed Presidency: Analyzing the Fall of Yoon Suk-yeol

October 16, 2026

The collapse of public support for former leader Yoon Suk-yeol served as the definitive prelude to his impeachment, characterized by a fundamental breakdown in communication and national dignity.

  • Polls by the Korea Society Opinion Institute (KSOI), a major polling firm, consistently showed Mr. Yoon’s approval ratings hitting post-inauguration lows during his tenure.
  • Disapproval was nearly universal, with voters across almost all age groups labeling his unfiltered social media use as a significant hindrance to state affairs.
  • Diplomatic and security anxieties alienated voters in every region, including the politically influential Honam region in the southwest, creating a total loss of trust.
  • This era of instability eventually led to a constitutional crisis, resulting in Mr. Yoon’s imprisonment and the rise of the current administration under President Lee Jae-myung.

A Crisis of Legitimacy and the Data of Discontent

The KSOI data from Mr. Yoon’s tenure was more than a mere political fluctuation; it was a warning that the public mandate for his power had effectively expired. While his office often dismissed record-low numbers as "incitement" by opposition forces, the figures reflected a deep-seated distrust in a leader perceived to be using the presidency for personal whims rather than the public good. Critics such as Park Eun-jung and Jung Chung-rae—lawmakers from the Democratic Party of Korea, the current ruling party—argued at the time that a power that loses the people’s heart is destined to collapse like a sandcastle.

The source material highlights that the decline was not caused by a single policy failure but by a "total distrust" in the administration’s direction. This was exacerbated by the perceived weaponization of the legal system. Figures like Han Dong-hoon, who served as Justice Minister under Mr. Yoon, were seen as central to a "prosecutorial dictatorship" that prioritized political suppression over governance. This environment of fear and lack of communication eventually catalyzed the public movement that brought the current government to power.

The Trivialization of Statecraft via Social Media

A major contributor to this downfall was the perceived lack of "presidential gravity," specifically through Mr. Yoon’s use of social media. KSOI data revealed that almost all age groups, with the minor exception of those in their 50s, viewed his SNS activities as inappropriate for a head of state. Rather than a tool for transparency, his digital presence was often a source of unnecessary conflict with opposition leaders like Kim Min-seok, now a leader in the ruling Democratic Party. This "SNS politics" increased market uncertainty and damaged the country's international credit, as citizens woke up daily fearing new controversies.

The Shadow of Diplomatic Failure

The administration’s foreign policy was another pillar of its collapse. The source describes a "humiliating diplomacy" that wounded national pride and created security instability. By prioritizing ideology over national interest, Mr. Yoon’s government saw a massive decline in support even in traditionally sensitive regions. Today, as Mr. Yoon remains in prison following his impeachment, his tenure serves as a cautionary tale. President Lee Jae-myung’s administration has since moved to restore the "weight of the message," treating politics not as a game of social media engagement, but as a serious endeavor for national survival.

"The fall of approval ratings was not just a warning; it was the final exit that Mr. Yoon refused to take, leading inevitably to his judicial judgment."
