Internal Rift and Economic Realities Threaten Lee Jae-myung’s Administration

October 30, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is confronting a severe governance crisis as controversial judicial reforms stall, household economic misery deepens, and factional infighting fractures the ruling liberal coalition.

  • Judicial reform backfire: The administration's push to strip prosecutors of supplementary investigative authority has triggered severe case backlogs in everyday criminal cases, prompting the government to hesitate and dump the issue on parliament.
  • Main Street economic distress: Despite export-driven macro figures, domestic spending is in severe distress: household debt approaches 90% of GDP, and middle-aged breadwinners in their 40s and 50s have become the top consumers of cheap convenience store lunchboxes.
  • The unraveling of the liberal bloc: Prominent progressive commentators like author Rhyu Si-min warn that Lee's pivot toward aggressive online fandoms is purging the Democratic Party’s traditional loyalists, driving party support down to 31.5% while the conservative People Power Party has rebounded past 40%.
  • Bypassing official institutions: Critics highlight an institutional breakdown inside the presidential office, where President Lee's late-night personal social media posts—including citations from contentious online boards like FM Korea—leave aides scrambling to explain policy and party veterans unwilling to voice dissent.

Factional Purity Over Coalition Politics

The political alliance that delivered the Democratic Party's narrow electoral victory following the impeachment and imprisonment of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol is disintegrating from within. Rather than cultivating a broad governing coalition, the presidential office and party leadership have catered to an assertive, hardline pro-Lee faction that treats veteran party loyalists from the Kim Dae-jung, Roh Moo-hyun, and Moon Jae-in eras as outdated obstacles. Former lawmaker Choe Kang-wook warned that when a leader alienates traditional supporters and slips into a lame duck phase, the leader inevitably becomes political prey for allies and opponents alike.

This factional rigidity is most obvious in the drive for prosecution reform. By framing the complete removal of prosecutors' supplementary investigative powers as an ideological litmus test, the administration has disregarded warnings from legal scholars that vulnerable victims of white-collar and domestic crimes face indefinite delays. Yet, when public polling revealed majority support for retaining these checks, the administration retreated without offering a clear legislative solution, exposing an unwillingness to absorb political risk.

Macro Statistics Mask Grassroots Pain

A parallel disconnect defines the administration’s handling of the economy. Government messaging celebrates semiconductor exports, but street-level sentiment tells a starkly different story. Taxi drivers report business conditions worse than during the pandemic, and surging living costs have squeezed the disposable income of households already burdened by debt. Rather than expanding affordable public rental housing for young families, housing policy debates remain centered on deregulating high-end reconstructions.

Instead of addressing these grievances through structured policymaking, governance has increasingly drifted into impulsive social media management. Late-night personal posts and polarizing rhetoric cannot substitute for cabinet coordination. With senior party figures reluctant to deliver candid counsel for fear of jeopardizing future election nominations, the administration risks isolating itself within an echo chamber while the broader public drifts away.
