DPK Leadership Under Fire After "Shincheonji" Conspiracy Theories Debunked

November 03, 2026

An internal investigation by the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) has officially cleared the Shincheonji Church of Jesus—a controversial religious group often described as a cult—of allegations that it secretly meddled in the party’s recent leadership race.

  • The party’s probe, led by Representative Park Sun-won, found zero evidence of a "secret three-way alliance" or organized mass membership drives by the religious sect to influence internal voting.
  • Kim Min-seok, a senior DPK leader who championed the allegations to frame rivals as "anti-Lee Jae-myung," issued a proxy apology through a spokesperson rather than appearing in person.
  • Internal critics are calling for legal action under the Political Parties Act, which carries a penalty of up to five years in prison for spreading false information to influence an election.
  • The scandal has reignited scrutiny over a 1.7 billion KRW (about $1.2 million) budget allocated to a "prosecution reform" task force led by Kim, which critics claim yielded no tangible results.

The Weaponization of Cult Allegations

Kim Min-seok attempted to weaponize South Korea's deep-seated social distrust of Shincheonji to purge internal dissent and solidify his standing within the Lee Jae-myung administration’s power structure. By labeling his rivals as "cult-backed," he effectively polarized the party base during the convention. However, the investigation's conclusion that these claims were "entirely groundless" has left Kim in a political trap of his own making. His decision to apologize via a spokesperson—rather than facing the party members he labeled as potential infiltrators—has been widely condemned as a "cowardly" move that undermines the transparency the DPK claims to uphold.

This incident reflects a dangerous trend of "YouTube-driven politics" within the ruling party. Kim seemingly validated unverified claims from aggressive pro-government YouTubers who alleged tens of thousands of cult members had joined the party. By allowing conspiracy theories to dictate party policy and personnel framing, Kim has damaged the moral authority of the DPK. For a party currently holding the presidency and a parliamentary majority, the reliance on such fringe narratives suggests a fragile internal unity that relies more on "witch hunts" than on policy substance.

Legal Risks and Historical Baggage

The fallout is now moving from the political to the legal sphere. Lawmakers such as Choe Kang-wook and Lee Ji-eun have pointed out that Kim’s actions may constitute a criminal violation of election laws. Party members who felt maligned by the "Shincheonji" label are reportedly preparing injunctions to suspend Kim’s duties. This is a significant headache for the Lee Jae-myung government, which is trying to maintain a "one-team" image. If the party fails to discipline Kim, it risks alienating the moderate voters who are wary of the party’s more extremist, factional impulses.

Furthermore, this scandal has revived long-standing grievances regarding Kim Min-seok’s political reliability. Many party veterans are drawing parallels to 2002, when Kim famously deserted the late President Roh Moo-hyun during the presidential race. Critics argue that his recent actions prove his "political DNA" remains unchanged—prioritizing personal survival and factional dominance over the party's integrity. As the DPK seeks to support President Lee Jae-myung’s agenda, the presence of a senior leader who manufactures internal enemies may become a liability the administration can no longer afford to ignore.
