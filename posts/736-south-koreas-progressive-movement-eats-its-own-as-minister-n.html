South Korea's Progressive Movement Eats Its Own as Minister Nominee Resigns

September 13, 2026

  • Yong Hye-in, a minor party lawmaker and nominee for the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family, resigned just 14 days after her nomination amid a cascade of ethical controversies and pressure from within the ruling Democratic Party.
  • The collapse marks the fourth cabinet nominee to fail under President Lee Jae-myung in just 15 months, outpacing the early stumbles of previous administrations.
  • The fiasco has blown wide open a bitter civil war within South Korea's progressive camp, pitting the presidential office against veteran commentators and alienating core supporters.

South Korea's ruling progressive camp is locked in a self-destructive spiral, and the abrupt downfall of a high-profile cabinet nominee has laid the dysfunction bare. Yong Hye-in, the leader of a minor progressive partner and the nominee to head the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family, stepped down before she even faced a parliamentary confirmation hearing. A flurry of scandals—ranging from dual-office holding disputes to questionable real estate purchases and travel expense claims—made her position untenable. Even her nominal allies in the Democratic Party, South Korea's main liberal party, realized she had become a political liability they could no longer defend.

What makes this crisis significant is not merely another botched appointment, but the toxic fallout it triggered within the progressive establishment. As the nomination imploded, party loyalists and YouTubers loyal to President Lee unleashed vicious verbal attacks on veteran progressive pundit Rhyu Si-min, who had dared to criticize the administration's insular style and warned that the president's handling of the party resembles a medieval court rather than a republic. Rhyu coined a sharp term for these partisan commentators—"mercenary critics"—who attack internal dissenters not out of ideological conviction, but to curry favor with power and secure future political spoils.

Cracking Foundations and the Echo Chamber of Power

The deeper worry for the Lee Jae-myung administration is that its core base is growing cold. While pundits focus on swinging moderate voters, the real warning sign is the stony silence from the progressive movement's most dedicated supporters. Plagued by plunging approval ratings and a growing disconnect from public sentiment, the presidential office has retreated into an echo chamber. Rather than taking constructive criticism on board, the administration and its media surrogates dismiss internal dissent as betrayal.

History suggests that when a reforming government shuts its ears to uncomfortable truths and surrounds itself exclusively with sycophants, it courts disaster. The progressives swept to power criticizing the arrogance of past conservative administrations, yet they are rapidly copying the exact same playbook. If President Lee and his team continue to mistake bootlicking for loyalty, this appointment fiasco will look less like an isolated bump in the road and more like the beginning of a much steeper fall.
