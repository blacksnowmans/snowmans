South Korea’s Main Opposition Splits Open Over Allegedly Rigged Party Discipline

November 27, 2026

According to South Korean news outlets including Yonhap News and the Kyunghyang Shinmun, a November 26 leadership meeting of the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, collapsed into open confrontation over allegations of fabricated disciplinary action against former party leader Han Dong-hoon.

  • Leadership chaos and walkout: Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk and Supreme Council member Woo Jae-joon traded accusations during the meeting, with remarks touching on expulsion and leaving the party, before floor leader Jeong Jeom-sik walked out lamenting "the state of this party," according to Kyunghyang Shinmun and JTBC.
  • Ethics official dismissed: Today Shinmun and Segye Ilbo reported that Jang dismissed Hwang Kyung-sung, the party ethics committee member who first raised the rigging allegations, intensifying friction between party leadership and Han’s allies.
  • Backlash from Han and local members: Han responded by warning he would "mobilize all methods," Sisa Journal reported, while local party members in the Chungcheong region issued a statement questioning whether current leaders fit a conservative party, according to Lee Sejong Economy.
  • Showdown shifts to lawmakers: News1 and Money Today reported that senior lawmakers forced a general assembly of all PPP legislators—scheduled for either November 30 or December 2—to address the dispute, following a floor strategy meeting set for November 27.

A Distracted Opposition as the Ruling Party Advances

The public breakdown reveals a party leadership losing control over both its internal messaging and its basic decision-making bodies. When a floor leader walks out of a top leadership meeting and participants call to purge "Democratic Party spies," as reported by Green Economy News, the conflict has moved well beyond normal policy debate. Dismissing the ethics official who raised the complaint, rather than investigating the claim transparently, also risks turning a procedural dispute into a broader crisis of legitimacy.

The timing leaves the main opposition poorly positioned to challenge the ruling Democratic Party. On the same day the PPP’s leadership meeting fell apart, NewsPim noted that the ruling bloc finalized its nominee for a special prosecutor investigating alleged "fabricated indictments." Instead of presenting a unified counter-strategy ahead of its November 27 floor meeting, the PPP finds its political bandwidth consumed by factional infighting.

Warnings Over Hardline Rhetoric

The internal turmoil is also colliding with growing concern over the leadership’s broader political direction. Jinju Internet News reported that Reform Party lawmaker Lee Jun-seok publicly asked whether Jang is following the path of Hwang Kyo-ahn, a former conservative leader whose reliance on street protests and election fraud claims preceded a major parliamentary election defeat. Whether the upcoming assembly of lawmakers can restore internal discipline and steer the party away from hardline tactics will test whether the PPP can function as a credible opposition.
