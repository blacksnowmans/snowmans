Smart Packing for Europe: What to Bring and What to Leave Behind

September 10, 2026

    Skip the shower filters and travel debit cards: European tap water is generally safe, and widespread card acceptance makes specialized travel cards redundant compared to a standard airline miles credit card. Prioritize physical anti-theft gear: Use wrist straps for phones, bicycle locks for luggage on trains, and small cross-body bags worn under jackets to deter pickpockets. Pack for unpredictable weather and comfort: Bring a portable electric blanket for chilly mountain regions like the Dolomites, a dual-voltage hair dryer, and broken-in walking shoes to survive cobblestone streets. European cash is on the way out: Regional digital payment markets are booming, with card transaction volumes projected to top $1.2 trillion by 2031.

Shedding Dead Weight: What Not to Pack

Internet travel forums are full of well-meaning advice telling you to pack heavy-duty shower filters, bulky travel pillows, and specialized compression packing cubes. In reality, these items mostly just add dead weight to your luggage as you drag it across Europe's infamous cobblestone streets. Shower filters are generally unnecessary unless you have severe skin conditions, and compression cubes often make balancing your bag's weight more difficult than it is worth.

Financial prep is another area where travelers overcomplicate things. Instead of messing with dedicated travel cards and foreign currency exchanges, stick to a standard credit card that earns airline miles—such as co-branded cards offering strong mileage accrual rates on everyday purchases. Keep just €100 to €200 in cash for emergencies, as card acceptance is nearly universal outside of remote rural areas.

Security, Comfort, and Survival Gear

Petty theft remains a major headache for tourists in major European cities. A few low-tech habits go a long way: secure your smartphone with a wrist strap, thread a bicycle lock through your luggage handles when storing bags on trains, and keep your passport and main cards in a slim cross-body bag hidden beneath your outer layer.

Never underestimate European weather swings or thin hotel heating. Packing a lightweight, dual-voltage electric blanket can save your trip if you hit unseasonal cold snaps in mountain regions like the Italian Dolomites. Combined with familiar comforts like a travel-sized multi-voltage water boiler for instant noodles and tea, these small additions will keep your energy up and your immune system intact without breaking your back.
