President Lee Jae-myung’s Approval Rating Hits Record Low

October 08, 2026

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung’s administration is facing a severe political crisis, as his approval rating has stalled at 37.1%, while his disapproval rating has climbed to a record high of 61.4%, according to the latest Jowon C&I poll.

  • Stagnant Support: Despite recent efforts to stabilize public sentiment through press conferences, the administration has failed to reverse the downward trend.
  • The 30s Demographic: Support among voters in their 30s has cratered, driven not just by real estate concerns, but by the tangible misery of high rental costs and heavy losses in the stock market.
  • Shrinking Lead: The ruling Democratic Party of Korea (the liberal party currently in power) is seeing its support gap with the main opposition party, the conservative People Power Party (PPP), narrow to a virtual dead heat.
  • Impending Storm: The upcoming parliamentary audit—a period where the opposition rigorously grills the government—threatens to further erode public trust as the government appears woefully unprepared.

A Crisis of Competence and Lost Confidence

The Lee Jae-myung administration has found itself in a precarious position. While the president’s recent press conference successfully halted a steeper freefall, it failed to trigger a rebound. Unlike past political recoveries—such as when former president Moon Jae-in acknowledged real estate failures to win back support—the current administration’s shift toward a "gentler" demeanor is being viewed by the public as an insufficient, stopgap measure.

The core issue is that the government is losing its traditional support base. While the ruling Democratic Party is preoccupied with internal power struggles and infighting, the opposition People Power Party is benefiting from a "default" surge in support. With the parliamentary audit approaching, the government lacks a cohesive defense strategy, leaving it vulnerable to intense scrutiny from opposition lawmakers and aggressive coverage by mainstream media outlets.

The Cracks in the "Concrete" Support Base

The demographic breakdown of the polling data is particularly alarming for the ruling camp. The disapproval rate among those in their 30s has surged past 70%. Political analysts suggest that the government’s failure to grasp the root of this frustration is a major strategic blunder. It is not merely about loan regulations; it is about the "survival crisis" faced by younger generations struggling with sky-high rent and decimated savings from volatile financial markets.

Furthermore, the 40s and 50s age groups—historically the "concrete" support base for the Democratic Party—are showing visible signs of fatigue and abandonment. With disapproval ratings in the 50s and 60s across almost every region of the country, including once-stable areas like Gangwon and Jeju, the administration’s reliance on ideological politics is no longer resonating with a public focused on economic survival.

Unless the administration pivots from internal party squabbling to radical, tangible economic reforms, this period of "stagnation" is likely to give way to a deeper collapse. The political reality is harsh: the opposition's base remains iron-clad, and as long as the ruling party remains divided and out of touch with the average citizen’s daily struggle, the current decline may well prove terminal.
