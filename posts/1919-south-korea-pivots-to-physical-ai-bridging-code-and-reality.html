South Korea Pivots to Physical AI: Bridging Code and Reality

November 04, 2026

South Korea is aggressively transitioning from digital-only AI to "Physical AI," embedding intelligent software into drones, robots, and manufacturing lines to address severe labor shortages and modernize national defense.

  • Kakao Mobility, the country’s dominant taxi-hailing platform, is partnering with the ROK Army to build an integrated control system for "Army TIGER," the military's flagship project to modernize infantry with advanced technology.
  • MegazoneCloud, a major cloud management service provider, has teamed up with KAIST (the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology) to establish technical standards for physical AI infrastructure and data protocols.
  • Venture capital is flowing into the sector, highlighted by startup Moovin securing about $7.2 million (10 billion KRW) in Series A funding for its motion-capture and physical AI solutions.
  • Regional governments, such as North Jeolla Province in the southwest, are lobbying for federal budgets to build "AI Robot Farms," aiming to transform traditional agricultural hubs into high-tech manufacturing clusters.

From Digital Platforms to Strategic Hardware

The move by Kakao Mobility into the defense sector represents a significant strategic pivot. Traditionally a consumer-facing platform, the company is now applying its expertise in route optimization and fleet management to military drones and robots. This reflects the "Software Defined Vehicle" (SDV) trend, where the "brain" of the machine is becoming more valuable than the hardware itself. By securing a role in national defense, Kakao Mobility is not only diversifying its revenue but also seeking a "tech sovereignty" shield at a time when its domestic platform business faces heavy regulatory scrutiny.

Furthermore, the collaboration between DeepX, a prominent AI semiconductor startup, and global giant AMD highlights Korea's ambition to provide the hardware "brains" for the global AI market. For Physical AI to work, machines need high-performance, low-power chips (NPUs) to process data on-site rather than relying on distant servers. This push toward "on-device AI" is where Korean startups see a window to compete in the global supply chain, moving beyond the shadow of traditional memory chips.

Policy Shifts and Regional Survival

The current Lee Jae-myung administration is emphasizing a "science-first" approach to regional development, contrasting sharply with the budget cuts and policy shifts experienced during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure. For industrial and agricultural regions, Physical AI is viewed as a matter of survival rather than a luxury. In cities like Yangsan and provinces like North Jeolla, the focus is on "AI-driven manufacturing" to combat the dual threats of a shrinking workforce and rising competition from China.

"In the era of Physical AI, the goal is to move beyond political slogans and create a tangible industrial base where intelligent machines handle the heavy lifting of the real-world economy."

As the government pushes for "K-Manufacturing Revolutions," the success of these initiatives will depend on whether local governments can secure the necessary budget to move from pilot projects to full-scale automation. The trend is clear: South Korean AI is no longer just talking to us through chatbots; it is starting to build, farm, and defend the country.
