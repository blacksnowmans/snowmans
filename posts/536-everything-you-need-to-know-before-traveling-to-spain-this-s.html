Everything You Need to Know Before Traveling to Spain This Summer

September 10, 2026

  • Extreme Heat: Summer temperatures in Spain regularly cross 45°C (113°F) and can peak at 55°C, making July and August a survival test rather than a relaxing vacation.
  • Essential Bookings: Major landmarks like the Alhambra in Granada and the Sagrada Família in Barcelona require advance booking at least a month ahead, with strict passport name matching.
  • Local Customs: Unlike other parts of Europe, flagging down restaurant servers with casual phrases like "Perdona" is completely normal and expected.
  • Off-the-Beaten-Path: Skip the standard tourist-heavy routes and include smaller coastal gems like Málaga for a true local experience.

Planning a trip to Spain? Skip the standard travel guide advice—visiting during peak summer without preparation can turn your dream European getaway into an endurance test. While the country remains one of Europe's top holiday destinations, recent extreme weather trends mean travelers need to rethink how they approach the Spanish sun.

Beyond the brutal climate, enjoying Spain like a local comes down to understanding its daily rhythms. From timing your meals around the traditional afternoon break—the siesta—to swapping tourist-heavy sangria for a refreshing clara (beer mixed with lemon Fanta), small cultural adjustments make a massive difference. Opting for an apartment rental with a kitchen instead of a sterile hotel room also opens up the chance to shop local markets and throw together quick tapas, changing the entire texture of the trip.

Beat the Heat and Master Local Etiquette

The single biggest trap for travelers is underestimating the climate. Spain's dry air means shaded areas feel remarkably cool, but when the sun goes down or rain hits, temperatures plummet—making a light windbreaker mandatory even in warmer months. When hunger strikes during the mid-afternoon closure of local kitchens, don't waste time waiting patiently for inattentive staff; call them over boldly. By slowing down your itinerary, pacing yourself through the heat, and embracing the unhurried pace of Mediterranean life, Spain transforms from a checklist of landmarks into an unforgettable experience.
