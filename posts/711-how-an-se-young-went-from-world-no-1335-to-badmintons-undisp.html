How An Se-young Went from World No. 1,335 to Badminton’s Undisputed Queen

October 05, 2026

South Korea’s An Se-young has cemented her status as the undisputed queen of world badminton, transforming a career that began at a lowly world ranking of 1,335 into a record-breaking legacy of Olympic gold and over 100 consecutive weeks at the top.

  • From prodigy to pioneer: An became South Korea’s first middle-schooler ever selected for the senior national badminton team in 2017, before enduring grueling setbacks on the international circuit.
  • Breaking historical droughts: She claimed South Korea’s first All England Open singles title in 27 years (2023) and secured the nation's first Olympic women's singles gold in 28 years at the 2024 Paris Olympics, despite a severe knee tendon injury.
  • Systemic shockwaves: Her public criticism of the national team's injury management following the Paris Games triggered a government investigation and 26 major reforms to better protect South Korean athletes.
  • Unstoppable momentum: Entering 2026, An has continued to dominate, capturing consecutive Asian Games titles and maintaining her iron grip on world badminton.

From a Harsh Debut to Relentless Global Domination

An Se-young’s rise is a masterclass in resilience. When she stepped onto the senior international stage in early 2018, she held a humbling world ranking of 1,335 and suffered early exits, including a bruising defeat to her career-long rival, China's Chen Yufei, at the Jakarta Asian Games. Rather than fold, the teenage An used the failures as fuel, sweeping five international titles in 2019 to rocket into the world's top 10.

Her journey was far from smooth. A heartbreaking quarterfinal exit at the 2020 Tokyo Olympics pushed her to train even harder, eventually culminating in a historic 2023 season where she captured the world championship and snatched the world number one ranking. Even a torn knee tendon sustained during the Hangzhou Asian Games final could not stop her; she played through the agonizing pain to secure a gritty gold medal, displaying the fierce work ethic that defines her career.

Beyond the Medals: Changing Korean Sports Culture

What makes An's story resonate beyond sports is her willingness to challenge the status quo. Following her emotional gold-medal victory at the Paris Olympics, she spoke out candidly about the rigid structures and inadequate injury care within South Korea's national team system. By demanding better player welfare and the right to choose her own medical treatment, she forced the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism to overhaul legacy practices.

Now firmly entrenched at the peak of the sport with over 160 cumulative weeks at number one, An rejects the label of a "natural genius," frequently reminding interviewers that her success is built entirely on grueling, unglamorous hard work. As she continues to break records through 2026, the 12-year-old girl who once cried through high-school-level drills has evolved into a generational icon for South Korean athletics.
