The "Shincheonji" Scandal Splitting the Democratic Party

November 04, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party is facing a severe internal crisis following the collapse of unverified accusations that party figures were colluding with Shincheonji, a controversial religious group. The scandal, initiated by high-ranking party official Kim Min-seok, has sparked intense backlash from party members who demand accountability for what they label a smear campaign.

  • The Allegation: Kim Min-seok claimed that rival candidates and supporters within the party were part of a "secret three-way alliance" with Shincheonji to undermine current party leadership.
  • The Reality: An internal investigation recently revealed there is no evidence to support these claims, leading to accusations that the narrative was a fabrication designed to silence political dissent.
  • The Fallout: Members targeted by these accusations, including senior party figures, are publicly demanding a formal, public apology from Kim, rejecting his attempt to resolve the issue through a private meeting of lawmakers.
  • Legal Stakes: Critics are now calling for an investigation into whether these actions violated Article 52 of the Political Parties Act, which prohibits the circulation of false information to sabotage primary candidates.

The Crisis of Accountability

The core of the frustration lies in the perceived arrogance of the party’s leadership. By branding loyal party members as religious infiltrators, the leadership effectively subjected them to a "witch hunt." Now that the accusations have been debunked behind closed doors, many rank-and-file members feel that a quiet apology among elites is an insult to the humiliation they endured. The incident has exposed a deep cultural rift within the Democratic Party: the tension between a leadership that favors internal control and a base that values transparency.

Politically, this is more than just an internal squabble. Kim Min-seok’s rhetoric was used to frame opponents as "anti-Lee" (opposed to current President Lee Jae-myung) dividers. This weaponization of loyalty has left a bitter taste, and if not addressed with a sincere public apology, it threatens to alienate the party’s core support base. The demand for a transparent report on the party’s internal task force findings is growing, as members insist that the party must prove it can hold its own leadership accountable to the same standards it applies to the opposition.

Beyond the Party: A Climate of Distrust

This incident occurs against a backdrop of broader institutional instability in South Korea. The public’s trust in the judiciary, currently led by Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, is at a record low. The perceived double standard in handling high-profile cases—ranging from the swift handling of cases involving President Lee to the slow-walking of cases involving Kim Keon-hee—has fueled calls for judicial reform and even the impeachment of the Chief Justice.

Meanwhile, the recent sentencing of military commanders for their roles in the December 3 martial law attempt has provided a rare moment of institutional clarity. By sentencing key figures to prison terms (up to 18 years for former Counterintelligence Command chief Yeo In-hyung), the courts have sent a strong message that "following orders" is not a valid defense for subverting a democratic government. However, as the Democratic Party navigates its own internal strife, the lesson remains clear: institutions, whether political or military, cannot maintain their integrity if they prioritize personal power over constitutional duty.
