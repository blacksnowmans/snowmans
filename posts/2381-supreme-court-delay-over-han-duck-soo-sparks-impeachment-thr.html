Supreme Court Delay Over Han Duck-soo Sparks Impeachment Threat

November 16, 2026

A Realmeter poll cited in South Korean political commentary shows 56.9% of respondents view the potential release of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo as problematic, fueling threats from the Rebuilding Korea Party to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.

  • Six-month court delay: The Supreme Court has postponed a ruling for six months regarding Han’s alleged role in the emergency martial law and insurrection crisis involving Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who is imprisoned following his impeachment and removal from office.
  • Impeachment warning: Rebuilding Korea Party leader Shin Jang-sik and floor leader Kim Jun-hyung warned that the minor progressive party would immediately move to impeach Chief Justice Cho if Han is released.
  • Detention deadline risk: Observers cited in South Korean commentary warn that prolonged delays could leave room for Han to be freed once his detention period expires.
  • Judicial independence pushback: Critics in political circles contend that threatening to impeach the head of the judiciary is an excessive measure that undermines judicial independence and risks sparking conservative backlash.

The Clash Over Accountability and Judicial Trust

The standoff exposes a widening divide between South Korea’s judiciary and public expectations in the wake of the martial law turmoil. Critics of the court argue that allowing a former prime minister to walk free while proceedings stall reinforces perceptions of a legal double standard—where high-ranking officials benefit from leniency while ordinary citizens face strict enforcement. From this perspective, the six-month silence from Chief Justice Cho’s bench looks less like legal deliberation and more like political calculation.

At the same time, the Rebuilding Korea Party’s ultimatum confronts lawmakers—including those in the ruling Democratic Party—with a difficult strategic choice. As Realmeter chief Lee Taek-soo and political commentator Park Si-young noted, pursuing the impeachment of a sitting Chief Justice carries heavy political burdens and risks freezing legislative politics. Still, with a clear majority of polled citizens opposing Han’s release, political leaders face strong pressure from voters not to settle for half-measures.

A High-Stakes Test for Korea’s Institutions

Ultimately, the dispute has grown beyond a single detention decision into a broader test of institutional credibility. If the Supreme Court delays further and Han is released on procedural grounds, public skepticism toward the courts could harden into a lasting crisis of trust. Conversely, if lawmakers press ahead with an impeachment drive, South Korea faces a rare constitutional confrontation between the legislature and its highest court.
