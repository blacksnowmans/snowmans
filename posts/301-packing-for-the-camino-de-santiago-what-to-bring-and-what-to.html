Packing for the Camino de Santiago: What to Bring and What to Leave Behind

September 07, 2026

  • What happened: Experienced pilgrims are sharing hard-earned advice on packing efficiently for the grueling 800-kilometer (about 500-mile) Camino de Santiago trek in Spain, where statistics show 68% of walkers suffer from blisters and 42% are forced to cut their trips short.
  • What it changes: It debunks the myth of ultra-minimalism without support and warns against bringing "just in case" items like bulky electronics, heavy books, and excessive toiletries that bloat pack weight.
  • Who is affected: Anyone planning to tackle the historic pilgrimage route in upcoming seasons, particularly first-timers prone to overpacking out of anxiety.

The golden rule of Spain's famous Camino de Santiago pilgrimage is simple: your backpack is your burden. While beginners often pack out of fear, loading their bags with hypothetical scenarios, veterans know that every extra kilogram directly multiplies your fatigue over a 30-day trek. Recent data underscores the stakes, with nearly half of all pilgrims experiencing trip-interrupting physical issues—often starting with poorly distributed weight or preventable foot trauma.

When it comes to gear, structured support trumps pure weight-savings. Choosing a backpack with a proper frame—such as models with waist belts that distribute weight off the shoulders—prevents physical breakdown. Footwear requires a similar reality check: buying walking shoes a full size (10mm) up is essential to accommodate severe swelling after pacing 25 kilometers a day, and frequent sock-changes are far more effective at preventing blisters than expensive waterproof linings. Meanwhile, most basic toiletries, utensils, and food can easily be sourced locally in towns along the route.

Ultimately, a successful pilgrimage isn't a race to the finish line. Ditching the urge to rigidly hit daily distance targets or pack heavy comforts allows travelers to actually enjoy the journey and protect their bodies. As infrastructure along the route continues to improve, trusting local availability and walking at your own natural pace remain the ultimate survival hacks.
