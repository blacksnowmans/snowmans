South Korea Under Fire: Scandals, Stifled Dissent, and a Plunging Approval Rating

October 07, 2026

  • Political Targeting: A progressive civic group has filed a criminal complaint against lawyer Lee Jong-keun, husband of opposition-aligned lawmaker Park Eun-jung, drawing heavy criticism as a tactic of political retaliation disguised as law enforcement.
  • Financial Fallout: The botched introduction of leveraged ETFs has triggered a massive $39 billion (54 trillion won) loss for retail investors, with top officials dodging accountability and resisting a special counsel probe.
  • Judicial Luxury Scandal: Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-de faces intense public backlash for misusing official expense accounts on high-end omakase meals and dozens of luxury wine bottles.
  • Plunging Support: President Lee Jae-myung’s administration is grappling with an approval rating sitting at just 37.1%, fueled by internal party purges, economic fallout, and growing voter alienation.

South Korea’s progressive administration, led by President Lee Jae-myung, finds itself mired in a deepening crisis of hypocrisy and misrule. Critics point out an unsettling irony: the very tactics of political targeted investigations once weaponized by the conservative establishment are now being replicated under a liberal government to silence internal dissent and opposition voices alike.

The recent legal filing against lawyer Lee Jong-keun by a civic activist group—targeting him ostensibly over past investigative work—is widely viewed as a thinly veiled proxy attack on his wife, lawmaker Park Eun-jung, who has openly criticized the administration's personnel policies. This heavy-handed approach mirrors the abrasive political engineering that has alienated traditional allies within the Democratic Party and accelerated an internal factional war between loyalists and moderates.

Financial Blunders and Luxury Scandals Deepen Public Anger

Beyond political infighting, the government is facing severe backlash over sweeping policy failures. The disastrous rollout of leveraged exchange-traded funds (ETFs) wiped out an estimated $39 billion (54 trillion won) in public wealth without a single paper trail or accountable official stepping forward. Despite calls for a special counsel investigation, the administration has stonewalled transparency efforts, intensifying comparisons to the opaque governance styles of the past.

Compounding the administration's woes is a major scandal involving Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-de, who allegedly misused official funds to finance luxury omakase dinners and expensive wine purchases amid severe economic hardship for average citizens. With President Lee’s approval ratings slumping to 37.1% against a 61.4% disapproval rate, the government's credibility is wearing thin across all demographics—including younger voters who are increasingly walking away from the ruling party.
