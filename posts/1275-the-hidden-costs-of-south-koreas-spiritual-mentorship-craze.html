The Hidden Costs of South Korea's Spiritual Mentorship Craze

October 18, 2026

South Korea’s growing market for spiritual counseling and personal mentorship is facing a reckoning as self-styled life masters leverage personal warmth and charismatic authority into financial extortion and psychological abuse.

  • The catalyst: Public fallout from popular Korean creators, such as the YouTube channel Sunnys, who rescinded endorsements of revered spiritual mentors after victims reported severe exploitation, has brought toxic mentorship under public debate.
  • The escalation: Exploitation routinely starts with modest seminar fees or private tea sessions before snowballing into demands for tens of thousands of dollars under the guise of "special spiritual transmissions" and advanced training tiers.
  • Institutional failure: The pattern spans both fringe spiritual retreats and established religious institutions—such as internal legal battles within the Presbyterian Church of Korea (Hapdong), the country’s largest conservative Presbyterian denomination—forcing disillusioned followers to seek relief through the civil and criminal justice systems.

The Two Critical Warnings: Big Money and Total Control

Personal intimacy is often the primary vehicle for manipulation. In private, one-on-one settings, predatory mentors present an exceptionally benevolent, empathetic persona that blinds followers to organizational rot. That intimacy makes it difficult for followers to separate individual warmth from structural toxicity, leaving them vulnerable to gaslighting rationalized as "spiritual rigor" or "clearing karma."

Healthy mentorship never requires exorbitant financial sacrifice, nor does it demand total exclusivity. The moment an organization demands life-altering sums—scaling from tens of dollars to tens of millions of won (thousands or tens of thousands of dollars)—the relationship transitions from guidance to spiritual extortion. When coupled with isolation tactics that brand outside influences as corrupt and insist that only one master possesses the path to enlightenment, mentorship effectively becomes psychological imprisonment.

The Neuroscience of Uncertainty and 'Negative Capability'

When controversies erupt, followers and observers often suffer intense paralysis and self-doubt. Clinical research from University College London highlights that human nervous systems experience significantly higher physiological stress when facing a 50 percent probability of harm than when facing absolute certainty of pain. The sheer ambiguity of who to believe—between a seemingly gentle teacher and an aggrieved victim—triggers sustained stress responses and cognitive overload.

Surviving such entanglements requires what 19th-century poet John Keats called "negative capability": the discipline to remain in uncertainty and doubt without aggressively rushing to premature conclusions or taking sides before facts emerge. True spiritual maturity is measured not by how dogmatically a follower defends a charismatic figure, but by whether one's relationships with family, friends, and everyday reality become healthier and freer.
