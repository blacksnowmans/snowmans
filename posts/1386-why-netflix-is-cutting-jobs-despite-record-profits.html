Why Netflix Is Cutting Jobs Despite Record Profits

October 22, 2026

Despite generating billions in operating profits, Netflix is facing a structural crisis as it loses living room viewing time to YouTube, struggles with subscriber fatigue, and writes off a failed billion-dollar venture into gaming.

  • Massive workforce reduction: Netflix cut roughly 1,000 staff members—about 6% of its workforce—in October 2026 alongside a major executive reshuffle, even after reporting $4.19 billion in quarterly operating profit.
  • Losing ground to YouTube: According to Nielsen data, Netflix’s share of U.S. TV watch time slipped to 7.8% in July 2026 while YouTube climbed to 14.2%, highlighting a dangerous disconnect between rising revenue and stagnant user engagement.
  • A failed $1.1 billion gaming bet: After pouring over 1.5 trillion won (about $1.1 billion) directly into game development and intellectual property acquisitions, daily user participation stayed stuck at around 1%, triggering studio shutdowns and abandoned projects.
  • Aggressive pivot to AI and K-content: To curb bloated production costs, Netflix is deploying generative AI to cut visual effects production timelines tenfold while leaning heavily on high-performing international hits like Squid Game to maintain subscriber retention.

Squeezed by YouTube and "Streamflation"

At first glance, Netflix’s financials look formidable, boasting a 33.4% operating margin and quarterly operating profits exceeding $4 billion. Yet its stock plunged 43% over the past year. As subscription prices climbed—a trend dubbed "streamflation"—viewers began practicing "subscription dieting," paying for a single month to binge a specific title and immediately canceling. While Netflix’s revenue grew 14.7% year-over-year in early 2026, total viewing time grew by a mere 2.0%.

The core structural threat is not traditional Hollywood rivals, but YouTube. Netflix bears all financial risk upfront to produce massive original shows like Stranger Things, which can cost up to $60 million per episode. YouTube, by contrast, relies on user-generated content where creators absorb production costs. As consumer attention shifts toward shorter, everyday creator content, multi-million dollar streaming productions are losing their grip on the living room.

Failed Diversification and the AI Pivot

Attempts to diversify beyond video streaming have stumbled. To build an ecosystem around its service, Netflix spent over 1.5 trillion won (about $1.1 billion) on game studio acquisitions and licensing rights for major titles like Grand Theft Auto. However, mobile performance issues and cloud streaming limitations resulted in games accounting for less than 0.5% of total user viewing time. By mid-2026, Netflix began shutting down internal development houses like Night School and Moonloot, effectively shelving its AAA gaming ambitions.

To protect its profit margins without scaling back flagship programming, Netflix is aggressively trimming production overhead. The company has begun integrating generative AI tools into visual effects and editing—slashing VFX production timelines by 10 times on shows like Argentina’s sci-fi series The Eternaut. Combined with massive investments in hit Korean content like Squid Game Season 3 and The Tyrant's Chef, Netflix is scrambling to defend its moat in an increasingly hostile attention economy.
