Police Raid on Progressive Channel Sisa Tapa Exposes Rift in Ruling Democratic Party

October 22, 2026

South Korean police raided the headquarters and private residence of political commentator Lee Jong-won, head of the progressive YouTube channel Sisa Tapa, sparking accusations of an internal purge orchestrated by factions loyal to President Lee Jae-myung.

  • The raid: Police deployed two riot buses on Wednesday, October 21, executing search and seizure warrants on Lee’s home and broadcast office just before a scheduled livestream.
  • The charges: Authorities accuse Lee of obstructing the Democratic Party of Korea's (DPK) election commission and polling firms by allegedly advising livestream viewers in August to misreport their age and location during party primary polls.
  • The backlash: Critics call raiding a private home over publicly archived YouTube comments blatant prosecutorial overkill, comparing the display of force to press intimidation seen during Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol’s tenure before his impeachment.
  • Factional friction: The operation is widely seen as an opening salvo by hardline pro-Lee groups to dismantle the influence of the "pro-Moon" faction loyal to former President Moon Jae-in.

Overkill Investigation Points to an Internal Power Struggle

The decision to deploy heavy police resources against a commentator whose remarks remain fully preserved on public video servers defies standard investigatory proportionality. Rather than merely verifying recorded footage, the raid on Lee's personal home strongly suggests an effort to seize devices and probe wider networks. For critics inside the ruling camp, this weaponization of law enforcement under the current Democratic Party administration uncomfortably mirrors the very methods once used by conservative prosecutors to muzzle critical outlets.

This aggressive posture arrives amid an escalating campaign to sideline traditional party moderates and pro-Moon loyalists. Former President Moon Jae-in faces a contentious bribery trial scheduled for January over living expenses and salary his former son-in-law received from Thai Eastar Jet, a case drawing fierce condemnation for relying on dozens of prosecutorial witnesses. At the same time, rumors of an impending shakeup targeting Senior Presidential Secretary for Political Affairs Hong Ik-pyo—a respected party regular seen as a brake on radical infighting—suggest hardliners are clearing the board of internal checks.

The political cost of this factional cannibalism is already mounting. Polls indicate presidential negative ratings hovering near 60%, alongside double-digit drops in party support following the rise of hardline leadership figures. By allowing fringe factions—including radical National Liberation (NL) activists—to dictate internal retribution and target fellow progressives, the Lee administration risks squandering public trust on ideological vendettas rather than stabilizing state affairs.
