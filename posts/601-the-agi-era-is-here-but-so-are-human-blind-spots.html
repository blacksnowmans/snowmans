The AGI Era is Here — But So Are Human Blind Spots

September 10, 2026

  • Advanced AI models like "GPT-6 Astra" are crushing complex reasoning tests and solving decades-old math puzzles, fueling intense debate over whether Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) has arrived.
  • While machines excel at hyper-scale computation and pattern recognition, commentators emphasize that raw intelligence is not the same as human wisdom or social competence.
  • Meanwhile, South Korea's liberal opposition party continues to slide in the polls amid internal factionalism and policy fatigue over taxes and economic levers, highlighting how real-world political dysfunction outpaces technological disruption.

As artificial intelligence begins to solve mathematical conjectures that would take human teams lifetimes to untangle, the boundary between sci-fi speculation and tomorrow's morning news has effectively collapsed. Recent commentary from tech analysts and legal experts in Seoul highlights a dizzying paradox: while algorithms achieve near-perfect scores on abstract reasoning tests, they remain entirely unequipped for the messy, low-data realities of human social life—like negotiating a bank loan or reading a stranger's anxious glance.

This rush toward machine supremacy risks blinding us to enduring human problems. AI can accelerate drug discovery or crack graph theory, but it cannot fix broken economic structures or heal factional splits within political parties. As Korean politicians squabble over internal strategy and tax policy instead of offering a united front during hard economic times, the public is left exhausted. Technology is scaling new heights, but the fundamental duty of navigating society's well-being remains stubbornly, unmistakably human.
