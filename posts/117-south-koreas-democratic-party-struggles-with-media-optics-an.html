South Korea’s Democratic Party Struggles with Media Optics and Sliding Approval

September 03, 2026

South Korea’s main opposition party, the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), is facing mounting scrutiny as its media arm, "Minjoo TV," comes under fire for broadcasting scenes that critics describe as performative and authoritarian, while the administration of President Lee Jae-myung grapples with a significant decline in public support.

  • Performative Politics: Minjoo TV has sparked controversy over footage showing staff bowing at 90-degree angles to DPK representative Kim Min-seok, raising concerns about a rigid, hierarchical party culture.
  • Approval Crisis: President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating has plummeted to the low 30% range, with disapproval ratings exceeding 60%.
  • Loss of Trust: Political analysts argue that the party’s focus on polished media production is failing to mask an increasing disconnect between the DPK’s internal power dynamics and the actual needs of the electorate.

Media Strategy vs. Political Reality

The DPK’s effort to modernize its communication strategy through Minjoo TV—hiring high-profile anchors to run professionalized news-style programming—was intended to broaden its appeal. However, the optics have backfired. Rather than fostering horizontal communication with the public, the behind-the-scenes footage reveals a culture obsessed with status and deference. By prioritizing internal "loyalty theater" over transparent engagement, the party has inadvertently reinforced the image of an exclusionary "boys' club."

This dissonance is proving costly. Representative Kim Min-seok’s earlier promises to boost approval ratings by 10 percentage points have failed to materialize. Instead, the party is seeing a sharp 8.4% drop in support over the past month. The data suggests that even the core base and voters from the liberal-leaning Rebuilding Korea Party are becoming disillusioned. When the visual presentation of a party contradicts the reality of its governance, voters perceive it as arrogance rather than leadership.

The Cost of Ignoring the Electorate

The core issue here is not the quality of the production, but the lack of substance. The current administration’s reforms are facing a 60% disapproval rate among the public, yet the DPK continues to prioritize controlled media messaging over addressing the underlying dissatisfaction on the ground.

In modern politics, the camera is a double-edged sword. While it serves as a powerful tool to disseminate policy, it also exposes the "unfiltered" truth of a party’s internal culture. If the DPK continues to prioritize rigid hierarchies and performative displays, they will remain trapped in an echo chamber of their own making. For a political movement, the most dangerous enemy is not a hostile media environment, but the cold, hard numbers of an indifferent public.
