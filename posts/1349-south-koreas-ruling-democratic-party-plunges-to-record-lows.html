South Korea's Ruling Democratic Party Plunges to Record Lows as Capital and Youth Defect

October 20, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party has dropped to an all-time low in public approval, sounding urgent alarms for President Lee Jae-myung as conservative rivals surge across key demographics ahead of parliamentary elections.

  • Historic polling slump: A recent Realmeter survey places the main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), comfortably ahead at 47.3%, while the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) has collapsed in the critical Seoul capital region, trailing 26.7% to the PPP's 45.7%.
  • Youth and moderates flip: Voters in their 20s and 30s now favor the PPP at rates exceeding 50%—more than double the support given to the DPK—while self-identified centrists have swung decisively behind the conservatives (37.7%).
  • Leadership crisis: DPK leader Kim Min-seok’s approval rating has sunk to just 28.0% against a 66.3% negative rating, fueling calls for a leadership overhaul as party insiders warn of winning fewer than 120 seats in the next general election.
  • Stumbling reforms: President Lee recently acknowledged that his administration lacked preparation and promised to moderate the pace of key reforms, but critics view the shift as an evasive tactic that dodges core governance failures.

Behind the Freefall: Alienated Moderates and Factional Feuds

The DPK's collapse in the greater Seoul area marks a disastrous unraveling of its traditional electoral fortress. Younger voters and moderates, who once powered liberal victories, have grown disillusioned with partisan warfare and ideological rhetoric. The ruling camp has instead alienated these pragmatists by fixating on divisive initiatives—such as an impeachment push against Chief Justice Jo Hee-de—and engaging in bitter infighting between pro-Lee loyalists and rival party factions over high-profile appointments.

For voters in their 20s and 30s, the administration's messaging on fairness and economic opportunity has failed to translate into tangible relief. With the PPP now leading in nearly every region outside the DPK's southwestern Honam stronghold, the ruling party's dream of building a durable, structural majority has ground to a halt.

Slowing Down Is Not a Substitute for Course Correction

President Lee's concession that the government must "control the pace" of its reform agenda misdiagnoses the fundamental problem. When a vehicle is headed toward a cliff, easing off the accelerator does not prevent a crash; the driver must change direction. Pacing alone cannot resolve flawed policy designs, lack of stakeholder consultation, or administrative missteps.

Direct communication via social media and appeals to partisan unity will no longer rescue the administration's numbers. To halt its slide before the general election, the Lee administration must abandon its reflex of branding critics as anti-reform obstructionists and start delivering competent, accountable governance that addresses middle-class realities.
