Controversy Erupts Over Nomination of Kim Ji-yong as Investigative Agency Chief

October 09, 2026

The nomination of Kim Ji-yong to lead the proposed Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (SCIA)—a body intended to strip the prosecution of its vast powers—has ignited a fierce political firestorm. Critics, led by former Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae, argue that appointing a "prosecution loyalist" to the very office meant to spearhead reform is an affront to the administration’s core promises.

  • The Nominee: Kim Ji-yong, a senior prosecutor previously involved in efforts to block investigations into former leader Yoon Suk-yeol (who was impeached and is currently imprisoned).
  • The Allegations: Kim is accused of actively suppressing prosecutorial reform and participating in the "unlawful" indictment of prosecutor Jeong Jin-woong, who was later cleared of charges by the Supreme Court.
  • The Political Fallout: The nomination has deepened the rift within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), with supporters of reform questioning whether President Lee Jae-myung’s administration has abandoned its foundational values.

A Betrayal of Reform?

The primary objection to Kim lies in his track record as a defender of the traditional, bloated prosecution system. During his time as a deputy chief at the Seoul High Prosecutors' Office, Kim was a vocal opponent of disciplinary actions against Yoon Suk-yeol, despite evidence of major misconduct, including the surveillance of judges. Critics argue that his background as a "prosecution loyalist" makes him fundamentally unfit to dismantle the very power structures he once protected.

The controversy is exacerbated by his involvement in the legal targeting of prosecutor Jeong Jin-woong. Jeong was indicted for alleged professional misconduct during a high-profile investigation into a scandal involving the press and the prosecution. Kim signed off on the indictment, a move critics characterize as a calculated political act to stifle investigations that threatened the status quo. Now that the Supreme Court has acquitted Jeong, the fact that Kim is being rewarded with a top-tier appointment is viewed by many as a "payback" promotion that undermines the credibility of the new agency.

President Lee’s Shifting Priorities

The nomination has forced a painful realization among many DPK supporters: the current government appears to be distancing itself from the reform-minded base that propelled it to power. By labeling critics of this appointment as "irresponsible extremists" and failing to provide a logical justification for Kim’s selection beyond "presidential prerogative," the administration risks alienating its core constituency.

Observers argue that this shift represents a broader consolidation of power. Instead of completing the promised "prosecution reform," the government appears to be utilizing existing institutional tools to suppress internal dissent and neutralize political rivals. The appointment is not just about one individual; it serves as a litmus test for the administration’s philosophy. For those who fought to remove the previous regime, seeing the current government align with the same institutional forces they once opposed creates a sense of betrayal. The question remains: is this a temporary political maneuver, or has the Lee Jae-myung administration permanently abandoned the reformist agenda?
