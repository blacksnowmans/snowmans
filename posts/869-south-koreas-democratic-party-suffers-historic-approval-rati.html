South Korea's Democratic Party Suffers Historic Approval Rating Plunge

September 21, 2026

South Korea’s governing Democratic Party (DP) is facing a severe political crisis as its approval ratings have plummeted, allowing the conservative People Power Party (PPP) to overtake it in recent polls.

  • What happened: The DP’s approval rating dropped significantly in one week, falling to 36.1% against the PPP’s 42.1%, according to Realmeter data.
  • The youth exodus: Support among voters in their 20s—a core base for the party—has collapsed to an all-time low of 18.6%, with young women showing the most dramatic decline.
  • Internal turmoil: Controversial personnel appointments, such as the naming of Kang Du-gu to head the Institute for Democracy, have deepened internal party rifts.
  • The outlook: Leadership is being accused of prioritizing factional interests over policy, leaving the party vulnerable to further loss of public trust.

The Youth Vote: A Catalyst for Collapse

The shockwaves in the DP’s support base are largely centered on voters in their 20s. Analysts point to the "personnel appointment disaster" involving candidates like Yong Hye-in and Kim Seung-won as the primary triggers for this exodus. Kim has faced allegations regarding influence-peddling, while Yong has been criticized for perceived nepotism in her nominations. When leadership failed to address these concerns—and instead dismissed them as purely legal matters—it alienated young voters who had previously championed the party's reformist image.

This is further exacerbated by the party’s silence on sensitive issues. For instance, when high-profile figures faced accusations of inappropriate conduct, the DP’s female leadership remained largely mute. This perceived hypocrisy has caused even the most loyal young supporters to feel betrayed, transforming their initial excitement into deep-seated resentment.

"Hostage Politics" and Internal Factionalism

Rather than seeking to heal these divisions, the party leadership—led by Kim Min-seok—appears to be doubling down on controversial tactics. The appointment of Kang Du-gu, a figure often associated with internal discord, as the head of the party’s primary think tank, the Institute for Democracy, has been widely criticized as an attempt to consolidate power rather than foster unity. Many within the party fear that the Institute is being turned into a tool for specific factional agendas rather than a platform for national policy development.

Furthermore, the leadership’s recent rhetoric has been dangerously inflammatory. By comparing internal criticism from figures like Gyeonggi Governor Choo Mi-ae to the "prelude to the impeachment of President Roh Moo-hyun," Kim Min-seok has inadvertently normalized talk of "impeaching President Lee Jae-myung." This "hostage politics"—where the party leadership attempts to shield the President through aggressive, polarizing tactics—has backfired, making the leadership appear out of touch and obsessed with power struggles rather than governance.

The Democratic Party is currently in a downward spiral of its own making. Unless leadership abandons its current path of factional hegemony and begins to address the genuine concerns of its base, the upcoming election cycle may result in a much more painful political reality for the party.
