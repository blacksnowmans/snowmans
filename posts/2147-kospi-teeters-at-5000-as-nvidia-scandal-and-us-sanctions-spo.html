KOSPI Teeters at 5,000 as Nvidia Scandal and U.S. Sanctions Spook Markets

November 09, 2026

South Korea’s financial markets are facing intense pressure at the 5,000-point level as a leadership scandal at Nvidia and tightening U.S. tech sanctions on China trigger a massive sell-off by foreign investors.

  • The Lee Jae-myung administration has introduced an emergency measure to waive the 140% margin maintenance requirement for three months to prevent a "death spiral" of forced liquidations.
  • Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s alleged ties to a Chinese intelligence asset have sparked fears of stricter U.S. export controls, directly threatening SK Hynix, South Korea’s second-largest chipmaker, and its operations in China.
  • Foreign investors offloaded over $430 million (600 billion won) in a single session, despite a brief technical rebound that saw the KOSPI rise 1.5% to 5,144 points.
  • The upcoming IPO of AI firm Anthropic is being watched as a critical bellwether; a failure to meet its $2 trillion valuation goal could signal a definitive end to the AI investment boom.

Geopolitical Fallout and the Silicon Shield

The current volatility is rooted in a "perfect storm" of geopolitical and corporate governance risks. Jensen Huang’s personal scandal has evolved into a national security issue for the U.S., potentially leading to a total blockade of advanced chips to China. This is a nightmare scenario for SK Hynix, which is currently awaiting U.S. approval to bring manufacturing equipment into its NAND flash plant in Dalian, China. If the U.S. uses the Nvidia scandal as a pretext to tighten the screws, the Korean semiconductor sector's recovery could be derailed indefinitely.

Furthermore, the "AI bubble" is facing a reality check regarding infrastructure. Companies like CoreWeave, which builds data centers for Big Tech, are facing localized backlash over power consumption and rising debt. While some firms are attempting to appease local governments by promising to fund infrastructure personally, the cooling sentiment is clear. If these infrastructure providers cannot maintain their lease income, the entire AI value chain—from Nvidia’s chips to SK Hynix’s memory—will feel the shock.

Emergency Measures and the Liquidation Floor

In response to the market slide, the Lee Jae-myung government and top regulators held an "F4" meeting—a gathering of the heads of the finance ministry, the central bank, and financial watchdogs. They decided to temporarily exempt brokerages from enforcing the 140% margin maintenance ratio. This ratio is the minimum collateral a "debt-investor" must keep; if it falls below the threshold, the brokerage automatically sells the shares. While this provides a temporary buffer, it is essentially a "delay of execution" that does not address the underlying lack of buying pressure.

Market analysts are pointing to the KOSPI’s Price-to-Book Ratio (PBR) of 1.0—essentially the market's liquidation value—as a potential floor. At this level, the market is pricing Korean companies at exactly what their physical assets are worth, with no premium for future growth. However, history shows that in times of extreme fear, the KOSPI can drop to a PBR of 0.8. For investors, the strategy must shift from passive endurance to active risk management. Holding a losing position without a plan is merely a hope; holding cash to buy the actual bottom is a strategy.
