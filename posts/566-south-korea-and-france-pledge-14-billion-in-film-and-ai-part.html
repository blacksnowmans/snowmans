South Korea and France Pledge $1.4 Billion in Film and AI Partnership in Paris

September 10, 2026

  • South Korean President Lee Jae-myung made a state visit to Paris, attending the Lumière Summit alongside leaders from over 50 countries.
  • Seoul and Paris announced the "Lumière Partnership," a five-year joint investment plan totaling €1 billion (about $1.1 billion), with each nation contributing €500 million (about $560 million) to film and visual content industries.
  • In his keynote speech, President Lee framed artificial intelligence as a tool that must serve human growth rather than replace people.
  • The tight two-day diplomatic schedule included a summit at the Élysée Palace, meetings with France's National Assembly leadership, and talks with OECD Secretary-General Mathias Cormann.

South Korea's recent diplomatic push in France breaks the mold of traditional, transactional state visits by anchoring high-level trade directly to cultural and technological philosophy. By bringing a massive $1.4 billion joint investment to the table at the Lumière Summit, the administration has moved beyond simply selling consumer entertainment, positioning South Korea as a co-creator of global industry standards.

The emphasis on human-centric artificial intelligence during the keynote is particularly telling. As anxieties mount globally over AI displacing workers and eroding creative industries, pitching technology as a cooperative partner rather than an existential threat hits the right chord in Europe. It allows Seoul to project soft power rooted not just in economic output, but in ethical leadership.

Turning Big Money into Real Creative Freedom

While the €1 billion price tag makes for impressive headlines, the ultimate test of the Lumière Partnership will be execution. Massive state-backed funds in the cultural sector often risk favoring entrenched industry insiders over the independent creators and fresh talent who drive genuine innovation. If this initiative is to truly bridge South Korea's production power with France's artistic heritage, civil society and industry observers will need to ensure the capital reaches the ground level.

Ultimately, this Parisian summit marks a confident step forward for South Korean diplomacy. By pairing substantial financial commitments with a clear philosophical stance on technology and art, the trip demonstrates that Seoul is no longer playing catch-up on the global stage—it is helping set the agenda.
