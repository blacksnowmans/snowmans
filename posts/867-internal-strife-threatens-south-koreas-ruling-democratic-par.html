Internal Strife Threatens South Korea’s Ruling Democratic Party

October 09, 2026

  • Factions Collide:
    •  Hardline members of the Democratic Party of Korea—South Korea's liberal ruling party—are facing severe internal backlash over calls to discipline veteran party figures, exposing deep ideological fractures.
  • Accusations of Betrayal:
    •  Critics within the grassroots argue that careerists and pro-capitalist insiders are hijacking the party’s reform agenda, comparing internal party purges to totalitarian tactics.
  • Labor and Economic Realities:
    •  The debate coincides with growing outrage over gig-economy labor exploitation, particularly low delivery wages that critics say expose the party's drift from its working-class roots.

The Democratic Party is currently navigating a fierce identity crisis. While progressive lawmakers and grassroots members push for structural reforms under President Lee Jae-myung, internal factionalism threatens to derail the administration's goals. Recent demands by former deputy spokesperson Kang Sung-pil to penalize former Justice Minister Choo Mi-ae have sparked outrage, with critics condemning the move as an authoritarian attempt to silence internal dissent and dismantle the party's democratic traditions.

At the heart of the dispute is a growing fear that the party is being co-opted by moderates and corporate-friendly figures who prioritize internal power struggles over working-class welfare. Commentators and frustrated party members argue that the ruling party risks repeating the mistakes of other global left-wing movements by compromising with elites and ignoring the fundamental economic hardships of ordinary citizens. The outcry over gig-economy realities—such as plummeting delivery fees for platform workers—has amplified these concerns, laying bare a disconnect between elite politics and the labor base.

The Battle for the Party's Soul

Grassroots members and progressive commentators argue that the Democratic Party must purge itself of internal opportunists if it hopes to succeed. Rather than engaging in factional infighting and targeting senior reformists like Choo Mi-ae, the party is being urged to refocus on promised judicial and media reforms. Discontent is particularly high regarding the perceived privatization of party administration, with critics warning that internal power-brokering undermines the accountability required to support President Lee’s agenda effectively.

Ultimately, the debate centers on who the party truly serves. For many long-standing supporters, the Democratic Party must return to the legacies of former progressive leaders like Kim Dae-jung, Roh Moo-hyun, and Moon Jae-in rather than capitulate to corporate interests. As internal pressure mounts, the path forward will depend on whether grassroots voices can reclaim the party from within or if factional divisions will cede control to political operators.
