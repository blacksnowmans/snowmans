Inside the Machine: Whistleblower Details Radical Faction’s Grip on Lee Jae-myung’s Inner Circle

October 19, 2026

Professor Choi Bae-geun has publicly detailed private recruitment overtures by President Lee Jae-myung’s closest confidants, alleging that a clandestine activist faction rooted in the 1980s student movement has captured the ruling administration's internal power structure.

  • The revelation: Choi disclosed that Jeong Jin-sang, President Lee's longtime right-hand man, and key policy architect Lee Han-joo offered him state project contracts and a senior provincial post for his student during Lee’s tenure as Gyeonggi Province governor in exchange for designing their national policy blueprint.
  • The network: The overtures originated from the Gyeonggi Dongbu Coalition, a disciplined, hardline progressive activist network from Seongnam that merged with Lee's local political machine during his 2010 mayoral campaign.
  • The target: Choi was asked to play the modern role of Jeong Do-jeon—the master architect of the Joseon Dynasty—serving as the intellectual figurehead while the faction provided the organizational muscle to seize state power.
  • The broader purge: The account sheds light on fierce infighting within the ruling Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), where radical loyalists have waged aggressive online campaigns targeting traditional party heavyweights, including former President Moon Jae-in and prominent liberal commentator Rhyu Si-min.

Patronage and the Seongnam Machine

According to Choi, Jeong Jin-sang wielded influence exceeding that of Yang Jeong-cheol, the famed gatekeeper of the previous Moon Jae-in administration. Operating outside transparent institutional channels, the Seongnam group offered Choi lucrative provincial projects and senior Grade-3 appointments within the Gyeonggi provincial government. Choi refused, recognizing the transactional patronage for what it was: an effort to buy academic credibility while organizational operatives handled the levers of command.

This dynamic mirrors the alliance forged in Seongnam a decade ago. When Lee Jae-myung won the city's 2010 mayoral race after a dramatic unity pact with the far-left Democratic Labor Party, former activist leaders from the Gyeonggi Dongbu Coalition immediately led his transition team and subsequently secured lucrative municipal sanitation contracts, such as the Nanum Environment concession. What began as a regional pact has now expanded into the center of national governance.

Echos of 2012 and the Cost of Factional Rule

The disclosures help explain the ideological rigidity fracturing the ruling coalition. Observers see unsettling parallels to the infamous 2012 brawl at the Unified Progressive Party convention, where the same Gyeonggi Dongbu faction physically assaulted moderates like Rhyu Si-min after a disputed primary. That factional ruthlessness has now resurfaced as organized intolerance toward mainstream progressive thinkers who refuse to submit unconditionally to the current inner circle.

By relying on insular loyalists rather than formal institutional checks, the administration risks sidelining independent expertise in favor of machine politics. When a political network treats public offices as factional spoils and labels even allied critics as adversaries, the casualty is not merely party unity, but the credibility of democratic governance itself.
