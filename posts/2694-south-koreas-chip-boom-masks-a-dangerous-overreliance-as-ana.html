South Korea’s Chip Boom Masks a Dangerous Overreliance as Analysts Warn of a 2028 Bust

November 24, 2026

While South Korea’s semiconductor exports are projected to break records and account for over half of all national exports next year, Wall Street is sounding the alarm on an impending memory market slump by 2028.

  • Wall Street Downgrades: Goldman Sachs slashed target prices for Samsung Electronics by 30% and SK Hynix by 43%, projecting a 20% drop in overall memory prices by 2028.
  • Market Polarization: High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) and advanced DRAM remain in high demand due to artificial intelligence, but legacy NAND flash and standard DRAM face severe price pressure from expanding Chinese production.
  • Skewed Export Growth: The Korea Institute for Industrial Economics and Trade (KIET), a state-funded think tank, forecasts 2025 national export growth at 14.1%, but excluding semiconductors leaves non-chip export growth at a sluggish 1.5%.
  • Ecosystem Vulnerability: Bloomberg warned that a 10% fall in global chip prices would erase 4.3% of South Korea's total exports, underscoring the economy's extreme exposure to a single tech sector.

A Record-Breaking Export Mirage

On paper, South Korea's macroeconomic indicators look extraordinarily strong. KIET projects total exports to hit a record $1.32 trillion next year, with semiconductors accounting for 50.7% of all outbound shipments—marking the first time a single industry has passed the half-way mark. However, this headline growth conceals a widening structural weakness across the rest of the economy. With automotive exports expected to contract by 1.3% and legacy sectors like petrochemicals and steel continuing to slump, South Korea’s export machine is effectively running on a single cylinder.

The 2028 Downcycle and "Dutch Disease" Risks

Wall Street’s aggressive downgrades reflect growing anxiety over what happens when the current AI infrastructure boom matures. Goldman Sachs warns that aggressive capital investments by chipmakers will tip the market into oversupply by 2027, triggering a 19% drop in standard DRAM prices and a 20% overall decline in memory pricing by 2028. While high-margin HBM chips remain lucrative for tech giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, commodity chips are increasingly vulnerable to aggressive price competition from state-subsidized Chinese manufacturers.

This dynamic presents classic symptoms of "Dutch Disease," where an extreme boom in one high-tech sector draws capital away from traditional manufacturing while masking weak domestic demand. Because modern semiconductor fabrication is heavily automated, massive export revenues generate relatively few domestic jobs compared to traditional industries like auto assembly. With chip price fluctuations now wielding three times the impact on South Korea's national trade account compared to previous cycles, any sudden correction in global memory demand will leave the economy without a secondary line of defense.
