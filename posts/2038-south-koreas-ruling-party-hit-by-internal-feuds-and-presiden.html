South Korea’s Ruling Party Hit by Internal Feuds and President Lee’s Social Media Echo Chamber

November 06, 2026

South Korea's ruling Democratic Party of Korea is facing a mounting leadership crisis as party chief Kim Min-seok retracted unproven cult-link allegations while President Lee Jae-myung dismissed growing concerns over his controversial social media habits.

  • What happened: Kim Min-seok, leader of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, admitted in a closed-door meeting that there was no evidence supporting his earlier claims that the Shincheonji religious group infiltrated the party's August convention, offering only a cursory 23-second public statement afterwards.
  • Internal fallout: Dissident factions within the ruling party condemned Kim's response as a hollow apology and demanded the full disclosure of the internal investigation, raising potential legal liabilities under party election laws.
  • Presidential controversy: On a YouTube program titled "President's Y," President Lee Jae-myung defended his frequent late-night social media activity and retweets of unvetted accounts, rejecting warnings that he is trapped in an online echo chamber.
  • Who is affected: The ruling party’s grassroots base faces deep disillusionment, while public trust in governance is threatened by escalating factional feuds and unvetted presidential communications.

Unsubstantiated Accusations Shatter Party Trust

During the ruling party's leadership election in July, Kim Min-seok aggressively pushed claims of a secret coalition involving Shincheonji, a controversial fringe religious group, accusing rivals of factional collusion. However, an internal task force led by Supreme Council member Park Sun-won uncovered no supporting evidence. Rather than offering a full public accounting, Kim quietly apologized behind closed doors to lawmakers on November 3 before issuing a 23-second statement during a live meeting the following day. Restricting access to the final report to lawmakers has further fueled accusations of a cover-up, alienating rank-and-file members who were falsely accused of factional sabotage.

This dismissive handling of false accusations has triggered fierce backlash from party leadership figures like Supreme Council members Lee Sung-yoon and Han Min-soo. Lee explicitly cited potential violations of Article 52 of the Political Parties Act regarding the dissemination of false information during party elections. Meanwhile, reports surfaced that senior party officials pressured dissenting members to remain silent, turning a failure of political verification into an escalating factional war that undermines the party's democratic integrity.

The Pitfalls of Executive Governance via Social Media

Compounding the party's troubles is President Lee Jae-myung’s continued reliance on social media as a primary governance tool. During a broadcast of "President's Y," Lee defended his late-night posts on X (formerly Twitter) and rejected warnings from political commentators and allies that he has fallen into an algorithmic echo chamber. Lee argued that reading critical comments proves he remains exposed to diverse viewpoints, dismissing suggestions that he should stop retweeting questionable accounts.

This refusal to implement vetting protocols poses real governance risks. Past incidents, such as President Lee retweeting the controversial "Pluto 11" account—which had a history of posting abusive remarks before the tweet was quietly deleted—highlight how unchecked online habits can compromise executive credibility. By treating severe criticism as mere calluses built from past political struggles, President Lee risks tuning out constructive warnings from allies, allowing personal communication habits to amplify political volatility.
