Gambling Allegations Surface Against Justice Minister Nominee Lee Yong-cheol

November 09, 2026

Lee Yong-cheol, President Lee Jae-myung’s nominee for Minister of Justice, is facing allegations of illegal gambling from a key figure in the Daejang-dong land development scandal, sparking a fierce political battle over judicial reform.

  • Yoo Dong-gyu, the former acting president of the Seongnam Development Corporation, claims he played high-stakes poker with the nominee on at least three occasions over a decade ago.
  • The Chosun Ilbo, South Korea’s leading conservative daily, published an exclusive report detailing stakes of about $1,400 (2 million won) at a chicken soup restaurant in Yongin, south of Seoul.
  • Lee Yong-cheol has flatly denied the claims, dismissing them as malicious slander intended to derail his confirmation and the government’s efforts to "normalize" the state prosecution.
  • The ruling Democratic Party of Korea views the timing as a coordinated attempt by conservative media and the People Power Party (the main opposition) to protect the interests of the old establishment.

A Coordinated Strike Against Judicial Reform?

The sudden emergence of these allegations is being interpreted by supporters of the administration as a desperate move to block the "Prosecution Normalization" project. Lee Yong-cheol is a heavyweight in the reformist camp, having served as a secretary under former President Roh Moo-hyun. He is seen as the architect of President Lee Jae-myung’s plan to overhaul the legal system and potentially withdraw charges in cases deemed politically motivated during the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was impeached and is currently imprisoned.

For the conservative establishment, Lee’s appointment represents a direct threat. If Lee takes office, he is expected to use his legal expertise to dismantle the legal foundations of past investigations into current government officials. This explains why the opposition and conservative media are focusing on a decade-old personal anecdote rather than Lee’s professional qualifications or policy vision. By framing the nominee as a "gambler," they aim to inflict enough moral damage to force a withdrawal or at least sap the administration’s political momentum.

The Credibility of the "Poker" Narrative

The specificity of Yoo Dong-gyu’s memory is raising eyebrows. Yoo claims to remember exact loss amounts—recalling how he lost about $300 (400,000 won) and then $600 (800,000 won) before breaking even—from a casual lunch game fifteen years ago. Critics argue that such vivid recall of minor financial details from the distant past suggests a prepared script rather than a natural memory. Yoo is currently a defendant in multiple trials and has a history of changing his testimony, which makes him a controversial source for such a high-stakes accusation.

The Chosun Ilbo’s decision to run the story without secondary verification from restaurant owners or other witnesses has led to accusations of "political engineering" rather than journalism. The report relies entirely on the word of a man whose credibility is a central point of contention in the Korean court system. However, the strategy of "mud-slinging" often works regardless of the eventual truth; the goal is to create a cloud of suspicion that lingers over the nominee throughout the confirmation process, making it harder for the government to claim a moral mandate for reform.
