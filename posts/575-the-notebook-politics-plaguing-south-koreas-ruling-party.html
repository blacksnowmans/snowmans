The "Notebook Politics" Plaguing South Korea's Ruling Party

October 02, 2026

Leaked notes from the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country’s main liberal party, reveal that party leadership is prioritizing internal surveillance and political infighting over national policy, signaling a deep-seated disconnect between the ruling elite and the public.

  • What happened: Photos of DPK policy committee chair Kim Min-seok’s notebook show lists of political commentators, YouTubers, and rivals—including a "Han Dong-hoon file"—rather than legislative agendas.
  • The shift: The party is increasingly operating like a secretive intelligence agency, obsessed with neutralizing internal dissent and managing "external speakers" (influential pro-liberal YouTubers).
  • Who is affected: The South Korean public, as the government’s focus remains locked on power dynamics and election strategy while the economy struggles under inflation.

A Party Obsessed with Information Warfare

The contents of Kim Min-seok’s notebook—including references to specific political YouTubers like "Sisa Tapa" and pollsters like Park Si-young—suggest that the DPK leadership views these influencers as central to internal discipline. It is a striking departure from the party’s duty to focus on legislation. The inclusion of an "Han Dong-hoon file" (referring to the leader of the conservative People Power Party) underscores that the DPK has shifted from policy competition to a permanent state of file-building and character assassination.

This "notebook politics" is matched by a rigid, bunker-mentality within the Blue House regarding personnel appointments. Despite a string of failed nominations—dubbed an "appointment tragedy"—the current administration refuses to withdraw controversial candidates. They treat personnel changes as a zero-sum game: admitting a mistake is seen as a sign of weakness that would embolden political rivals. By framing these basic vetting failures as matters of presidential dignity, the administration is effectively isolating itself from reality.

The Illusion of a "Core" Support Base

There is a growing delusion within the ruling camp that their plummeting approval ratings (which dropped from 77% to 66% among DPK supporters in just a few weeks) are merely temporary noise caused by "troublemakers." Advisors have reportedly argued that the "core" support base remains loyal and that only a few fringe voices are causing the current unrest.

Analysis: The Trap of Purity

The DPK and the current government are trapped in a feedback loop of their own making. By labeling any critic—even those within the liberal camp—as "disloyal" or "un-core," they are shrinking their own political space. It is akin to a teacher removing all the students who failed a test and then celebrating that the class average has finally increased.

This strategy of exclusion and ideological purity is self-destructive. Democracy is not a club for true believers; it is a system that requires the ability to hear and accommodate dissent. If the government continues to view its own voters and legitimate critics as enemies to be managed via "notebooks" rather than citizens to be served, it will find itself increasingly alone in a self-constructed fortress, oblivious to the changing tides of public sentiment.
