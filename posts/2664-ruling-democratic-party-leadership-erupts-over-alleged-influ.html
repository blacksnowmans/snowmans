Ruling Democratic Party Leadership Erupts Over Alleged Influx of Shincheonji Members

November 24, 2026

According to a daily briefing report by Korean political outlet Black Snowman, a top-level meeting of the ruling Democratic Party of Korea descended into shouting and walkouts on November 23 after a supreme council member alleged that tens of thousands of members from the controversial Shincheonji religious sect have infiltrated the party's membership rolls.

  • The allegation: Supreme Council member Park Sun-won claimed internal tracking shows 14,388 confirmed Shincheonji members on the party roster, estimating the total figure could reach as high as 43,000, with roughly 7,000 concentrated in the party's traditional Honam stronghold.
  • The clash: Party leadership fractured mid-meeting when Park compared colleagues resisting internal investigations to "collaborators during Japanese colonial rule," triggering furious objections, verbal sparring, and walkouts by figures aligned with rival faction leader Jung Chung-rae.
  • Party discipline: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok issued a strict verbal warning to Park over the inflammatory historical comparison as the leadership attempts to contain factional fallout ahead of Kim's 100th day in office.
  • The fallout: The conservative main opposition People Power Party seized on the claims to attack the governing party's integrity, while civic groups like People's Solidarity for Participatory Democracy criticized the leadership for descending into factional warfare instead of focusing on state affairs.

Explosive Claims and Broken Trust at the Supreme Council

The confrontation reportedly brought the ruling party’s formal decision-making process to a temporary standstill at a critical juncture for President Lee Jae-myung’s administration. Park Sun-won escalated tensions by offering to disclose the identity of internal informants, while Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee countered by exposing the online handle of a leaked-transcript whistleblower, arguing the accusations were part of a coordinated smear campaign. The dispute shifted rapidly from whether the sect had organized an influx into mutual recriminations, leaving the party unable to address major agenda items such as the proposed impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae.

The friction exposed deep factional fault lines within the party leadership. Park pushed back against Kim Min-seok’s verbal warning, alleging double standards by pointing to past controversial remarks by lawmakers such as Lee Seong-yoon and party spokesperson Han Min-soo. Meanwhile, grassroots party members flooded internal noticeboards with disciplinary petitions targeting various lawmakers, illustrating how an unverified roster dispute quickly metastasized into open factional hostility.

Opposition Pressure and Public Backlash

The revelation provided immediate ammunition to the conservative People Power Party, which argued that if 43,000 sect members entered the ruling party, it amounts to a severe disruption of constitutional governance. Opposition lawmakers openly mocked the Democratic Party, recalling past protests directed at conservative headquarters over alleged sect links and suggesting reciprocal demonstrations outside ruling party offices.

The spectacle of governing party leaders walking out of meetings in tears over an internal dispute drew severe reprimands from outside observers. Leading civic groups questioned whether the ruling bloc possesses the discipline required to steer economic and domestic policy, warning that trading historical insults over unvetted membership lists risks alienating voters grappling with inflation and economic headwinds.
