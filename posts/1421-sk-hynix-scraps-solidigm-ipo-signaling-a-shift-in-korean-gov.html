SK Hynix Scraps Solidigm IPO, Signaling a Shift in Korean Governance

October 24, 2026

In a decisive concession to shareholders and regulatory authorities, South Korean memory chipmaker SK hynix has officially abandoned its plan to list its US-based NAND subsidiary, Solidigm, on the Nasdaq.

  • What happened: SK hynix halted the US initial public offering of Solidigm, the enterprise SSD maker it acquired from Intel, following severe pushback from investors and financial watchdogs.
  • Why it matters: The decision marks a rare retreat from "duplicate listings"—the controversial practice of carving out lucrative subsidiaries, which has long diluted parent-company shareholders and worsened the structural undervaluation known as the "Korea Discount."
  • Regulatory pressure: The Financial Services Commission (FSC) and Financial Supervisory Service (FSS), operating under President Lee Jae-myung’s administration and its mandate to protect minority shareholders, explicitly labeled the move a problematic duplicate listing during parliamentary audits.
  • What it changes: SK hynix shareholders will retain 100% exposure to Solidigm's rebounding enterprise SSD earnings without suffering corporate dilution across the multi-tiered SK Group conglomerate structure.

The Beginning of the End for Carve-Out Listings

For decades, South Korea’s family-run conglomerates (chaebols) treated subsidiary spin-offs and secondary IPOs as a zero-consequence playbook to raise capital without diluting controlling families. The resulting destruction of parent-company value—most notoriously seen during the LG Energy Solution spin-off—infuriated retail investors and drove foreign capital away from Seoul. SK hynix's sudden reversal shows that this playbook is no longer viable.

Facing a punitive market sell-off and regulatory warnings that pushing ahead could invite administrative scrutiny, SK hynix chose capital discipline over an opportunistic carve-out. The shift toward shareholder alignment is already reshaping domestic corporate strategy: physical spin-offs designed for duplicate market debuts are rapidly dropping, replaced by equity spin-offs that distribute new shares directly to existing holders. For SK hynix, keeping Solidigm wholly inside the tent ensures that high-margin NAND and enterprise storage profits feed directly into the parent company’s bottom line.

Memory Fundamentals Beyond the Macro Noise

The announcement arrived in the middle of broader market turbulence, as Wall Street's jitters over tech giants' expanding AI capital expenditures triggered heavy short selling and dragged the benchmark Kospi down near the 5,400 mark. Yet SK hynix shares held their ground, defying both tech sell-offs and peak-cycle anxieties.

Despite hedge funds building near-record short positions on AI tech hardware, the underlying supply-demand reality has not cracked. As Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang recently reiterated, structural shortages of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and high-performance server components are poised to continue through 2028 as the market transitions toward robotics and autonomous systems. By eliminating governance overhangs at home, SK hynix enters the next phase of the AI memory cycle with its balance sheet intact and its shareholder base defended.
