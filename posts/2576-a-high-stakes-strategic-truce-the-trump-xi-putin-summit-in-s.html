A High-Stakes "Strategic Truce": The Trump-Xi-Putin Summit in Shenzhen

November 21, 2026

Despite theatrical displays of camaraderie at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, the trilateral meeting between the leaders of the U.S., China, and Russia failed to deliver concrete solutions for the war in Ukraine or North Korean denuclearization, resulting in what experts call a "strategic truce."

  • The "G3" leaders signed a joint statement reaffirming that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought," though the document lacked specific enforcement mechanisms or timelines for peace.
  • U.S. President Donald Trump praised the talks as a massive success, but domestic critics in the U.S. Senate characterized the event as a hollow victory where the U.S. was outmaneuvered by Chinese and Russian interests.
  • Chinese President Xi Jinping leveraged the stage to assert regional leadership, pledging $20 million (about 28 billion KRW) to support digital and green initiatives across the Asia-Pacific while reinforcing "red lines" regarding Taiwan.
  • South Korean President Lee Jae-myung and his administration are pivoting to a "pragmatic national interest" strategy after the Korean Peninsula was largely sidelined during the high-level discussions.

The Hollow Theater of the G3 Summit

The meeting in Shenzhen was heavy on symbolism but light on substance. Observers noted the carefully choreographed seating arrangement, which placed President Xi at the center of the table with Trump and Putin on either side—a move interpreted by many as China’s attempt to position itself as the new fulcrum of global order. While the White House promoted footage of friendly interactions, including Xi escorting Trump on an escalator, the reality behind the scenes was far more guarded. Security was so tight that U.S. officials reportedly confiscated the mobile phones of journalists aboard Air Force One upon departure to prevent potential espionage.

The joint statement on nuclear non-proliferation served as a convenient "strategic pause" for all three powers, but it did little to address the urgent realities of the Ukraine conflict. In fact, the exclusion of European leaders from the core discussions sparked a backlash, leading over 40 European heads of state to convene an emergency meeting in Berlin. They expressed deep concern that a "Yalta 2.0" was being forged, where the fate of Ukraine and European security is decided by a handful of superpowers without the consent of the parties involved.

South Korea’s Strategic Recalibration

For South Korea, the Shenzhen summit was a sobering reminder of the shifting diplomatic landscape. Despite rumors of a potential surprise meeting between Trump and North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, the North Korean issue was noticeably absent from the G3's primary agenda. Kim Jong-un has maintained a calculated silence, ignoring Trump’s recent overtures in favor of strengthening ties with Beijing and Moscow. This neglect has forced the South Korean government to assert its own agency in regional security.

President Lee Jae-myung, representing the ruling Democratic Party of Korea, responded by convening a senior secretary meeting to demand thorough preparations for the fallout of the G3 summit. President Lee emphasized that South Korea must not lose its "initiative" on the peninsula's affairs. Following this, Unification Minister Chung Dong-young visited the border village of Panmunjom to coordinate with U.S. and UN Command officials. Meanwhile, the People Power Party (PPP), South Korea’s main conservative opposition, criticized the administration’s response, arguing that the country is being treated as an "outsider" in the face of secret diplomacy between the great powers.

As China expands its influence through financial pledges to its neighbors and the U.S. faces internal criticism over "empty-handed" diplomacy, the Lee Jae-myung administration faces the difficult task of navigating a world where traditional alliances are being tested by transactional "G3" politics.
