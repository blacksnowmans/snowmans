Data Distortion and Internal Strife: The Crisis Within South Korea's Ruling Party

October 27, 2026

Sensationalist reports claiming a massive exodus of supporters from President Lee Jae-myung are being decried as a "political operation" designed to fracture the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK) from within.

  • Distorted Polling Data: A recent forum held by the Hankyoreh, a major liberal daily, and polling firm STI, sparked headlines claiming a 55% desertion rate among "New Lee Jae-myung" supporters, though critics argue the figures are being weaponized to create a narrative of a failing administration.
  • Internal Sabotage: Factions within the DPK are reportedly attacking party pillars like the Roh Moo-hyun Foundation and commentator Rhyu Si-min, reviving long-debunked scandals to sow discord.
  • Economic Priorities: The friction comes as the public demands the Lee Jae-myung administration focus on pressing "bread and butter" issues, specifically real estate reform and inflation.
  • Razor-Thin Margins: With the last presidential election decided by just 0.7 percentage points, observers warn that even a 3.5% loss in core support could be fatal for the ruling party’s future.

The "SillaJen" Ghost and Internal Division

The core of the current controversy involves the revival of the "SillaJen" scandal—a past financial investigation involving a biotech firm that the prosecution, under Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol before his impeachment, failed to link to liberal figures. Despite the case being settled years ago, certain online communities and "political entrepreneurs" are recycling these allegations to attack Rhyu Si-min, a prominent liberal intellectual and key supporter of the President.

This internal bickering is viewed by many as a distraction from the President’s reform agenda. While the administration attempts to stabilize housing prices and manage the cost of living, these "fake supporters" are accused of prioritizing a purge of party veterans over the welfare of the people. The "Emergency Committee Gallery," an anonymous online community, has become a hub for these attacks, mimicking the aggressive tactics once used by far-right groups to harass DPK members.

The 0.7% Lesson and the Risk of Apathy

Why Minor Shifts Matter

Some political analysts argue that a 3.5% drop in core support is negligible. However, in the context of South Korean politics, where the gap between the DPK and the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) is historically narrow, such apathy is dangerous. The memory of the 0.7% victory remains a sobering reminder that the ruling party cannot afford to alienate any segment of its base through internal "witch hunts."

The Role of Political Entrepreneurs

There is growing criticism of media figures and "political entrepreneurs" who profit from this division. These individuals are accused of "vampirizing" the party’s energy—clinging to the administration when it is popular, but attacking its core assets like the Roh Moo-hyun Foundation (which honors the late liberal president) as soon as they see a chance to consolidate their own influence. For the Lee administration to succeed, it must distance itself from these divisive elements and return its focus to the economic reforms that voters actually care about.

"The attempt to isolate President Lee Jae-myung by destroying the party’s intellectual and moral foundations is not a critique; it is an act of political subversion that only benefits the opposition."

Ultimately, the "55% exit" narrative serves as a warning. It is less a reflection of a total loss of faith in President Lee and more a sign of voter exhaustion with internal power struggles. If the DPK cannot protect its symbolic leaders and maintain a unified front, it risks squandering the mandate it won in a historically tight race.
