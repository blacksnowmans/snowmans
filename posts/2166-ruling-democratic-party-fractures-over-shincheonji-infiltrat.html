Ruling Democratic Party Fractures Over Shincheonji Infiltration Probe

November 10, 2026

The ruling Democratic Party of Korea is facing a severe internal crisis as senior members trade barbs over a controversial investigation into organized infiltration by the Shincheonji religious group.

  • Internal rift deepens: Supreme Council member Lee Sung-yoon slammed party leader Kim Min-seok, comparing his handling of the probe to the military coup of the late dictator Chun Doo-hwan.
  • Disputed evidence: While party heavyweights like Song Young-gil claim the data proves a mass entry of cult members into the party, leadership remains cautious, downplaying the significance of the findings.
  • External pressure: The main opposition People Power Party (PPP) is seizing the chaos to demand a special counsel investigation into what they call a "secret alliance" between the ruling party and the controversial sect.
  • Leadership crisis: Despite multiple apologies from Chairman Kim, critics within the party are calling for emergency disciplinary measures and a full-scale audit of the party membership roster.

A Crisis of Transparency and Trust

The Democratic Party (DP), currently the ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung, is struggling to contain a scandal involving Shincheonji—a fringe religious group often labeled a cult. The controversy centers on whether the group systematically instructed its members to join the DP to influence internal elections or policy. The party's "Membership Cleanup Task Force" recently completed its investigation, but the leadership's decision to allow only "private viewing" of the report has backfired, fueling suspicions of a cover-up.

By attempting to manage the fallout quietly, Chairman Kim Min-seok has inadvertently triggered a high-stakes power struggle. Lee Sung-yoon, a member of the party’s top decision-making body, took the extraordinary step of invoking the legacy of Chun Doo-hwan—the military strongman who seized power in 1979—to criticize Kim. Lee’s comparison suggests that the leadership is treating the alleged infiltration as a "successful political maneuver" that should be ignored rather than punished, a stance he finds morally indefensible.

Political Vulnerability and the Opposition’s Gambit

The timing is disastrous for the DP. As the ruling party, it is expected to lead the legislative agenda for the Lee Jae-myung administration, yet it is currently paralyzed by internal "friendly fire." Lawmaker Song Young-gil has confirmed that his review of the membership data reveals clear signs of organized infiltration. He is now demanding the immediate expulsion of suspected members and a total overhaul of the party’s charter to prevent religious interference in secular politics.

The main conservative opposition, the People Power Party (PPP), has wasted no time in capitalizing on the DP's disarray. Representative Jeong Jeom-sig of the PPP is framing the issue as a "secret alliance" between the DP and Shincheonji, arguing that only a Special Counsel—an independent prosecutor—can uncover the truth. For the DP, the stakes are high: failing to purge these elements transparently could lead to a loss of public trust and a prolonged legal investigation that would drain the government's political capital.

"If the Democratic Party refuses a special counsel, they are essentially admitting to a collusion with Shincheonji," said Jeong Jeom-sig, South Korea’s main opposition lawmaker.

Strategic Implications

The DP’s current strategy of "controlled disclosure" appears to have failed. To regain control of the narrative, the leadership may be forced to choose between a painful, public purge of its membership or a defensive battle against an opposition-led special probe. In the high-pressure environment of the Lee Jae-myung presidency, any sign of moral compromise regarding secretive religious groups is a vulnerability the opposition will continue to exploit.
