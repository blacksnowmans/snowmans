South Korea's Former Prime Minister Sentenced to 23 Years for "Self-Coup"

September 03, 2026

A South Korean court has sentenced former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo to 23 years in prison, delivering a landmark ruling that classifies the recent declaration of martial law as an act of insurrection against the state.

  • The Sentence: Han received a 23-year prison term, exceeding the prosecution’s request of 15 years, and was taken into custody immediately.
  • The Charge: The court officially designated the state of emergency as a "self-coup"—an attempt by an elected leader to bypass the constitution and subvert democratic order to maintain power.
  • Judicial Rejection: Judges dismissed claims of memory loss and appeals for leniency based on Han’s age (79) and public service record, prioritizing the preservation of constitutional values.
  • Broader Context: The ruling acknowledges that the short-lived insurrection was thwarted not by the regime's conscience, but by the physical resistance of citizens and the refusal of rank-and-file personnel to follow illegal orders.

A Landmark Ruling on Constitutional Defiance

The trial was defined by a moment of heavy silence: presiding Judge Lee Jin-kwan paused for 27 seconds before delivering the verdict. This was not a mere display of emotion, but a stark recognition of the severity of the crime. By labeling the martial law declaration an act of insurrection, the court established that a public official’s attempt to weaponize state systems for personal political survival is an unforgivable betrayal of the constitution.

Han’s defense team had argued that his age and health should mitigate the sentence, but the court was unmoved. The ruling serves as a stern reminder that democratic principles cannot be traded for years of public service. Furthermore, the court rejected Han’s claims that he could not remember authorizing the martial law documents, noting that a man capable of serving as Prime Minister cannot credibly plead ignorance of such a monumental administrative act.

Accountability Beyond Politics

The 23-year sentence is a clear signal that the South Korean judiciary is moving to reinforce the rule of law after a period of extreme institutional instability. By exceeding the prosecution’s sentencing request, the court highlighted the gravity of the attempted cover-up, including the destruction of government documents and the dissemination of false information intended to frame the emergency as a legal act.

This verdict is not just about one man’s prison time; it is a declaration that the "minimum cost" of restoring faith in the republic is the strict enforcement of consequences for those who treat the constitution as a tool for their own power. As the country moves forward, this case stands as a cautionary tale: authority in a democracy is not absolute, and it is only as legitimate as its adherence to the rule of law.
