South Korea’s Legacy Media Suffers from “YouTube Schizophrenia”

October 27, 2026

South Korea’s traditional newspapers are increasingly compromising their journalistic integrity by adopting sensationalist, clickbait strategies on YouTube that directly contradict their own serious print editorials.

  • Kyunghyang Shinmun, a major center-left daily, has come under fire for publishing critical print editorials about the government while simultaneously releasing YouTube videos that defend the administration to chase views.
  • Traditional newsrooms are imitating the aggressive, mocking style of partisan YouTubers, using inflammatory thumbnails and "bait" tactics to game the platform's algorithm and secure ad revenue.
  • The media’s reliance on simple, personality-driven frames—specifically targeting or defending influential liberal broadcaster Kim Eo-jun—is replacing deep policy analysis with partisan entertainment.
  • This "downward equalization" is eroding public trust in journalism at a time when the public needs rigorous fact-checking to navigate a highly polarized political landscape.

The Identity Crisis of the Digital Newsroom

The disconnect within organizations like Kyunghyang Shinmun highlights a growing rot in the industry. While the newspaper’s editorial board recently expressed concern over President Lee Jae-myung’s unfiltered social media communication style, its YouTube arm, Kyunghyang TV, produced content vigorously defending the President. This suggests a fractured corporate identity where the pursuit of the "attention economy" has become entirely decoupled from a brand’s core journalistic values. When a legacy outlet uses thumbnails that mock political opponents or use slang common among extremist streamers, it loses the moral authority required to criticize those very same actors.

This is not an isolated incident. Other major outlets like Ohmynews and the Hankook Ilbo (a major centrist daily) are struggling with similar internal conflicts. By prioritizing "clicks" over context, these organizations are essentially admitting that their traditional standards are secondary to YouTube's engagement metrics. The result is a media ecosystem where even the most respected institutions are descending into the same gutter as unverified political influencers, trading long-term credibility for short-term traffic.

The Obsession with Media "Gatekeepers"

Legacy media’s fixation on figures like Kim Eo-jun—the founder of the Ddanzi Ilbo and host of the massive YouTube channel Humility is Hard News Factory—reveals their inability to adapt. Kim has mastered the art of building a direct, loyal audience across podcasts and digital platforms. Instead of learning from his media savvy, legacy outlets often resort to "lazy framing," attributing complex public movements or the popularity of the Democratic Party of Korea to Kim’s "behind-the-scenes" influence. This ignores the agency of the public and reveals a media class that is stuck in a reactionary loop.

Furthermore, the standard of criticism remains wildly inconsistent. While conservative commentators and legacy outlets are quick to attack liberal "speakers" like Kim or Rhyu Si-min, they often give a pass to figures like Han Dong-hoon, the leader of the People Power Party (South Korea’s main conservative opposition). This partisan bickering obscures the real issues facing the country. During the tenure of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment, the public saw how sensationalist framing could distract from national crises. If legacy media continues to prioritize mockery over the "slow work" of investigative journalism, they will find themselves obsolete in an era that desperately requires truth over tropes.
