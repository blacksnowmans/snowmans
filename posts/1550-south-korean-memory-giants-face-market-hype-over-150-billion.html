South Korean Memory Giants Face Market Hype Over $150 Billion Shareholder Return Rumors

October 27, 2026

Rumors of a record-breaking shareholder return program totaling nearly 200 trillion won ($150 billion) from Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are sweeping Yeouido, Seoul's main financial district, ahead of upcoming quarterly earnings announcements.

  • What happened: While official broker forecasts expect combined shareholder returns of around 120 trillion won ($90 billion)—comprising 80 trillion won from Samsung Electronics and 40 trillion won from SK Hynix—aggressive market speculation suggests total returns could top 200 trillion won.
  • What it changes: Investors are demanding structural share cancellations over simple cash dividends, supported by recent Commercial Act amendments that mandate companies retire acquired treasury shares within one year.
  • Who is affected: Over 11 million domestic retail shareholders across South Korea's top two semiconductor manufacturers, who have endured historic valuation discounts despite booming AI memory demand and severe equipment bottlenecks.

Why Treasury Stock Retirement Matters More Than Cash Dividends

South Korea’s top chipmakers remain trapped in a valuation paradox. Despite strong structural demand—highlighted by an estimated global shortage of more than 300,000 DRAM wafers per year and equipment supply bottlenecks restricting capacity expansions—Samsung and SK Hynix trade at historically depressed price-to-book (PBR) and price-to-earnings (PER) multiples. Market doubts over the duration of the current memory cycle have kept foreign investors cautious, sparking frustration among retail investors who feel sidelined while companies report high profits and pay out internal bonuses.

Cash dividends offer brief relief, but they routinely fail to protect stock prices after the ex-dividend date. Shareholders are instead pressing for aggressive buybacks accompanied by permanent stock cancellations. Under the capital market reforms championed by President Lee Jae-myung's administration, newly passed Commercial Act revisions require listed firms to retire acquired treasury shares within 12 months. This structural shift blocks companies from using treasury stock as a defensive shield for family ownership control, compelling them to boost per-share metrics like earnings per share (EPS) through actual share count reductions.

The Strategic Balancing Act: Global AI Capex vs. Domestic Valuation Pressures

Korean memory suppliers face a tricky capital allocation strategy compared to their global counterparts. Major U.S. tech giants are paring back buybacks to fund massive capital expenditures, with tech industry AI investments projected to reach $562 billion (813 trillion won) this year. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix must strike a delicate balance between funding high-bandwidth memory (HBM) infrastructure and deploying cash to satisfy domestic value-up expectations.

For investors holding underpriced shares, dumping stock ahead of earnings appears premature. With equipment shortages capping output growth (Q), company management must clearly demonstrate how premium pricing strategies (P) will defend operating margins. If management pairing solid margin guidance with transparent, structured share cancellation roadmaps materializes, it could serve as the long-awaited catalyst to break the valuation discount on South Korea's tech flagships.
