Korean Designers Rethink the Adult Daily Bag with 19th-Century Engineering and Vegan Leather

October 27, 2026

South Korean design houses Arak Nine and EIV Factory have unveiled a new lineup of executive briefcases and backpacks aimed at solving common daily usability hassles through historic engineering and modern vegan materials.

  • What happened: Design founders Kim Eun-woo of Arak Nine and Kwak Chang-hoon of EIV Factory completed a collaborative six-month development project to create daily bags built specifically for working adults.
  • What it changes: The collection introduces a steel-frame opening mechanism that keeps bag mouths wide open when unzipped, alongside custom "Anarillezum" vegan leather that offers structured flexibility without excessive weight.
  • Who is affected: Working professionals seeking functional, lightweight carrying options—ranging from ultra-thin hand-carried briefcases to structured backpacks capable of fitting 17-inch laptops without ruining formal wear silhouettes.

Engineering Usability into Executive Style

The centerpiece of the collaboration is Arak Nine’s reinterpretation of the classic 19th-century doctor’s Gladstone bag. CEO Kim Eun-woo integrated an internal steel frame into modern briefcase silhouettes, enabling the bag's mouth to stay upright and open on its own. This solves the persistent frustration of bags collapsing inward while users try to load or retrieve items. To cater to modern commuting demands, Arak Nine developed multiple variations, including ultra-thin handheld briefcases, trapezoid shapes for women, and a nylon-and-leather hybrid safari model designed to carry up to 17-inch laptops while remaining remarkably lightweight.

Notably, Kim made the deliberate design choice to omit shoulder straps on several briefcase models. Arguing that shoulder straps on thin briefcases ruin the clean lines of formal suits and project a cluttered appearance, the brand focused heavily on clean, handheld aesthetics for urban professionals.

Redefining the Backpack for the Office

Meanwhile, EIV Factory CEO Kwak Chang-hoon tackled the issue of backpacks suited for mature office environments. Moving away from rigid, student-like school bags, Kwak utilized "Anarillezum"—a specialized, high-durability vegan leather designed to mimic the textured aging of traditional leather while holding natural, soft curves. The interior features dedicated layout pockets for smartphones, power banks, and sunglasses, while the shoulder strap width was engineered to distribute weight without appearing bulky over business attire.

The duo completed the entire line in just six months—a sharp departure from the typical two-to-three-year development cycle standard in traditional leather goods. By prioritizing practical utility, lower weight, and purposeful features over heavy luxury leather traditions, the project highlights a growing shift in Korean design toward user-first functional aesthetic accessories.
