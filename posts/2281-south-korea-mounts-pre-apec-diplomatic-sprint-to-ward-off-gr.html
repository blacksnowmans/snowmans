South Korea Mounts Pre-APEC Diplomatic Sprint to Ward Off Great-Power Sidelining

November 13, 2026

South Korean Foreign Minister Cho Hyun has made an urgent trip to Beijing to lay the groundwork for a potential summit between President Lee Jae-myung and Chinese President Xi Jinping, as Seoul maneuvers to prevent its security and economic interests from being overshadowed by backchannel diplomacy among Washington, Beijing, and Moscow.

  • Emergency bilateral talks: Cho sits down with Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi on November 13–14 to negotiate deliverables for a potential Lee-Xi meeting and ease lingering bilateral tensions.
  • High-stakes tour: President Lee Jae-myung begins a four-day diplomatic tour on November 16 covering the ASEAN summit in Manila and the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) leaders' meeting in Shenzhen, focusing on supply chain diversification and economic security.
  • Avoiding "Korea Passing": Seoul is actively coordinating with Beijing and Washington to ensure the Korean Peninsula—including stalled denuclearization talks—is not leveraged by major powers without South Korea at the negotiating table.
  • Managing great-power frictions: The summit unfolds alongside sensitive U.S.-China negotiations on trade and artificial intelligence, as well as heightened tensions over Taiwan following Xi's declaration that reunification remains a historic imperative.

Walking the Economic Tightrope in Shenzhen

For President Lee Jae-myung's administration, the Shenzhen APEC gathering represents a crucial test of its self-styled "pragmatic diplomacy." Bilateral relations with China have remained chilled since the deployment of the U.S. THAAD missile defense system years ago, hampering South Korean exporters. By sending Cho ahead of the summit, Seoul aims to reset economic cooperation and open doors for its high-tech manufacturers, even as Washington continues to tighten tech-export restrictions against Beijing.

Xi Jinping's recent rhetoric underscores the delicate landscape Lee must navigate. While Xi recently reiterated that unifying Taiwan with the mainland is a non-negotiable historical mission, he has concurrently adopted a conciliatory posture toward dialogue to stabilize trade. Seoul must capitalize on this tactical opening for economic relief without compromising its baseline security commitments to regional stability.

Guarding Seoul's Voice on the North Korean Issue

Beyond economic concerns, Seoul's diplomatic core is on alert over backchannel negotiations between major powers. With potential engagements involving Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin, and Xi Jinping looming over the multilateral calendar, South Korean officials fear being sidelined on Peninsula security. South Korea's foreign ministry confirmed it is monitoring trilateral diplomatic movements closely to ensure that any discussions on Korean peace treaties or denuclearization do not bypass Seoul entirely.

While the presidential office has downplayed the likelihood of an immediate breakthrough between Washington and Pyongyang on the sidelines of APEC, Lee is seeking international backing to restart long-dormant inter-Korean dialogue. Cho's mission in Beijing is effectively an attempt to secure China's constructive leverage over North Korea before the major powers set their own terms for Northeast Asian security.
