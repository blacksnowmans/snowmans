Ruling Party Rocked by Internal Clash Over Shincheonji Sect Investigation

October 27, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea is embroiled in escalating internal strife as leaders face fierce pushback over their decision to delay and conceal the findings of an internal investigation into controversial ties with the Shincheonji religious group.

  • What happened: The leadership of the Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), South Korea's ruling party, decided to report its internal probe into alleged infiltration by Shincheonji—a controversial fringe religious group—during a closed-door meeting in November, prompting lawmaker Choi Min-hee to publicly deny that any consensus was reached.
  • Internal explosion: DPK Supreme Council member Han Min-soo directly confronted party leadership, exposing that officials attempted to resolve months of controversy with an undocumented 10-minute briefing and demanding political accountability if false allegations were used to gain party power.
  • Key factions involved: Internal critics, including a faction aligned with lawmaker Jung Chung-rae, demand the immediate disclosure of the probe's findings regarding an alleged "Shincheonji three-party alliance," warning that secret meetings breach public trust under President Lee Jae-myung's administration.
  • What it changes: The confrontation puts Senior Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok’s leadership under severe strain and threatens to stall key legislative initiatives amid deepening factional warfare.

A Breakdown in Secrecy and Party Governance

The decision to handle the investigation into Shincheonji behind closed doors has backfired on the DPK leadership. Having condemned the secretive governance of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's past administration, the DPK now faces harsh criticism from its own ranks for concealing critical information. Critics within the party argue that hiding behind a private November briefing suggests a cover-up, fueling internal claims that "the one hiding it is the culprit."

The clash also exposes a severe breakdown in party communication. Rep. Choi Min-hee’s public refutation of leadership consensus and Han Min-soo’s outrage over a superficial 10-minute explanation highlight a party operating via informal channels rather than transparent institutional procedures. Instead of quelling rumors, the leadership's attempt to suppress open debate has escalated a probe into a full-blown crisis of confidence.

Factional Risk for the Ruling Party

This internal rift poses a significant risk for President Lee Jae-myung's government, which requires a cohesive ruling party to execute its legislative agenda. As the main opposition People Power Party (PPP) attacks the DPK over double standards, the ruling party is squandering political capital on internal squabbling. Senior Supreme Council member Kim Min-seok faces mounting pressure to resolve the dispute by fully disclosing the investigation's findings rather than pushing the issue to November, as lingering doubts over religious influence threaten to further fracture party unity.
