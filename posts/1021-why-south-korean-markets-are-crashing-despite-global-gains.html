Why South Korean Markets Are Crashing Despite Global Gains

October 12, 2026

South Korean stocks are currently caught in a severe downdraft, with the KOSPI index struggling to find a floor even as U.S. markets show signs of resilience. The primary culprit is a toxic combination of surging U.S. Treasury yields, massive foreign sell-offs, and structural domestic weaknesses.

  • Capital Flight: Foreign investors have dumped approximately $5 billion (roughly 6.9 trillion KRW) worth of Korean stocks over the past three sessions.
  • Structural Volatility: A $14 billion (approx. 19 trillion KRW) rebalancing of semiconductor ETFs—triggered by last Thursday's options expiration—is continuing to exert downward pressure on the market.
  • Macro headwinds: U.S. 10-year Treasury yields have spiked to 5.2%, draining liquidity from emerging markets like Korea.
  • Geopolitical Risks: Rising energy price uncertainty, fueled by regional instability, is hitting South Korea’s energy-dependent economy particularly hard.

The "Korea Discount" and Structural Failures

The current market rout is not merely a reaction to external interest rate hikes; it is a manifestation of the long-standing "Korea Discount." Despite many firms possessing strong fundamentals and high dividend capacity, they are trading below book value. This is a direct consequence of opaque corporate governance and poor shareholder return policies that have been left unaddressed for years.

Critics point to the legacy of the previous administration, noting that the political and economic instability accumulated during the tenure of Yoon Suk-yeol—who is currently imprisoned following his impeachment—has left the capital market fragile. Under the current government led by President Lee Jae-myung, the market is struggling to overcome these deep-seated structural inefficiencies while simultaneously facing global liquidity tightening.

What to Watch: The Path to a Bottom

For investors, the immediate focus must shift toward risk management rather than "bottom fishing." Several key indicators will determine whether the market can stabilize:

  • U.S. CPI Data: The September Consumer Price Index release on the 14th is the critical pivot point for future interest rate expectations.
  • Semiconductor Sentiment: Investors are closely monitoring the earnings reports of TSMC and ASML to gauge the sustainability of the AI semiconductor boom.
  • Institutional Support: Samsung Electronics, a bellwether for the local market, has historically used share buybacks to defend its price. However, recent data suggests this support is waning, signaling that investors should exercise extreme caution before entering the market.

Currently, risk-averse capital is rotating into "asset-rich" stocks—such as shipping companies—where intrinsic land or property values provide a perceived safety net. However, in a liquidity-starved environment, chasing these trends is dangerous. The consensus for individual investors remains clear: preserve cash, avoid over-leveraging on speculative stocks, and wait for a definitive technical signal that the downward trend has exhausted itself.
