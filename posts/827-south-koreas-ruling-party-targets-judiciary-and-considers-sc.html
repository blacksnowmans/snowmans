South Korea's Ruling Party Targets Judiciary and Considers Scrapping Military Exemptions for Athletes

October 08, 2026

  • Showdown with the Courts: The ruling Democratic Party ramped up pressure on Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae to resign after he refused to take a witness oath at a parliamentary audit.
  • Parliamentary Chaos: The first day of national audits saw sharp shouting matches and near-collapse as the Democratic Party and the conservative opposition People Power Party clashed over past policies and witness behavior.
  • Overhauling Exemptions: Democratic Party Chairman Kim Min-seok proposed an overhaul of military service exemptions for athletes and artists, arguing the current system is outdated.
  • Focus on Livelihoods: Amid political bickering, the ruling party advanced legislation on cryptocurrency taxation, whistleblower protections, and rural basic income.

Judicial Standoff and National Audit Clashes

South Korea's political arena was consumed by a high-stakes standoff between the legislative majority and the judiciary. The liberal Democratic Party, currently the ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung, launched a severe offensive against Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. Party leadership demanded his resignation, framing his refusal to take a witness oath during the parliamentary audit as an unacceptable blow to democratic accountability and equality before the law. The conservative People Power Party, the main opposition, fired back, condemning the move as an assault on the separation of powers.

That friction spilled directly into the national audits, which kicked off to a chaotic start. Rather than serving purely as a venue for policy scrutiny, day one devolved into hostile shouting matches, picket-waving, and partisan finger-pointing over solar energy projects and historical defector controversies. While opposition lawmakers accused the ruling bloc of undermining state institutions, the Democratic Party attempted to steer attention toward practical governance, pushing forward regional development projects and pushing for long-delayed cryptocurrency taxation rules.

Tackling Military Perks and Shifting Party Machinery

In a notable policy pivot, Democratic Party Chairman Kim Min-seok called for a total overhaul of the country's military service exemption system. The current rules, which grant alternative service or exemptions to medal-winning athletes at events like the Asian Games, face mounting public skepticism regarding fairness and national security in a rapidly changing environment. While the proposal is bound to draw pushback from the sports and arts sectors, the ruling party appears willing to challenge long-standing privileges to align with public sentiment on mandatory conscription.

Parallel to these high-profile debates, the Democratic Party is quietly tightening its internal organization. The party initiated the formation of candidate nomination panels and disciplinary review committees in preparation for upcoming local elections. By balancing aggressive political messaging against the judiciary with grassroots voter outreach—such as backing rural basic income legislation and strengthening whistleblower protections—the ruling bloc is attempting to prove its administrative competence while laying the groundwork for future electoral battles.
