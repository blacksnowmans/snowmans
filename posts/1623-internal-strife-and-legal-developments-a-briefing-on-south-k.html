Internal Strife and Legal Developments: A Briefing on South Korean Politics

October 28, 2026

The Democratic Party of Korea (DPK), the country's ruling party under President Lee Jae-myung, is facing intense internal pressure as senior lawmakers demand a leadership overhaul and the potential formation of an emergency committee.

  • Leadership Crisis: Growing dissatisfaction with current party leadership has led to public calls for a "special emergency committee" to address sliding approval ratings.
  • Legal Resolution: A defamation case against prominent author and liberal commentator Rhyu Si-min has been dropped after President Lee Jae-myung formally requested that charges not be pursued.
  • Judicial Spotlight: Kim Keon-hee (the wife of the imprisoned former president, Yoon Seok-youl) remains in detention, with a court-ordered extension of her custody signaling a firm stance on her role in past influence-peddling scandals.

DPK Leadership Under Fire

The DPK is currently grappling with a disconnect between its leadership and veteran lawmakers. During a recent party meeting, senior members expressed blunt criticism, warning that the party risks total collapse if it continues to project public infighting. The consensus among these critics is that the current leadership has failed to provide a cohesive vision, contributing to the party's declining support.

The push for an emergency committee is being framed not as a simple power grab, but as a "last-ditch" effort to stabilize the party. However, skeptics within the party warn that simply changing the leadership structure could trigger further factional conflicts. For the DPK to survive and effectively support the Lee Jae-myung administration, these lawmakers argue that the party must pivot back to "livelihood politics"—focusing on tangible economic issues rather than internal power struggles.

Legal Clarifications and Judicial Scrutiny

The closure of the defamation case against Rhyu Si-min provides a moment of relief for the liberal bloc. Under South Korean law, defamation is a "crime punishable only upon complaint" (the victim must desire prosecution). By declining to press charges, the President has effectively neutralized a legal battle that had become a rallying point for political opponents, particularly regarding the "SillaJen" scandal, which Rhyu has vowed to address through fact-checking.

Meanwhile, the judicial system continues to hold Kim Keon-hee accountable. Her continued detention, following an earlier conviction related to influence-peddling, has prevented her release. The court’s decision to extend her custody—despite some public criticism that the judiciary is overly cautious—suggests that the legal system is maintaining a hard line on the corruption charges linked to the previous administration. For the current government, these legal developments are crucial in distancing the current political landscape from the controversies of the past.
