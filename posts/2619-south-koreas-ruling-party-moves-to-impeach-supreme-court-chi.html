South Korea's Ruling Party Moves to Impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice

November 22, 2026

South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea has officially adopted the impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae as its party line, setting up a high-stakes constitutional showdown with the opposition.

  • What happened: The Democratic Party of Korea (DP) agreed on a timeline to introduce an impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho by early December, arguing that the judiciary must not grant "impunity for insurrection."
  • The opposition reaction: The main conservative opposition People Power Party (PPP) launched street protests, branding the move a "100% fake" retaliatory attack designed to protect President Lee Jae-myung from his legal trials.
  • Rhetoric escalates: Opposition lawmakers denounced the ruling party as "impeachment cats" guilty of constitutional sabotage, threatening counter-moves including impeachment calls targeted at President Lee himself.
  • What it changes: The clash pushes inter-branch tension between the legislature and the judiciary to unprecedented levels, paralyzing normal parliamentary dialogue.

An Escalating Battle over Judicial Independence

By formalizing Cho’s impeachment as an official party stance and targeting Dec. 3 for the motion's introduction, the Democratic Party has moved past political rhetoric into a concrete legislative campaign. The ruling party frames the move as a vital constitutional check to ensure judicial accountability regarding insurrection-related matters. However, targeting the head of the nation's highest court marks an aggressive exercise of legislative power that places severe strain on the doctrine of separation of powers.

High-Stakes Politics with No Room for Compromise

Lacking the parliamentary seats to block the motion on its own, the People Power Party has turned to street rallies and aggressive messaging to counter the ruling bloc. By tying the impeachment push directly to President Lee Jae-myung's ongoing trials, the opposition aims to frame the DP's offensive as an abuse of power intended to neutralize judicial oversight. With both parties exchanging incendiary rhetoric, South Korea faces an intensifying struggle over the boundaries of legislative authority and judicial independence.
