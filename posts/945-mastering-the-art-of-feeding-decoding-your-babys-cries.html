Mastering the Art of Feeding: Decoding Your Baby’s Cries

October 10, 2026

When a baby cries uncontrollably, most new parents panic, cycling through diaper changes and feeding attempts in a frantic search for relief. According to childcare expert Kim Young-mi, the secret to calming a distressed infant isn't in checking a checklist, but in establishing a "rapport" through intentional, observant feeding techniques.

  • Focus on the latch: Audible gulping or clicking sounds during feeding indicate the baby is swallowing excessive air, leading to gas and discomfort.
  • Abandon the "one-shot" myth: Forcing a baby to finish a full bottle without breaks often causes painful bloating.
  • Active Observation: Physical cues like clenched fists or curled toes are clear signals of distress that require an immediate pause in feeding.
  • Medical Context: With South Korea facing a critical shortage of pediatric specialists, parents must become the first line of defense in managing infant digestive health.

The Mechanics of Feeding and the "Burp" Strategy

Feeding is not just about nutrition; it is a physical interaction that requires the parent to "read" the baby's tongue movements. If the baby cannot properly seal their lips against the bottle nipple, air enters the stomach, causing the infant to become fussy or refuse food entirely. Kim emphasizes that parents should feel the rhythm of the baby's jaw and tongue, keeping the bottle firmly sealed without applying excessive pressure.

The "one-shot" approach—expecting a baby to drink 120mL (about 4 ounces) in one go—is a common mistake. Kim suggests taking a break after 6 to 8 minutes of feeding to burp the baby. When burping, avoid keeping your fingers together; instead, tap with an open hand to create a percussive sound that effectively helps the baby release trapped air and regulate their heartbeat.

Building Rapport in a Challenging Healthcare Environment

Parenting in South Korea has become more complex due to the strain on the national medical system. A recent industry report revealed that over 50% of pediatric clinics doubt they can maintain current service levels within the next four years. Under the administration of President Lee Jae-myung, while the government pushes to expand public medical access, the reality remains that long wait times at hospitals make at-home care knowledge essential.

When a baby begins to wail, the best response is often to do nothing but offer physical closeness. Standing up, holding the baby upright, and making eye contact provides the emotional security they crave. By treating the baby as an individual capable of communication—rather than a machine to be "fixed"—parents can prevent the escalation of distress. Ultimately, understanding your baby’s cues and maintaining your own calm are the most effective strategies for navigating the intense early months of parenthood.
