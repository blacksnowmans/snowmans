Semiconductors Lead the Market Charge as Investors Look Past Holiday Jitters

September 20, 2026

  • The Big Picture: The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index has broken through its long-standing resistance level, signaling that the "sideways" slump for South Korean tech giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix may finally be coming to an end.
  • Market Drivers: Capital is aggressively flowing into three core pillars: semiconductors, power infrastructure, and optical communications.
  • Holiday Noise: While historical data suggests a slight dip before the Chuseok (Korean Thanksgiving) holiday, current market fundamentals and global industry growth cycles are far more influential than calendar dates.
  • Tech Dominance: Despite massive volatility during the recent U.S. "triple witching" (simultaneous expiration of index futures, index options, and stock options), technology stocks were the only sector to drive the market higher, led by giants like Nvidia, Broadcom, and Micron.

Semiconductors: The Engine of Growth

The semiconductor sector is entering a new phase of valuation expansion, underpinned by insatiable demand. TSMC, the world’s largest contract chipmaker (foundry), recently notified clients of impending price hikes. When the industry leader raises prices—and clients like Intel and AMD accept them—it proves that demand for AI and data center chips remains robust enough to absorb higher costs without sacrificing volume.

For South Korean investors, this shift is critical. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are showing strong signs of reclaiming their 60-day moving averages. Beyond the giants, "parts, materials, and equipment" (so-called so-bu-jang) companies—such as Hanmi Semiconductor, which leads in high-bandwidth memory (HBM) bonding technology—are poised for a significant rebound. Even with short-term adjustments, the underlying fundamentals of these firms remain exceptionally strong, with massive profit margins and clear long-term growth trajectories through 2026.

The Power Infrastructure Boom

As AI data centers proliferate, the global power grid is facing unprecedented strain. This has turned power infrastructure—specifically companies involved in cables and transformers—into a structural, non-negotiable growth sector. Firms like LS ELECTRIC, Iljin Electric, and Sanil Electric are seeing sustained interest as they provide the essential hardware required for AI energy requirements.

Conversely, investors should approach "thematic" sectors with caution. While optical communication and cybersecurity stocks are generating headlines due to government policies and AI-driven security needs, some of these companies lack the fundamental earnings power of the semiconductor or power infrastructure sectors. It is vital to distinguish between companies providing tangible, high-tech components and those riding temporary hype waves.

The bottom line for investors is clear: ignore the temporary noise caused by holiday schedules or interest rate anxiety. The global market is signaling a transition toward a year-end rally driven by companies with concrete earnings and structural growth, particularly in the semiconductor and power sectors.
