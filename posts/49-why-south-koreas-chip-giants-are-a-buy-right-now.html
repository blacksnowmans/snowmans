Why South Korea's Chip Giants Are a Buy Right Now

September 24, 2026

Global markets are swinging wildly on US political theater and oil price spikes, but South Korea's tech heavyweights are anchored by a simple reality: massive, undeniable corporate earnings.

  • Earnings over noise: Despite fears of slowing growth, giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are pulling in record operating profits driven by an ongoing semiconductor shortage.
  • AI infrastructure spending: Big Tech capital expenditure on artificial intelligence is surging toward $1 billion (about 1.3 trillion won), directly fueling demand for Korean memory chips.
  • KOSPI vs. KOSDAQ divergence: While export-driven large caps on the main KOSPI index hold strong, smaller tech and biotech firms on the secondary KOSDAQ index are buckling under high interest rates.
  • Shareholder returns: Upcoming quarterly dividend payouts—yielding around 1.5% to 2% for Samsung—offer crucial downside protection for long-term investors.

The Macro Noise: Oil, Elections, and US Treasuries

For the average retail investor looking at a sluggish account balance while major indices hover near historic highs, the market feels entirely disconnected. Much of the recent volatility in New York and Seoul stems from political posturing. US political maneuvers regarding Iran have driven crude oil prices and Treasury yields higher, creating jitters worldwide. Yet, beneath the geopolitical theater, the market's risk appetite has been stabilized by shrinking term premiums and cautious optimism around potential US-China trade talks.

At the same time, the divergence between South Korea's mainboard and its smaller cap index couldn't be sharper. While large-cap exporters withstand macroeconomic pressures through sheer cash flow, companies listed on the tech-heavy KOSDAQ are getting squeezed by high borrowing costs. Without immediate relief from interest rates, speculative KOSDAQ rallies remain a high-stakes game, forcing investors to pivot toward quality stocks with verified balance sheets.

Why Korean Memory Giants Hold All the Cards

The core thesis for South Korean equities rests on the memory chip supercycle. Spurred by Meta's aggressive push into consumer-facing AI agents—such as its new wearable-integrated AI services—and skyrocketing global data center demand, big tech firms are loosening their purse strings. US memory maker Micron's recent earnings beat confirms that the chip boom has plenty of runway left, giving investors little reason to panic over short-term cyclical noise.

Ultimately, chasing speculative tech narratives is a mug's game in a high-interest-rate environment. When macro uncertainty strikes, solid fundamentals win out. With memory giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix delivering robust cash flows, aggressive shareholder returns, and reliable dividend yields, patient investors who tune out the political static are positioned to capture the market's next leg up.
