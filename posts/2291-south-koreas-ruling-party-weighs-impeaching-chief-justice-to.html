South Korea’s Ruling Party Weighs Impeaching Chief Justice to Block Ex-PM Han Duck-soo's Release

November 13, 2026

Lawmakers from South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party of Korea are pushing to impeach Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de ahead of a pivotal parliamentary session on the 19th, fueled by outrage over the judiciary's move toward releasing former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo.

  • The Spark: The potential release of Han Duck-soo—a key figure from the administration of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol, who was removed from office and is currently imprisoned—has ignited fierce criticism that the courts are coddling figures from the disgraced former regime.
  • Targeting the Chief Justice: Ruling party lawmakers argue that Chief Justice Jo Hee-de’s judiciary is substituting political calculation for constitutional justice, creating grounds for a rare legislative impeachment of the nation's top judge.
  • Inquiry vs. Direct Action: A sharp internal dispute has erupted within the Democratic Party over whether to launch a time-consuming parliamentary investigation first or move straight to an impeachment vote to meet the ticking clock.
  • Enforcing Party Discipline: Following strategic consultations involving President Lee Jae-myung, party leadership is working to secure a unified front to prevent internal dissent ahead of the critical plenary vote.

The Dilemma: Fast-Track Impeachment or Fact-Finding Probe

The Democratic Party’s internal deliberations reflect deep tactical divisions. During party caucuses, lawmakers such as Noh Jong-myeon pushed back against leadership proposals for a preliminary parliamentary investigation. Noh argued that an inquiry would simply drag on due to administrative resistance, squandering the political window before the 19th plenary session when Han’s release could become irreversible. In this view, legislative impeachment is not an attack on the rule of law, but the only remaining constitutional check on an unaccountable bench.

Even so, party strategists acknowledge that pursuing the head of the judiciary requires bulletproof legal justification. Lawmakers Han Min-soo and Noh have both cautioned that moral outrage alone cannot sustain an impeachment trial. To survive constitutional scrutiny and public skepticism, the party must document concrete statutory or constitutional violations committed by Chief Justice Jo, rather than leaning solely on accusations of judicial arrogance.

High Stakes for the Lee Administration and the Bench

Attempting to remove a sitting Supreme Court Chief Justice carries enormous political peril. Critics and moderates warn that dragging the highest court into partisan crossfire risks triggering a public backlash against judicial interference. The judiciary has pushed back against these threats, dismissing judicial impeachment as a symptom of political regression.

Yet for the ruling party, the risk of inaction is seen as far worse. Democratic Party leaders argue that hesitating out of fear of blowback would signal impunity for a judicial establishment protecting relics of Mr. Yoon Suk-yeol's ousted government. With President Lee urging rigorous internal debate, the December 19 session stands as a decisive trial of the ruling party’s legislative power and its appetite for a direct confrontation with the courts.
