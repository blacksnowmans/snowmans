Record Exports Meet Market Panic in South Korea’s Chip Sector

November 17, 2026

South Korea’s semiconductor industry is posting historic export gains even as a warning over plunging memory prices and leadership turmoil at Nvidia trigger a massive foreign sell-off in Seoul.

  • Flash-memory shock: Bank of America warned that spot prices for NAND flash—a memory chip widely used in consumer electronics—could drop 20% in a month due to sluggish PC and smartphone demand, prompting foreign investors to dump roughly $1.2 billion (1.7 trillion won) in South Korean equities and sending the benchmark KOSPI index down 3.59% to 5,100.
  • Nvidia fallout ripples outward: Following CEO Jensen Huang’s sudden resignation and growing U.S. scrutiny over alleged China export-control violations, HSBC slashed Nvidia’s price target by 54% to $170, while Taiwanese foundry giant TSMC launched a $500 million venture fund to back new chip startups and U.S. rival Micron paused a $4.5 billion high-bandwidth memory (HBM) plant in Taiwan.
  • Korea’s chipmakers diverge: Domestic memory leaders Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both suffered steep share declines, yet Samsung is exiting legacy 8-inch foundry lines to focus on advanced AI chips while SK Hynix is ordering equipment for next-generation HBM4 memory ahead of planned 2027 mass production.
  • Booming exports, nervous economists: South Korea’s October information and communications technology (ICT) exports surged 202% year-on-year to a record $70.3 billion (about 98 trillion won), though Bloomberg Economics warned that this chip-driven surge masks weak domestic demand and risks pushing South Korea’s central bank, the Bank of Korea, into overly tight monetary policy.

The K-Shaped Chip Divide

The market turbulence exposes a widening split inside the global memory industry. Demand for specialized enterprise solid-state drives and HBM for AI data centers remains tight—so much so that AI developer Anthropic flagged HBM supply constraints as a business risk in its pre-listing filing. Meanwhile, everyday consumer devices remain stuck in a slump, leaving standard memory chips oversupplied.

In my view, soaring AI memory costs are now cannibalizing the broader hardware market. Counterpoint data shows memory has jumped from 20% to 54% of production costs for budget smartphones over the past year, forcing makers like Samsung and Xiaomi to raise prices in emerging markets and turn more heavily to recycled memory.

A Fragile One-Engine Economy

For South Korea, the bigger danger is structural over-reliance. Semiconductors now account for 42.7% of total exports and drove more than 73% of October’s export growth, pushing cumulative national exports toward a historic $1 trillion milestone. Yet with SK Hynix drawing over 60% of its revenue from the U.S. market and Washington weighing tighter curbs on China—potentially inviting Chinese export retaliation on critical minerals like gallium and germanium—Seoul’s flagship industry sits squarely in the geopolitical crossfire.

That leaves South Korean markets unusually exposed. When a single Wall Street note and a shake-up at one American chip designer can trigger a sharp sell-off across the KOSPI, it underscores how urgently South Korea needs growth engines beyond semiconductors.
