How to Survive South Korea's Chuseok Holiday Without Ruining Your Back

September 18, 2026

South Korea's major Chuseok holiday brings family reunions and delicious food, but it also triggers grueling hours of traffic and inevitable weight gain—making pre-holiday stretching essential to protect your hips, spine, and waistline.

  • The Threat: Hours trapped in holiday traffic paralyze the hips and put intense pressure on the spine, while rich holiday meals rapidly pack on body fat.
  • The Fix: Simple in-seat or roadside stretches—such as internal and external hip rotations using a wiper-like movement—relieve pelvic stiffness during rest stops.
  • The Combatant: Activating the calves (the body's "second heart") through heel raises and engaging in targeted core leg-lifts helps burn post-meal blood sugar before it converts to fat.
  • The Takeaway: Neglecting your body out of laziness is the true culprit behind holiday syndrome; a quick 10-minute stretch at a highway rest stop can cut travel fatigue in half.

Relieving Travel Fatigue and Back Pain on the Road

Long hours behind the wheel force the pelvis to tilt and compress the spine under relentless pressure. During a recent broadcast featuring host Kim Ou-joon alongside guests Moon Gyo-hun and Baek So-dam, the panel demonstrated practical mobility routines to combat this. Simple movements like hip rotations—turning the legs inward and outward like windshield wipers—can breathe life back into a stiff pelvis. The panel emphasized that a quick 10-minute movement break at a highway rest stop is far more effective than just grabbing snacks and rushing back to the driver's seat.

Beyond driving fatigue, the holiday brings the dual terror of blood sugar spikes and weight gain from endless streams of jeon (pancakes) and songpyeon (half-moon rice cakes). To prevent excess sugar from turning into stubborn belly fat, the studio workshop introduced simple, high-impact routines: standing calf raises to stimulate circulation and dynamic leg-and-arm combinations designed to squeeze the abdominal muscles. While laughing at each other's awkward attempts at fitness, the participants highlighted a stark truth: we pour immense effort into preparing holiday food, yet neglect the basic upkeep of our own blood vessels and joints.

Protecting Your Body This Holiday Season

It is easy to get swept up in family gatherings and culinary traditions, but letting your physical health collapse in the process is a mistake. You do not need an expensive gym membership or fancy gear to stay healthy over the break. Whether it is doing a quick side-stretch by the car door at a rest stop or doing rhythm-based heel raises in the living room, taking a few minutes to care for your body prevents the post-holiday slump. This Chuseok, swap out groans of back pain for intentional movement, and protect your physical well-being before the scale pushes you to tears.
