Why Reaching $700,000 in Financial Assets Is Just the Beginning of the Hardest Part

September 01, 2026

  • The milestone trap: Hitting 1 billion won (about $700,000) is widely viewed as the finish line of economic freedom, but experts argue it is merely the starting line for fighting aggressive inflation.
  • Shift from offense to defense: Choking away savings in traditional bank accounts is financial neglect; investors must pivot from chasing high-growth stocks to preserving capital and generating steady cash flow.
  • Tax literacy matters: Overlooking tax-advantaged accounts like Korea's pension savings systems or ignoring capital gains tax rules can quietly erode wealth faster than market downturns.

Accumulating a net worth of about $700,000 (1 billion won) feels like reaching the summit of financial independence, but financial commentators warn that the real war begins right after. Leaving this sum sitting safely in low-interest savings accounts is an absolute death sentence for your purchasing power in an inflationary environment. Instead of popping champagne, the focus must immediately shift from asset accumulation to aggressive asset preservation and cash-flow generation.

Navigating this next phase requires mastering boring, unglamorous fundamentals like tax efficiency and strategic gifting rather than chasing high-risk home runs. For instance, ignoring tax-advantaged retirement accounts over fears of early-withdrawal penalties often costs investors more in lifelong taxes than any penalty ever would. Similarly, timing asset transfers to children during market downturns can dramatically reduce future tax burdens. Ultimately, failing to understand tax strategy—what some call "tax illiteracy"—is far more dangerous than simple financial ignorance.

The Playbook for Surviving Inflation and Taxes

Why Capital Preservation Trumps Speculative Growth

Once your portfolio crosses the $700,000 threshold, the rules of the game change completely. Pouring all your money into aggressive growth stocks is no longer a smart strategy; it is a liability. Investors in their 50s and beyond need to build a defensive shield centered around dividend stocks and reliable yield-generating assets to beat inflation by a modest 1 to 2 percentage points. High-net-worth households typically keep cash deposits under 20%, channeling the rest into a mix of dividend-yielding equities, exchange-traded funds, and tax-sheltered vehicles.

Minimizing tax drag is just as critical as chasing returns. For example, direct investments in overseas equities come with a stiff 22% capital gains tax above basic exemptions, making year-end tax-loss harvesting an essential December ritual. Utilizing government-backed tax credits through retirement accounts is another non-negotiable step. The takeaway is simple: beating inflation and taxes is tedious work, but those willing to endure the bureaucratic headache are the only ones who truly secure their financial future.
