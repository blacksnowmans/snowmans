Ruling Party Fractures as Factional Warfare Over Leaked Audio Escalates to Ethics Tribunal

November 16, 2026

Internal strife within South Korea's ruling Democratic Party reached a boiling point on November 15 as party leader Kim Min-seok referred Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee to the party's Ethics Tribunal over leaked remarks concerning President Lee Jae-myung.

  • What happened: Supreme Council member Choi Min-hee publicly apologized for incorrectly claiming in a leaked recording that President Lee Jae-myung had opposed the impeachment of Yoon Suk-yeol, but she immediately launched a counter-offensive accusing party rivals of secret ties to the religious group Shincheonji.
  • What it changes: Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok bypassed lengthy deliberation to send Choi to the Ethics Tribunal just three days after initiating an inspection, prompting rival factions to retaliate by filing a disciplinary petition against Kim himself.
  • Who is affected: President Lee Jae-myung’s administration faces growing political distraction from inside his own party, while first-term lawmakers and regional party members are drawn into an escalating loyalty war.

Leaked Remarks and Mutual Accusations Trigger Open Conflict

The feud erupted after a leaked audio recording revealed Choi claiming that President Lee Jae-myung had opposed the impeachment of Yoon Suk-yeol during the former leader's removal from office. Choi issued a public apology on the morning of November 15, attributing her statement to a memory lapse. However, the apology failed to cool tempers after President Lee reportedly expressed bitter frustration, noting that "the hardest thing is an arrow shot from behind." Hardline pro-Lee factions immediately demanded Choi’s complete expulsion from the party, accusing her of undermining the president's legitimacy.

Cornered by calls for her removal, Choi launched a sharp counter-attack. She demanded a formal party investigation into an alleged "anti-Lee" secret alliance connected to the controversial religious group Shincheonji. While five local council members from her constituency in Namyangju rallied to her defense, party leadership figures dismissed her allegations as absurd. The conflict rapidly transformed from an apology over misstatements into an all-out war over party identity and religious ties.

Leadership Escalation and Deepening Factional Divides

Facing unmanageable chaos ahead of his 100th day in office, Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok took swift action by transferring Choi’s case to the Ethics Tribunal. Late that evening, "Deomincho," an influential association of first-term Democratic Party lawmakers, held an urgent virtual meeting that ran past 11 p.m. While lawmakers agreed on the gravity of the situation, they deferred a final stance on Choi's fate until the following day.

Meanwhile, factions aligned with senior lawmaker Jung Cheong-rae pushed back by demanding structural party reform and filing a disciplinary petition against Kim Min-seok, framing his ethics referral as factional suppression. With the main opposition People Power Party demanding a special prosecutor into the Shincheonji allegations and civic groups filing police complaints, the ruling party's internal clash threatens to consume the government's legislative momentum regardless of the tribunal's ultimate decision.
